# A Stackelberg Game Framework with Drainability Guardrails for Pricing and Scaling in Multi-Tenant GPU Cloud Platforms 

Junji Yan, Asrin Efe Yorulmaz, Hanchen Zhou, and Tamer Bas ¸ar 

Abstract — Modern Graphics Processing Unit (GPU)-backed services must satisfy strict latency service-level objectives (SLOs) while controlling spare-capacity cost. In multi-tenant GPU cloud platforms, this trade-off is inherently dynamic because workload demand is endogenous; specifically, pricing shapes the submissions of heterogeneous tenants, which subse-quently impact congestion and delay. We formulate the joint pricing-and-scaling problem as a large-population Stackelberg game problem, and we derive an explicit equilibrium demand map. The resulting closed-loop model reveals a structural failure mode in which delay-insensitive workloads sustain a residual demand floor, making the backlog undrainable under bounded price and service capacity. This observation motivates a computable drainability guardrail that certifies uniformly negative drift in the residual-demand regime. For any fixed price-capacity pair satisfying the drainability guardrail, we establish a unique operating point and global convergence towards it under a checkable step-size condition. Building on this fixed-pair analysis, we further develop an optimizer-agnostic action shield for the full dynamic problem and show empirically that it improves safety and robustness for model-free reinforcement learning (RL) in this setting. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

In recent years, latency-oriented service-level objectives (SLOs) have become a hard constraint for many Graphics Processing Unit (GPU)-backed services. Meanwhile, meeting SLOs under bursty demand often requires keeping spare GPU capacity online, which can substantially increase costs [1]. GPU platforms therefore face a persistent tension: they must maintain service quality while limiting the cost of ex-cess capacity in systems with frictional capacity adjustment. This challenge has motivated a growing body of work on SLO-aware GPU serving and capacity management [2], [3]. Most existing SLO-aware serving and autoscaling systems focus on the supply side and treat workload demand as externally given [4], [5]. In multi-tenant GPU cloud plat-forms, however, demand is often endogenous and shaped dynamically by posted prices and congestion. The posted price acts as a direct demand-side lever, influencing which tenants participate and how aggressively they submit work, while congestion-induced delay feeds back into demand as service quality changes. To address this gap, we study a joint price–capacity control problem for a multi-tenant GPU cloud platform in a closed loop, aiming to maximize long-run profit while meeting latency SLO constraints. We adopt a Stackelberg game formulation [6] to make the demand-side feedback explicit. At each decision epoch, the platform, leader, posts a unit price and a capacity target, and tenants, followers, respond by choosing submission rates based on the posted price and the public congestion signal. To capture heterogeneity, we approximate the population by finitely many mean-field types [7] with distinct price and delay sensitivities, including delay-insensitive but price-sensitive types that represent opportunis-tic or speculative demand. Each type solves a strictly concave stage-wise problem, yielding a unique best response. Aggre-gating across types gives a unique equilibrium demand map, which, together with the backlog recursion and frictional capacity dynamics, induces a low-dimensional closed-loop system. This Stackelberg formulation also reveals a structural fail-ure mode that is absent in exogenous-demand formulations and therefore prevents a direct move to nominal dynamic op-timization. That is, under admissible leader actions, the sys-tem may become undrainable in the high-congestion regime. This issue arises for two main reasons. First, although demand can change rapidly, physical GPU capacity adjusts slowly due to provisioning frictions, such as cold starts [8], setup delays [9], and scale-down inertia [10]. Moreover, frequent capacity adjustments can induce oscillations and incur switching costs. Second, tenant demand responses to price and congestion are heterogeneous and need not vanish uniformly as congestion grows. Specifically, while delay-sensitive users eventually drop out, delay-insensitive types continue submitting work, sustaining a residual demand floor. As a result, even under severe congestion, the platform may continue to face persistent demand pressure. These observations motivate characterizing the admissible leader actions under which the backlog remains in a stable and drainable regime. Such a structural characterization is a necessary pre-requisite for dynamic learning. Standard model-free rein-forcement learning (RL) relies on trial-and-error exploration [11] and may sample actions that push the system into an undrainable regime. Given capacity frictions and persistent residual demand under congestion, recovery can be difficult and service quality may degrade. Explicitly identifying the drainable action region thus provides a theoretical guardrail

Related Work. Our work relates to several lines of research on cloud control, pricing, and congestion-sensitive demand. SLO-aware serving and autoscaling systems develop serving policies and capacity control mechanisms to meet latency targets under provisioning frictions (e.g., MArk [4] and QLM [5]), and they typically treat demand as exogenous. GPU pricing systems study pricing mechanisms and charging models for GPU resources, emphasizing price formation and price–resource mismatch [13]. In queueing and congestion models, dynamic pricing uses price together with delay information to shape demand [14]–[16]. Related leader– follower formulations appear in large-population network settings [17], in congestion pricing with heterogeneous users [18], and in cloud pricing and provisioning models [19], [20]. These queueing and Stackelberg game models provide equilibrium demand responses under congestion. Building on these ideas, we study a SLO-oriented multi-tenant GPU cloud problem with endogenous demand under provisioning frictions and derive a computable drainability guardrail to-gether with an online action shield for safe learning.

V. E XPERIMENTS 

Having constructed the guarded control interface in Sec-tion IV, we now evaluate its numerical behavior. The con-ducted experiments address two questions. Second, in Section V-C, we evaluate whether the proposed guardrail-aware action shield improves safety and learning stability in model-free RL for the pricing-scaling task under both steady and bursty demand.

C. Guardrail Effects in Model-Free RL 

We next test whether the proposed action shield improves execution safety and training stability in model-free RL. We compare unshielded and shielded tabular Q-learning to isolate the action shield as an execution-time safety layer. Both agents are trained on the same discretized table and executed on the continuous off-grid dynamics via nearest-grid state lookup. Training uses 8000 episodes of length 200 ,with step size α = 0 .15 and an ϵ-greedy schedule decaying from 1.0 to 0.05 over the first 80% of training. Every 400 episodes, we run greedy evaluation rollouts of length 250 and record the mean number of unsafe executed steps; we also track the cumulative number of training episodes that crash by hitting the truncation boundary. As shown in Fig. 3, guardrail-aware action shield drives un-safe executions to zero and substantially suppresses crash accumulation, indicating that it blocks unsafe exploratory actions that would trigger rapid backlog growth. Without the action shield, exploration repeatedly enters a residual-demand regime, leading to frequent crashes. Overall, the results corroborate the drainability analysis and highlight the action shield as an effective mechanism for safe exploration. Finally, we test whether the shielded policy can preserve safe and stable operation under a burst demand shift. To this end, we stress-test the learned policy under an out-of-distribution demand perturbation relative to the steady-demand training setting [26]. Concretely, we scale the utility weights of all tenant types by a factor of 3 during the time window t ∈ [40 , 70) , i.e., wk(t) = 3 wk during the burst and wk(t) = wk otherwise. We use a mild initial condition 

sburst 0 = (0 , 2, 2, 1) so that both policies remain well-behaved before the burst. We evaluate greedy policies learned under steady demand, with no exploration during evaluation: the unshielded policy executes at = arg max a bQ(st, a ),while the shielded policy executes ˜at = Γ( st, a t) using a time-varying guardrail recomputed under the current wk(t).Fig. 4 shows a clear separation. The unshielded policy fre-quently proposes prices below the guardrail threshold, enters an effectively undrainable regime, and drives the backlog toward the truncation boundary. In contrast, shielded execu-tion keeps the realized actions guardrail-feasible throughout, raises the executed price to the elevated safe threshold during the burst, maintains bounded backlog dynamics, and returns to a stable operating level after the burst subsides. Overall, the burst test shows that action shield improves robustness to abrupt demand shifts, whereas the unshielded policy fails to recover safely. Taken together, these experiments support two empirical claims: finite-horizon planning reliably approximates the VI benchmark on guarded tables and transfers well to off-grid execution, while guardrail-aware action shield substantially improves safety and stability in model-free RL under both steady and bursty demand. VI. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

In this work, we have studied the joint pricing-and-scaling control problem in multi-tenant GPU cloud platforms with provisioning frictions and heterogeneous demand through a large-population Stackelberg game formulation. The model assumes the demand is endogenous to posted prices and congestion, and reveals a residual-demand mechanism under extreme congestion that can render the backlog structurally undrainable under bounded price and capacity. To address this, we have analyzed a fixed-pair backlog recursion, proved operating-point uniqueness and global convergence under a checkable step-size condition, and derived an interpretable drainability guardrail that certifies uniform negative drift in the residual regime. Building on the insights from the fixed-pair backlog recursion, we have proposed a piecewise-explicit safe-price map and an optimizer-agnostic action shield for the dynamic problem. Furthermore, the experi-ments have shown that the proposed guardrail-aware control interface provides a reliable planning approximation and improves the safety and robustness of model-free RL for this pricing and scaling task under off-grid execution and demand shifts. The present analysis has adopted a deterministic stage-wise demand model and a stylized queue-based congestion proxy to keep the closed-loop structure explicit and tractable. An important direction for future work would be to extend the framework to stochastic demand and service dynamics. Nevertheless, our framework suggests a tractable way to combine equilibrium demand modeling, safety certification, and learning-based control in congestion-sensitive systems with endogenous demand and delayed capacity adjustment.
