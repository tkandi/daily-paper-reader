## ABSTRACT 

Federated training of foundation models is constrained by client memory and communication costs. LoRA-based methods reduce these costs through low-rank adapters, but their fixed rank budget can limit adaptation. Gradient low-rank opti-mization offers greater flexibility, yet independently chosen client subspaces create a problem we term subspace fragmentation : local projections interact with data heterogeneity to bias aggregated directions, while aggregation can increase update rank and communication cost. Thus, accurate local gradient compression need not preserve global descent. We propose FedLore , which shares a low-rank opti-mization basis within each round and refreshes it across rounds. The shared basis enables exact aggregation in low-rank coordinates and eliminates the identified projection bias. Subspace refresh allows the accumulated model update to exceed the per-round rank budget. We characterize the aggregation bias and establish an O(T −1/2) stationarity bound for the projected-SGD variant under a global-gradient coverage condition and standard smoothness and variance assumptions, with bounded gradient heterogeneity. Experiments on vision and language tasks, including federated pre-training, show that FedLore outperforms the evaluated low-rank adapter baselines and matches or exceeds full-parameter training, while reducing communication and optimizer-state memory. 

## INTRODUCTION 

Foundation models have become central to modern vision and language systems, yet much of the data needed for further training resides on private devices or across institutions with restricted data access Bommasani et al. (2021). Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative training without centralizing these data McMahan et al. (2016); Kairouz & McMahan (2021). Scaling this paradigm to foundation models, however, requires addressing two coupled resource bottlenecks: maintaining optimizer states on memory-constrained clients and repeatedly exchanging high-dimensional model updates. Full-parameter federated optimization ( FedFull ) incurs substantial costs on both fronts, particularly with adaptive optimizers such as Adam/AdamW Kingma & Ba (2014). A common approach combines FL with parameter-efficient fine-tuning, particularly LoRA (Hu et al., 2021). Methods such as FedIT Zhang et al. (2023a) reduce optimizer-state memory and communication by training and exchanging low-rank adapters (Wu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Yi et al., 2023). For a standard rank-r adapter, however, the model correction remains rank-constrained, even though its row and column spaces can evolve during training. This constraint can limit adaptation when heterogeneous data or training from scratch requires updates spanning many directions. The key question is therefore: Can federated training retain the resource efficiency of low-rank updates without imposing a fixed rank budget on the accumulated model change? 

Gradient low-rank optimization offers a promising starting point. GaLore (Zhao et al., 2024) main-tains optimizer states in projected gradient coordinates while updating model weights in the original parameter space. This separates the dimension of the optimization state from the rank of the accumu-lated model change: successive low-rank increments can span an increasingly rich set of directions as the projection changes. The second term captures the interaction between projection disagreement and data heterogeneity. It vanishes when all clients share a projector, but need not vanish when each local projector captures most of its own gradient energy. Thus, accurate local gradient compression does not by itself ensure an informative global update . Our analysis makes this distinction explicit through a sharp bias bound and a quadratic example in which exact local SVD projections stall away from stationarity. Subspace fragmentation also affects communication. With a basis fixed within each round and zero weight decay, each client’s accumulated update remains rank at most r and admits an exact low-rank factorization. Nevertheless, aggregating updates from N distinct client subspaces can produce a matrix of rank up to min {m, n, N r }. Low-rank client updates therefore do not automatically yield a rank-r global update. This exposes two connected challenges: 

Challenge 1: Client-specific subspaces increase the cost of exact communication. 

Challenge 2: Federated subspace fragmentation under heterogeneous clients. 

We propose FedLore , a federated optimization framework that shares optimization coordinates within each round and refreshes them across rounds. Clients optimize in a common orthonormal basis, upload their accumulated low-rank coordinates, and provide candidate subspaces for the next round. The server constructs the next shared basis from the leading eigenspace of the averaged candidate projectors, making subspace aggregation invariant to the signs and rotations of the candidate bases. This design separates two roles of low-rank structure. Within a round , sharing the basis makes coordinate aggregation exactly equivalent to averaging the local endpoint models, without additional rank truncation. Under full participation with synchronized model caches, the aggregated coordinates 2and the next basis suffice for low-rank model synchronization. Across rounds , refreshing the basis allows the accumulated model change to extend beyond a single rank-r correction (Figures 2). Sharing removes the projection–heterogeneity coupling term, whereas subspace selection determines how much of the global gradient is retained. We distinguish these properties explicitly: our convergence guarantee requires global-gradient coverage, which agreement alone does not ensure. 

Contributions. Our main contributions are as follows: 

• We identify subspace fragmentation : client-specific projections couple with data heterogeneity, allowing locally accurate compression to suppress global descent and cost of exact communication. 

• We propose FedLore , which shares a low-rank basis within each round and refreshes it across rounds. This enables exact coordinate aggregation, compact optimizer states, and accumulated updates beyond rank r. With synchronized caches and full participation, communication remains low-rank. 

• We show that shared projections eliminate projection–heterogeneity coupling in projected SGD and establish an O(T −1/2) stationarity bound under standard smoothness, variance, and heterogeneity assumptions with global-gradient coverage. Across vision and language tasks, including federated pre-training, FedLore combines competitive model quality with reduced communication and optimizer-state memory, while allowing accumulated updates beyond the per-round rank budget. 

## RELATED WORK 

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Large Models. As pretrained models scale, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) enables adaptation under limited computational and memory budgets (Che et al., 2023). LoRA parameterizes updates to frozen pretrained weights using trainable low-rank factors, substantially reducing gradient and optimizer-state memory (Hu et al., 2021). Subsequent methods extend this approach through adaptive rank allocation and alternative parameterizations (Zhang et al., 2023b; Liu et al., 2024b). However, although standard LoRA’s update subspaces evolve during optimization, its accumulated weight updates remain rank-constrained, potentially limiting expressivity under substantial distribution shift or when training from scratch. Low-rank factorization can also introduce optimization challenges (Cosson et al., 2023), motivating memory-efficient methods with more flexible weight updates (Zhao et al., 2024). 

Extending LoRA to Federated Learning. LoRA is widely used for parameter-efficient federated fine-tuning because it reduces communication and optimizer-state memory costs. FedIT (Zhang et al., 2023a) aggregates client LoRA factors, while subsequent methods refine aggregation, initialization, or factor sharing. FLoRA (Wang et al., 2024) and LoRA-FAIR (Bian et al., 2025) improve aggregation, whereas FedSA-LoRA (Guo et al., 2025) and FFA-LoRA (Sun et al., 2024) modify how the A and B

factors are shared or optimized. FR-LoRA (Yan et al., 2025) combines factor reinitialization with residual low-rank updates, allowing the accumulated model change to exceed the per-round rank budget. In contrast, FedLore optimizes projected gradients in a shared, evolving basis and enables exact aggregation of accumulated updates in low-rank coordinates.

ABLATION STUDY 

Effect of Subspace Rank. Figure 3(d) studies the effect of the projection rank r on LLaMA-350M pre-training. Larger ranks accelerate convergence and reduce the final validation loss, since the projected subspace captures more informative update directions. The improvement is significant from r = 64 to r = 256 , while increasing r to 384 brings only marginal gains. This indicates that 

FedLore achieves a favorable accuracy and communication trade-off with a moderate rank. 

Ablation study. Table 4 examines the roles of shared projections and dynamic subspace updates in LLaMA-350M pre-training on C4. Removing the shared projector increases validation loss from 3.554 to 3.890 and communication from 0.36 to 0.68 GB under the evaluated protocol, supporting the benefits of shared optimization coordinates for both optimization and communication efficiency. Fixing the shared subspace reduces communication to 0.18 GB but increases validation loss to 5.952, indicating that subspace refresh is important for maintaining optimization capacity throughout training. Together, these results support the complementary roles of subspace sharing and refresh in balancing model quality and communication cost. 

Communication and Memory Efficiency Analysis. Table 4 summarizes the communication and memory efficiency on LLaMA 350M. FedFull incurs the largest cost, requiring 0.68 GB communica-tion cost and 1.37 GB optimizer memory. Local GaLore reduces optimizer memory to 0.54 GB, but still communicates full updates. FedIT lowers communication to 0.36 GB, while requiring 0.72 GB optimizer memory. By contrast, FedLore achieves the best trade-off, with 0.36 GB total communi-cation and 0.54 GB optimizer memory. This represents approximately 47% lower communication than FedFull/Local GaLore. 

## CONCLUSION 

We introduced FedLore , a communication- and memory-efficient framework for FL foundation-model training. A shared, evolving basis enables exact low-rank aggregation without imposing a fixed rank constraint on accumulated updates. Our analysis characterizes projection–heterogeneity coupling and establishes projected-SGD convergence under global-gradient coverage. Experiments across vision, language, and federated pre-training support the approach. This work studies communication- and memory-efficient federated learning using existing vision and language benchmarks: CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, Food-101, C4, SNLI, AG News, QQP, DBPedia-14, QNLI, and MNLI. Our experiments simulate federated clients using benchmark partitions rather than collecting data from real users. Although FedLore keeps training data local within the federated protocol, communicated updates and shared subspaces may still expose information about local data. Neither federated training nor low-rank projection alone provides a formal privacy guarantee; FedLore does not establish differential privacy or resistance to inference attacks. Models trained with this approach may also inherit biases or harmful content from the underlying datasets and pretrained models. Deployment with sensitive data therefore requires additional privacy safeguards and application-specific evaluation.

Domain-based grouping. Since C4 does not provide classification labels, we construct heteroge-neous client datasets using the source information associated with each document. For every training document, we extract the hostname from its URL, convert it to lowercase, and remove a leading www.

These groups serve only as partitioning metadata; they are not prediction targets and are not added to the model inputs. Source identity provides a proxy for differences in document content and writing style, rather than an explicit semantic topic label.

## RELATED WORK 

Communication-efficient federated optimization. Communication-efficient federated learning commonly allows clients to perform multiple local updates between aggregation rounds. FedBCGD reduces the amount of information transmitted in each round by updating and communicating parameter blocks, and further incorporates drift control and variance reduction in its accelerated variant (Liu et al., 2024a). This approach addresses the size of each message. In contrast, CTP-FL 

communicates one model-sized vector per participating client and changes where local gradients are evaluated: clients query the same server-defined predictive trajectory, so their updates can be averaged without first combining models that have followed different local trajectories. 

Alignment under heterogeneous data. Several recent methods study different forms of local– global misalignment. FedSWA and FedMoSWA use stochastic weight averaging and momentum-based control to improve generalization under highly heterogeneous data (Liu et al., 2025a). FedNSAM examines the mismatch between local and global flatness and uses a global Nesterov di-rection to improve their consistency (Liu et al., 2025b). These methods primarily target the properties of the resulting solution, including flatness and generalization. Our focus is the geometry of gradient evaluation during a communication round: when all clients evaluate at common points, averaging their gradients estimates the gradient of the global objective at those points, irrespective of how different the individual client gradients are.
