## ABSTRACT 

Data selection is already a central bottleneck in large-language-model training, where web-scale corpora are noisy and token budgets are finite. In continual pre-training (CPT), it becomes a forgetting-control problem: a poorly chosen target-domain corpus can overwrite capabilities encoded in the pretrained check-point. Existing CPT practice either scores candidates with parameter-agnostic scalars such as perplexity, or mitigates forgetting by spending many extra general-domain replay tokens. Neither strategy directly asks how training on a candidate will move the model parameters. We show that loss-based selection causes the post-CPT Fisher diagonal to drift downward on exactly the high-Fisher coordinates the pretrained model had committed to, while leaving low-Fisher coordinates largely untouched. This asymmetry exposes a parameter-space mechanism for catastrophic forgetting. Motivated by this observation, we propose a Fisher-aware CPT selector that decomposes each candidate’s gradient into an anchor component, which mea-sures perturbation along committed parameter directions, and a frontier component, which measures update capacity in unconstrained low-Fisher subspaces. We aggre-gate these signals with a log-determinant submodular objective and optimize it in a single pass using a scalable streaming data selection pipeline. On TinyLlama-1.1B and Llama-3.1-8B CPT over medical data, our selector improves target-domain quality while bounding forgetting on held-out pretraining benchmarks. Most im-portantly, it is substantially more token-efficient than forgetting-aware replay. 1B selected tokens already outperform the replay strategy trained with 10B tokens on both adaptation and forgetting, giving a 10 × token-efficiency advantage. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Data selection (Xie et al., 2023b;a; Zhou et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023a;b) is one of the few practical levers for making large-language-model training efficient: web-scale corpora are noisy, token budgets are finite, and the examples that enter training determine what knowledge is acquired per unit compute. The lever becomes even more consequential in continual pre-training (Ibrahim et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025a) (CPT), where a pretrained LLM is updated on a new domain, language, or temporal slice rather than trained from scratch (Ibrahim et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025a). In CPT, data selection is not only about learning faster; it is also a forgetting-control mechanism. A poorly chosen target-domain update can silently overwrite the very capabilities that made the pretrained checkpoint valuable (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017; Li & Lee, 2024; Shi et al., 2025). Thus a CPT selector must solve two coupled problems at once: acquire new knowledge from the target domain while preserving the old knowledge already encoded in the model. The standard toolkit is poorly matched to this requirement. Quality classifiers and rule-based filters (Peng et al., 2025; Calian et al., 2025; Marion et al., 2023) score a candidate by cleanliness or topical fit, while per-sample loss and perplexity (Sorscher et al., 2022) treat the model as a black box whose only output is a scalar. These signals describe a document under the current model, but they do not say how training on it would move the parameters. Replay-based CPT mitigates forgetting from another direction by mixing general-domain data back into training, but it pays for retention with many additional tokens and still leaves the target-domain selection problem unresolved. What is 1arXiv:2610.02593v1 [cs.LG] 1 Oct 2026 Preprint. missing is a selector that can identify target-domain examples that are both useful for adaptation and structurally unlikely to interfere with retained capability. Our starting point is a simple parameter-space observation. The Fisher information (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) of a pretrained model encodes, direction by direction, how strongly the predictive distribution depends on perturbing the parameters. We empirically find that parameter-agnostic method cause the post-CPT Fisher diagonal to drift downward on exactly the high-Fisher coordinates the pretrained model had committed to, while leaving low-Fisher coordinates largely untouched. This is precisely the asymmetry of catastrophic forgetting: committed directions are damaged, and uncommitted directions are underused. The observation suggests that selection should be performed not only in data space or loss space, but in the Fisher geometry of the pretrained model. In this paper, we propose to use the Fisher information to decide which candidates enter CPT. For each candidate, we decompose its gradient into two parameter-aware components. An anchor component quantifies perturbation along high-Fisher directions that carry retained capability, while a frontier component captures update capacity in low-Fisher subspaces where the pretrained model is less committed. An effective CPT sample must balance both: it should avoid destructive interference with existing capability while supplying genuinely new, non-redundant information. This curvature-aware decomposition turns forgetting control and acquisition into a single selection signal rather than two post-hoc training heuristics. To select a subset rather than rank candidates independently, we aggregate the anchor and frontier signals with a log-determinant objective. The resulting utility rewards gradient volume in the two Fisher-induced geometries, so it favors candidates that are high-value and mutually non-redundant. This objective is monotone submodular, so offline greedy selection attains the classical (1 − 1/e )

approximation guarantee of (Nemhauser et al., 1978). For large-scale CPT streams, we maximize the same objective in one pass with Sieve-Streaming (Badanidiyuru et al., 2014), restrict the decomposi-tion to the LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) adapter subspace, replace the full Fisher with its diagonal, project gradients via TRAK (Park et al., 2023), and use Sherman–Morrison rank-1 updates of the per-sieve Gram inverses to reduce the per-candidate cost to quadratic in a small projected dimension. Empirically, on CPT of TinyLlama-1.1B and Llama-3.1-8B over medical data, our selector improves target-domain quality while bounding degradation on held-out pretraining benchmarks. The most direct comparison is against forgetting-aware replay: on TinyLlama-1.1B, our 1B-token selected subset already outperforms the replay strategy trained with 10B tokens on both adaptation and forgetting, giving a ≥ 10 × token-efficiency advantage. The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • We propose decomposing each candidate’s gradient into an anchor component and a frontier component using the pretrained model’s diagonal Fisher information, yielding a parameter-aware signal that simultaneously measures interference with existing knowledge and acqui-sition of new knowledge. • We aggregate the two components into a log det Gram-volume set function and instantiate the classical Sieve-Streaming algorithm to maximize it in a single pass over the candidate pool, with per-sample cost independent of pool size. • On TinyLlama-1.1B and Llama-3 8B, our selector improves target-domain quality at a fraction of the training tokens used by random and perplexity baselines, while bounding forgetting on held-out benchmarks. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

We give a compressed overview here and defer detailed discussion to Appendix F. 

Continual pre-training of LLMs. Continual pre-training (CPT) adapts a pretrained LLM to a new domain, language, or temporal slice while combating catastrophic forgetting. One line stabilizes the optimizer through learning-rate re-warming, re-decay, and replay (Ibrahim et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025a); another targets domain specialization in finance (Xie et al., 2024), mathematics and coding (Fujii et al., 2025), with scaling-law analyses given by the D-CPT law (Que et al., 2024). Forgetting during CPT (Li & Lee, 2024; Yıldız et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2025) of aligned/instruction-tuned models 2Preprint. has been characterized, and mitigated via routing and model-merging (Chen et al., 2023c; Yang et al., 2024; Ilharco et al., 2022; Wortsman et al., 2022; Kleiman et al., 2025). CPT has also been extended to knowledge-intensive (Guo et al., 2025), multimodal (Zhao et al., 2025; Roth et al., 2024), and architecture-level (Gong et al., 2024) settings. Classical elastic weight consolidation (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) uses the diagonal Fisher as a regularizer ; we share the Fisher lens but invert its direction of use—Fisher decides which tokens enter the stream rather than constraining the optimizer afterward. 

Data selection for language-model training. Selection has been studied at every training stage. From-scratch pre-training methods include domain-mixture optimization (Xie et al., 2023a), im-portance resampling (Xie et al., 2023b), learned scorers (Yu et al., 2024; 2025; Peng et al., 2025; Calian et al., 2025; Tan et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024b), and large-scale pruning (Marion et al., 2023; Longpre et al., 2024; Du et al., 2022). Instruction-tuning selectors achieve strong results with small curated sets (Zhou et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023a;b; Liu et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023; Das & Khetan, 2023; Tsai et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025a; Nikdan et al., 2025; Kung et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024b; Cheng et al., 2026; Alam et al., 2026; Hoang et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2024a). Loss- and difficulty-based pruning has a long pedigree in classification and NLU (Sorscher et al., 2022; Paul et al., 2021; Attendu & Corbeil, 2023; Nguyen et al., 2025). Unlike these methods, which target either the tabula rasa regime or small-budget instruction tuning, our selector decomposes each sample into an anchor component aligned with existing capability and a frontier component injecting mass into uncommitted directions—a distinction that loss, perplexity, and feature-similarity surrogates cannot make. 

Coreset selection and submodular subset methods. Our global formulation builds on the coreset literature (Guo et al., 2022): geometry-based representativeness (Welling, 2009; Farahani & Hek-matfar, 2009; Sener & Savarese, 2017); uncertainty- and loss-based scoring (Toneva et al., 2018; Paul et al., 2021); decision-boundary methods (Ducoffe & Precioso, 2018; Margatina et al., 2021); gradient-matching with submodular guarantees (Mirzasoleiman et al., 2020; Killamsetty et al., 2021a); and bi-level selection (Killamsetty et al., 2021b;c). Pruning at scaling-law scale (Sorscher et al., 2022) and the dataset-distillation line (Wang et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2020) condense rather than select; we also draw inspiration from simple local-rule emergent coordination (Reynolds, 1987). In contrast to these methods, which target a static loss over a fixed labeled pool, we lift the submodular framing to a token-budgeted, unlabeled language-modeling setting yielding a constant-factor approximation whose per-sample cost is independent of the candidate-pool size. 

## 3 METHOD 

We present a Fisher-guided, submodular selector for the continual pre-training (CPT) of large language models. Following the problem setup in §3.1, §3.2 outlines our core intuition: because the diagonal Fisher of a pretrained model identifies coordinates encoding existing knowledge, an effective selector should prioritize preserving these exact parameters while allowing updates elsewhere. To formalize this, in §3.3, we utilize Fisher information and decompose each candidate’s gradient. We then generalize these per-sample signals in §3.4 into a subset-level submodular objective that optimally balances gradient magnitude and non-redundancy. To handle large-scale data streams, §3.5 discusses the engineering approximations required to scale this framework to billion-parameter regimes. 3.1 PROBLEM FORMULATION

The Fisher information matrix F = Eg(x) g(x)⊤ of the pretrained model quantifies, direction by direction in parameter space, how strongly the predictive distribution depends on a perturbation. The eigenvectors of F associated with large eigenvalues are directions the model has committed to: perturbing the parameters along them moves the output distribution, so they carry the capability the pretrained checkpoint encodes. Eigenvectors associated with near-zero eigenvalues are directions the model holds no opinion about: perturbing along them barely changes the output, so they are free for new content. A CPT procedure that ignores this geometry cannot tell whether its updates cancel mass in high-Fisher directions (forgetting) or inject mass into low-Fisher directions (acquisition). Scalar signals such as loss and perplexity (Sorscher et al., 2022; Marion et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2025) fall in exactly this category when used to choose or prioritize CPT data. They describe how surprised the model is by a document, but not about which parameter directions an update on it would move. Figure 1 visualizes the failure mode that motivates our selector. The left panel shows the Fisher drift after parameter-agnostic CPT: the training process updates the model without access to whether its gradients move retention-sensitive directions or acquisition-friendly directions. The result is a destructive asymmetry. Fisher mass decreases on committed coordinates, while low-Fisher coordi-nates, where new information could be absorbed with less interference, remain underused. The right panel reports the corresponding outcome under our Fisher-guided selector, where this high-Fisher drift is suppressed and adaptation is concentrated more in low-Fisher regions. The key lesson is the left-panel failure mode. Forgetting can be read as a Fisher-geometric failure of parameter-agnostic CPT, and candidates should be judged by where their gradients move the pretrained model. 3.3 FISHER DECOMPOSITION

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets and default settings. Our corpus contains four preprocessed sources: PMC full-text articles (National Library of Medicine, 2003) ( 38 B tokens), PubMed abstracts (White, 2020) ( 7Btokens), FineWeb (Penedo et al., 2024) ( 400 B tokens), and the English portion of Llama3-SynE (Chen et al., 2025a) ( 90 B tokens). The target-domain candidate stream is the medical subset formed by PMC and PubMed, while the reference distribution ppre used to estimate Λ is a held-out general-domain shard sampled from FineWeb and English Llama3-SynE. Before any method runs, the candidate pool is decontaminated with a PubMedQA/BioASQ source-ID blocklist, a 13 -gram overlap filter against every evaluation instance (Brown et al., 2020), and MinHash near-duplicate removal, which together remove 2.68% of its documents (App. B). TinyLlama-1.1B (Zhang et al., 2024a) is the primary backbone for the main results and all ablations, and Llama-3.1-8B (Grattafiori et al., 2024) serves as the scale-up validation. All candidate gradients and the Fisher diagonal Λ are computed in a LoRA subspace with rank r = 128 and α = 512 , applied to the query, key, value, and output attention projections. For TinyLlama-1.1B, Λ is estimated from 50 ,000 reference documents. All forward passes, gradient computations, and Fisher-diagonal estimation are run in bfloat16. TinyLlama-1.1B uses a cutoff length of 2048 and is trained with PyTorch DDP across 4 nodes under a default token budget of 4B. Llama-3.1-8B uses a cutoff length of 4096 and is trained with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 (Rasley et al., 2020) and activation/gradient checkpointing across 10 nodes under a default token budget of 10 B. 4.2 MAIN RESULTS 

We compare our method against standard data selection strategies: Random, Low-PPL, and High-PPL (top-k by lowest/highest per-token perplexity at θ⋆). We also benchmark against forgetting-aware controls, Replay (Ibrahim et al., 2024) strategy and EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) training. EWC uses the Fisher information as a traditional regularization penalty. As shown in Table 1, our approach establishes a strictly better (A, Φ) Pareto frontier. The adaptation gain exceeds the strongest perplexity baseline by 4.2. Furthermore, forgetting is more than halved relative to the next-best method. While Replay narrowly wins on LAMBADA, benefiting from its explicit general-domain replay mixture, our method remains vastly superior overall. Notably, EWC only reduces forgetting at the severe expense of adaptation, being outperformed on both axes. We evaluate our method on 8B models on the harder medical-QA suite. We maintain the CPT baselines and additionally introduce two pretrained-only references that bypass our pipeline entirely: Galactica (Taylor et al., 2022) and Mistral-7B (Jiang et al., 2023). Our method achieves the best adaptation and the lowest forgetting. It yields the largest medical-QA gain of any CPT method and is the only approach whose general-suite scores remain robustly flat, whereas every target-only baseline suffers a non-trivial degradation in MMLU accuracy. Moving beyond CPT baselines, our method surpasses the domain-specialized Galactica across the medical-QA suite by a wide margin, while preserving a substantial advantage on general benchmarks. Furthermore, this same adapted model matches or exceeds the inherently stronger Mistral-7B on medical QA while achieving parity on the general suite. ## 5 CONCLUSION 

We present a Fisher-guided submodular data selector for continual pre-training (CPT) that intrinsically balances target-domain acquisition with the preservation of pretraining capabilities at selection time. By decomposing candidate gradients into Fisher-weighted anchor and frontier components, we formulate a log det objective that efficiently extracts a high-utility, non-redundant subset via Sieve-Streaming and low-rank approximations. Empirically, our method strictly Pareto-dominates perplexity- and replay-based baselines across 1.1B and 8B LLMs, yielding at least a 10 × token-efficiency gain and explicitly isolating the superiority of Fisher geometry as a data selector. While currently bounded by a diagonal Fisher approximation and reliance on a warmup checkpoint θ⋆, this principled geometric framework naturally scales to richer curvature models and provides a direct pathway for multimodal streaming and instruction-tuning curation. 9Preprint.

## A IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

This appendix expands the practical pipeline in §3.5. We describe the concrete artifacts, numerical safeguards, and streaming data path used in all experiments. The emphasis is on reproducibility: the same LoRA adapter, tokenizer, Fisher estimate, and sample-level data stream must be used consistently across warmup, Fisher estimation, selection, and final CPT. A.1 WARMUP ADAPTER AND FISHER ESTIMATE 

LoRA operating point. All selection signals are computed in the LoRA adapter subspace used by downstream CPT. For the TinyLlama runs, we attach LoRA modules to the query, key, value, and output attention projections, with rank r = 128 and LoRA scale 512 . The adapter is first warmed up on target-domain text to produce the operating point θ⋆. This step is not used as an evaluation result; it only makes the LoRA coordinates non-degenerate and provides a local point at which candidate gradients are meaningful. The warmup checkpoint is treated as a single source of truth. Its adapter weights define θ⋆, and its optimizer state is cached for the preconditioner in §A.3. In practice, every later stage loads this exact checkpoint rather than re-initializing a fresh adapter. This is important because LoRA’s zero-product initialization can make part of the adapter-gradient signal vanish at the raw pretrained checkpoint, which in turn produces an ill-conditioned Fisher estimate. 

Diagonal Fisher estimation. We estimate the diagonal Fisher in the same LoRA subspace and at the same operating point θ⋆. The base model is frozen, dropout is disabled, and only LoRA parameters require gradients. For each proxy document zj from the held-out reference distribution, we compute the next-token loss and accumulate squared LoRA gradients in fp32:

A.2 CANDIDATE STREAM AND SAMPLE -LEVEL GRADIENTS 

Preserving document boundaries. The selector consumes the candidate corpus as an iterable stream. During selection, packing is disabled so that each stream element corresponds to one candidate document and yields one per-sample gradient. We use the same tokenizer family as the warmup model and truncate candidates to the selection cutoff length. Each incoming candidate receives a globally unique, monotonically increasing stream index; this index is used for replaying the calibration window, tracking accepted candidates across sieves, and reconstructing the final selected set. 

Perplexity gate before backward. For a micro-batch of stream candidates, we right-pad to the longest sequence in the micro-batch and compute the standard causal-LM next-token loss for each candidate separately. The corresponding perplexity is obtained from this per-sample loss. Candidates outside a broad interval [plo , p hi ] are discarded before any backward pass is run. This gate removes documents that are either already too easy for θ⋆ or so far out-of-distribution that their gradients are dominated by noise, while avoiding an additional forward pass. For the remaining candidates, we compute sample-level LoRA gradients from the retained compu-tation graph. When more than one candidate survives in the micro-batch, all but the last backward call retain the graph; the last one releases it. The resulting LoRA gradients are flattened in a fixed parameter order and are the only gradients passed to the Fisher reweighting and projection stages. A.3 CONDITIONED FISHER SIGNATURES

## B BENCHMARK DECONTAMINATION 

Our target-domain candidate pool (PMC full text and PubMed abstracts) comes from the same literature from which several biomedical benchmarks are built. This appendix lists where overlap can arise, describes the decontamination applied during data preparation, and summarizes the evidence that residual overlap does not drive the reported gains. 

Sources of overlap. Each PubMedQA instance is built from one PubMed article: the question comes from the title, the context is the abstract without its conclusion, and the conclusion is the long answer that determines the yes/no/maybe label (Jin et al., 2019). BioASQ questions are answered from gold PubMed documents and snippets (Tsatsaronis et al., 2012). These abstracts, or the PMC full texts of the same articles, can therefore appear in our pool. MedQA-USMLE and MedMCQA (Jin et al., 2021; Pal et al., 2022) are derived from medical licensing and entrance exams rather than from PubMed articles, so they have no lexical path to the candidate stream through their source documents. 

Decontamination protocol. Three filters are applied, in order, while preparing the PMC/PubMed candidate pool (Table 5), and all experiments reported in this paper, for our method and for every baseline, take their target-domain data from the filtered pool. (1) Source-ID blocklist : every PMC or PubMed record whose PMID or DOI appears in the source lists of PubMedQA (the PQA-L, PQA-U, and PQA-A subsets, 273 .5k PMIDs) or of BioASQ is dropped. (2) n-gram overlap : every remaining candidate that shares a 13 -gram with any evaluation instance (question, context, and answer) is dropped, following the 13 -gram criterion of Brown et al. (2020). (3) Near-duplicate removal : candidates are deduplicated with MinHash LSH, dropping pairs whose Jaccard similarity on 5-gram shingles is at least 0.8. The general-domain reference shard used to estimate Λ is held out and disjoint from both the candidate stream and every evaluation set.

## E LIMITATIONS 

Our method is designed for scalable CPT data selection, so its approximations are deliberately chosen to preserve the main Fisher geometry while remaining deployable for billion-parameter LMs. In particular, we estimate curvature with a diagonal Fisher in the LoRA adapter subspace, evaluate gradients at a short warmup operating point θ⋆, and preserve the subset-level log-determinant geometry through random projection rather than full-parameter covariance matrices. These choices are part of the scalable formulation and are examined through the ablations and streaming diagnostics in §4.3 and §4.4; they also leave natural extensions to richer block-diagonal or low-rank curvature models, full-parameter adaptation, and adaptive warmup schedules. Our experiments focus on medical-domain CPT for two LLM backbones, so the empirical claims are scoped to this setting, with other domains, instruction-tuning regimes, and multimodal streams left for future validation. 

## F EXTENDED RELATED WORK 

This appendix expands the compressed discussion of §2 with the descriptions and method-level distinctions that were elided for space. All citations referenced here also appear in the main related work. 

Continual pre-training of large language models. Continual pre-training (CPT) adapts a pretrained LLM to a new domain, language, or temporal slice without retraining from scratch, and the dominant concern is catastrophic forgetting of capabilities acquired during the original run. A first line of work stabilizes the optimizer itself: a combination of learning-rate re-warming, re-decay, and replay of a small fraction of prior data is sufficient to handle weak (English →English) and strong 21 Preprint. (English →German) distribution shifts (Ibrahim et al., 2024), and follow-up work further layers topic-based mixture reweighting and a perplexity-based curriculum on top (Chen et al., 2025a). Domain-specialized CPT has been studied in finance (Xie et al., 2024) and in mathematics and coding (Fujii et al., 2025), and scaling-law analyses of the domain/general trade-off are given by the D-CPT law (Que et al., 2024). Another thread explicitly analyzes forgetting during CPT of aligned or instruction-tuned models (Li & Lee, 2024; Yıldız et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2025), and several methods mitigate it by routing or model-merging rather than by data: distribution-specialized experts (Chen et al., 2023c), language-inclusive CLIP fine-tuning (Yang et al., 2024), task arithmetic for selective forgetting (Ilharco et al., 2022), WiSE-FT-style robust fine-tuning (Wortsman et al., 2022), and the Soup-to-Go model-averaging recipe (Kleiman et al., 2025). CPT has also been extended to knowledge-intensive generative models (Guo et al., 2025), to multimodal LLMs (Zhao et al., 2025; Roth et al., 2024), and to architecture-level adaptation from autoregressive to diffusion language models (Gong et al., 2024). Our contribution inverts the role of the Fisher relative to EWC. EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) uses the diagonal Fisher as a regularizer that pins parameters near their pretrained values after the data is fixed. We use the same Fisher upstream of optimization, to decide which tokens enter the CPT stream, so adaptation can proceed with an unconstrained optimizer and no auxiliary penalty. 

Data selection for language-model training. Selecting training data for LMs has been investigated at every training stage. For from-scratch pre-training, DoReMi optimizes domain mixtures via group DRO on a proxy model (Xie et al., 2023a); DSIR reframes the choice as importance resampling against a target distribution (Xie et al., 2023b); and a broad family of scorers learns to rank candidates, including data-influence models (MATES (Yu et al., 2024)), group-level selection (Yu et al., 2025), quality classifiers (DataMan (Peng et al., 2025)), meta-learned raters (DataRater (Calian et al., 2025)), differentiable mixture weights (Tan et al., 2026), relative scoring via Bradley–Terry comparisons (Chen et al., 2025b), sensitivity-aware greedy selection (GREATS (Wang et al., 2024)), autonomous LM-based selection for mathematical text (Zhang et al., 2024b), and large-scale data pruning at pre-training scale (Marion et al., 2023; Longpre et al., 2024; Du et al., 2022). A parallel thread targets instruction tuning , where strong results are obtained with dramatically smaller curated sets: LIMA and its “0.5% is enough” successor (Zhou et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023a), AI-scored filtering in AlpaGasus (Chen et al., 2023b), alignment-quality studies (Liu et al., 2023), LIFT (Xu et al., 2023), unsupervised core-set selection DEFT (Das & Khetan, 2023), code-oriented pruning (Tsai et al., 2024), visual instruction-tuning selectors (Liu et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025a), influence distillation (Nikdan et al., 2025), active and self-guided schemes (Kung et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024b), task-aware distribution matching (Cheng et al., 2026), activation-stability pruning (Alam et al., 2026), difficulty–diversity collaborative filtering (Hoang et al., 2026), tree-structured synthesis (Wang et al., 2025b), and a growing list of IT-focused selectors (Li et al., 2024a). More broadly, loss- and difficulty-based pruning has a long pedigree in classification and NLU (Sorscher et al., 2022; Paul et al., 2021; Attendu & Corbeil, 2023; Nguyen et al., 2025). Our selector differs from these methods on two axes. First, it operates in the post-pretraining regime where there is prior competence to protect, unlike pre-training selectors that assume a tabula rasa ,and outside the small-budget instruction-tuning regime targeted by LIMA-style filters. Second, it decomposes a sample’s expected contribution into an anchor component aligned with high-Fisher directions of established competence and a frontier component aligned with low-Fisher directions where the pretrained loss is flat. This curvature-weighted decomposition is invisible to loss, perplexity, and feature-similarity surrogates, which integrate over directions without weighting them by Fisher mass. 

Coreset selection and submodular data subset methods. Our global selection formulation builds on the coreset literature surveyed in DeepCore (Guo et al., 2022). Geometry-based methods retain representative points via herding (Welling, 2009) or k-center-greedy coverage (Farahani & Hekmatfar, 2009; Sener & Savarese, 2017); uncertainty- and loss-based methods use forgetting events (Toneva et al., 2018), gradient norms and error norms (GraNd, EL2N) (Paul et al., 2021), or sensitivity-based importance sampling; decision-boundary methods exploit adversarial deep-fool margins and contrastive active learning (Ducoffe & Precioso, 2018; Margatina et al., 2021); gradient-matching methods greedily select subsets whose aggregate gradient tracks the full training gradient (CRAIG (Mirzasoleiman et al., 2020), GRAD-MATCH (Killamsetty et al., 2021a)) and give OMP-based 22 Preprint. submodular approximation guarantees; and bilevel methods treat the subset as an inner optimization variable (GLISTER (Killamsetty et al., 2021b), RETRIEVE (Killamsetty et al., 2021c)). Data pruning at neural-scaling-law scale (Sorscher et al., 2022) established that well-chosen subsets can beat power-law scaling, and the dataset-distillation line (Wang et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2020) condenses rather than selects. We also draw inspiration from simple local-rule emergent coordination (Reynolds, 1987).
