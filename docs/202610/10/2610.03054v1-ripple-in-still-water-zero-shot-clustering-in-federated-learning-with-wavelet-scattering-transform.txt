# RIPPLE in Still Water: Zero-Shot Clustering in Federated Learning with Wavelet Scattering Transform 

Alessandro Licciardi 

Department of Mathematical Sciences Politecnico di Torino, Turin, Italy 

alessandro.licciardi@polito.it 

## Abstract 

Clustered Federated Learning (FL) partitions a client population into groups of similar local distributions and trains one specialized model per cluster, mitigating client drift that degrades single-model methods under non-IID data. Prior methods discover cluster structure inside the training loop through gradient similarity, loss evaluation, or EM-style updates, thus increasing communication overhead, expos-ing gradients to inversion attacks, and providing no mechanism to assign clients absent from training. We propose R IPPLE , a clustered FL framework in which cluster assignment is computed entirely offline from a spectral characterization of each client’s local data: a variance-weighted principal-component prototype em-bedded via the Wavelet Scattering Transform and decoded by a Gaussian Mixture VAE trained server-side on synthetic client populations before federation begins. Per-round communication cost matches F ED AVG exactly, and a client absent from training obtains a personalized model from a single forward pass, without gradient computation, model evaluation, or extra communication round. We prove that the gap between R IPPLE ’s surrogate clustered objective and the oracle is bounded by a computable quantity decaying with client sample size and independent of federation duration; per-cluster convergence matches the minimax-optimal rate for non-convex smooth objectives. Across five benchmarks spanning controlled and realistic heterogeneity, R IPPLE consistently outperforms all baselines, with margins growing on the most realistic partitions. 

## 1 Introduction 

Ripple in still water, when there is no pebble tossed, nor wind to blow. 

> — R. Hunter & J. Garcia (1970)

The latent clustered structure of a federation need not be stirred into being by training, but already ripples through the spectral geometry of its data. Federated Learning (FL) (McMahan et al., 2017; Kairouz et al., 2021) trains shared models without pooling private data, but its canonical algorithm F ED AVG (McMahan et al., 2017) silently assumes that all clients sample from the same distribution. Under the statistical heterogeneity that is ubiquitous in real deployments (Zhao et al., 2018; Hsieh et al., 2020), gradient updates from clients with structurally distinct data point in conflicting directions, which Karimireddy et al. (2020) called 

client drift ,degrading every client’s accuracy at once. Variance-reduction and adaptive methods (Karimireddy et al., 2020; Acar et al., 2021; Reddi et al., 2020; Li et al., 2020) attenuate the symptom 

> 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026). arXiv:2610.03054v1 [cs.LG] 2 Oct 2026

but do not address the cause: a single global model cannot represent clients whose data lie in qualitatively different regimes. Clustered FL addresses the cause directly, training one specialized model per group of compatible clients (Sattler et al., 2020; Ghosh et al., 2020; Long et al., 2023; Fenoglio et al., 2025). Yet every prior method discovers cluster structure inside the training loop, e.g., through gradient cosine similarity (Sattler et al., 2020), repeated loss evaluation (Ghosh et al., 2020), or EM-style gradient statistics (Long et al., 2023). These methods accumulate communication overhead with each round, exposing updates to gradient-inversion attacks (Zhang et al., 2022), and offering no pathway to assign a client that was not part of training. Moreover, while prior methods such as I FCA and F ESEM require the number of clusters to be specified a priori, R IPPLE needs only a loose upper bound, and R IPPLE -HDB then automatically adapts the cluster count via density-based clustering on the posterior simplex. We propose a different route, motivated by recent work showing that the Wavelet Scattering Transform (WST) (Mallat, 2012; Bruna and Mallat, 2013) can identify anomalous clients in a federation from spectral signatures of their local data (Licciardi et al., 2026), with no federated communication whatsoever. The same spectral characterization extends naturally to distinguishing semantically distinct client populations: framing the problem as probabilistic inference over a simplex-valued mixture model yields a soft cluster-weight vector for each client from a single forward pass, computed from local data statistics alone. This is the central design principle of R IPPLE : cluster assignment is a property of the data, not an artifact of training. The assignment is fixed before the first communication round and never revised; per-round cost matches F ED AVG exactly; and a client absent from training receives a personalized model by transmitting nothing but a small spectral embedding of its own data. 

Contributions. We summarize the contributions as follows. • We reframe clustered FL routing as an offline statistic computed from local data, eliminating per-round communication overhead and fixing the routing decision before the first federation round. • We prove a surrogate-fidelity certificate: the gap between R IPPLE ’s clustered objective and the oracle is bounded by a computable quantity that decays with client sample size and is independent of the number of rounds. • A client absent from training obtains a personalized model from a single WST embedding and one encoder forward pass, without any gradient computation or model evaluation required. • R IPPLE outperforms all baselines across five benchmarks, with the largest margins on the most realistic partitions (Venkateswara et al., 2017; Weyand et al., 2020); zero-shot assignment matches or exceeds other clustered FL methods. 

## 2 Related Work 

Federated learning under data heterogeneity. Non-IID client data has been central to FL research since Zhao et al. (2018) quantified how label skew degrades F ED AVG (McMahan et al., 2017) convergence. Variance-reduction methods address client drift directly: S CAFFOLD (Karimireddy et al., 2020) corrects local-SGD bias via control variates, while F ED DYN (Acar et al., 2021) adds a dynamic regularization term penalizing divergence from the global model. Adaptive-optimizer variants (Reddi et al., 2020) improve convergence under heterogeneous gradients; FedProx (Li et al., 2020) constrains local updates via a proximal term. All these methods pursue a single global model, which cannot fully resolve clients whose data lie in fundamentally distinct semantic regimes (Hsieh et al., 2020). Personalized FL sidesteps this by training per-client models via meta-learning (P ER -F ED AVG (Fallah et al., 2020), FedALA (Zhang et al., 2023)), Moreau-envelope regularization (PFED ME (T Dinh et al., 2020)), or shared representations (Collins et al., 2021); yet when clients share significant within-group structure, per-client models discard exploitable inter-client information that clustered training would leverage. 

Clustered federated learning. Clustering has proven to be an effective strategy in FL for handling client heterogeneity and improving personalization (Huang et al., 2022; Duan et al., 2021; Briggs et al., 2020; Caldarola et al., 2021; Ye et al., 2023). Sattler et al. (2020) group clients by cosine gradient similarity, but the splitting threshold is sensitive and prone to over- or under-fragmentation under strong non-IID. I FCA (Ghosh et al., 2020) alternates hard cluster assignment with gradient descent, requiring each client to evaluate all C models per round. F ESEM (Long et al., 2023) uses 2EM-style soft assignments but still iterates gradient updates from every candidate center. Graph-and hierarchy-based methods (Ye et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2022; Briggs et al., 2020; Duan et al., 2021) discover collaboration structure adaptively yet tie cluster inference to in-training gradient statistics. FedGWC (Licciardi et al., 2025) and FLUX (Fenoglio et al., 2025) refine aggregation with interaction-aware weighting and data descriptors, respectively, the first by modeling interaction between clients’ empirical losses, while the latter infer cluster structure from gradient feedback exchanged during training. All prior methods share three structural limitations. (i) Hard or in-training assignment : membership is either binary (Sattler et al., 2020; Ghosh et al., 2020) or requires ongoing gradient communication (Long et al., 2023; Licciardi et al., 2025; Fenoglio et al., 2025), coupling cluster discovery to the training loop. (ii) No zero-shot capability : a new client must participate in at least one communication round or evaluate all C models locally to obtain an assignment. (iii) Communication overhead above 

FED AVG : discovering cluster structure consumes extra bandwidth every round. R IPPLE overcomes these limitations, as cluster weights wk ∈ ∆C−1 are inferred once , before any federated round, from a frozen GMM-VAE requiring no gradient exchange; per-round communication matches F ED AVG 

exactly; and a new client is assigned via a single forward pass of the frozen encoder.

3.2 Wavelet Scattering Transform 

The Wavelet Scattering Transform (WST) (Mallat, 2012; Bruna and Mallat, 2013) builds a parameter-free signal representation by cascading wavelet convolutions with pointwise modulus nonlinearities, followed by lowpass averaging. The resulting second-order representation Φ(x) ∈ RP satisfies three properties that underpin our theoretical guarantees: it is non-expansive (∥SJ x − SJ x′∥2 ≤∥x − x′∥2), translation-invariant at scale 2J , and Lipschitz-stable to diffeomorphisms with constant 

C2 = 2 ∥∇ ψ∥1. Together these properties ensure that two clients whose distributions differ only through sensor miscalibrations, object translations, or mild domain shifts produce representations 

Φk at a distance proportional to the magnitude of those perturbations, thus making Φ a stable, label-free fingerprint for pre-federation cluster assignment. The full construction, formal proofs, and a sensitivity study are given in Appendix A.2. We refer to Appendix A for a self-contained probabilistic derivation of the federated and clustered objectives from first principles, and for the full WST construction and stability properties that underpin Theorems 5.1–5.2. 

## 4 The R IPPLE Algorithm 

RIPPLE solves the cluster assignment problem zero-shot: before any federation round, each client transmits a single deformation-stable summary of its local distribution, and the server maps it to a soft cluster-weight vector wk ∈ ∆C−1. This supports clients with genuinely mixed data modalities and generalizes to unseen clients via a single encoder forward pass, following one architectural choice: a Gaussian Mixture VAE trained server-side on synthetic client populations before federation begins.

## Conclusion 

We presented R IPPLE , a clustered FL framework that computes client-to-cluster assignments once, offline, from a spectral summary of each client’s local data, before any communication round. This separation of cluster discovery from training yields three concrete properties: per-round cost matching FED AVG ; a surrogate-oracle gap bounded by local sample size and spectral gap, independent of the number of rounds; and zero-shot personalization for unseen clients via a single encoder forward pass. Across five benchmarks spanning controlled and realistic heterogeneity, R IPPLE consistently outperforms both global and clustered FL baselines, with margins growing on the most realistic partitions. 

Limitations and Broader Impact. Our analysis is information-theoretic, future work could include extend to formal differential privacy; adding calibrated noise to the spectral embedding would yield a formal (ε, δ )-DP guarantee at the cost of a larger routing error. R IPPLE ’s offline assignment design reduces the number of communication rounds needed to discover cluster structure, directly lowering the energy footprint of federated training. Enabling zero-shot personalization, R IPPLE reduces the computational burden on client devices, which is particularly relevant for federated deployments on energy-constrained hardware.

Server-Side Offline Training 

The MSDL pipeline trains a GMM-VAE (Algorithm 1) over Tpre steps on batches of B synthetic clients. Each step involves three operations. 

1. Synthetic prototype generation. At each step, a Dirichlet target y(t) ∼ Dir( α(t)1C ) is sampled and a sub-batch of B images is drawn from Daux with label proportions matching y(t). ## F Privacy of R IPPLE 

Raw client data never leaves the device at any stage. Each client k computes the spectral prototype 

ˆxk and its scattering embedding Φk = SJ (ˆ xk) ∈ RP entirely on local hardware, and transmits only 

Φk to the server in the one-shot pre-federation round. During federated training, each client transmits gradient updates ∆θk — standard F ED AVG behaviour, compatible with secure aggregation (Bonawitz et al., 2017). The embedding Φk is a privacy-safe aggregate for two complementary reasons: (1) the spectral prototype is a distributional summary, not a sample, and the WST is non-invertible The prototype ˆxk = ˆ μk +∑Mj=1 ˆαk,j ˆvj is the variance-weighted superposition of the top-M principal directions of the local dataset. It encodes the dominant modes of variation across all Nk samples, 23 analogously to transmitting the sample mean or the leading eigenvectors of the empirical covariance matrix. Communicating aggregate spectral statistics is an established practice in privacy-preserving FL (Goswami et al., 2025), and carries no greater disclosure risk than covariance-sharing schemes already accepted in the literature. The scattering operator SJ applies the pointwise complex modulus at every propagator step, ir-reversibly discarding phase information (Mallat, 2012). Recovering ˆxk from Φk is therefore a computationally intractable inverse problem: even if an adversary obtained Φk, they would recover only the (already privacy-safe) prototype, not any individual sample from Dk.Together, these two properties form a two-layer privacy barrier between the raw local dataset and the transmitted quantity. The server processes Φk in a single forward pass of the frozen inference network qϕ⋆ , producing the cluster-weight vector wk = qϕ⋆ ( · | Φk) ∈ ∆C−1. Once wk is computed, Φk can be discarded; the server need only retain the C-dimensional probability vector wk. This is a further compression: wk

retains no information about the geometry of Dk beyond its soft cluster membership, as confirmed by the non-invertibility of the composition Dk 7 → ˆxk 7 → Φk 7 → wk.

GMM-VAE Training: Auxiliary Data Only 

The GMM-VAE is trained exclusively on synthetic embeddings derived from the server-side auxiliary dataset Daux via the Dirichlet hallucination procedure of Algorithm 1. No real client data participates in pre-training, and no client embedding is used to update model parameters. This follows the auxiliary-data paradigm of prior FL methods (Sattler et al., 2021; Lin et al., 2020; Goswami et al., 2025). After the pre-federation phase completes, no client-specific data or embeddings are stored by the server; only the frozen parameters (θ⋆, ϕ ⋆) and the vectors {wk}Nk=1 remain. 

Federation Phase 

During the T communication rounds, R IPPLE follows the standard F ED AVG protocol: each selected client downloads a personalised model initialisation (a convex combination of cluster models) and uploads a gradient update. All existing privacy mechanisms compatible with F ED AVG — including differentially private local SGD (Kairouz et al., 2021), secure aggregation (Bonawitz et al., 2017), and gradient compression — apply to R IPPLE ’s federation loop without modification, since the per-round client-server interface is identical. 

Comparison with Gradient-Based CFL 

Prior CFL methods expose a strictly larger attack surface. I FCA (Ghosh et al., 2020) and F ESEM (Long et al., 2023) require clients to transmit gradient updates across all C cluster models during the clustering phase, each of which can be partially inverted to expose training samples (Zhang et al., 2022). C FL (Sattler et al., 2020) exposes full model updates during the warm-up phase for cosine-similarity computation. By contrast, R IPPLE performs all cluster discovery from a single non-invertible embedding Φk ∈ RP transmitted before any gradient update occurs. 24 G Implementation Details and Further Experiments
