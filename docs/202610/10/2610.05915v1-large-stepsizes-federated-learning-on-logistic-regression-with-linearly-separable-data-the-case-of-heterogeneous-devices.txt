# Large Stepsizes Federated Learning on Logistic Regression with Linearly Separable Data: The Case of Heterogeneous Devices 

Hok Fong Wong, Hoi-To Wai, and Chung-Yiu Yau 

Abstract — This paper revisits the distributed learning prob-lem for training a multinomial logistic regression model with the Federated Averaging ( FedAvg ) algorithm. We concentrate on a scenario with arbitrarily large stepsizes and heterogeneous update rules where the devices may perform a different number of local updates in each round. We show that, with linearly separable data, FedAvg is stable with any stepsizes and the objective values converge to zero at the rate of O(1 /R ), where 

R is the number of communication rounds. Our result also demonstrates that the effects of device heterogeneity vanish asymptotically. For sufficiently large R, the objective values decrease monotonically and is bounded by O(1 /(RT avg )) ,where Tavg is the average number of local update steps per communication round across devices. Numerical experiments support our findings. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Distributed optimization has been a major workhorse in multi-agent control and learning systems [1]–[3], where devices (a.k.a. agents) collaboratively optimize a global objective function by interleaving between local computa-tion and communication. In recent years, federated learning (FL) has gained popularity as a mainstream distributed optimization paradigm as it offers a disciplined way to account for deployment reality, where heterogeneous devices differing in hardware, battery, connectivity operates in an asynchronous manner [4], [5]. Such aspect has become increasingly relevant to multi-agent systems [6] which are intrinsically heterogeneous due to hardware differences. FL has been successfully applied in problems such as system identification [7], [8], reinforcement learning and control [9]. The challenges of heterogeneous devices have set the study of FL apart from traditional distributed optimization by workers performing local updates before global aggregation of their local solutions, introducing local drift error that results from data and computational heterogeneity. The issue of local drift in FL has inspired the studies of algorithms such as FedAvg [10]–[12], SCAFFOLD [13], FedPD [14], and FedProx [15]. For instance, FedAvg relies on asimple averaging of local updates, and SCAFFOLD , FedPD ,

FedProx utilize control variates or proximal updates to reduce local drift errors. Convergence is guaranteed together with sufficiently small stepsizes with respect to (w.r.t.) problem-specific parameters such as smoothness.

On the other hand, several recent researches found that gradient algorithms may still converge with large stepsizes [16]–[19]. These pioneering works showed that for logistic regression with linearly separable data, gradient descent with arbitrarily large stepsizes stops oscillating after a transient phase, and even exhibits an accelerated convergence rate. This marks a significant departure from the classical analysis that demands small stepsizes to ensure monotonic descent. In light of the above developments, this paper investigates the impact of large stepsizes on FL. We revisit the conver-gence of FedAvg with large stepsizes for logistic regression with linearly separable data. We are particularly interested in the effects of device computational heterogeneity, i.e., when the number of local update steps varies across devices, on the robustness of FedAvg with large stepsizes. Notice that a closely related work is the recent paper [20] which studied a special case of FedAvg with only homogeneous devices taking the same number of local updates. We inquire: 

Does the convergence of FedAvg with large stepsizes still hold under heterogeneous local updates, and how does device heterogeneity affect the convergence bound? 

The practical relevance of our study extends further to fed-erated control with reinforcement learning (FCRL) where a central server coordinates distributed agents to learn policies without sharing sensitive experience data [21], [22]. Our set-ting captures the core update structure of federated softmax policy gradient methods under computational heterogeneity. From a networked control perspective, our setup models a high-gain feedback loop with heterogeneous actuators with different duty cycles. It is thus natural to consider whether the closed loop remains stable, and how the resulting dynamics depend on the spread of the numbers of local update steps. To address the above question, we notice that the introduc-tion of heterogeneous devices in local updates in FedAvg 

has led to a nontrivial complex dynamics with large stepsizes. Our main contributions are summarized below as:  

> •

Through utilizing a new proof technique, we show that 

FedAvg with large stepsizes and heterogeneous devices is stable and the objective values converge to zero at O(1 /R ),where R is the number of communication rounds.  

> •

We demonstrate that the effects of device heterogeneity on the convergence rate vanish asymptotically. For R ≫ 1, the convergence of optimality gap in objective values behaves as O(1 /(RT avg )) , where Tavg is the average number of local update steps per communication round.  

> •

Our numerical experiments demonstrate that the asymp-totic convergence of FedAvg with large stepsizes is not arXiv:2610.05915v1 [cs.LG] 5 Oct 2026 sensitive to the degree of device heterogeneity, corroborat-ing with our theoretical findings. We also developed our analysis for the multinomial logistic regression setting, extending from prior works such as [16].

FedAvg to the case of heterogeneous devices under alinear separability condition. A. Hard Sample Placement on Heterogeneous Devices 

The heterogeneity condition in Theorem 6 is usually sufficient but conservative, as seen in Section IV. In this experiment, we argue for the necessity of imposing such a condition by considering the interaction of the misaligned gradient updates among clients. The basic setting of the empirical experiment follows the description as in Section IV, except that we generate nhard =625 hard samples with margin in [γ, 2γ], where γ = 0 .001 ,and neasy = 4375 easy samples with margin at least 0.1.All m = 8 clients perform Ti = 57 local SGD steps in the homogenerous setup, and clients 1-4 perform Ti = 13 and clients 5-8 perform Ti = 101 in the heterogeneous setup, yielding Tavg = 57 in both cases. The 625 hard samples are assigned entirely to a single client. The 4375 easy samples are partitioned equally among the remaining seven clients. Every client therefore holds ex-actly 625 samples.
