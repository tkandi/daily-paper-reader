# Communication-Efficient Differentially Private Gradient Tracking for Distributed Optimization via Local Updates 

Mihitha Maithripala, Chenyang Qiu, and Zongli Lin 

Abstract — This paper studies privacy-preserving distributed optimization using gradient tracking with one communication-free local update between consecutive communication rounds. Since local computation alone does not protect gradient infor-mation, we perturb both the primal state and the gradient-tracking direction at communication iterations while keeping local updates noise-free. We establish an aggregate variable for tracking, characterize the limiting consensus point in the presence of added random noise, and prove convergence in mean under certain parameter conditions. We also establish infinite-horizon pure differential privacy for the complete communication transcript under an affine objective adjacency relation and geometrically decaying Laplace perturbations. Simulation results illustrate the advantages of the proposed method in terms of the convergence and optimization accuracy given a privacy budget. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Distributed optimization allows a network of agents to minimize an aggregate objective through local computa-tion and information exchange with neighboring agents. It provides a natural framework for coordinating networked energy systems [1], [2], processing distributed sensor mea-surements [3], and learning from decentralized data [4]. Gradient-tracking methods, including DIGing, combine local descent with an auxiliary variable that tracks aggregate gradient information and achieve geometric convergence un-der suitable smoothness, strong-convexity, and connectivity conditions [5]–[7]. However, their implementation raises two practical concerns: frequent communication can be costly, and the exchanged states and tracking variables can disclose information about local objectives. Motivated by the success of local updates in federated learning [8], distributed optimization algorithms incorpo-rating local updates [9]–[11] have been proposed. These algorithms reduce the frequency of information exchange by allowing agents to perform additional local computation between communication rounds. Recent work [12] inves-tigated local updates in gradient-tracking based distributed optimization. Their analysis shows that, with appropriately selected stepsizes, local updates can improve optimization performance per communication round. In particular, their analysis identifies a regime in which one communication-free local update between consecutive communication rounds achieves performance improvement, while repeating the local updates provide no further gain. a simple alternating structure: a communication-free local update followed by an update involving network mixing. However, whether this communication structure preserves the privacy of sensitive local information remains to be established. This question is important because exchanged states and gradients may reveal sensitive information about participating agents, such as robot positions in multi-robot rendezvous, sensor locations in distributed information fu-sion, or private training data in distributed learning [13]. Indeed, as we will show in this paper, under the stated observation and initialization assumptions, an observer with information of the complete communication sequence can recursively reconstruct the uncommunicated intermediate states and the entire local gradient trajectory of the gradient-tracking method in [12] with one local update between consecutive communication rounds. Thus, communication-free local updates alone do not protect gradient information, motivating an explicit privacy-preserving mechanism. In the absence of local-update mechanisms, various ap-proaches have been developed to preserve privacy in dis-tributed computation and optimization. Cryptographic meth-ods, including homomorphic encryption [14] and secure mul-tiparty computation [15], enable computations on protected data, at the expense of additional cryptographic operations and communication. Another line of work uses structured perturbations to conceal private information while preserving the accuracy [16]–[18]. However, privacy guarantees based on structured perturbations often rely on restrictions on the adversary’s access to information. In particular, protecting an agent may require at least one of its neighbors to remain non-colluding, a condition that can be difficult to ensure in general multi-agent systems. Differential privacy (DP) offers a widely adopted framework for quantifying privacy protection through independent additive noises [13], [19], [20]. The noise must be carefully calibrated to control the cumulative privacy loss while maintaining optimization accuracy. Incorporating DP into gradient tracking with local updates offers a promising approach to improving communication efficiency while protecting private information. An additional local update allows further computation without injecting fresh noise, potentially improving optimization performance per communication round under the same privacy budget. However, this update changes both the convergence dynamics and the sensitivity of communicated information, requiring a separate convergence and privacy analysis. To address this challenge, we propose in this paper a differentially private gradient-tracking algorithm with one local update between arXiv:2610.04854v1 [math.OC] 4 Oct 2026 consecutive communication rounds. The algorithm perturbs both the primal state and the tracking direction only at communication iterations. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to establish both convergence and infinite-horizon pure differential privacy for this alternating local-update gradient-tracking scheme under the stated objective adjacency relation. Our main contributions are summarized as follows. 1) We show that communication-free local updates alone do not protect gradient information. Specially, an at-tacker who knows the mixing weights and stepsize and observes the primal and tracking states at each communication iteration can uniquely reconstruct all uncommunicated intermediate states and every agent’s complete local gradient trajectory. 2) We develop a differentially private gradient-tracking algorithm that perturbs both the primal state and the gradient-tracking direction only during communication, while performing local updates without fresh noise injection. We establish sufficient conditions for conver-gence to a common random limiting point and charac-terize this point through the accumulated communica-tion perturbations. 3) We derive a matched-transcript sensitivity recursion and an explicit infinite-horizon pure differential-privacy budget under the stated affine adjacency. Joint condi-tions on the stepsize and geometric noise-decay rates ensure convergence and privacy. 4) Numerical comparisons demonstrate improved conver-gence per communication round relative to an existing differentially private method under equal certified pri-vacy budgets in the tested setting. PRIVATE GRADIENT TRACKING ALGORITHM 

The analysis in the previous section shows that reducing the communication frequency alone does not protect the pri-vate information contained in gradient tracking. In particular, although one communication-free local update is performed between two consecutive communication rounds, the states exchanged at the communication iterations are sufficient for an attacker to reconstruct the complete gradient trajectory. Therefore, an additional privacy mechanism is required to prevent the communicated information from revealing the underlying states and local gradients. Motivated by this observation, we introduce random per-turbations into the information exchanged at communication iterations. The proposed method has two main objectives. First, the state information communicated by each agent is perturbed to prevent an attacker from directly observing its intermediate optimization state. Second, the gradient-tracking variable is independently perturbed to prevent the exact reconstruction of local gradient trajectories.

We proposed a privacy-preserving gradient-tracking algo-rithm with one local update between communication rounds. By perturbing the communicated states and tracking di-rections, the method achieves infinite-horizon differential privacy under the stated adjacency relation. We established convergence in mean to a random consensus point under sufficient parameter conditions. Simulations compared dif-ferentially private gradient tracking with and without local updates, illustrating the benefits of an additional local update under the same certified privacy budget.
