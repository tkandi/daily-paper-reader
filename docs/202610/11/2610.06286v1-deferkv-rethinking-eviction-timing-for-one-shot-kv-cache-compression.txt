## DeferKV: Rethinking Eviction Timing for One-Shot KV Cache Compression 

Zhe Wang 1,2 Jiakai Li 1,2 Yujia Sun 1,2 Rongzheng Wang 1,2 Shuang Liang 1,2*

Abstract 

Long-context large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities across a wide range of tasks, but the growing KV cache introduces substantial memory and in-ference overhead. Existing one-shot KV cache compression methods typically commit to ir-reversible eviction immediately after prefill, before any signal from actual generation be-comes available. Our quantitative analysis shows that early queries from the actual gen-eration stage provide attention signals that are more consistent with subsequent decode atten-tion, with the largest single-step gain occur-ring at the prefill–decode boundary. Based on this observation, we propose DeferKV, which moves the eviction decision from the end of prefill to the first real decoding step and tem-porally combines prompt-side and decode-side observations, thereby better aligning KV impor-tance estimation with subsequent generation requirements. DeferKV requires no additional training, draft model, or future-query predic-tion module, making it simple and easy to de-ploy. Experiments on LongBench, RULER, and Needle-in-a-Haystack demonstrate that De-ferKV consistently improves model perfor-mance under KV cache compression while maintaining low inference latency. 

1 Introduction 

Large language models (LLMs) with long-context capabilities (OpenAI, 2023; Team, 2025) have demonstrated strong performance in document question answering (Dasigi et al., 2021), multi-document summarization (Fabbri et al., 2019), code understanding (Bai et al., 2024), retrieval-augmented generation (Lewis et al., 2020), and complex reasoning (Yao et al., 2023). However, long-context inference also introduces substantial system overhead. In particular, the KV cache has

become a major bottleneck affecting memory con-sumption and inference throughput (Li et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2024). For autore-gressive Transformer decoders (Vaswani et al., 2017), the key and value representations of histori-cal tokens must be cached at every layer to avoid redundant computation during generation. As the context length increases, the memory consump-tion of the KV cache grows linearly with the se-quence length, making long-context inference more challenging on memory-constrained devices and in high-concurrency serving scenarios. Therefore, compressing the KV cache while preserving gen-eration quality has become an important research problem in efficient LLM inference. Existing KV cache compression methods often perform one-time KV eviction immediately after the prefill stage. Representative methods such as SnapKV (Li et al., 2024) use a small set of queries near the end of the prompt as an observation win-dow and estimate the importance of historical to-kens based on the attention scores between these queries and historical keys, thereby selecting the KV tokens to retain. These methods are simple to implement, require no additional training, and offer favorable efficiency in practice. However, these methods (Li et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025) typically rely on a key assumption: the queries within the observation window at the end of the prompt are sufficient to reflect the ac-tual attention requirements of subsequent decoding. This assumption does not always hold in long-text generation tasks. As shown in Figure 1, more his-torical KV tokens lie closer to the diagonal when using the first real decode query. Its attention scores are therefore more consistent with subsequent de-code attention than those aggregated from the final 32 prefill queries. This suggests that prompt-end attention alone may not fully capture the access patterns observed during subsequent generation. Recent methods have begun to address this issue by incorporating information related to future gen-eration into KV token importance estimation. For example, SpecKV (Galim et al., 2026), LAQ (Wang et al., 2025), and LookaheadKV (Ahn et al., 2026) construct or predict future queries or attention patterns to make KV compression more closely aligned with the requirements of the actual gener-ation stage. Although these methods alleviate the limitations of prompt-only observation from dif-ferent perspectives, they usually require additional models, auxiliary modules, or extra training proce-dures and inevitably face uncertainty introduced by prediction errors. Based on these observations, we propose a more direct and lightweight approach. Unlike existing methods that rely solely on prompt-side informa-tion, our strategy postpones irreversible KV evic-tion until the first real decode-side signal becomes available and incorporates a small amount of atten-tion feedback from actual generation. This explic-itly aligns the importance estimation of historical tokens with the requirements of subsequent decod-ing. We further consider how to effectively inte-grate observations from the input and generation stages. To this end, we introduce a simple yet ef-fective temporal weighting strategy to improve the reliability and accuracy of importance estimation. Our main contributions are summarized as fol-lows: • We revisit the timing assumption of exist-ing KV cache compression methods that per-form eviction immediately after prefill. We propose a compression approach that post-pones irreversible KV eviction until the earli-est real decode-side feedback becomes avail-able, thereby better aligning KV selection with subsequent decoding. • Our method is simple, direct, and lightweight. It does not require additional training, a draft model, an auxiliary network, or a future-query prediction module. It only requires minor modifications to the existing inference pro-cess, enabling straightforward implementa-tion and deployment. • Extensive experiments show that our method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art base-lines under constrained KV cache budgets, achieving relative performance gains of up to 3.58% on LongBench and 8.03% on RULER. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 KV Cache Compression and Token Eviction 

To reduce the memory consumption of the KV cache, existing studies (Zhang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) have explored compression by reduc-ing the number of cached tokens. These methods typically retain important Key-Value pairs accord-ing to the potential contribution of historical tokens to subsequent generation, while evicting KV en-tries with lower importance, thereby controlling the cache size during decoding. Prompt-only com-pression methods usually perform a one-time se-lection of prompt KV tokens after the prefill stage and before decoding begins (Li et al., 2025). They are generally simple to implement and introduce limited scheduling overhead. SnapKV (Li et al., 2024) uses an observation window near the end of the prompt to estimate the importance of historical KV tokens and selects clustered important KV positions within each at-tention head. PyramidKV (Cai et al., 2024) fur-ther considers layer-wise budget allocation and assigns larger cache budgets to lower layers and smaller budgets to higher layers according to their different information aggregation characteristics. Ada-KV (Feng et al., 2025) focuses on the im-portance differences across attention heads and adaptively allocates more cache capacity to heads that have a greater influence on the model output. ChunkKV (Liu et al., 2025) treats semantic chunks rather than individual tokens as the basic compres-sion units and introduces layer-wise index reuse to preserve contextual integrity while reducing selec-tion overhead. These methods rely primarily on prompt-side observations and may not fully reflect the actual attention requirements of subsequent decoding. 

2.2 Future-Aware KV Cache Compression 

To alleviate the limitations of prompt-only ob-servation, recent studies have begun to introduce future-aware signals. These methods improve KV selection by predicting or approximating future queries, making the compression strategy more closely aligned with the requirements of the actual generation stage. SpecKV (Galim et al., 2026) uses a lightweight draft model to generate approximate future tokens and employs these lookahead tokens to help the target model estimate the importance of prompt KV pairs for subsequent generation. LAQ (Wang et al., 2025) performs low-budget lookahead decoding after prefill to generate a small number of pseudo-response tokens. It then caches their corresponding query states as a Q-Cache and uses them to re-evict the KV cache. LookaheadKV (Ahn et al., 2026) further intro-duces learnable lookahead tokens and combines them with LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) modules to pre-dict future attention patterns, thereby obtaining future-aware signals for KV selection. Although these methods better align KV com-pression with generation requirements, they gener-ally introduce additional model components, train-ing procedures, or inference overhead, increasing system complexity and deployment cost.

3.2 Obs. 1: Prefill-to-Decode Attention Shift 

As shown in Figure 2, prefill queries already show moderate consistency with the reference attention distribution, indicating that prompt-side attention contains useful signals for KV importance estima-tion (Li et al., 2024). However, moving from the final prefill query at r = −1 to the first real decode query at r = 1 increases JSD-Based Similarity and KV Selection Recall by 0.056 and 0.052 , respec-tively. This sharp improvement indicates that the first real decode query reveals generation-specific ac-cess requirements that are not fully captured during prefill, making it a more reliable signal for KV importance estimation. 

3.3 Obs. 2: Largest Gain at First Decode 

Among all adjacent query positions, the largest single-step gain occurs at the prefill–decode bound-ary. In comparison, the average gains between ad-jacent subsequent decode positions are only 0.009 

for JSD-Based Similarity and 0.007 for KV Selec-tion Recall, both substantially lower than those at the boundary. Later decode queries can further improve con-sistency with the reference attention distribution. However, using these later queries requires retain-ing the full prompt KV cache for additional decode steps, thereby increasing memory usage and end-to-end inference latency. Therefore, the first real decode query provides the earliest decode-side ob-servation with the largest gain, offering a favorable trade-off between KV selection quality and system overhead. 

3.4 Obs. 3: Temporal Relevance Decay 

Beyond the improvement at the prefill–decode boundary, both metrics generally increase as query positions approach the decode stage. Although the trend is not strictly monotonic, earlier prefill queries are typically less consistent with the refer-ence attention distribution. This suggests that queries contribute differently to KV importance estimation. We therefore assign larger weights to queries closer to the decode stage and gradually discount earlier queries, emphasizing generation-related signals while preserving prompt-side context. 

4 Method 

4.1 Overall Workflow 

Prompt-side attention does not fully reflect the KV access requirements of subsequent decoding, while the first real decode query provides a more reliable signal and queries closer to decoding are generally more informative. Based on these observations, DeferKV postpones KV cache compression until after the first standard decoding step. As illustrated in Figure 3, DeferKV obtains the first real decode query from the target model and combines it with recent prompt queries to form a decode-aware observation window. Their attention scores are aggregated using temporal weighting, as-signing greater importance to queries closer to the decode stage. The resulting scores are used to es-timate prompt-token importance and construct the compressed KV cache for subsequent generation. 5 Experiments 

5.1 Experimental Setup Benchmarks and Models. We conduct ex-periments on LongBench (Bai et al., 2024), RULER (Hsieh et al., 2024), and Needle-in-a-Haystack (Liu et al., 2024a). LongBench contains 16 long-context datasets across six task categories, covering question answering, summarization, few-shot learning, synthetic tasks, and code understand-ing. RULER evaluates model capabilities on chal-lenging long-context tasks across varying context lengths. Needle-in-a-Haystack assesses the ability to retrieve critical information from long sequences, with detailed results reported in Appendix F. We evaluate Qwen3-8B (Team, 2025), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Team, 2024), and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (Jiang et al., 2023). For each model, we use its official tokenizer and prompt template and adopt greedy decoding. 

Selected Baselines. We compare against four representative KV cache compression methods: (1) SnapKV (Li et al., 2024), which estimates historical KV importance using a local observa-tion window near the end of the prompt and per-forms one-time compression before decoding; (2) 

LAQ (Wang et al., 2025), which performs low-budget lookahead decoding with an initially com-pressed cache and uses the resulting query states to guide a second KV eviction; (3) SpecKV (Galim et al., 2026), which uses a small draft model to generate a proxy response and processes it with the target model to estimate prompt-KV importance; (4) LookaheadKV (Ahn et al., 2026), which uses learnable lookahead tokens and lightweight LoRA modules to estimate prompt-KV importance. 

Implementation Details. We evaluate DeferKV and all baselines under varying cache budgets (Btotal = nL , with n ∈ [64 , 1024] ), where n is the prompt KV budget for each layer and L is the number of layers. We use greedy decoding without sampling for all experiments to ensure de-terministic and reproducible evaluation. We use the official baseline implementations with standard eviction settings, with further details provided in Appendix B. All experiments are conducted on NVIDIA A100 GPUs with 80 GB of memory. 

5.2 Results on LongBench 

We evaluate DeferKV on 16 LongBench datasets using three language models. Table 1 reports de-tailed results under a tight cache budget of Btotal =64 L and a larger cache budget of Btotal = 512 L.DeferKV achieves the highest average score across all six model–budget settings. Under the tight 64 L budget, DeferKV consis-tently ranks as the top-performing method across models, achieving up to a 3.58% relative improve-ment over the strongest baseline with Qwen3-8B. This advantage is particularly pronounced under constrained cache budgets, where real decode feed-back improves KV importance estimation and helps preserve information essential for subsequent gen-eration. As the budget increases to Btotal = 512 L, the performance gaps among methods narrow because more prompt KV tokens can be retained. Neverthe-less, DeferKV continues to achieve the best aver-age performance and approaches Full KV across all three models. In addition to its strong perfor-mance, DeferKV requires no additional training, draft model, or future-query prediction module, making it simple to implement and deploy. We fur-ther integrate DeferKV with PyramidKV, AdaKV, and ChunkKV to demonstrate its compatibility with existing KV cache compression methods. The corresponding results are provided in Appendix D.

6 Conclusion 

In this paper, we revisit a basic design choice in one-shot KV cache compression: when to commit to irreversible eviction. DeferKV moves this decision to the first decoding step, where real generation-side feedback becomes available, and uses tem-poral weighting to integrate the first real decode query with recent prompt queries. Extensive evalu-ations on LongBench, Needle-in-a-Haystack, and RULER show that DeferKV improves long-context performance with low inference latency. It requires no additional training, draft model, or auxiliary pre-diction module, providing a lightweight approach to efficient long-context inference. Limitations 

DeferKV postpones one-shot KV eviction until the earliest real decode query becomes available, which requires retaining the full prompt KV cache through the first decoding step. This design there-fore shifts the memory reduction to subsequent de-coding. Our experiments focus on models with standard token-wise KV caches under softmax attention. Extending DeferKV to architectures with different cache organizations, such as MLA or hybrid linear-attention models, may require architecture-specific adaptation.

B Implementation Details 

DeferKV. We use an observation-window size of 

W = 32 , a temporal discount factor of η = 0 .7,and average pooling with kernel size 5. Compres-sion is performed after the first standard decode step ( d = 1 ). The aggregate prompt-KV budget is Btotal = nL , where n is the retained prompt-KV budget per layer and L is the number of model layers. 

SnapKV. The final 32 prompt queries are used as the observation window. Importance scores are smoothed by average pooling with kernel size 5,after which the highest-ranked prompt KV pairs are retained under the target budget. LAQ. Starting from a temporary KV cache ob-tained by applying SnapKV to the original prompt cache, the target model performs up to eight looka-head decoding steps and stores the correspond-ing query states in the Q-Cache. These looka-head queries, together with the final eight prompt queries, are then used to recompute the attention-based importance scores of the original prompt KV entries before the second eviction. 

SpecKV. The draft models are Qwen3-0.6B for Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and mistral-7b-instruct-v0.3-bnb-4bit for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3. The target-model queries corresponding to the prompt observation window and the generated draft tokens are used to rank historical prompt KV pairs. In the reproduc-tion, the resulting importance scores are smoothed using average pooling with kernel size 5.

LookaheadKV. LookaheadKV is configured with 32 learnable lookahead tokens, and a sepa-rate set of lookahead modules is trained for each target model. The training data consist of 50K sam-ples from ChatQA2-Long-SFT-data and 20K from tulu-3-sft-olmo-2-mixture. The predicted impor-tance scores are smoothed using average pooling with kernel size 5 before prompt-KV selection. 

Common Settings. All methods use random seed 42. Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 are evaluated using their official checkpoints, tokenizers, and prompt tem-plates. Greedy decoding with batch size one is used. All experiments are conducted on NVIDIA A100 80 GB GPUs.
