# Co-Optimizing Graph Sparsification and Approximate Computing for Energy-Efficient FPGA-Based GCN Inference 

Nathaniel Kaye Mellor, Shreejith Shanker, George Floros 

Department of Electronic and Electrical Engineering, Trinity College Dublin, Ireland 

{kayemeln, shankers, florosg }@tcd.ie 

Abstract —Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful framework for learning from graph-structured data, yet their deployment on resource-constrained edge platforms remains challenging due to the computational and memory demands of sparse graph aggregation. This work presents an FPGA-based GCN accelerator that combines DSpar graph sparsification, 8-bit quantization, and approximate mul-tipliers on the AMD Kria KV260. Evaluated on Cora, LastFM Asia, and Amazon Photo, the design explores the interaction between sparsification and approximation across graphs with widely varying densities. Results show that the effectiveness of approximate arithmetic is governed by accumulation depth within GCN computations. Approximate multipliers are most effective when applied to sparse aggregation operations, while graph sparsification further improves their viability by reducing aggregation depth. The combined approach achieves up to 9.88 ×

speedup while maintaining 86.6% classification accuracy on Amazon Photo, and 1.52 × speedup with 77.0% accuracy on Cora, with total power consumption below 1 W. These results demonstrate that graph sparsification and approximate comput-ing are complementary techniques whose co-optimization enables efficient low-power GCN inference on edge FPGA platforms. 

Index Terms —Graph Convolutional Networks, FPGA Acceler-ators, Approximate Computing, Graph Sparsification 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a power-ful class of machine learning models for learning from graph-structured data, achieving state-of-the-art performance across applications including recommender systems, robotics, traffic forecasting, molecular analysis, and anomaly detection [1]. Among the various GNN architectures, Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have gained widespread adoption due to their simplicity and strong predictive performance [2]. As graph-based learning continues to move into latency-sensitive and privacy-critical domains, efficient deployment of GCN inference on edge devices has become increasingly important. However, deploying GCNs on resource-constrained edge platforms remains challenging [3]. A typical GCN layer con-sists of a sparse aggregation operation followed by a dense feature transformation. The aggregation stage is commonly implemented as a sparse-dense matrix multiplication (SpMM) involving the graph adjacency matrix, which is inherently sparse and characterized by irregular memory access patterns and highly skewed node-degree distributions [4]. As a result, sparse aggregation often dominates inference latency and energy consumption, limiting the efficiency of conventional processing platforms. FPGAs have emerged as an attractive platform for GCN ac-celeration due to their energy efficiency and reconfigurability. Recent FPGA-based GCN accelerators address the challenges of sparse and irregular graph computation through a variety of architectural techniques. LW-GCN employs a software-hardware co-design approach with custom sparse formats, workload balancing, and quantization [5], while BoostGCN improves memory efficiency through graph partitioning and pipelined execution [6]. Other works exploit graph properties such as adjacency matrix symmetry for improved data reuse [7] or graph structural similarity to reduce convolution oper-ations [8]. While these approaches improve throughput and energy efficiency, they primarily focus on optimizing sparse computation and memory access patterns. The combined use of graph sparsification and approximate arithmetic remains largely unexplored. The contributions of this work are as follows. First , we present an FPGA-based GCN accelerator that combines DSpar graph sparsification [9], 8-bit quantization, and approximate multipliers within a unified HLS framework. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first integration of DSpar and approxi-mate multipliers within a dedicated GNN accelerator. Second ,we show that the effectiveness of approximate multipliers is governed by accumulation depth, and that graph sparsification improves their viability by reducing aggregation depth. Third ,we demonstrate this interaction across three datasets spanning a wide range of graph densities, from Cora (4.9 average neighbors per node) and LastFM Asia (8.3) to Amazon Photo (32.1), showing that the benefits of combining sparsification and approximation become increasingly pronounced on denser graphs. Finally , the proposed design achieves up to 9.88 ×

speedup while maintaining 86.6% classification accuracy on Amazon Photo, with total power consumption below 1W. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II introduces the GCN inference model and the graph sparsi-fication technique used in this work. Section III presents the proposed accelerator architecture and implementation method-ology. Section IV describes the experimental setup and eval-uation methodology. Finally, Section V concludes the paper. II. GCN I NFERENCE CHALLENGES

C. Graph Sparsification and Approximate Arithmetic 

To further improve efficiency, DSpar is applied as an offline preprocessing step. DSpar removes a subset of graph edges ac-cording to degree-aware sampling probabilities while preserv-ing the essential structural characteristics required for message passing. The resulting sparsified adjacency matrix contains fewer non-zero entries, directly reducing the workload of the SpMM stage. Since sparsification is performed prior to deployment, no additional runtime overhead is introduced during inference. All computations are performed using fixed-point arithmetic to reduce memory footprint and hardware cost. An 8-bit fixed-point implementation was investigated, with wider accumula-tors employed during reduction operations to prevent overflow. Building on the quantized design, approximate arithmetic is introduced by replacing exact multipliers with approximate multipliers from EvoApproxLib [10]. Three signed 8-bit mul-tiplier variants – 1KV8, 1L2J, and 1L2N – spanning near-exact, moderate-error, and high-error points on the library’s power-error Pareto front were evaluated. Each unit is supplied as a synthesizable gate-level C function, which HLS maps into LUT fabric; a lightweight wrapper substitutes it for the 

* operator at the MAC site, with the active variant selected at compile time. In this work the approximate multiplier is applied only to the SpMM stage of the first GCN layer; the GEMM stage and the entire second layer remain exact. IV. E XPERIMENTAL EVALUATION AND DISCUSSION 

A. Experimental Setup 

The proposed accelerator was evaluated on the AMD Kria KV260 FPGA platform using Vitis HLS at 100 MHz. Ex-periments were performed on the Cora, LastFM Asia, and Amazon Photo node-classification datasets. These datasets span a wide range of graph densities, from Cora (4.9 average neighbors per node) and LastFM Asia (8.3) to the significantly denser Amazon Photo graph (32.1), providing a suitable testbed for evaluating the interaction between sparsification and approximate computation. The evaluated model is a two-layer GCN, with inference executed layer-by-layer using the proposed HLS kernel. Accuracy is measured against an FP32 baseline, while latency and power are obtained from the FPGA implementation reports. We evaluate four configurations: an exact 8-bit fixed-point accelerator, an accelerator using approximate multipliers, an accelerator using DSpar graph sparsification, and the combined DSpar plus approximate-multiplier design. Speedup is reported relative to the unsparsified exact 8-bit implementation for each dataset. Latency, speedup, and classification accuracy are summarized in Table I.

Figure 3 shows node-classification accuracy across the full sparsification sweep for all three datasets and the four multi-plier configurations. Two key trends emerge. First, the exact and 1KV8 kernels track the FP32 baseline closely until spar-sification removes sufficient edges to disrupt message passing: on the sparse Cora graph this occurs beyond ϵ = 0 .35 , whereas the denser Amazon Photo graph tolerates sparsification up to ϵ = 0 .7.Second, the impact of approximation scales with graph density. On sparse graphs such as Cora, approximation causes only minor degradation, whereas on Amazon Photo aggressive approximation leads to substantial accuracy loss unless sparsi-fication is applied. As sparsification reduces average node de-gree, the accuracy of approximate multipliers is progressively restored. LastFM Asia exhibits intermediate behavior between these two extremes. This trend supports the hypothesis that approximation sensitivity is governed by accumulation depth rather than dataset-specific characteristics. V. C ONCLUSIONS 

This paper presented an FPGA-based GCN accelerator that combines graph sparsification, 8-bit quantization, and approximate arithmetic within a unified HLS framework. Experimental results show that graph sparsification and ap-proximate computation are complementary optimization tech-niques: sparsification reduces aggregation workload while also improving the viability of approximate multipliers by reducing accumulation depth. Evaluated on Cora, LastFM Asia, and Amazon Photo, the proposed design achieves up to 9.88 ×

speedup while maintaining 86.6% classification accuracy on Amazon Photo, with total power consumption below 1 W. These results demonstrate that jointly exploiting graph-level and arithmetic-level approximations can enable efficient low-power GCN inference on edge FPGA platforms. REFERENCES [1] G. Corso, H.
