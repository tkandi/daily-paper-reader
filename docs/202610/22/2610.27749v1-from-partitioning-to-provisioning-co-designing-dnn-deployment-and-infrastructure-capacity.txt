# From Partitioning to Provisioning: Co-Designing DNN Deployment and Infrastructure Capacity 

Zongyao Huang, Fan Yang, Qi Cao, Yulin Shao 

Abstract —Deep neural network (DNN) partitioning enables distributed inference by dividing model computation across interconnected devices. Existing approaches have largely focused on optimizing DNN deployment over infrastructure with fixed computing and communication capacities. Yet when these re-sources cannot adequately support inference demands, optimizing deployment alone is insufficient: the supporting infrastructure must also be provisioned. Crucially, deployment and provisioning are inherently coupled. How a DNN is partitioned determines where capacity is needed, while capacity provisioning can fun-damentally change the preferred deployment. Motivated by this interdependence, we investigate the co-design of DNN deployment and infrastructure capacity for pipelined inference. Despite the coupling between these decisions, we reveal an efficiently solvable structure: on acyclic networks, the problem admits an exact shortest-path reformulation, enabling globally optimal joint design in polynomial time. Numerical evaluations demonstrate the benefits of co-design over treating deployment and provi-sioning separately, highlighting the potential of inference-driven infrastructure planning. 

Index Terms —Distributed DNN inference, DNN partitioning, infrastructure co-design, capacity provisioning. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Distributed deep neural network (DNN) inference [1], [2] enables computationally demanding models to be executed across interconnected devices. A key enabling technique is DNN partitioning [3], [4], which distributes different portions of a model across devices. For streaming workloads, these portions can operate as a pipeline, allowing successive inputs to be processed concurrently [5], [6]. How to partition and deploy DNNs across computing networks has thus become a fundamental problem in distributed inference. Existing research has largely approached this problem from a deployment perspective: given an infrastructure with fixed computing and communication capacities, how should a DNN be partitioned and deployed to achieve the best inference per-formance? Extensive efforts have been devoted to optimizing inference latency [3], [7], [8], throughput [2], [6], and other performance metrics [9], [10] under such resource constraints. While this perspective addresses how to make effective use of existing infrastructure, it leaves open a complementary question: how should the infrastructure itself be provisioned to support the inference workloads deployed on it? 

The answer depends on the deployment itself. Partitioning determines where computation occurs and how much data

traverses each link [3], [8], thereby shaping where addi-tional capacity is needed. Yet capacity expansion can change which deployment is preferable: a deployment that maximizes throughput with existing resources may be far more expensive to upgrade than an alternative. Deployment and infrastructure capacity must therefore be designed jointly, rather than opti-mized in sequence. In this paper, we develop such a co-design framework for pipelined DNN inference. Given a computing network with heterogeneous capacity costs, we jointly optimize the physi-cal deployment path, the partitioning of DNN computational blocks, and the additional computing and communication capacities needed to sustain a target inference throughput. Al-though this problem couples discrete deployment choices with continuous provisioning decisions, we show that it possesses an exact and efficiently solvable structure on acyclic networks. The key insight is that the bottleneck-stage characterization of pipeline throughput allows the capacity requirements of individual stages to be determined locally. These requirements induce additive provisioning costs along a deployment path, enabling an exact reduction of the joint design problem to a shortest-path problem on a state graph. The main contributions of this paper are summarized below:  

> •

We formulate an inference-infrastructure co-design problem that jointly determines DNN partitioning, deployment paths, and computing and communication capacity increments. Unlike fixed-capacity deployment optimization, our formu-lation treats infrastructure capacity as a design variable, allowing inference deployment and capacity provisioning to be optimized together.  

> •

We establish an exact equivalence between the co-design problem on acyclic networks and a shortest-path prob-lem on an appropriately constructed state graph. Based on this structural result, we develop a dynamic program-ming algorithm that obtains a globally optimal solution in 

O(|V| N + |E| N 2) time and O(|V| N ) space.  

> •

We evaluate the proposed approach on 400 instances with VGG-16 and ResNet-50 workloads. Compared with two heuristic provisioning strategies that do not jointly optimize deployment and capacity expansion, our approach achieves substantial cost reductions, with median cost ratios reaching 

25 and worst-case ratios exceeding 100 . These results highlight the importance of co-designing DNN deployment and its supporting infrastructure capacity. II. P ROBLEM FORMULATION

These results show that joint optimization can lead to much lower provisioning costs, even when deployment is updated during provisioning. In our Python implementation on an AMD Ryzen 7 8845H CPU, the median runtimes are 0.25, 0.28, and 1.8 ms for PSG, PFP, and PWF, respectively, showing that PSG remains computationally efficient on the tested instances.
