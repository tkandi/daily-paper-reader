# Hard and Soft Real-Time DNN Inference Scheduling on Heterogeneous Multi-Accelerator Systems via Reinforcement Learning 

Francesco Giulio Blanco ⋆,Enrico Russo , Giuseppe Ascia , Davide Patti , and Maurizio Palesi 

University of Catania, Catania, Italy 

francesco.blanco@phd.unict.it , {name.surname}@unict.it 

Abstract. We present a scheduling framework for heterogeneous multi-accelerator systems executing both hard real-time and soft real-time QoS-aware DNN inference workloads. The framework combines an on-line reinforcement learning (RL) scheduler with a deterministic runtime safety layer. The RL scheduler dynamically generates candidate sched-ules by jointly selecting sub-job priorities and accelerator assignments, while runtime certification guarantees that all HRT deadlines remain satisfied independently of the learned policy. To enable runtime certification in heterogeneous precedence-constrained systems, we introduce a schedulability-preserving prefix replacement cri-terion based on machine-wise cuts. The proposed criterion guarantees that runtime-generated schedule prefixes preserve the existence of a fea-sible continuation without requiring complete finish-time dominance with respect to a feasible reference schedule. Experimental results on heterogeneous DNN accelerator platforms show that the proposed framework preserves HRT schedulability while improv-ing QoS-aware deadline satisfaction, client-level service quality, and ac-celerator utilization compared to state-of-the-art scheduling approaches. 

Keywords: Real-time · Scheduling · DNN · Reinforcement learning · QoS · Heterogeneous · Multi-tenant · Multi-accelerator · Multi-processor 

1 Introduction 

Deep Neural Network (DNN) inference is increasingly used in safety-critical cyber-physical systems such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, industrial au-tomation, and edge AI infrastructures [2,29]. These systems often execute mixed workloads on heterogeneous multi-accelerator platforms, where hard real-time (HRT) inference tasks coexist with soft real-time (SRT) requests generated by multiple clients [36,40,25].

Scheduling such workloads is challenging because DNN inference induces precedence-constrained execution graphs, accelerator heterogeneity couples allo-cation and priority decisions, and SRT requests arrive dynamically at runtime [30,22]. At the same time, HRT tasks must always satisfy their deadlines under worst-case execution assumptions. Existing real-time scheduling approaches can provide timing guarantees, but are often based on static schedules that poorly adapt to runtime variability [20,12]. Conversely, adaptive online schedulers and multi-tenant inference sys-tems improve utilization and responsiveness, but generally lack strict HRT guar-antees [10,6]. Reinforcement Learning (RL) is attractive for online scheduling because it can learn workload-dependent policies without handcrafted heuristics [31], and this is the reason why it is employed in this work, but learned policies alone cannot be trusted for safety-critical scheduling. This paper presents a scheduling framework for heterogeneous multi-accelerator systems that combines adaptive online scheduling with strict HRT guarantees. The framework separates optimization from safety. An RL-based online scheduler dynamically generates candidate schedule prefixes 1 by jointly selecting acceler-ator assignments and execution priorities, while a deterministic runtime safety layer validates each candidate before execution. To enable runtime certification in heterogeneous precedence-constrained sys-tems, we introduce a schedulability-preserving prefix replacement criterion based on machine-wise cuts, inspired by transfer schedulability [38]. The proposed cri-terion guarantees that runtime-generated schedule prefixes preserve the existence of a feasible continuation without requiring complete finish-time dominance with respect to a feasible reference schedule. Experimental results on heterogeneous DNN accelerator platforms show that the proposed framework preserves HRT schedulability while improving SRT deadline satisfaction, client-level QoS, and accelerator utilization compared to state-of-the-art scheduling approaches. – A formulation of mixed hard/soft real-time DNN inference scheduling on heterogeneous multi-accelerator systems using a DAG-based task model. 

– A schedulability-preserving runtime certification mechanism for heteroge-neous precedence-constrained schedules.

2 System and Scheduling Model 

We consider the problem of scheduling periodic hard real-time (HRT) tasks together with aperiodic soft real-time (SRT) jobs on a heterogeneous process-ing platform. The model follows standard real-time scheduling terminology and DAG-based task models [7,4,13,35,5].

Communication and data-movement assumptions. Prior real-time DNN schedul-ing approaches explicitly model fine-grained data-movement effects, including cross-processor communication, CPU/GPU data transfers, and layer-level pipelin-ing or migration [24,16,39]. Indeed, after a runtime prefix replacement, another HRT or SRT sub-job may execute between two consecutive layers, or the successor may be assigned to a different accelerator, invalidating assumptions on local data availability.

2.4 Scheduling Model and Objective 

As in standard multiprocessor scheduling, scheduling consists of two coupled problems [13]: an allocation problem, which decides on which processor a com-putation executes, and a priority problem, which decides when and in what order ready computations execute. In system-architecture terminology, this cor-responds to space-time scheduling: the spatial decision is processor assignment, while the temporal decision is execution ordering. The scheduler operates at sub-job granularity and uses dynamic priorities to decide the execution ordering. The priority of a ready sub-job may change over time as a function of deadlines, laxities, processor availability, queue state, graph progress, and online scheduling decisions. HRT sub-jobs execute non-preemptively. If an executing SRT sub-job must be interrupted to preserve HRT correctness, its partial execution is discarded and any later execution is treated as a new dispatch. We model this event as cancellation rather than preemption with resumption, which reflects accelerator architectures where saving and restoring the internal execution state may be unsupported or prohibitively expensive. A schedule assigns each ready sub-job u to a processor mu and to an execution interval [su, f u). A schedule is valid if it satisfies: (i) eligibility, mu ∈ M u for 6 F. G. Blanco et al. 

every executed sub-job u; (ii) processor exclusivity, no processor executes more than one sub-job at any time; (iii) nonparallel execution, no sub-job executes on more than one processor at any time; and (iv) precedence preservation, (u, v ) ∈

EJ implies sv ≥ fu.The scheduling objective is lexicographic. First, all admitted HRT jobs must meet their deadlines under all admissible runtime executions. Second, subject to this hard constraint, the scheduler should improve SRT responsiveness, soft-deadline hit ratios, client-level SLO satisfaction, and accelerator utilization by reducing idle intervals across heterogeneous accelerator resources. 

3 Framework Overview 

The proposed framework combines adaptive online scheduling with strict hard real-time (HRT) guarantees for heterogeneous multi-accelerator systems execut-ing both HRT and soft real-time (SRT) DNN inference workloads. The key idea is to separate safety from performance optimization . Runtime performance is optimized by an online scheduler, while HRT correctness is en-forced independently through a deterministic runtime certification mechanism. Figure 1 illustrates the overall framework. Offline, the system constructs a feasible HRT-only reference schedule under worst-case execution assumptions. At runtime, an online scheduling policy observes the current system state and generates candidate schedule prefixes that jointly schedule HRT and SRT sub-jobs. Before execution, each candidate prefix is validated by a runtime safety check. If the prefix is certified safe, it is executed; otherwise, execution falls back to the corresponding portion of the certified HRT reference schedule. As a consequence, HRT correctness does not depend on the online scheduler itself. The scheduler may improve SRT responsiveness, fairness, and accelerator utilization, but unsafe runtime decisions are filtered by the safety layer before execution. Although this work employs a reinforcement learning (RL) scheduler, the framework itself is policy-agnostic. Any online scheduling policy capable of gen-erating runtime candidate schedules can be enclosed within the same safety envelope.

3.2 Runtime Scheduling 

The online scheduler is activated periodically with period TS . At activation time 

tk = kT S , the scheduler observes the current runtime state, including ready HRT and SRT sub-jobs, processor availability, queue occupancy, graph progress, and remaining execution tails inherited from previous scheduler windows. In heterogeneous DNN inference systems, allocation and priority decisions [13] are tightly coupled because execution times depend on the selected accelerator. Based on the observed runtime state, the scheduler generates a candidate schedule prefix for a bounded future interval. Unlike the offline reference sched-ule, the runtime scheduler is fully adaptive and may execute HRT sub-jobs earlier than in the reference schedule, dynamically reorder HRT and SRT work, select different eligible accelerators, leave processors temporarily idle, or opportunisti-cally exploit runtime slack for SRT execution. 8 F. G. Blanco et al. 

The scheduler activation period TS determines the tradeoff between run-time adaptability and scheduling overhead. Smaller activation periods improve responsiveness to newly arrived SRT jobs but increase runtime scheduler over-head. Let τsch denote an upper bound on the total runtime overhead including schedule generation, runtime certification, dispatching, and cancellation han-dling. This overhead is included in the certified timing model. 

3.3 Runtime Safety Certification 

The online scheduler is not trusted for HRT correctness. Consequently, every runtime-generated candidate prefix must be validated before execution. A natural solution would be to directly test whether a candidate prefix pre-serves future HRT schedulability. However, exact runtime feasibility analysis is computationally impractical for heterogeneous DAG-based systems with prece-dence constraints and non-preemptive execution. Instead, the framework em-ploys a bounded-time sufficient acceptance test derived from the offline refer-ence schedule. Intuitively, the runtime certification mechanism verifies whether the candidate prefix preserves enough certified progress to guarantee that all unfinished HRT work can still complete feasibly after the checked cut. If the candidate prefix is certified safe, execution proceeds according to the online schedule. Otherwise, execution falls back to the corresponding portion of the offline HRT reference schedule and all SRT work contained in the rejected prefix is discarded. The acceptance test therefore acts as a deterministic safety envelope surrounding the online scheduler. Unsafe runtime decisions may reduce performance, but they cannot compromise HRT correctness. Section 4 formalizes the runtime certification mechanism through the notion of schedulability-preserving prefix replacement. 

4 Runtime Safety Certification 

This section formalizes the runtime certification mechanism used to guarantee hard real-time correctness independently of the online scheduler. We first recall the intuition behind transfer schedulability on uniproces-sors and explain why its classical scalar slack formulation does not directly extend to heterogeneous precedence-constrained systems. We then introduce a schedulability-preserving prefix replacement criterion suitable for heterogeneous multi-accelerator DNN scheduling. 

4.1 From Transfer Schedulability to Prefix Certification 

Transfer schedulability [38] provides a way to preserve schedulability by com-paring an online execution with a feasible reference schedule. In its classical uniprocessor form, the online scheduler monitors whether the work that must Hard and Soft Real-Time DNN Inference Scheduling 9

match the reference schedule has become locally urgent. When a slackless in-terval is detected, the scheduler must give priority to the corresponding locally urgent work; otherwise, schedulability may no longer be transferable from the reference schedule. We use this idea only as a design principle. We do not attempt to ex-tend the uniprocessor slackless test directly to heterogeneous multiprocessor DAG scheduling. In our setting, feasibility depends jointly on machine assign-ment, non-preemptive execution, precedence constraints, and future resource contention across unrelated accelerators. Therefore, a scalar slack quantity is not sufficient to characterize whether the online state can still match a feasible continuation. To see why, consider even the simpler case of a set J of independent, non-preemptive, non-migratory jobs on M identical processors over an interval of length L. Let

Consequently, the runtime safety layer deliberately uses a bounded-time sufficient test. If the test accepts, HRT schedulability is preserved; if it rejects, the system falls back to a certified continuation.

5 Online Scheduler 

This section describes how the proposed framework applies the schedulability-preserving prefix replacement theory of Section 4 at runtime. The framework employs an online scheduler that periodically generates can-didate schedule prefixes for the currently available HRT and SRT sub-jobs. The scheduler itself is not trusted for hard real-time correctness. Consequently, every runtime-generated candidate prefix is validated through the runtime certification mechanism derived from Theorem 1 before execution. If the candidate prefix satisfies the certification conditions, it is committed for execution. Otherwise, the system falls back to the corresponding portion of the certified HRT reference schedule and any SRT work contained in the rejected prefix is discarded. Although the framework is scheduler-agnostic, this work employs a reinforce-ment learning (RL)-based scheduler that jointly optimizes accelerator assign-ment and execution ordering to improve SRT QoS and accelerator utilization while remaining enclosed within the deterministic HRT safety envelope. Section 5.1 describes the runtime HRT certification mechanism, Section 5.2 briefly reviews the RL background used in this work, and Section 5.3 presents the proposed RL scheduler policy.

5.3 RL-based Scheduler Policy 

The online scheduler considered in this work is based on the DDPG [23] al-gorithm described in Section 5.2. We considered alternative RL formulations, Hard and Soft Real-Time DNN Inference Scheduling 15 

including discrete-action methods such as Q-learning. However, directly repre-senting the joint sub-job ordering and accelerator-assignment decisions as dis-crete actions would result in a combinatorial action space that grows rapidly with the number of ready sub-jobs and available accelerators. Even other formu-lations, such as multi-armed bandit formulations [19,26], are also not well suited to this problem because scheduling decisions are inherently sequential and affect future system states; capturing such state transitions and delayed consequences therefore requires a sequential decision-making formulation. We adopt DDPG and encode the scheduling decisions through continuous priority values, which are subsequently decoded into execution priorities and accelerator assignments. Importantly, priorities are an internal mechanism used by the online policy to encode and construct a candidate schedule. Once the candidate schedule has been generated, its validity and certification depend only on the resulting machine assignments, execution intervals, precedence constraints, and not on the priority values that produced it. Therefore, priorities do not appear in Definition 6. We consider the state as the ready sub-jobs and the current accelerator oc-cupancy. At each scheduler activation, the policy observes the current runtime state and generates a candidate schedule by jointly selecting sub-job priorities and accelerator assignments. This formulation allows the scheduler to adapt to time-varying workload arrivals and heterogeneous accelerator availability while accounting for precedence constraints, resource contention, and execution tails. 6.1 Experimental Setup 

We evaluate the proposed framework on a heterogeneous multi-accelerator plat-form with six DNN accelerators, following the established heterogeneous setup 18 F. G. Blanco et al. 

of [6,31], enabling the comparison with prior scheduling techniques. In archi-tectural terms, three accelerators implement a row-stationary dataflow and an organization close to Eyeriss [9]; the other three implement a weight-stationary dataflow and an organization close to Simba [32]. For each accelerator fam-ily, two instances are small and one instance is large. Hence, the platform exposes both dataflow heterogeneity and size heterogeneity, and sub-job ex-ecution times depend on the selected accelerator. We consider representative DNN inference workloads from image classification and object detection. The image-classification set includes AlexNet, InceptionV3, and ResNet50, while the object-detection set includes YOLO-v2. Each inference request is represented as a DAG of layer-level sub-jobs, with precedence constraints induced by the network topology. For every layer and eligible accelerator, we use profiled execu-tion latencies as the scheduling costs. SRT arrivals follow a Google-like workload model derived from large-scale production traces. Following the workload gener-ator of Da Costa et al. [11], inter-arrival times are sampled from a heavy-tailed Pareto process. Let d denote the target mean inter-arrival time, corresponding to the generator’s dynamism parameter. For each submitted SRT job i, we sample

We evaluate two workload intensities: d = 40 ms , denoting a moderate-pressure workload, and d = 10 ms ,denoting a high-pressure workload with four times more frequent SRT arrivals. 6.2 Baselines 

We compare against representative online DNN schedulers, slack-based real-time mechanisms, and simple dispatch heuristics, all implemented at layer granular-ity with the same profiled accelerator latencies. Methods that do not natively provide HRT guarantees in our heterogeneous DAG-based model are evaluated as HRT-safe wrapped variants, obtained by enclosing them in the same prefix-replacement safety envelope used by our framework. This also shows that the proposed certification-and-fallback mechanism is not specific to our RL sched-uler. Any online scheduling policy can be enclosed within the same safety en-velope: if the policy is effective at scheduling real-time jobs on heterogeneous platforms, the fallback mechanism can be used to make its execution HRT-safe. We include PREMA [10], adapting its priority/allocation logic to our hetero-geneous layer-level simulator. In particular, our adaptation selects among the highest-aging ready layers according to their expected latency, and assigns them to accelerators considering current availability. We also include a client-aware RL policy [31] that augments the policy state and reward with client-level SLO information based on the deadline-hit rate SLI. We also employ the algorithms proposed by Tang et al. [35] by constructing an HRT schedule with H-EDF or FF, deriving per-accelerator slack windows, and admitting SRT layers only when they can be placed by RC, TC, or TCSD without violating release times, latest-start-time bounds, machine order, or DNN precedence constraints. We im-plement SSML [15], originally formulated for a uniprocessor setting, as a conser-vative scheduler-core adaptation that computes a modified-laEDF slack budget 

σ from the remaining HRT layer workload and admits a non-preemptive SRT layer only if its whole execution fits both the available slack budget and the cur-rent scheduling window. We adapt CD-MSA [37] by applying its EDF ordering,

Scheduler overhead. We account for the end-to-end runtime overhead incurred at each scheduling instant, including state encoding, policy inference, runtime cer-tification, and dispatching/cancellation bookkeeping. We denote this overhead by τsch = τenc + τpolicy + τcert + τdisp . This term is treated as the worst-case exe-cution time of the online scheduler and is charged at every scheduling activation in the experimental timing model. The scheduler is activated every TS = 5 ms .To make τsch bounded, the number of sub-jobs visible to the online policy is limited to a fixed maximum Nmax : all ready HRT sub-jobs are always included, 24 F. G. Blanco et al. 

while the SRT ready queue is truncated when necessary; more priority is given to the SRT jobs pertaining to clients with a SLO achievement rate lower than the target. This bounds the cost of both state encoding and policy inference independently of the total SRT backlog. For the GRU policy with hidden size 192, the measured cost is 592 cycles per encoded sub-job on the smallest accel-erator. With Nmax = 64 , this gives τpolicy = 64 · 592 = 37888 cycles = 37 .9 μs

at 1 GHz, i.e., 0.76% of the scheduling period. The runtime certification step is also bounded: it scans at most all the N HRT sub-jobs, therefore it has O(N )

complexity and is bounded by Nmax . Moreover, since sub-jobs are distributed across scheduling periods, N is only an upper bound. Increasing the number of HRT tasks or DAG length increases N linearly. 

Scheduling period analysis. We evaluate the impact of TS under high pressure (d = 10 ms) through two experiments: (i) deploying a policy trained with TS =5 ms at different scheduling periods, and (ii) training and testing a separate policy for each period. For TS ∈ { 0.5, 1, 1.25 , 2.5, 5, 10 } ms, the policy trained at 5 ms achieves SRT hit ratios HS of 73 .73% , 76 .84% , 69 .45% , 74 .96% , 81 .40% , and 71 .12% , respec-tively. Although performance peaks at the training period, the policy retains effectiveness across the tested periods, demonstrating transfer across scheduling frequencies. Conversely, policies trained specifically at 0.5, 1, 1.25 , 2.5, and 10 ms achieve only 25 .69% , 27 .92% , 46 .82% , 48 .79% , and 62 .81% , respectively. We at-tribute the poorer results of training with short periods to the following reasons. Shorter windows contain fewer completed sub-jobs contributing to the reward and provide less execution time over which the policy can exploit accelerator availability. Moreover, scheduling overhead accounts for a larger fraction of ex-ecution time. Conversely, large periods are constrained by timing requirements of both HRT and SRT jobs. Their relative deadlines must accommodate waiting time, scheduling overhead, and the required execution time. Additional timing slack also leaves more room for online optimization. 

7 Related Work 

Table 2 compares representative related work along key dimensions. 

Hard-periodic/soft-aperiodic scheduling on heterogeneous resources. Tang et al. [35] study the joint scheduling of hard periodic and soft aperiodic tasks on hetero-geneous resources. Their approach is divided into two phases: an offline phase, where hard periodic tasks are scheduled using either Heterogeneous EDF (H-EDF) or Fastest First (FF); then, online, soft aperiodic tasks are scheduled using the per-resource slack windows extracted from the resulting hard sched-ule, using Resource-Centric (RC), Task-Centric (TC), or Task-Centric with Slack Defragmentation (TCSD). TCSD can shift hard periodic instances earlier than their latest start times to enlarge later slack windows. DNN inference on domain-specific accelerators. Extensive research has investi-gated scheduling strategies for DNN inference on accelerator-based platforms. Static solutions such as LayerWeaver [27] and AI-MT [3] assign DNN layers to accelerators based on analytical models, but they cannot adapt to runtime vari-ability. Schedulers like PREMA [10] and MAGMA [17] adjust accelerator alloca-tion in response to workload fluctuations. Techniques such as Planaria [14] and MoCA [18] redistribute compute resources at finer granularity to improve utiliza-tion and co-location efficiency. Orion [33] considers multiple priority queues in GPU scheduling. The considered systems optimize different system-level metrics, without concentrating on single client QoS, and without providing deterministic HRT guarantees. CD-MSA [37] targets EDF-based real-time DNN inference on multi-systolic-array accelerators, which are homogeneous accelerator platforms. In parallel, several works have focused on enhancing the time predictability of DNNs on general-purpose processors, including efforts to improve timing isola-tion and scheduling analysis [8]. 

Slack stealing and modified look-ahead EDF. Slack stealing is a classical mech-anism for improving the responsiveness of soft aperiodic work in systems where hard periodic tasks have already been guaranteed. A slack stealer exploits unused processing time from the periodic schedule to execute the aperiodic work, while ensuring that previously guaranteed periodic deadlines are not violated [21]. This idea is closely related to the look-ahead EDF (laEDF) mechanisms used in real-time dynamic voltage scaling: Pillai and Shin [28] compute the minimum amount of periodic work that must execute before the next deadline, deferring the remaining work to later intervals when this is safe. SSML [15] adapts laEDF principle to hard-periodic and soft-aperiodic scheduling for uniprocessor systems with independent tasks. 

8 Conclusion 

This paper presented a scheduling framework for heterogeneous multi-accelerator systems executing both hard real-time and soft real-time QoS-aware DNN in-ference workloads. The proposed approach combines an online RL-based sched-26 F. G. Blanco et al. 

uler with a deterministic runtime certification mechanism that guarantees HRT correctness independently of the learned policy. To enable safe runtime adapta-tion, we introduced a schedulability-preserving prefix replacement criterion for heterogeneous precedence-constrained schedules. Experimental results showed that the framework preserves HRT schedulability while improving QoS-aware deadline satisfaction, client-level service quality, and accelerator utilization com-pared to state-of-the-art scheduling approaches. Future work includes extending the framework to support additional runtime uncertainties, workload prediction mechanisms, and alternative QoS optimization objectives.
