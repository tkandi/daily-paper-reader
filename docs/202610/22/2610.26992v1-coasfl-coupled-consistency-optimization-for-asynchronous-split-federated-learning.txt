# CoASFL: Coupled Consistency Optimization for Asynchronous Split Federated Learning 

Junhao Cheng, Yunming Liao , Hongli Xu, Member, IEEE, Yang Xu, Member, IEEE, Zuan Xie, 

Abstract —Split Federated Learning (SFL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training large-scale deep models on resource-constrained edge devices. Although asynchronous SFL alleviates the straggler bottleneck caused by system heterogeneity, it inevitably introduces two coupled dimensions of inconsistency: horizontal inconsistency across clients and vertical inconsistency between clients and the server. These inconsistencies, further ex-acerbated by statistical heterogeneity, result in biased activation representations, model divergence, and severe client drift. To address these challenges, we propose CoASFL, an inconsistency-aware asynchronous SFL framework that incorporates a dy-namic activation merging mechanism and a consistency-aware model aggregation mechanism. Specifically, CoASFL constructs more consistent activation sequences from heterogeneous clients to alleviate horizontal inconsistency, and dynamically adjusts aggregation weights according to client participation frequen-cies and progress gaps to mitigate vertical inconsistency. By jointly coordinating activation-level and model-level consistency, CoASFL effectively suppresses inconsistency propagation during asynchronous training. Extensive experiments on an 80-device heterogeneous edge testbed demonstrate that CoASFL consis-tently outperforms state-of-the-art SFL approaches, accelerating training by 1.61 × to 3.74 × and improving final model accuracy by up to 3.26% under heterogeneous scenarios. 

Index Terms —Split Federated Learning, Asynchronous Train-ing, Edge Intelligence, Consistency Optimization. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# THE rapid advancement of deep learning has driven the deployment of intelligent applications at the network edge, including autonomous systems, intelligent healthcare, and industrial Internet of Things (IoT) [1], [2]. However, training increasingly large deep models on distributed edge devices remains challenging due to the limited computation capability, storage capacity, and communication resources of individual devices [3], [4]. Federated Learning (FL) provides a privacy-preserving paradigm for collaborative model training by keeping raw data locally while aggregating knowledge from distributed clients [5]–[7]. Nevertheless, conventional FL requires frequent exchanges of complete model param-eters between clients and the central server, resulting in substantial communication and computation overhead when training large-scale models [8]. Split Federated Learning (SFL) addresses this limitation by partitioning the model into client-side bottom models and a server-side top model, where clients

By reducing the resource burden on edge devices, SFL has become an attractive solution for collaborative intelligence in heterogeneous edge environments [11], [12]. Despite its advantages, conventional SFL generally follows a synchronous training paradigm, where the server must wait for all clients to complete local training before updating the global model [13]. Such synchronization barriers severely restrict training efficiency in practical edge systems, where clients often exhibit significant heterogeneity in computation capability, network conditions, and resource availability [14]– [17]. For instance, heterogeneous edge devices may require substantially different execution times to process identical workloads, while unstable wireless networks can further in-crease activation transmission delays [18]–[20]. Consequently, slow clients become stragglers that postpone global updates and leave the resources of faster clients underutilized. To address this issue, recent studies [21]–[23] have introduced asynchronous execution into SFL, allowing the server to con-tinuously process available client updates without waiting for full synchronization. Although asynchronous SFL effectively improves resource utilization and alleviates the straggler prob-lem, it also fundamentally changes the optimization dynamics of SFL by introducing new consistency challenges. Specifically, asynchronous SFL suffers from two cou-pled inconsistency dimensions: horizontal inconsistency across clients and vertical inconsistency between clients and the server. Horizontal inconsistency arises because heterogeneous computation and communication capabilities cause activations to arrive at different rates. As the server updates the top model based on these arrivals, faster clients exert disproportionate influence on the optimization, and under non-IID data this imbalance is further amplified, deviating the top model from the global objective and impairing convergence [24], [25]. Vertical inconsistency arises because the server continuously updates the top model, while each bottom model is updated only through its own client interactions. The resulting progress gaps distort split-layer gradients and bottom model updates, and their accumulation further biases bottom model aggre-gation and impairs convergence [26], [27]. More importantly, these two inconsistencies are not independent and may interact during asynchronous training: inconsistent activations impair the quality of server-side optimization, whereas biased model aggregation may further amplify client drift among hetero-geneous clients. Therefore, jointly mitigating horizontal and vertical inconsistencies becomes a fundamental challenge for achieving efficient and accurate asynchronous SFL. arXiv:2610.26992v1 [cs.LG] 19 Oct 2026 IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON MOBILE COMPUTING 2

Existing asynchronous SFL approaches only partially ad-dress these challenges. Some studies primarily focus on improving execution efficiency through asynchronous paral-lelism. For example, PipeSFL [21] introduces a fine-grained pipeline execution mechanism to overlap client computation and server processing, thereby reducing idle time caused by stragglers. However, it does not consider the inconsistency among asynchronously received intermediate activations. GAS [22] further investigates asynchronous SFL under heteroge-neous data distributions by generating intermediate activations to alleviate the impact of statistical heterogeneity, but its ag-gregation strategy cannot effectively handle model divergence caused by stale client updates. Meanwhile, MergeSFL [28] improves robustness against heterogeneous data by merging in-termediate activations from multiple clients, but it is designed for synchronous SFL and cannot fully exploit asynchronous execution. Therefore, existing methods either improve system efficiency or alleviate heterogeneity issues, while lacking a unified framework that simultaneously addresses the coupled horizontal and vertical inconsistencies in asynchronous SFL. To address the above challenges, we propose CoASFL, an inconsistency-aware asynchronous SFL framework that jointly suppresses inconsistency propagation during asynchronous training. Instead of treating horizontal and vertical inconsisten-cies separately, CoASFL coordinates them through a unified consistency optimization framework. Specifically, CoASFL integrates dynamic activation merging and consistency-aware model aggregation into a coupled optimization strategy. The dynamic activation merging coordinates activation merging at the server to improve the consistency of top model updates, while the consistency-aware model aggregation regulates bot-tom model aggregation under unequal client participation and progress mismatch. Rather than independently optimizing activation representations or aggregation reliability, the two mechanisms reinforce each other to preserve activation-level and model-level consistency throughout training. As a result, CoASFL achieves both efficient convergence and high model accuracy under heterogeneous edge environments. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:  

> •

We identify the dual inconsistency problem in asyn-chronous SFL, including horizontal inconsistency across clients and vertical inconsistency between clients and the server, and analyze their impact on model convergence and accuracy.  

> •

We propose CoASFL, an inconsistency-aware asyn-chronous SFL framework that jointly coordinates activation-level and model-level consistency through dy-namic activation merging and consistency-aware model aggregation, effectively suppressing inconsistency propa-gation throughout asynchronous training.  

> •

We implement CoASFL on an 80-device heterogeneous edge testbed and conduct extensive experiments. The results demonstrate that CoASFL accelerates training by 1.61 × to 3.74 × and improves final model accuracy by up to 3.26% under heterogeneous scenarios, compared to state-of-the-art SFL approaches. B. Characterization of Horizontal Inconsistency 

In asynchronous SFL, heterogeneous client computation and communication capabilities cause activations to arrive at the PS at different rates. Since each arriving activation immedi-ately triggers a top model update, faster clients participate in substantially more updates than slower ones, leading to 

horizontal inconsistency . Under IID data, this inconsistency mainly changes the relative contribution of clients. Under non-IID data, however, repeatedly arriving activations from the same clients skew the effective activation and label distribu-tions observed by the PS, causing the optimization direction of the top model to gradually deviate from the global objective. between the bottom model on each client and the top model on the PS. D. Discussion 

Although horizontal and vertical inconsistencies are ana-lyzed separately above, they are tightly coupled during asyn-chronous SFL. Horizontal inconsistency biases the optimiza-tion direction of the top model, while the resulting server-side model evolution further enlarges the progress mismatch experienced by delayed clients. In turn, bottom models up-dated under different partition states generate less consistent activations in subsequent rounds, further amplifying the server-side optimization bias caused by unequal client influence. Therefore, mitigating only one type of inconsistency cannot fundamentally stabilize asynchronous SFL. Instead, activation-level consistency and model-level consistency should be jointly optimized in a unified framework. Motivated by this observation, we propose CoASFL, which integrates dynamic activation merging and consistency-aware model aggregation into a coupled consistency optimization framework. III. V. E VALUATION 

A. Experimental Settings 

System Deployment. We conducted a series of compre-hensive experiments on an edge computing hardware pro-totype system. For the PS, we employed a deep learning GPU workstation equipped with an Intel(R) Xeon(R) Platinum 8360Y CPU, four NVIDIA A100 80GB PCIe, and 503 GiB of RAM. To create a heterogeneous computing environment, we incorporated 80 NVIDIA Jetson devices, consisting of 30 Jetson TX2, 40 Jetson NX, and 10 Jetson AGX units. The detailed technical specifications of the Jetson TX2, NX, and AGX models are provided in Table I. The experimental setup utilized a software platform based on Docker Swarm [42] and the PyTorch deep learning framework [43]. Docker Swarm, a distributed systems orchestration tool, facilitates the deployment and management of a multi-node architecture, enabling seamless monitoring and coordination of the devices’ operational states. PyTorch, a widely adopted deep learning framework, was used for model training across the distributed hardware nodes. To optimize inter-device communication, we integrated the Message Passing Interface (MPI) [44], a high-performance communication protocol that provides a robust set of functions for efficient data transmission and synchro-nization across the system. This combination of hardware and software enabled the evaluation of CoASFL’s scalability and performance in a heterogeneous edge computing environment. 

Setting of System Heterogeneity. The following experi-mental setup is employed to enable clients with heterogeneous computational and communication capabilities. 1) Computation Setting. The Jetson TX2, NX, and AGX devices are configurable to operate in distinct modes (4 for TX2, 8 for NX/AGX), each specifying a particular number of active CPU cores and CPU/GPU frequencies. These modes enable devices to exhibit different computational performance. For instance, the AGX in its highest-performance mode (Mode 0) can achieve training speeds up to 100 × faster than the TX2 in its lowest-performance mode (Mode 1). Furthermore, to simulate time-varying resource availability, the device modes are randomly altered every 10 aggregation rounds. 2) Communication Setting. Devices are connected to the PS via Wi-Fi routers, with a total of 80 devices grouped into four groups of 20 devices each. These groups are positioned at varying distances from the Wi-Fi routers, specifically at 2m, 8m, 14m, and 20m. Due to random channel interference and contention between devices, the bandwidth between the PS and the devices fluctuates during training, ranging from 1 Mbps to 30 Mbps, as measured by iperf3. 

Applications and Models. To evaluate the performance of the CoASFL in real-world scenarios, we conduct experiments on three representative applications using pre-trained trans-former models of diverse scales: 1) Object Recognition. CIFAR-100 [45] is a widely used benchmark dataset for object recognition, which consists of 60,000 32 ×32 colour images (50,000 for training and 10,000 for test) across 100 fine-grained classes. We utilize a well-known vision transformer model, Deit-Base [46], which has a size of 475MB and comprises 12 transformer layers that process images through 16 ×16 patch embeddings. 2) Natural Language Processing. We utilize the Question-answering Natural Language Inference (QNLI) dataset [47], a classification dataset consisting of question-sentence pairs, in this application. There are 104,473 samples for training and 5,463 samples for testing in the QNLI dataset. We train a RoBERTa-Base [48] model with a size of 501MB on the QNLI dataset, which is composed of 12 transformer layers. 3) Text Classification. We adopt the 20 Newsgroups dataset [49], a classical text classification benchmark containing 18,846 documents (11,314 for training and 7,532 for testing) across 20 categories. For this task, we employ the Qwen2.5-0.5B [50], a size of 1,884MB LLM with 24 transformer layers. 

Setting of Statistical Heterogeneity. In the experiments, training samples of each client are drawn from a Dirichlet distribution [51], i.e. , v ∼ Dir (δψ ), where ψ characterizes a prior class distribution, and δ > 0 is a concentration parameter controlling the identicalness among clients. With δ → ∞ , all clients have identical distributions to the prior class distribu-tion ( i.e. , IID); with δ → 0 each client holds data samples from only one class, which indicates high degree of statistical heterogeneity. We specify 6 values ( e.g. ,∞,1,0.5,0.25,0.2,0.1) for δ to generate different data distributions that cover a spec-trum of identicalness, and define p = 1 

> δ

(e.g. , p=0,1,2,4,5,10) to quantify the non-IID levels. The degree of statistical het-erogeneity increases as p = 0 is a special case of IIDness. 

Baselines. We measure the effectiveness of CoASFL through a comparison with four baselines. All the asyn-chronous baselines adopt an intra-round asynchronous and inter-round synchronous training strategy, which is the same as CoASFL. 1) PipeSFL [21] addresses the challenge of system het-erogeneity in computation and communication during typical SFL by proposing a novel pipeline asynchronous framework that accelerates SFL execution across heterogeneous devices. 2) GAS [22] represents the state-of-the-art in asynchronous SFL approaches, which addresses statistical heterogeneity challenges through an innovative approach of sampling and generating intermediate activations. 3) SplitFed [10] is the first SFL approach, which combines FL with SL by utilizing parallel client-side computing to accelerate model training in a synchronous setting. Metrics. We adopt the following evaluation metrics: 1) Test Accuracy measures the global model’s predictive performance on the test set across training rounds, reporting the final converged accuracy. 2) Time-to-Accuracy measures the wall-clock time to reach a target accuracy achievable by all approaches. Clients’ aver-age waiting time is also recorded to reflect training efficiency. 3) Network Traffic is calculated by summing the traffic for transmitting models or activations between the PS and clients when achieving a target accuracy. 

Experimental Parameters. All experiments are run for 100 rounds with an average local update frequency of 20. For DeiT on CIFAR-100, we adopt a learning rate of 0.1, a decay of 0.993 [41], [52], and a batch size of 64. Conversely, for RoBERTa on QNLI and Qwen2.5 on Newsgroups, we employ a learning rate of 5e-5, a linear scheduler with 10% warm-up [53], a batch size of 16, and a sequence length of 128. For the SFL approaches, DeiT and RoBERTa are split at the 6th layer, while Qwen2.5 is split at the 3rd layer.

C. Effect of Non-IID Levels 

To demonstrate the effectiveness of the CoASFL under non-IID data, we present the test accuracy of different approaches at varying non-IID levels in Fig. 8, in which the model accuracy of the five approaches on all the datasets decreases as the non-IID level increases. However, the performance of CoASFL is very close to MergeSFL and outperforms the other baselines on all datasets. PipeSFL ignores statistical heterogeneity and exhibits the lowest accuracy. To handle statistical heterogeneity, GAS employs an adaptive approach that samples and generates intermediate activations, while MergeSFL performs client selection via activation merging, partially mitigating non-IID effects on training. Specifically, as illustrated in Fig. 8a, CoASFL can achieve an improvement of test accuracy by about 2.06%, 3.26%, 1.76% on CIFAR-100 with non-IID level of p=10, compared to the baselines ( i.e. ,GAS, PipeSFL, SplitFed), and differs from MergeSFL by only 1.15%. Notably, by Fig. 8b, with non-IID level of p=10 on QNLI, CoASFL achieves an improvement of final accuracy by about 1.62%, 2.44%, 1.14%, compared to the baselines (i.e. , GAS, PipeSFL, SplitFed), and differs from MergeSFL by only 0.22%. Besides, as shown in Fig. 8c, while transitioning from IID to non-IID level of p=10 on Newsgroups, CoASFL and MergeSFL suffer from only 2.7%, 1.5% loss in accuracy, while the accuracy loss for GAS, PipeSFL, and SplitFed is 3.71%, 4.15%, and 3.84%, respectively. Collectively, these results demonstrate that CoASFL maintains competitive model accuracy as statistical heterogeneity increases, confirming its robustness across different non-IID levels. 

D. Effect of Key Strategies 

There are two key strategies of CoASFL, i.e. , dynamic activation merging and consistency-aware model aggregation, being developed to enhance the performance of SFL. Herein, we conduct several sets of experiments for training DeiT on CIFAR-100 with IID distribution ( p=0) and non-IID dis-tribution (p=10) to evaluate the effectiveness of the two critical strategies. We adopt the CoASFL without dynamic activation merging (CoASFL w/o DAM) and CoASFL without consistency-aware model aggregation (CoASFL w/o CMA) as the baselines. In CoASFL w/o DAM, the PS directly updates the top model upon each activation arrival without activation selection or merging. While in CoASFL w/o CMA, the ag-gregation weight of each client follows the typical assignment method, i.e. , the more iterations a client participates in, the greater the weight assigned. By Fig. 9, CoASFL w/o CMA converges faster than CoASFL w/o DAM on the IID and Non-IID datasets, but CoASFL w/o DAM achieves a higher test accuracy than CoASFL w/o CMA on the Non-IID dataset. Specifically, CoASFL w/o CMA reduces the total training time by about 56.1% compared to CoASFL w/o DAM, but CoASFL w/o DAM improves the final test accuracy by about 1.27% on the non-IID dataset, compared to CoASFL w/o CMA. compared to CoASFL w/o DAM, and improve the final test accuracy by about 2.1% compared to CoASFL w/o CMA, which reflects the positive roles of the two strategies.

In the presence of pronounced heterogeneity in computation capability and communication bandwidth across clients, a common research addresses these challenges through struc-tural adaptation of split models. Several studies dynamically determine split points under heterogeneous resource con-straints. For example, SmartSplit [54] analyzes each client’s memory budget and runtime training capability to dynamically assign client-specific split layers, thereby improving training efficiency while maintaining model accuracy. Similarly, ESFL [55] allocates bottom models of different depths to hetero-geneous clients and jointly optimizes server-side computation resource allocation, enhancing training efficiency under sys-tem heterogeneity. HFSL [56] introduces auxiliary layers to accommodate heterogeneous model partitions and leverages self-distillation to improve training accuracy. HeteroSFL [57] employs bottleneck layers of varying widths to accommodate heterogeneous communication capabilities and uses bidirec-tional knowledge sharing to mitigate non-IID data effects. Similarly, HASFL [58] jointly optimizes adaptive batch-size scheduling and split-layer selection according to client capabil-ities, balancing training latency and convergence performance. MergeSFL [28] combines feature merging with batch-size regulation to address system and statistical heterogeneity under synchronous SFL. Although these approaches improve training efficiency under heterogeneous resource constraints, most still rely on synchronous training, where the progress of each round is constrained by the slowest participating client. As compu-tation and communication delays vary across clients, faster clients must wait at synchronization barriers, leaving their resources underutilized and limiting overall training efficiency. To alleviate this bottleneck, several studies further explore asynchronous execution, allowing the server to process avail-able client activations without waiting for slower clients. DT-ASFL [59] leverages digital twins to monitor device states and support asynchronous model updates. PipeSFL [21] adopts a priority-based scheduling strategy at the server and enables asynchronous execution within aggregation rounds to improve training efficiency. From another perspective, RingSFL [15] constructs a ring topology to distribute model training across heterogeneous clients and exploits overlapping layers to im-prove model updates. These approaches primarily focus on al-leviating straggler effects and improving execution efficiency, rather than explicitly addressing the optimization inconsis-tencies arising from asynchronous client participation. GAS [22] further considers the biased top-model updates caused by unequal activation arrivals and generates supplementary activations to balance client influence. However, its bottom-model aggregation does not explicitly account for the progress mismatch between the client and server model partitions. In contrast, CoASFL mitigates both the horizontal inconsistency caused by unequal client participation and the vertical incon-sistency arising from progress mismatch between client-side bottom models and the server-side top model through dynamic activation merging and consistency-aware model aggregation. VII. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we characterize horizontal inconsistency across clients and vertical inconsistency between clients and the server in asynchronous SFL. Then we propose CoASFL, an inconsistency-aware asynchronous SFL framework that incorporates a dynamic activation merging mechanism and a consistency-aware model aggregation mechanism to effec-tively suppress inconsistency propagation throughout asyn-chronous training. The experimental results show that the CoASFL can speed up model training by at least 1.61 × to 3.74 × and improve final model accuracy by up to 3.26% in heterogeneous scenarios, compared to the baselines.
