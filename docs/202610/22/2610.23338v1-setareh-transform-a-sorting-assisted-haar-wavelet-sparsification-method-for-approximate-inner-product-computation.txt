# Abstract 

Modern deep learning systems increasingly rely on high-dimensional vector oper-ations, in which large input and parameter vectors are repeatedly stored, trans-ferred, and processed. Although substantial research has focused on reducing neural-network model size through pruning, quantization, knowledge distilla-tion, and efficient architectures, the storage, memory-traffic, and communica-tion costs associated with repeatedly presented high-dimensional input vectors remain important practical considerations, particularly in distributed and edge-computing environments. This paper proposes the SETAREH (Sorting-assisted Energy-concentrating Transform for Approximate Representation using Haar wavelets) transform, a Haar-wavelet-based sparsification approach for approximating the inner product between a fixed high-dimensional coefficient vector and successive input vectors. By applying the same sequence of permutations and Haar transforms to both vectors, SETAREH preserves the exact inner product before truncation. It then retains only a subset of the transformed coefficient-input pairs, reducing their representation cost while introducing a controllable approximation error. To evaluate SETAREH, we consider two complementary experimental settings: a synthetic Gaussian-vector baseline and a real apparent-age estimation task using a convolutional neural network on the APPA-REAL dataset. The results show that SETAREH can approximate the evaluated inner products using substantially fewer transformed coefficient-input pairs than the original vector dimension. The findings support its use as a potential structured data-representation method for repeated inner products with fixed weights, while the practical benefit depends on approximation accuracy and the cost of storing or transmitting permutation metadata. Introduction 

The rapid development and scaling of deep learning have resulted in neural net-work models with increasingly large numbers of parameters, wider intermediate representations, and higher-dimensional activation vectors [1–3]. The increasing size and computational complexity of deep neural networks have consequently motivated extensive research into efficient architectures. Modern architectures range from convolutional neural networks to attention-based and Transformer architectures, with increasing model capacity serving as an important mecha-nism for improving predictive performance. Representative architectures such as ResNet, MobileNet, and EfficientNet illustrate different approaches to balanc-ing predictive accuracy, computational complexity, memory requirements, and model size [4–6]. Residual networks demonstrated that very deep models could be effectively optimized through residual learning while maintaining practical computational requirements [4]. MobileNets introduced depthwise separable convolutions as a mechanism for substantially reducing computation and model size, particularly for mobile and embedded applications [5]. EfficientNet further demonstrated that jointly scaling network depth, width, and input resolution can achieve improved accuracy–efficiency trade-offs compared with conventional scaling strategies [6]. At the same time, the computational burden of deep neural networks is not limited to arithmetic operations. Memory access, storage, and data movement can represent significant system-level costs, particularly when models and inter-mediate data must be processed on resource-constrained devices or transferred between computational units. Consequently, neural-network efficiency has been studied from several complementary perspectives, including architectural design, parameter pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, and model compres-sion [4–9]. For example, Han et al. proposed Deep Compression, which com-bines pruning, trained quantization, and Huffman coding to substantially reduce neural-network storage requirements. Their work demonstrated that large re-ductions in model storage can be achieved while preserving predictive accuracy [7]. Efficient architecture design has similarly been investigated in MobileNets and EfficientNets, where reductions in computation and model complexity are obtained through architectural choices and scaling strategies [5,6]. Beyond model compression and architectural optimization, reducing data move-ment has also emerged as an important consideration in efficient neural-network processing. Chen et al. demonstrated that data movement and memory access can represent major sources of energy consumption in deep neural-network pro-cessing. Their Eyeriss architecture explicitly targets data movement through the memory hierarchy by exploiting data reuse and an optimized dataflow, highlighting the importance of reducing data movement in addition to reduc-ing arithmetic operations [8]. Quantization provides another widely studied approach to reducing the memory footprint and computational cost of neu-ral networks. Jacob et al. proposed an integer-only inference framework that quantizes both weights and activations to lower-bit representations while main-2taining accuracy close to floating-point inference [9]. Most existing approaches, however, primarily focus on reducing model size, computational complexity, or the representation cost of model parameters and activations. A different but related problem arises when a fixed coefficient vector is repeat-edly applied to a large number of input vectors to compute their inner products. Such inner-product operations arise in many neural-network computations, in-cluding fully connected layers and, more generally, linear projections used in convolutional and attention-based architectures. In these settings, the same co-efficient vector may participate in a large number of successive inner-product operations, while the corresponding input vectors repeatedly enter the compu-tation. Consequently, reducing the amount of input data that must be stored, transferred, or processed for each inner-product operation could provide a com-plementary form of data reduction without modifying the underlying network architecture or its learned parameters. This observation motivates the approach proposed in this work. Rather than compressing the neural-network model itself, we investigate whether the per-mutation invariance of the inner product can be exploited to construct a com-pact representation of repeatedly occurring high-dimensional input vectors. By appropriately reordering a fixed coefficient vector, applying a Haar Discrete Wavelet Transform, and promoting a more compact distribution of its transform-domain representation, the proposed method seeks to approximate the original inner product using only a small subset of the corresponding input samples. We refer to the proposed framework as SETAREH (Sorting-assisted Energy-concentrating Transform for Approximate Representation using Haar wavelets). The method is designed as a complementary data-reduction technique, with po-tential applications in memory-constrained processing, repeated inference, and communication- or bandwidth-constrained neural-network systems. In this work, two variants of the proposed framework are investigated. SETAREH-3 applies an initial sorting operation, followed by a Haar Discrete Wavelet Transform and a second sorting operation. SETAREH-5 extends this procedure by repeating the sorting and Haar-transform stages once before the final sorting operation. The two variants therefore provide different trade-offs between approximation accuracy, representation compactness, and computational cost. Their performance is systematically evaluated across different vector dimensions and retained coefficient counts, with particular emphasis on approximation error and the number of retained input samples. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the proposed SETAREH framework and formally describes the SETAREH-3 and SETAREH-5 processing procedures. Section 3 presents the experimental methodology and defines the two experimental settings, statistical evaluation protocol, and performance metrics. Section 4 reports and analyses the results, including approximation accuracy, robustness, compression characteristics of SETAREH and also the effects of vector-dimension on its performance using synthetic and real scenarios. Finally, section 5 discusses the implications, 3limitations, and potential applications of the proposed algorithm.

# 3. Experimental Methodology 

# 3.1 Experimental Settings 

To evaluate the SETAREH framework two experiments based on synthetic and real scenarios are considered. The first experiment uses independently generated Gaussian random vectors as coefficient and input vectors and serves as a controlled numerical baseline for analyzing and comparing the behavior and robustness of SETAREH-3 and SETAREH-5 across different vector dimensions and various retention levels. The vector dimensions 

𝑁 ∈ {1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384, 65536} were selected from powers of two so that the full dyadic Haar transform could be applied without boundary 7extension. For each of the 50 different coefficient vectors, 50 independent input vectors were also generated providing a total of 2500 inner products. The same coefficient vectors and exactly the same input vectors were used for SETAREH-3 and SETAREH-5, ensuring a paired comparison. The second experiment replaces the randomly generated coefficient vectors with the actual 4096-dimensional regression vector derived using the training image set and the 4096-dimensional feature vectors extracted from the fc2 layer of a pretrained VGG16 network [13] on the validation and test image sets of the APPA-REAL dataset [20] for apparent age estimation.

As Table 1 shows, the results at K=10 reveal a distinct operating regime for all vector sizes. Unlike the results at larger values of K, SETAREH-5 does not consistently outperform SETAREH-3 when only ten coefficient–input pairs 11 are retained. For example, at N =4096, the conditioned median relative errors are 14.11% for SETAREH-3 and 14.80% for SETAREH-5. At such extreme retention levels, the approximation depends on a very small number of trans-formed coefficient–input pairs and is therefore highly sensitive to the detailed distribution and signs of the retained coefficients. As K increases, this sensitiv-ity decreases and the advantage of SETAREH-5 becomes increasingly consistent. The K=10 results are therefore useful as a stress test of the extreme-compression regime rather than as the representative operating point of the method. 

# 4.1.1 Accuracy Improvement Achieved by SETAREH-5 

The accuracy advantage of SETAREH-5 in comparison to SETAREH-3 can be quantified directly through the ratio between the corresponding errors. The improvement is relatively modest in the extreme K=10 regime but increases rapidly with K. As shown in Table 1, the accuracy advantage of SETAREH-5 becomes increasingly pronounced as the number of retained coefficients increases. For example, at N =4096, SETAREH-5 reduces the conditioned median relative error by approximately 11-fold, 25-fold, and 61-fold at K=50, 100, and 200. At 

K=100, the error-reduction factors are 33.5×, 30.5×, 25.5×, 23.8×, 23.7×, and 22.3× for N =1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384, and 65536, respectively. At K=200, the corresponding factors increase to 94.4×, 83.4×, 61.2×, 62.6×, 52.7×, and 44.9×. The improvement therefore persists across the complete dimension range investigated.

# 4.2. APPA-REAL Apparent-Age Regression: Model Con-struction and SETAREH Evaluation 

To evaluate SETAREH in a real-world regression setting, we considered an apparent-age estimation problem using the APPA-REAL image dataset [20]. In this framework, each image was represented by a 4096-dimensional feature vector extracted from the fc2 layer of a pretrained VGG16 network [13]. These feature vectors serve as the inputs to a Ridge regression model [21] trained to estimate apparent age.

# 5. Conclusions 

In this paper a novel Haar-wavelet transform based sparsification approach for reducing the number of input-vector samples required to approximate an inner 18 product with a fixed high-dimensional coefficient vector was presented. Such inner-product operations arise in many neural-network computations, including fully connected layers and, more generally, linear projections used in convo-lutional and attention-based architectures. This makes the proposed method potentially relevant to systems in which the same parameters are repeatedly applied to a large number of input vectors. We called the proposed algorithm SETAREH (Sorting-assisted Energy-concentrating Transform for Approximate Representation using Haar wavelets) where the central idea is based on the permutation invariance of the inner prod-uct which allows to reorder the coefficients vector so that, after Haar-wavelet transformation, its representation can be approximated using a relatively small number of transformed coefficients–input pairs. This enables aggressive trun-cation while retaining the potential to preserve the inner-product value with relatively small approximation error, enabling reducing the amount of input data that must be stored, transferred, or processed, providing a complementary form of data reduction without modifying the underlying network architecture or its learned parameters. Two variants of the proposed method, namely SETAREH-3 and SETAREH-5, were investigated. The synthetic experiments showed that SETAREH-5 gener-ally achieved lower CMRE than SETAREH-3 for 𝐾 ≥ 20 , with a consistent advantage at 𝐾 ≥ 30 across the tested dimensions. Therefore, it was selected as the main SETAREH algorithm. To evaluate the performance of SETAREH at a real scenario, we considered an apparent-age estimation problem using the APPA-REAL image dataset where each input image was represented by a 4096-dimensional feature vector extracted from the fc2 layer of a pretrained VGG16 network and each vector serves as the input to a Ridge regression model trained to estimate apparent age. The study showed that by retaining only 75 processed coefficients compared to the 4096 original coefficients (1.83%) yielded a CMRE of 0.13283% on the test set and retaining 200 coefficients (4.88%) the test CMRE is 0.0079891%. Thus, a small retained subset can closely approximate the dot product used by the original fixed regression model. An important distinction should nevertheless be made between coefficient-level reduction and complete representation compression. The coefficient-only com-pression ratio N /K reported in this work describes the reduction in the number of retained coefficient–input values. It does not include the storage or trans-mission cost of the permutation information required by the current implemen-tation. Therefore, the reported ratios should not be interpreted as direct end-to-end model-storage compression ratios. This distinction is important when comparing SETAREH with established model-compression techniques such as pruning, quantization, and entropy coding [7,9,15,16]. The conceptual position of SETAREH is therefore complementary to conventional neural-network com-pression [14]. 19 The storage analysis shows that the practical benefit of SETAREH depends on the number of input vectors processed using the same fixed coefficient vector. Under the storage assumptions described in Section 4.3, SETAREH requires more storage than the dense representation for a single input vector because of the one-time permutation overhead, resulting in a storage increase of approxi-mately 52.7%. From the second input vector onward, the SETAREH represen-tation becomes smaller than the dense representation. The resulting storage savings are approximately 22.3%, 82.3%, 95.8%, and 97.1% for 2, 10, 100, and 1000 input vectors, respectively. These results illustrate the importance of amor-tizing the permutation metadata across repeated inputs. The results should also not be interpreted as evidence that SETAREH neces-sarily reduces arithmetic computation in its current implementation. The sort-ing and Haar-transform stages introduce additional operations, and the present study primarily evaluates approximation accuracy and coefficient-level data re-duction rather than end-to-end hardware latency or energy consumption. Thus, the principal contribution demonstrated by the present experiments is struc-tured reduction of the number of retained coefficient–input pairs, rather than demonstrated computational acceleration [17]. The transform-domain formulation of SETAREH may be relevant beyond neu-ral networks. Any computational system containing repeated inner products between a fixed or slowly changing coefficient vector and a large number of in-put vectors could potentially benefit from the proposed representation. Candi-date areas include signal processing, similarity computation, embedded sensing, and selected scientific or biomedical computing workloads [19]. These applica-tions, however, require dedicated validation and should be considered potential directions rather than demonstrated applications of the present study. The characteristics demonstrated by SETAREH suggest several potential ap-plication areas including communication-constrained neural-network inference for edge devices, or distributed computing nodes [8,16], memory-constrained inference, edge and embedded inference and distributed or federated machine learning [18], where communication cost can dominate system performance. Future work could develop an efficient representation of the permutation metadata and evaluate the true end-to-end storage and communication cost of SETAREH. This would allow coefficient-level reduction to be converted into a rigorous system-level compression metric. Moreover, hardware-aware implementations should be investigated to quantify latency, memory traffic, energy consumption, and the trade-off between trans-formation overhead and data-reduction benefits. Such studies would determine whether SETAREH can provide a practical advantage in real edge, accelerator, or distributed-computing systems.
