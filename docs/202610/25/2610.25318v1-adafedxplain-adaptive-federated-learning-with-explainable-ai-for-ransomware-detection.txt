Abstract. Attacks using ransomware have developed into complex multi-phase campaigns that target vital infrastructure, resulting in annual damages of billions of dollars. Conventional detection techniques are in-effective against zero-day variations, and privacy issues are raised by centralized machine learning algorithms. Federated learning allows for cooperative model training without data sharing, however current FL approaches suffer from explainability issues, high communication cost, and non-IID data heterogeneity. In this study, we present AdaFedX-plain, an explainable AI-based adaptive federated learning system for ransomware detection. Our approach includes progressive XGBoost com-plexity to avoid overfitting, a decaying learning rate schedule for stable convergence, and dynamic client grading based on ransomware preva-lence and data sufficiency. Using 62,485 real-world ransomware samples from four OS-based non-IID clients, we compare AdaFedXplain against FedAvg, FedProx, and SCAFFOLD. According to experimental data, AdaFedXplain outperforms all baselines with 99.59% detection accuracy and 99.64% F1-score while cutting energy usage by 75%, latency by 65%, and communication overhead by 75%. The ablation investigation demon-strates that every element enhances performance. Security analysts can use SHAP explainability to identify important PE header characteristics that influence detection decisions. Our system provides a collaborative ransomware detection solution that is efficient and interpretable, while reducing privacy risks through federated learning’s data-local training paradigm. 

Keywords: Federated Learning · Ransomware Detection · Explainable AI · AdaFedXplain · Non-IID Data · Machine Learning, Cybersecurity 

## 1 Introduction 

The scope and complexity of ransomware attacks have increased dramatically, making them one of the most serious cybersecurity risks in the world. Modern arXiv:2610.25318v1 [cs.CR] 18 Oct 2026 2 Biswas et al. 

ransomware has developed from simple file encryption to complex multi-stage attacks that target government infrastructure, healthcare, and banking. Accord-ing to recent research, the frequency of attacks has been steadily increasing, and the yearly global damages are expected to exceed trillions of dollars [1, 2]. The need for strong defenses is highlighted by the growing use of sophisticated evasion strategies. Heuristic analysis, manual reverse engineering, and signature-based detection are the mainstays of conventional ransomware mitigation techniques. Although these techniques work well against known families, they are unable to identify polymorphic and zero-day assaults, produce a large number of false positives, and scale poorly [3, 4]. By using automated feature learning and behavior-based detection, machine learning gets around these restrictions and allows for gen-eralization to previously unknown attack patterns without the need for signa-tures [5, 6]. In this research, we propose a federated learning architecture that allows collaborative model training across remote clients without revealing sensitive data, thereby addressing the shortcomings of centralized ML-based ransomware detection. Our solution specifically addresses the non-IID heterogeneity present in real-world deployments, where various businesses display differing operat-ing system distributions and ransomware prevalence, in contrast to prior FL approaches that assume IID data distributions. On a real-world ransomware dataset of 62,485 samples, we do a thorough comparison of three FL settings, assessing XGBoost model across four non-IID clients divided by MajorOSVer-sion. Our proposed AdaFedXplain framework addresses the non-IID challenge by partitioning training data across four heterogeneous clients based on MajorOSVer-sion (Windows NT/2000, XP/2003, Vista/7, and 10/11), reflecting real-world OS diversity. For each communication round, we compute a client score S(i) = 0.6·ρi +0 .4·min(1 , |Di|/10000) , where ρi is the ransomware prevalence ratio, and select the top 60% of clients for aggregation. ηt = max(0 .01 , 0.1 · (1 − t/T )) to ensure stable convergence and progressive XG-Boost complexity ( Et = 60 + t estimators) to prevent overfitting. Experimental results demonstrate that AdaFedXplain achieves 99.59% detection accuracy and 99.64% F1-score using XGBoost, outperforming FedAvg, FedProx, and SCAF-FOLD. Critically, our method reduces communication overhead by 75% (80 MB → 20 MB), latency by 65% (4.0s → 1.4s), and energy consumption by 75% (40J → 10J). Per-client F1-scores consistently exceed 99.5%, while SCAFFOLD exhibits performance degradation with per-client F1-scores dropping to approxi-mately 98.3%, confirming the robustness of AdaFedXplain across heterogeneous OS environments. ## 2 Literature Review 

Federated Learning (FL) has become a potential paradigm for cybersecurity applications that protect privacy, especially in malware categorization and in-trusion detection systems (IDS). According to recent research, FL preserves data confidentiality in sensitive situations like IoT and cloud systems by en-abling distributed model training across numerous clients without exchanging raw data [7, 8]. To manage distributed optimization, popular aggregation algo-rithms like FedAvg and its extension FedProx have been frequently used. How-ever, because of varied network settings, device-specific behaviors, and different threat profiles, their performance drastically deteriorates under non-IID data dis-tributions, which are common in real-world cybersecurity datasets. Even though FedProx adds a proximal term to stabilize local updates, previous research shows that dealing with severely skewed client data continues to present convergence, fairness, and robustness issues [7, 8]. These drawbacks highlight the urgent need for flexible and context-aware FL techniques designed especially for cybersecu-rity jobs like ransomware detection. The capacity of machine learning-based ransomware detection to recognize intricate assault patterns that are beyond the scope of conventional signature-based techniques has drawn a lot of interest. Ransomware has been classified using both static data (file entropy, opcode sequences, API call frequency) and dynamic behaviors (system call traces, execution patterns) using techniques like XGBoost, Logistic Regression, and deep neural networks. According to recent research, deep learning techniques are excellent at identifying temporal and be-havioral patterns in execution traces, whereas gradient boosting models like XGBoost achieve high detection accuracy because of their resilience to high-dimensional and heterogeneous data [9, 10]. Despite their efficacy, these models mostly function in centralized environments, which raises serious issues with 4 Biswas et al. 

data privacy, scalability, generalization across distributed organizational envi-ronments, and regulatory compliance (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA). The integration of FL with ransomware detection is motivated by this centralized constraint. In security applications, where interpretability and transparency are crucial for regulatory compliance, forensic investigation, and analyst trust, Explain-able Artificial Intelligence (XAI) has grown in importance. By measuring the contribution of each input feature to model predictions, techniques like SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) give feature-level attribution, allowing security teams to comprehend why a certain activity is reported as dangerous. In order to improve interpretability and facilitate post-incident forensic analysis, recent research incorporates SHAP and other XAI approaches into malware and intru-sion detection frameworks [10, 11]. Despite these developments, there is still a significant gap in explainable, privacy-preserving security solutions due to the extremely restricted integration of XAI into distributed and federated environ-ments. Several research gaps still exist despite significant advancements. First, no previous effort has combined detection models specific to ransomware with adap-tive federated learning. Second, even though OS-based heterogeneity is a crucial feature of real-world ransomware datasets where various operating systems dis-play distinct susceptibility patterns, existing FL frameworks fail to sufficiently handle non-IID data distributions resulting from this phenomenon. Third, al-though XAI techniques have been used in centralized environments, there isn’t a comprehensive explainable federated learning framework created especially for ransomware detection. These gaps inspire us to work hard. To address these challenges, we propose AdaFedXplain , an adaptive fed-erated learning framework with integrated explainability for ransomware de-tection. 3.1 Dataset Description 

The ransomware detection dataset presented by Bensalah [12], which is openly accessible on Kaggle, is used to assess our suggested approach. This dataset, 6 Biswas et al. 

which includes about 62,485 samples with 18 columns, contains static features that were taken from Windows Portable Executable (PE) files. MajorOSVersion ,

NumberOfSections , DllCharacteristics , ResourceSize , and BitcoinAddresses 

are important features. In order to build a binary ransomware detection job where 1 denotes ransomware, we invert the label column Benign , which signifies benign (1) or malicious (0) samples. The operating system version information provided by this dataset makes it especially appropriate for our investigation be-cause it allows for realistic non-IID client partitioning based on MajorOSVersion 

across four different Windows versions. 

3.2 Data Preparation 

We retain 15 static PE header features as inputs and remove the identification columns FileName and md5Hash because they have no predictive value. We flip the Benign column, which is the target variable, so that 0 indicates benign and 1 indicates ransomware. Stratified sampling is used to divide the dataset into train-ing (60%), validation (20%), and testing (20%) sets, producing 37,491, 12,497, and 12,497 samples, respectively. To stop data leakage, features are standardized using StandardScaler fitted exclusively on the training set. We divide the training data according to MajorOSVersion into four clients in order to simulate a non-IID federated environment [13]: Client 0 (OS=4, Windows NT/2000) with 14,556 samples, Client 1 (OS=5, Windows XP/2003) with 5,257 samples, Client 2 (OS=6, Windows Vista/7) with 9,183 samples, and Client 3 (OS=10, Windows 10/11) with 8,495 samples. The prevalence of ransomware varies from 56.2% to 56.8% across clients, resulting in statistical variation that is realistic.

## 4 Results and Discussion 

4.1 Experimental Setup 

Every experiment was carried out using a Tesla T4 GPU and Google Colab. The dataset, which is divided into training (60%), validation (20%), and testing (20%), consists of 62,485 samples with 15 static PE header features. Based on 

MajorOSVersion , we divided the training data into four clients: Client 0 (OS=4, Windows NT/2000) with 14,556 samples, Client 1 (OS=5, Windows XP/2003) with 5,257 samples, Client 2 (OS=6, Windows Vista/7) with 9,183 samples, and Client 3 (OS=10, Windows 10/11) with 8,495 samples in order to replicate a realistic non-IID environment. Over 20 communication rounds, we developed and compared four FL settings: FedAvg, FedProx ( μ = 0 .01 ), SCAFFOLD, and our suggested AdaFedXplain. We trained XGBoost classifier for every configuration. Every outcome is assessed using the 12,497-sample held-out test set.

## 5 Conclusion and Future Work 

We presented AdaFedXplain, an adaptive federated learning system with ex-plainable AI for ransomware detection that addresses interpretability, commu-nication efficiency, and non-IID heterogeneity. Our methodology adds a decay-ing learning rate schedule, progressive XGBoost complexity, and dynamic client grading based on ransomware prevalence and data sufficiency. AdaFedXplain outperforms FedAvg, FedProx, and SCAFFOLD, with experimental results on 62,485 samples across four OS-based non-IID clients showing 99.59% accuracy and 99.64% F1-score. Crucially, it lowers latency by 65%, communication over-head by 75%, and energy consumption by 75%. Robustness across Windows ver-sions NT/2000 to 10/11 is confirmed by per-client F1-scores exceeding 99.5%. The ablation investigation verifies that every component makes a positive con-tribution; the F1-score drops by 0.05% when any component is removed.
