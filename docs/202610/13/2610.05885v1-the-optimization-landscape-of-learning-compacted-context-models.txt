## Abstract 

Many works approach continual learning through the lens of infinite context win-dows. As an agent puts more observation into context (concretely the KV cache), compacting said context is akin to direct memory manipulation, without affecting the base model’s weights. Many works pose KV compaction as an optimization problem: learn a smaller set of KV vectors that matches the behavior of the full KV cache. While this preserves base model behavior, optimizing through a frozen base model results in a highly nontrivial optimization problem with a brittle and flat loss landscape. In this paper, we characterize what makes these optimization problems difficult and demonstrate that a heavily simplified Perceiver-based architecture not only matches performance of a full Perceiver transformer in continuous context compaction, but outperforms baselines on compaction utility. Results are presented on MCQ tasks across Finance, Legal, Gutenberg, and Code. 

## 1 Introduction 

A key component of any continually-deployed model, whether text-based or robotic, is its memory management; what to keep, what can be forgotten, and what can be compressed down. Most language models today are at least in some part attention-based: as tokens are observed and the context grows (in the form of the KV cache), computation time scales quadratically, and observed tokens are less attended to. As such, rather than designing new architectures that bound memory or linearly scale with context, a growing line of work revolves around compacting the KV cache: either by evicting entries [Zhang et al., 2023, Li et al., 2024], by optimizing a cache per document [Eyuboglu et al., 2025, Zweiger et al., 2026], or by training a model that writes compact entries [Chari et al., 2025, O’Neill et al., 2026]. We refer to these kinds of models as compacted context models .However, this optimization can often be difficult: per-document methods spend minutes of opti-mization per document, and optimizing through a fixed deep model introduces a highly nonlinear, highly non-convex structure. Without a deeper understanding of where and how these optimization difficulties arise, many of the same mistakes are silently made in parallel. This paper provides a deeper characterization of the difficulty in optimizing through KV caches, starting from the smallest setting that exhibits the difficulty. A compression-free linear map already showcases difficulty in the optimization: near-identity maps can still fall into bad local minima. Measuring the loss surface directly shows why: the global solution sits in a needle-sharp basin surrounded by a wide, flat plateau (Figure 1b).

Each of the loss landscape characteristics point to a specific potential fix that has been explored in some form in the literature. For the flat landscape, one could hope that a loss more densely supervised over the model could unflatten the landscape. This could be through the base model’s forward pass in regulating attention passes through the full network the same way, or through more generated self-study examples on the input context. For the sharp basin, one could hope that relaxing the base model weights could make the overall optimization smoother and more tractable. However, we find that attempted fixes to the KV landscape quickly fail once compressions ratios increase, or compactions are iterated on each other (an inevitability for continually compacted memory models). We then find that the most performant compacted context model is a heavily simplified, single-layer single head attention layer, depicted in Figure 6 and detailed in Section 5. Our KV compactor beats all compared baselines, both in terms of MCQ accuracy and compaction speed. In summary, our contributions are: 1. A set of synthetic experiments to visualize and characterize the optimization landscape of KV space optimization, 2. An empirical account of why common fixes, including proxy losses, more data, and weight relaxation, do not reliably help, and 3. A minimal cache writer, which we call Fast Diamond, that matches or beats more expressive writers and standard baselines under matched MCQ evaluation.

## 4 Attempts to improve the landscape 

We now go through common fixes that have been explored in the literature, each tied to a specific geometric aspect of the loss landscape depicted in Figure 3. 

4.1 A denser loss signal 

Flatness of the bad local minima indicates we may need a loss that is more densely supervised than eq. (2). One way to supervise further is to have the query tokens pass through the full model in the same way, e.g. by supervising an L2 norm over all layers:

For linear map experiments, this greatly improves the optimization landscape, and the global optimum at the identity becomes a much more attractive basin (see e.g. Figure 10 in the appendix for results). How-ever, these benefits do not carry over to compressive settings where gϕ outputs fewer tokens. We observe that the internal squared norm can be sensitive with respect to the target KL loss in eq. (2), see e.g. Fig-ure 9 in the appendix. And in the token compressive setting (8k →1k), adding this internal squared norm loss does not meaningfully help the trained perfor-mance (Figure 4). The failures above suggest three seemingly attractive fixes: give the optimizer a denser signal, add self-study queries or relax the frozen weights. None of them reliably worked, and the ways they fail are informative. 

4.2 Adding self-study queries 

While we train on a dataset of MCQ questions, it may be helpful to further optimize on self-study queries , i.e. using the base model to generate questions about the document, then matching answer-token KL between the full and compressed contexts. This process is at the core for a number of KV compression algorithms, notable Cartridges [Eyuboglu et al., 2025]. In our linear setting further self-study queries beyond our training dataset do not notably help to find the global optimum.

## 6 Results 

We now evaluate our simplified architecture, denoted Fast Diamond, against a suite of baseline methods over multiple-choice questions (MCQ) from on the compressed cache. 

6.1 Data and evaluation 

Akin to [O’Neill et al., 2026], Fast Diamond and compatible baseline methods are trained on a four-domain extractive MCQ corpus containing Financial filings, Project Gutenberg books, Legal documents, and Code 1. Each example contains a document prefix, an MCQ prompt, and an answer target. Questions are generated from document spans, verified using the frozen base model with context, and filtered to remove questions that it can answer without context. Training and evaluation documents are separated at the document level. Appendix A gives the data sources, split construction, and per-domain counts. The matched single-pass comparison uses a separate held-out set of 256 medical transcripts (outside of the training domains) and 758 extractive MCQs. Each transcript contains 8,192 tokens, which each method compresses to 1,024 latents.

## 7 Related Work 

KV token retention. One KV compression strategy is to simply select and omit certain tokens from the cache, either as a function of the current context [Zhang et al., 2023, Xiao et al., 2024, Li et al., 2024, Cai et al., 2025], or in a query-agnostic way [Kim et al., 2025, Chari and Durme, 2025]. Learned variants predict future token utility or distill a retention policy [Moschella et al., 2026, Chari et al., 2025]. These methods are efficient, but are limited in expressivity to the discrete token dictionary. 

Synthetic KV representations. In contrast to token-side retention models, synthetic KV repre-sentations can generate examples in the full embedding space. Some methods optimize these KV vectors, either optimize a cache using self-study examples [Eyuboglu et al., 2025] or reconstruct attention behavior with closed-form solves [Zweiger et al., 2026]. Other methods train a model over a wider dataset to learn a KV compression model. LoCoCo merges cache states with learned convolutions [Cai et al., 2024], while Still uses layer-wise latent queries and cross-attention [O’Neill et al., 2026]. Our simplified Fast Diamond model belongs closest to this second group, but studies why such writers are hard to train through a frozen model and whether a simpler writer improves optimization. 

Training models to use compacted KV representations. A number of methods also specifically train the base model to be able to read a token compression mechanism [Mu et al., 2023, Chevalier et al., 2023, Ge et al., 2024, Nawrot et al., 2024, Li et al., 2026]. While we likewise find in our paper that trainable weights helps for single-pass compression (compressing tokens a single time), it does not fully solve the recurrent case (iteratively compressing tokens). 

Optimization through frozen models. Optimizing the inputs of a transformer or the KV cache has been explored in previous works [Li and Liang, 2021]. Prior work reports an unstable and sharp loss landscape [Chen et al., 2023], and ameliorate this through input perturbations. Our work isolates specific properties and behaviors of the optimization landscape in the KV compaction regime. 

## 8 Discussion & limitations 

Our simplified architecture performs well in our experiments, but the best way to add expressivity without sacrificing performance remains unclear. Methods that add expressivity, such as the methods we baseline against, don’t perform as well as the heavily simplified architecture. However, this lunch does not come free, as when evaluated on iterated compaction regimes, the quality decays much faster than alternatives that can remain more expressive, although still the best performing method in the iterative context is well below the full-context threshold. Finding the right way to scale up expressivity of the KV compressor while allowing for repeated compaction remains an interesting direction for future work. While our synthetic/linear environment and analysis provides more understanding for the phenomena often observed in KV compression, it is not exhaustive. A larger variety of small-parameter families and a series of increasingly-complex environments to analyze more complex phenomena both remain interesting directions for future analysis. 

## 9 Conclusion 

We studied the optimization problem created by learning compact KV caches through a frozen language model. Even a compression-free linear map displays important observed phenomena: a 8narrow basin around the global optimum, and broad, flat regions elsewhere. A number of fixes in the literature tie to these loss landscape attributes: denser losses, additional self-study queries, and weight relaxation, which help in restricted settings, but do not reliably carry over to compressive or recurrent caches. Motivated by the inflexibility of the optimization landscape, we show that a heavily-simplified Perceiver architecture outperforms baseline methods on MCQ over compressed caches, obtaining 92.5% accuracy with 0.05 seconds of compaction per document. Iterative compaction remains a challenge for all methods, but in particular this simplified architecture. Finding the best balance of a compactor that is expressive enough to preserve long-term information while being able to reliably optimize through the difficult landscape of KV space remains an open and important problem for memory in continual learning systems.
