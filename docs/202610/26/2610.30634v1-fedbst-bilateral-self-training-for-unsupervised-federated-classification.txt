Abstract —Unsupervised federated learning with Vision-Language Models (e.g., CLIP) offers a promising solution for label-free classification, yet its performance often degrades under severe non-IID shifts. A pioneering method, FedCoPL, typically relies on self-training strategies that exclusively prioritize high-confidence predictions for pseudo-labeling. However, this selective approach neglects the information of ambiguous samples (often referred to as “waist” samples), leading to the underutilization of data and biased decision boundaries. To address this limitation, we propose FedBST, a bilateral self-training framework that effectively exploits both high-confidence and ambiguous data. Specifically, for local optimization, we introduce uncertainty-aware confidence penalization (UACP). UACP targets ambigu-ous samples by penalizing overconfident predictions to implic-itly promote higher uncertainty, thereby preventing erroneous pseudo-labeling. At inference, we employ a visual-text ensemble strategy. This approach aggregates uncertainty-aware local visual prototypes and ensembles them with textual prototypes, achieving lightweight adaptation to local distributions via prototype-based calibration. Experiments demonstrate that FedBST achieves consistent improvements over existing methods with standard federated aggregation protocols. 

Index Terms —unsupervised federated learning, vision-language models, uncertainty estimation, pseudo labeling 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Federated Learning (FL) has established itself as a de facto paradigm for privacy-preserving collaborative training, allowing multiple decentralized clients to train a global model without sharing raw private data [1], [2]. While conventional FL relies heavily on high-quality annotated data [3], [4], obtaining such supervision is often impractical in real-world edge scenarios due to the prohibitive cost of manual labeling and strict privacy constraints [5]. Consequently, Unsupervised Federated Learning (UFL) has emerged as a critical research direction [6], [7]. Early UFL approaches primarily focused on representation learning or clustering [8], [9], which limits their applicability to direct classification tasks. Recently, Large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP [10], have significantly advanced this field by

providing rich semantic supervision. By leveraging the zero-shot capability of VLMs, a pioneering work, FedCoPL [11] has explored UFL to Unsupervised Federated Classification (UFC) settings through cooperative pseudo-labeling, establish-ing strong baselines. This method employs a selective strategy that prioritizes high-confidence predictions as positive pseudo-labels while discarding uncertain ones. However, this selective paradigm exhibits two limitations under Non-IID shifts. First, the exclusion of ambiguous sam-ples leads to biased decision boundaries. By ignoring informa-tive “waist” samples near the boundaries, local models tend to overfit to high-confidence easy patterns, failing to learn the robust decision surfaces required for complex Non-IID data. Second, the exclusive reliance on global textual prompts limits inference adaptability. Such generic prompts, while semantically correct, lack the granularity to capture client-specific visual variations. Relying on this global prior neglects the local visual distribution, thereby degrading performance when local data diverges from the generic textual description. To address these challenges, we introduce FedBST , a Bi-lateral Self-Training framework designed to maximize data utilization and inference reliability. Unlike previous methods that rely solely on positive learning from high-confidence data, FedBST employs a bilateral optimization strategy. For local training, we propose uncertainty-aware confidence penalization (UACP). Instead of discarding ambiguous sam-ples, UACP treats them as valuable signals for regularization. Specifically, it explicitly suppresses the maximum predicted probability of these uncertain candidates. This operation pe-nalizes overconfident predictions, thereby promoting higher predictive entropy to reduce overconfident early assignments to incorrect classes. For inference, we employ a visual-text ensemble strategy. This mechanism dynamically aggregates uncertainty-aware local visual prototypes and ensembles them with generic textual prototypes. This allows the model to leverage the stability of pre-trained textual knowledge while adaptively correcting predictions based on local visual dis-tributions, all without additional parameter optimization. Our contributions are summarized as follows:  

> •

We propose FedBST, a robust UFC framework that arXiv:2610.30634v1 [cs.CV] 22 Oct 2026 integrates bilateral self-training mechanisms. It enables the model to learn simultaneously from high-confidence data while regularizing with ambiguous samples, ensuring robust performance under heterogeneous settings.  

> •

We design two complementary components: the uncertainty-aware confidence penalization (UACP) module to regularize decision boundaries using ambiguous data, and a visual-text ensemble (VTE) strategy to bridge generic textual semantics with local visual distributions for reliable inference.  

> •

Extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks demon-strate that FedBST outperforms state-of-the-art methods in accuracy and robustness with standard server-side aggregation protocols. II. R ELATED WORK 

Unsupervised Federated Learning. Existing UFL approaches primarily focus on representation learning rather than direct classification. Methods like FedU [12] and FedEMA [13] ad-dress heterogeneity via divergence-aware updates, while others employ knowledge distillation [5], [6] or clustering [14] to transfer structural knowledge. However, these representation-focused methods cannot be directly applied to downstream classification tasks without labels. 

Federated Learning with VLMs. VLMs like CLIP have been integrated into FL to reduce communication costs. Works such as PromptFL [15] and FedCLIP [16] optimize prompts but require supervision. Recent personalization methods [17]–[20] improve global-local alignment, but still depend on ground-truth, rendering them unsuitable for fully label-free scenarios. 

Unsupervised Federated Classification. FedCoPL [11] re-cently addressed this challenge by introducing a cooperative pseudo-labeling framework. It utilizes CLIP’s zero-shot pre-dictions to generate pseudo-labels and mitigates label skew through global budget allocation. As a state-of-the-art baseline in this setting, FedCoPL demonstrates the feasibility of label-free federated classification. However, it primarily relies on selecting high-confidence samples for training, which inher-ently neglects the informative value of ambiguous (“waist”) samples. Building upon this foundation, our work introduces a bilateral self-training strategy to effectively exploit both high-confidence and ambiguous data, further enhancing per-formance and robustness. B. Uncertainty-Aware Confidence Penalization 

The budget-based selection strategies discard a substantial portion of local data. Although these discarded samples are unsuitable for positive pseudo-labeling, they provide informa-tion about regions where the model exhibits high predictive uncertainty. To leverage this signal, we introduce uncertainty-aware confidence penalization (UACP). Rather than assigning these ambiguous samples to specific classes, which may in-troduce noise, UACP uses them to regulate model confidence and mitigate confirmation bias. 

Identifying Ambiguous Candidates. We partition the local dataset Dk according to the selection protocol. Let ˜Dk denote the selected subset of samples with high-confidence positive predictions. The remaining samples, defined as Rk = Dk \

˜Dk, are retained for further consideration, as they may still provide useful regularization signals. However, Rk inevitably contains both informative, ambiguous samples and low-quality noise. To disentangle these two cases, we utilize a dual-filter mechanism to isolate the most informative “waist” samples: 1) Symmetric Confidence Interval: We filter samples based on the confidence of their tentative predictions. IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Experimental Setup 

Datasets and Baselines. We conduct evaluations on six bench-marks: DTD [23], RESISC45 [24], UCF101 [25], CUB [26], CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 [27]. Following [11], we simu-late two non-IID scenarios: (1) Dirichlet-based skew , where class distributions are sampled from a Dirichlet distribu-tion ( β); and (2) Quantity-based skew , where samples are grouped by class and distributed via heterogeneous shards. We compare with state-of-the-art methods including Fed-CoPL [11], FedOPT [19], pFedPrompt [17], PromptProx [2], and PromptFL [15]. Supervised baselines are combined with pseudo-labeling strategies FPL [21] or CPL [22] for unsuper-vised adaptation. 

Implementation Details. We use a frozen CLIP (ViT-B/32 by default). Prompts are optimized via SGD (lr=0.1, momen-tum=0.9) with cosine decay. We report the mean accuracy of three trials. Default hyperparameters are set as τcon = 0 .2,

τent = 0 .5, λ = 0 .001 , and α = 0 .8. Other settings are the same as FedCoPL [11].

This work introduces FedBST, a bilateral self-training framework for unsupervised federated classification that is explicitly designed to operate under severe non-IID conditions. Rather than discarding ambiguous data, FedBST makes sys-tematic use of both high-confidence and uncertain samples, allowing a more complete exploitation of local data. Uncer-tain predictions are incorporated as regularization signals via uncertainty-aware confidence penalization, while inference is further strengthened through a visual-text ensemble, which together help alleviate confirmation bias and improve the alignment between global semantics and client-specific dis-tributions. Experimental results across multiple benchmarks indicate that these design choices lead to stable performance improvements, with the largest gains observed in settings characterized by strong label skew and limited data.
