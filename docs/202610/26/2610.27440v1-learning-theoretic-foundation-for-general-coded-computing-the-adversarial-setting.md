## 摘要
对抗性工作节点通过返回受损输出，对分布式计算系统构成了严峻挑战，这些输出可能会显著扭曲最终恢复的计算结果。编码计算通过在工作节点任务分配中引入冗余来应对这一威胁。现有的精确和近似编码计算方法通常通过显式定位并纠正或剔除错误计算结果来处理对抗性输出，这会给主节点带来显著的计算开销。此外，现有分析尚未刻画恢复误差如何随工作节点数量和对抗预算而变化。本文提出了通用编码计算（GCC）的对抗鲁棒扩展。GCC是一种基于学习理论的近似编码计算框架，通过最小化端到端恢复误差的上界来设计编码器和解码器。所提出的方法并不识别受损的工作节点，而是利用平滑性正则化来限制其输出的影响。所得编码器和解码器可通过平滑样条实现，且主节点侧的复杂度为线性。我们通过将最坏情形下的端到端近似误差分解为三个部分，推导出其非渐近上界：（1）编码器近似误差；（2）在所有结果均诚实时的解码器重构误差；以及（3）对抗扰动误差。当N个工作节点中至多有q≥1个对抗性工作节点时，通过适当选择平滑参数，最坏情形误差的量级为O((q/N)^{6/5})。这表明，只要对抗者数量相对于网络规模呈次线性增长，近似误差便会渐近消失。在标量计算和神经网络推理上的实验结果与所预测的收敛行为一致。

## Abstract
Adversarial workers pose a severe challenge in distributed computing systems by returning corrupted outputs that can substantially distort the recovered computations. Coded computing addresses this threat by introducing redundancy across worker assignments. Existing exact and approximate coded-computing methods typically handle adversarial outputs by explicitly locating and correcting or removing erroneous evaluations, which adds significant computational overhead at the master node. Moreover, existing analyses do not characterize how the recovery error scales with the number of workers and the adversarial budget.   In this paper, we develop an adversarially robust extension of general coded computing (GCC), a learning-theoretic approximate coded-computing framework that designs the encoder and decoder by minimizing an upper bound on the end-to-end recovery error. Rather than identifying corrupted workers, the proposed method uses smoothness regularization to limit the influence of their outputs. The resulting encoder and decoder can be implemented using smoothing splines with linear master-side complexity. We derive a non-asymptotic bound on the worst-case end-to-end approximation error by decomposing it into three components: (1) the encoder approximation error, (2) the decoder reconstruction error under fully honest results, and (3) the adversarial perturbation error. For maximum of $q\geq 1$ adversarial workers among $N$ workers results in a worst-case error of order $\mathcal{O}((q/N)^{6/5})$ with appropriate selection of smoothness parameter, demonstrating that the approximation error vanishes asymptotically whenever the number of adversaries grows sublinearly with the network size. Experiments on scalar computation and neural-network inference are consistent with the predicted convergence behavior.
