ABSTRACT
Long-context inference makes the key-value (KV) cache a major memory bottleneck for large
language models. We propose BELT, a low-rank KV cache compression framework. Its key-side
objective targets attention distributions rather than raw keys or pre-softmax logits, yielding a quadratic
surrogate with a closed-form low-rank solution. A learned global rank allocator uses differentiable soft
selection during training and hard selection at deployment to meet a user-specified component budget.
Experiments demonstrate improved model quality under aggressive compression and increased
decoding throughput at comparable accuracy. Evaluated across three LLMs and multiple benchmarks,
BELT achieves up to 70% low-rank KV cache compression while outperforming the strongest baseline
by up to 23.3 points on LongBench, and up to 17.8× overall KV cache reduction when combined
with quantization. With Triton-based GPU kernels and a quantized cache, BELT delivers up to 2.7×
speedup for the attention module and 3.3× end-to-end decoding throughput.

Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have become ubiquitous in modern AI applications, yet their growing scale—in both
model size and context length—poses significant inference challenges. Modern LLMs support 128K-token contexts or
more [1, 2, 3], making the key-value (KV) cache a critical inference bottleneck. During autoregressive generation,
the cache stores keys and values for preceding tokens, and its memory footprint grows linearly with sequence length,
potentially exceeding available GPU memory [4]. Efficient KV cache management is therefore essential, motivating
approaches such as token eviction [5, 6], quantization [7, 8], and low-rank decomposition [9, 10].
Among these, low-rank decomposition exploits hidden-dimension redundancy to reduce KV cache memory. Prior work
follows two broad directions: adapting general-purpose decomposition methods to the KV cache [11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16],
and developing KV-specific methods tailored to attention and caching dynamics [17, 9, 18, 19, 20, 21]. STAR-KV [10]
and A3 [22] represent recent state-of-the-art advances in adaptive rank control and analytical attention approximation,
respectively.
Despite this progress, two challenges still remain. First, the compression criterion of the state-of-the-art does not reflect
the effect of approximation errors on attention behavior. Methods based on KV reconstruction do not explicitly account
for how key errors interact with queries and the nonlinear softmax map. As illustrated in Figure 1, retaining dominant
singular directions of the KV tensors can distort attention and change the generated answer. A3 moves beyond raw
key reconstruction by targeting pre-softmax attention logits, which, however, is not the same as preserving attention
probabilities or the resulting attention output. This motivates a key-compression criterion that explicitly accounts for
post-softmax behavior.
Second, allocating ranks across heterogeneous KV projections under a prescribed global budget remains challenging.
Uniform allocation ignores differences in compression sensitivity, while sensitivity-based searches and learned threshold

these learned thresholds concentrate under certain conditions, potentially limiting allocation effectiveness.
We address these challenges from two complementary perspectives. First, we design compression objectives to preserve
the attention output of the uncompressed model. While our value-side construction is closely related to A3 [22], our
key-side objective targets attention-distribution preservation rather than pre-softmax logit reconstruction like A3. By
accounting for query-dependent softmax sensitivity and RoPE interactions, BELT better preserves attention behavior
under aggressive compression, as demonstrated empirically. Second, we learn ranks across KV blocks under an explicit
global budget. Our allocator learns how to distribute ranks across attention heads according to their compression
sensitivity. We use a differentiable relaxation of top-B selection for end-to-end training, followed by hard selection of
B components, where B is the user-specified budget.
Extensive experiments evaluate BELT across multiple models, compression budgets, and tasks. At 70% KV cache
compression on Qwen3-32B, BELT improves the average score over the strongest evaluated baseline by 23.3 points on
LongBench and 13.8 points on RULER. We additionally evaluate perplexity, zero-shot accuracy, and the contributions
of the compression bases and rank allocation; combined with 3-bit KV quantization, BELT reaches 17.8× KV cache
compression within 2 points of the uncompressed zero-shot accuracy. System-level measurements show up to 3.26×
higher decoding throughput and 87% lower KV cache memory than full-cache inference on LLaMA-2-7B with KV
quantization. These results demonstrate the practical benefits of attention-aware compression and globally coordinated
rank allocation.
In summary, our contributions are:
• We formulate an attention-output-motivated approach to low-rank KV compression, with a key-side metric derived
from attention-distribution KL rather than pre-softmax logit reconstruction.
• We derive quadratic compression surrogates with exact weighted-SVD solutions, extend the construction to RoPE
and GQA, and explicitly characterize their approximation errors.

3.3
Integration with Quantization
To further compress the KV cache, BELT can be combined with existing low-rank KV quantizers without modification.
We integrate Palu [9] and STAR-KV [10]. Since the leading coordinates of the compressed representation dominate
the quantization range, both quantizers apply an orthogonal transform—a norm-preserving change of basis R with
R⊤R = I that mixes coordinates to spread outliers—before quantization. We fuse these transforms into the factors
offline, adding no inference cost. Arbitrary learned ranks are handled with orthogonal matrices, and no retraining is
required. Full details are in Appendix F.2.

Experiments
4.1
Experiment Settings
Models and Tasks.
We evaluate on four LLMs: LLaMA-2-7B [31], LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct [32], Qwen3-32B [33],
and MPT-7B. They span both MHA (LLaMA-2-7B, MPT-7B) and GQA (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-32B), as
well as RoPE (first three) and NoPE (MPT-7B). For accuracy, we measure perplexity on WikiText-2 [34] and C4 [35],
and zero-shot accuracy on six tasks via LM-Evaluation-Harness [36]. For long-context evaluation, we additionally test
on LongBench [37] and RULER [38], covering diverse document-level and structured tasks.
Baseline.
We compare BELT against two types of baselines for low-rank KV cache compression: (1) general
low-rank decomposition methods for LLM compression, including SVD-LLM [11], ASVD [12], Zero Sum SVD [13],
and ERC-SVD [14]; and (2) KV-specific low-rank methods, including PALU [9] and the state-of-the-art STAR-KV [10]
and A3 [22]. Other baselines such as S2VD [16] are excluded due to code unavailability.
4.2
Accuracy Analysis
Perplexity and Zero-Shot Evaluation.
Figure 2 shows that BELT maintains model quality under aggressive KV
cache compression. On LLaMA-2-7B and LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, BELT consistently achieves the best perplexity
and zero-shot accuracy at both 50% and 70% compression, with only marginal degradation from the uncompressed
model. In contrast, baselines degrade sharply at 70% compression, with several collapsing entirely. These results
demonstrate that BELT preserves generation quality even at high compression rates.
Long Context Evaluation.
Figure 2 shows that BELT achieves the best average score at both rates, surpassing even
the uncompressed LLaMA-3.1-8B at 50% compression. Baselines competitive on LM-Eval-Harness collapse here,
confirming that short-context metrics underestimate long-context damage. Retrieval-intensive tasks (Qasper,
TriviaQA) are the most compression-sensitive and where BELT gains the most, while summarization remains
insensitive—consistent with our motivation that query–key matching dominates retrieval.
Comparison with Fine-tuning.
BELT constructs compressed KV projections without fine-tuning model weights,
whereas the methods in Table 1 additionally update weights on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct. Fine-tuning constantly
improves the results of all methods, but baselines still fall behind BELT. Direct FT trains low-rank key and value
projection matrix from a random orthogonal initialization. Despite using the same objective and training data as
BELT+FT, it achieves substantially lower accuracy, which can potentially be attributed to the local minimum issue.

these baselines at 50%. More details about these configurations are given in Appendix G.

5
Related Work
Low-rank decomposition for general LLM compression.
A broad line of work decomposes projection weights to
reduce model memory and computation, including SVD-LLM [11], ASVD [12], Zero Sum SVD [13], ERC-SVD [14],
and S2VD [16]. These methods motivate data-weighted factorizations, but their layer- or weight-reconstruction
objectives do not directly reflect attention behavior under KV caching. BELTinstead derives its weighting from attention
outputs and attention-distribution curvature.
General KV-cache compression.
KV-cache compression has also been studied along the token and precision axes:
token-selection methods retain a subset of cached tokens [5, 6, 40, 41, 42, 43], while quantization methods reduce
cache precision [44, 45, 8, 46, 7, 47]. These are complementary to low-rank compression, which reduces the hidden
dimension rather than the sequence dimension or bit-width. Our joint allocator further treats rank and bit-width as
choices under a shared memory budget.
6
conclusion
We revisited low-rank KV cache compression and addressed two limitations of existing methods: objectives that
minimize KV reconstruction error rather than preserve attention outputs, and rank allocation that collapses to uniform
thresholds. We reformulated compression to align with generation outputs, derived a closed-form solution via an
approximate objective, and proposed a global rank allocation mechanism with a hard budget that preserves the truly
important components across heads. Extensive experiments show consistent accuracy and efficiency gains under
aggressive compression, with up to 3.26× faster decoding and 87% lower memory at comparable accuracy.

Scope of the bound.
Equation 12 requires the same finite M throughout the considered candidate class; a rank
constraint alone does not ensure this condition. Without a uniform norm bound, Equation 10 still holds, but its
coefficients depend on c
WV . In that setting, LK is an upper-bound-motivated, distribution-preserving surrogate, rather
than a value-independent upper bound on the unrestricted joint problem.
The value objective has an exact quadratic representation, and its positive-definite regularization admits the weighted-
SVD solution in Theorem 1. For the nonlinear key objective, we derive its local quadratic term, apply pre-fitting
regularization and a Kronecker approximation, and solve the resulting regularized surrogate in closed form. These
solutions exactly minimize their respective surrogates, not necessarily the original joint objective. The analysis concerns
attention-output errors on fixed calibration inputs, rather than a guarantee of final autoregressive generation preservation.

Algorithm 2 summarizes the rank-1 Kronecker-factored low-rank approximation of the key projection matrix WK.
Given the KV cache X, the query vectors {qx}, and the current key matrix WK, the algorithm aims to find a rank-r
approximation c
WK that minimizes a quadratic surrogate of the token-summed KL divergence between the original and
approximated attention distributions. The algorithm proceeds in three main stages. First, for each query token x, it
computes the attention distribution px, the softmax Jacobian Jx, the Fisher weight Ax, and the regularized per-token left
weight Gϵ

AK and BK to obtain LK and CK, and then solves the resulting weighted low-rank approximation problem in closed
form via truncated SVD of LKWKCK. The output is the optimal rank-r key matrix c

I.4
Scope and Interpretation
Theorem 3 is a stationary-point characterization, not a convergence theorem. At stationarity, concentrated positive
distillation gradients and concentrated thresholds are linked by an exact identity. Observing both at the same stationary
checkpoint therefore does not independently establish that gradient balancing caused threshold concentration.
Although λ cancels explicitly from the stationary spread identity, it can affect the stationary point and its gradients. The
result consequently does not imply concentration independent of the regularization strength.
Finally, concentrated thresholds need not imply uniform ranks: the same threshold applied to different singular
spectra can retain different numbers of components. Claims about rank-allocation quality must therefore be supported
separately.

Other related works
KV-specific low-rank compression.
KV-specific methods include LoRC [17] and PALU [9] (factorized key/value
projections), TALE [18] (token-adaptive ranks), SALS [19] (latent sparse attention), EchoKV [20] (on-demand
reconstruction), and xKV [21] (shared subspaces across layers). A3 [22] develops analytical low-rank approximations
for attention components, while STAR-KV [10] learns soft thresholds for adaptive rank control. Unlike reconstruction-
centered methods, BELT weights approximation error by attention behavior; unlike independent threshold learning, its
rank allocator satisfies a hard model-wide budget.
