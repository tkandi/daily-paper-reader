Abstract 

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization enables memory-efficient fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs). However, ZO optimization suffers from high variance in high-dimensional parameter spaces. While recent methods restrict perturba-tions to random low-rank subspaces, these subspaces often misalign with task-specific descent directions. We propose AdaSub-ZO, a novel forward-only fine-tuning framework in learnable low-rank subspaces. Instead of relying on fixed ran-dom matrices, AdaSub-ZO iteratively learns layer-wise pro-jection matrices directly from finite-difference feedback to track task-aware descent directions. Furthermore, AdaSub-ZO incorporates a subspace momentum acceleration mecha-nism to smooth noisy gradient estimates across evolving sub-space bases. Extensive experiments on natural language un-derstanding tasks demonstrate that AdaSub-ZO outperforms existing ZO baselines, substantially reducing gradient estima-tion variance and accelerating convergence. 

## Introduction 

Large language models (LLMs) have become indispens-able for real-world applications such as natural language understanding, text generation, code intelligence, and com-plex reasoning. To adapt general-purpose LLMs to task-specific domains, fine-tuning is crucial for capturing spe-cialized knowledge and aligning models with downstream objectives. Benefiting from the powerful optimization capability of first-order methods, conventional fine-tuning has achieved promising performance across diverse tasks. However, first-order methods are constrained by the expensive backprop-agation procedure, which requires storing intermediate ac-tivations, gradients, and optimizer states. As the parame-ter scale of LLMs continues to grow, this memory burden becomes increasingly prohibitive. Parameter-efficient meth-ods, including adapters (Houlsby et al. 2019), prefix tuning (Li and Liang 2021), prompt tuning (Liu et al. 2022), LoRA (Hu et al. 2022), and QLoRA (Dettmers et al. 2023), re-duce the number of trainable parameters. However, they still depend on backpropagation and thus cannot fundamentally eliminate the activation memory bottleneck. Recently, zeroth-order (ZO) optimization has emerged as a promising alternative for memory-efficient fine-tuning

(Gautam et al. 2024). By estimating gradients only through forward-pass loss evaluations, ZO methods avoid backprop-agation and reduce memory consumption to a level close to inference. Although ZO methods achieve impressive mem-ory efficiency, they are challenged by high-dimensional per-turbations. Specifically, standard ZO methods perturb the full parameter space, making the variance of gradient esti-mates scale with the number of trainable parameters (Nes-terov and Spokoiny 2017; Duchi et al. 2015). This leads to noisy update directions, slow convergence, and unsta-ble fine-tuning for billion-scale LLMs. To alleviate this is-sue, a straightforward approach is to restrict perturbations to a low-rank subspace, as explored by recent subspace ZO methods such as LOZO and SubZero (Chen et al. 2024; Yu et al. 2025), where full-dimensional perturbations are re-placed with layer-wise low-rank perturbations constructed from Gaussian random matrices. However, these subspaces are usually random and non-learnable, making their pertur-bation directions poorly aligned with task-specific loss de-scent directions and unable to track the evolving gradient subspace (Zhang, Liu, and Shao 2023). Consequently, even in a reduced low-rank space, ZO updates are still affected by noisy and inefficient search directions, limiting the sta-bility and convergence efficiency of billion-scale LLM fine-tuning. To address these issues, a natural direction is to make the subspace learnable and incorporate momentum to smooth noisy finite-difference updates, enabling the per-turbation directions to better track the evolving loss land-scape (Sutskever et al. 2013; Jiang et al. 2024). Despite the promise of replacing random low-rank perturbations with learnable low-rank ones, this transition is non-trivial, as two main challenges need to be addressed. (1) Descent direction ambiguity under limited forward feedback. When apply-ing ZO fine-tuning within a learnable low-rank subspace, the projection matrices that construct the low-rank pertur-bation must be learned only from forward finite-difference feedback. Since backpropagation gradients are unavailable, there is no direct signal indicating which directions are most aligned with loss descent, while excessive probing would weaken the efficiency advantage of ZO optimization. There-fore, the key challenge is how to identify the optimal loss-descent direction under limited computational cost. (2) Mo-mentum inconsistency under evolving subspace bases. arXiv:2610.15225v1 [cs.LG] 10 Oct 2026 Even with a learned low-rank perturbation, each ZO up-date still relies on noisy finite-difference estimates, making the model update direction unstable. A natural solution is to accumulate historical information with momentum inside learnable low-rank subspaces. Yet, projection matrix learn-ing changes the subspace bases across learning cycles, so that historical momentum cannot be directly reused. There-fore, the key challenge is how to maintain momentum con-sistency after the subspace bases are updated. Motivated by the above, we propose AdaSub-ZO , a method for zeroth-order fine-tuning of LLMs in learnable low-rank subspaces. To the best of our knowledge, AdaSub-ZO is the first method that incorporates learnable low-rank subspaces into ZO optimization. By alternating forward-only projection matrix learning with model parameter up-dates, AdaSub-ZO reduces gradient estimation variance and stabilizes the model update direction. Our primary contribu-tions are summarized as follows: • We introduce a low-rank projection matrix learning mod-ule that updates layer-wise projection matrices through forward-only finite-difference feedback to learn task-aware perturbations that better align with task-specific descent directions. • We introduce a subspace momentum acceleration mod-ule for learnable low-rank subspaces. Within each sub-space, the module accumulates ZO gradient estimates via an exponential moving average. Across evolving sub-space bases, the module transfers historical momentum, which can smooth estimation noise and preserve momen-tum consistency across learning cycles. • We conduct experiments on multiple natural language understanding tasks. The experimental results demon-strate that AdaSub-ZO improves fine-tuning performance over existing ZO baselines. 

## Related Work 

Memory-Efficient Fine-Tuning of LLMs 

Fine-tuning large language models has become a standard paradigm for adapting pre-trained models to downstream tasks. Conventional full-parameter fine-tuning updates all model parameters and typically achieves strong task perfor-mance. However, this approach requires storing activations, gradients, and optimizer states during backpropagation. This memory cost becomes prohibitive as model size and se-quence length increase. To reduce the number of trainable parameters, parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods have been widely studied (Houlsby et al. 2019; Li and Liang 2021). LoRA (Liu et al. 2022; Hu et al. 2022) freezes the pre-trained weights and injects trainable low-rank adapters into Transformer layers, while QLoRA (Dettmers et al. 2023) further combines low-rank adaptation with quantiza-tion to reduce the memory footprint. These methods sub-stantially decrease the number of trainable parameters and optimizer states. However, they still rely on backpropaga-tion and therefore need to cache intermediate activations, which remains a major memory bottleneck for long-context or large-scale LLM fine-tuning. 

Zeroth-Order Optimization for LLM Fine-Tuning 

Zeroth-order optimization provides an alternative route for memory-efficient fine-tuning by estimating update direc-tions only through forward-pass loss evaluations. Classical ZO optimization estimates gradients with finite differences along randomly sampled perturbation directions (Nesterov and Spokoiny 2017; Duchi et al. 2015). Recently, MeZO introduces memory-efficient ZO optimization to LLM fine-tuning, showing that LLMs can be adapted with memory consumption close to inference by avoiding backpropaga-tion (Malladi et al. 2023). This makes ZO fine-tuning attrac-tive in resource-constrained scenarios. Despite its memory advantage, ZO fine-tuning suffers from high-variance gradient estimation in high-dimensional parameter spaces. Since standard ZO methods perturb the full trainable parameter space, the variance of the gradi-ent estimator scales with the number of perturbed parame-ters. For billion-scale LLMs, this leads to noisy update di-rections, slow convergence, and unstable training dynamics. Existing ZO methods mainly improve the optimizer (Jiang et al. 2024) or the perturbation strategy (Guo et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025). However, the perturbation directions are still sampled from random distributions and are not explic-itly adapted to task-specific loss landscapes (Gautam et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2026). 

Low-Rank and Subspace Zeroth-Order Optimization 

The low-rank structure of neural network updates motivates restricting ZO perturbations to a lower-dimensional sub-space (Zhang, Liu, and Shao 2023; Zhao et al. 2024). Instead of sampling perturbations from the full parameter space, subspace ZO methods first sample low-dimensional random variables and then map them back to the original parame-ter space. This strategy reduces the effective perturbation dimension and can therefore lower the variance of finite-difference gradient estimates (Chen et al. 2024; Yu et al. 2025). LOZO (Chen et al. 2024) applies low-rank gradient es-timation to LLM fine-tuning by replacing full-dimensional random perturbations with low-rank perturbations. By ex-ploiting the empirical low-rank structure of fine-tuning gra-dients, LOZO reduces the effective perturbation dimension and improves the efficiency of ZO fine-tuning while pre-serving the forward-only memory advantage (Chen et al. 2024). LOZO further introduces lazy sampling and a low-rank momentum variant to stabilize updates across itera-tions. However, the low-rank perturbation of LOZO is still randomly sampled rather than learned from task-specific for-ward feedback, so that the perturbation subspace may re-main misaligned with the true descent directions of down-stream tasks. SubZero (Yu et al. 2025) also adopts layer-wise low-rank perturbations and combines them with a structured perturbation design tailored for LLM weight matrices. For each layer, SubZero samples two column-orthogonal pro-jection matrices and a small Gaussian matrix, and then maps the Gaussian perturbation back to the original weight space through these projections. In this way, the perturbation is restricted to the low-rank subspace spanned by the projec-tion matrices. However, its projection matrices are randomly generated and non-learnable. As a result, the perturbation subspace may be poorly aligned with the true descent direc-tions of downstream tasks and cannot adapt to the evolving gradient subspace during optimization. Motivated by the ability of learnable low-rank subspaces to better align ZO perturbations with task-specific descent directions, in this paper, we propose AdaSub-ZO, the first framework to make low-rank projection matrices learnable for zeroth-order LLM fine-tuning. Specifically, AdaSub-ZO alternates between forward-only projection matrix learning and model parameter updates, so that the low-rank subspace gradually tracks task-specific descent directions. In addi-tion, AdaSub-ZO introduces subspace momentum to stabi-lize noisy finite-difference updates. Unlike directly accumu-lating momentum in the full parameter space, which would increase memory cost, AdaSub-ZO maintains momentum in the low-rank coordinate space and transfers momentum across evolving subspace bases. This design preserves the memory efficiency of ZO fine-tuning while improving the stability and convergence efficiency of low-rank subspace optimization. 

## Methodology 

Overall Framework 

AdaSub-ZO is a forward-only zeroth-order fine-tuning framework that alternates between learnable subspace con-struction and model parameter optimization. In the subspace learning stage, AdaSub-ZO freezes the model parameters and updates the layer-wise projection matrices using only finite-difference feedback, so that the perturbation subspace can gradually align with task-specific descent directions. In the model update stage, AdaSub-ZO fixes the learned sub-space and performs zeroth-order parameter updates within this compact low-rank space, thereby reducing the effective perturbation dimension and gradient estimation variance. To further stabilize optimization, AdaSub-ZO maintains momentum inside the low-dimensional subspace rather than in the full parameter space. When the projection bases are refreshed, the historical momentum is transferred to the new subspace coordinates, which preserves useful update information while avoiding coordinate mismatch. The two stages are repeated periodically until convergence, enabling AdaSub-ZO to improve update direction quality while pre-serving the memory efficiency of forward-only fine-tuning.

## Experiments 

Experimental Settings 

We evaluate AdaSub-ZO on seven natural language under-standing tasks from SuperGLUE: SST-2, RTE, CB, BoolQ, WSC, WIC, and COPA (Wang et al. 2019). These tasks cover sentiment classification, textual entailment, question answering, coreference resolution, word sense disambigua-tion, and causal reasoning. We use OPT-1.3B as the back-bone model (Zhang et al. 2022). All experiments are imple-mented in PyTorch 2.1.0 and Python 3.10, and are conducted on a single NVIDIA A800 GPU with 80 GB memory. The batch size is set to 16 and the maximum sequence length is 2048. We compare AdaSub-ZO with representative zeroth-order fine-tuning methods, which can be divided into two cate-gories. The first category includes general ZO fine-tuning methods that do not explicitly construct low-rank perturba-tion subspaces: MeZO (Malladi et al. 2023), ZO-AdaMU (Jiang et al. 2024), and S-MeZO (Liu et al. 2026). The sec-ond category includes low-rank perturbation-based methods that are most related to our work: LOZO (Chen et al. 2024) and SubZero (Yu et al. 2025). We also use the first-order optimizer SGD as a benchmark. Since appropriate prompts are critical for ZO optimization, all experiments incorporate prompt templates. For a fair comparison, AdaSub-ZO uses the same model learning rate and finite-difference perturbation scale as the corresponding ZO baselines. The low-rank dimension r is set to 36 unless otherwise specified, matching the subspace size used by SubZero. Other AdaSub-ZO-specific hyperpa-rameters, including the subspace learning interval, the num-ber of subspace learning steps, and the subspace momentum coefficient are reported in Appendix C.

## Conclusions 

In this paper, we propose AdaSub-ZO, a zeroth-order fine-tuning framework for LLMs in learnable low-rank sub-spaces. Instead of relying on fixed random perturbation sub-spaces, AdaSub-ZO learns layer-wise projection bases from forward-only finite-difference feedback and performs model updates within low-rank subspaces. To stabilize noisy ZO updates under evolving bases, AdaSub-ZO further main-tains subspace momentum and transfers historical update in-formation across basis refreshes. This design improves the quality and stability of update directions. Extensive exper-iments on SuperGLUE with OPT-1.3B show that AdaSub-ZO achieves the best overall performance among represen-tative ZO baselines. Further empirical analyses confirm the effectiveness of learnable projection bases and subspace mo-mentum for stable and efficient LLM fine-tuning. References
