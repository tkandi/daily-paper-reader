Abstract 

Deep neural networks (DNNs) deployed on heterogeneous edge accelerators are increasingly susceptible to runtime hardware faults. In heterogeneous edge deployments, stringent power and resource constraints often necessitate aggressive voltage scaling and limit the use of hardware fault mitigation techniques. Process variation, thermal stress, and device aging further increase the susceptibility of edge accelerators to runtime faults, making reliable DNN inference a key deployment challenge. These faults manifest as transient bit-level corruptions that propagate through DNN inference, resulting in significant degradation of prediction accuracy. Existing DNN partitioning frameworks primarily optimize latency and energy under fault-free assumptions, producing deployment strategies that often fail to maintain reliable inference under realistic operating conditions. This paper presents RAPID-DNN, a reliability-aware DNN partitioning framework for heterogeneous edge accelerators that explicitly incorporates runtime reliability into the deployment optimization process. Unlike existing partitioning frameworks that optimize only latency and energy, RAPID-DNN introduces runtime reliability as a third optimization objective through layer-wise fault sensitivity modeling and multi-objective deployment optimization. It first performs layer-wise reliability characterization through systematic fault injection in the weight and activation domains to identify vulnerability-critical layers. The resulting sensitivity profile guides a three-objective NSGA-II optimization framework that jointly minimizes inference latency, energy consumption, and expected fault-induced accuracy degradation. Experimental evaluation on AlexNet, SqueezeNet, ResNet18, VGG16, and MobileNetV2 using heterogeneous Eyeriss and SIMBA accelerator profiles demonstrates that RAPID-DNN consistently improves inference robustness over the fault-agnostic baseline across diverse fault models and injection probabilities. Across the evaluated benchmarks under the representative mixed random fault configuration, RAPID-DNN achieves an average improvement of 9.81 percentage points in Top-1 inference accuracy while reducing the average expected fault-induced accuracy degradation by 36.86%, incurring only 2.38% average latency overhead and 3.34% average energy overhead relative to the fault-agnostic baseline. These results demonstrate that incorporating reliability into DNN partition optimization enables robust deployment of AI applications on heterogeneous edge accelerators under runtime hardware faults.

Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) have become a fundamental component of modern edge intelligence systems, enabling real-time inference in applications such as autonomous driving, industrial automa-tion, medical diagnostics, smart surveillance and intelligent IoT platforms [1–3]. These deployments must satisfy strict constraints on latency, energy consumption, and runtime reliability while operating under limited computational and power budgets. Thus, DNN inferencing is increasingly partitioned across heterogeneous edge accelerators including CPUs, GPUs, FPGAs and domain-specific neural processing units (NPUs) within System-on-Chip (SoC) architectures [4–6]. Existing DNN partitioning frameworks such as CNNParted [1], Edgent [2], Neurosurgeon [3] and RoaDRuNNer [5] primarily optimize layer-to-device mappings for latency, throughput and energy efficiency. However, these approaches generally assume fault-free hardware execution and therefore overlook the impact of runtime hardware faults on inference reliability. Recent surveys on DNN reliability assessment and AI fault injection have identified hardware reliability as a critical challenge for edge AI deployment, emphasizing the growing need for reliability-aware design methodologies [21,22,30,31]. Modern edge accelerators operate under aggressive voltage scaling, high integration density, process variation, thermal stress and device aging, making them increasingly susceptible to transient runtime faults. These hardware faults typically manifest as bit-level corruptions in weights, activations, fea-ture maps, and intermediate tensor computations during inference execution. Prior studies have shown that such faults may originate from naturally occurring phenomena including voltage fluc-tuations, timing violations, single-event upsets (SEUs), memory instability, and electromagnetic disturbances [7, 9, 24, 30]. Although this work focuses on naturally occurring transient faults, similar bit-level corruptions may also arise under fault-injection attacks such as voltage glitching, electro-magnetic interference and Rowhammer-style memory disturbance attacks [10, 16]. Regardless of origin, these runtime faults can silently propagate through DNN computations and significantly degrade prediction accuracy without modifying the model architecture or retraining the network. The reliability challenge becomes considerably more complex in heterogeneous edge deployments because different accelerators differ in architecture, memory hierarchy, dataflow and execution characteristics, leading to different fault propagation behavior. A fault introduced in one layer may propagate and amplify through subsequent tensor computations, leading to large-scale inference degradation. This behavior is further influenced in partitioned DNN execution by inter-device communication and partition boundaries. A partitioning strategy may either attenuate or amplify the propagation of hardware faults, depending on the placement of partition boundaries and the vulnerability of individual layers. Consequently, a partition strategy that appears optimal from a latency-energy perspective may become highly unreliable if vulnerability-critical layers are mapped to vulnerable hardware resources. Despite extensive research on DNN partitioning and hardware reliability independently, their interaction in heterogeneous edge deployments remains insufficiently explored. This paper ad-dresses this gap through RAPID-DNN ( Reliability-Aware Partitioning for Robust DNN Inference 

Deployment on Edge AI), a framework for deploying DNNs on heterogeneous edge accelerators under runtime hardware faults. RAPID-DNN combines layer-wise reliability characterization with sensitivity-guided multi-objective partition optimization. Candidate partitions are evaluated us-ing hardware performance profiles within a three-objective NSGA-II [12] framework that jointly minimizes inference latency, energy consumption, and expected fault-induced accuracy degradation. The principal contributions of this work are summarized as follows. • Reliability-aware DNN partitioning framework: We propose RAPID-DNN, a framework 2for reliability-aware partitioning of DNN inference across heterogeneous edge accelerators. Unlike conventional partitioning approaches that optimize only latency and energy, RAPID-DNN explicitly considers expected fault-induced accuracy degradation during deployment planning, enabling robust partition selection under runtime hardware faults. • Layer-wise reliability characterization: We introduce a stochastic fault-injection method-ology in the weight and activation domains for layer-wise reliability characterization. The resulting sensitivity profile identifies vulnerability-critical layers used during partition opti-mization. • Layer-wise sensitivity-guided candidate partition evaluation: We propose a sensitivity-guided evaluation strategy that combines hardware performance profiles with the identified layer sensitivities to estimate the expected fault-induced accuracy degradation of candidate deployment configurations. By prioritizing the most sensitive layers during stochastic fault injection, the proposed evaluation efficiently estimates the reliability of candidate partitions while reducing computational cost. • Comprehensive experimental evaluation: We comprehensively evaluate RAPID-DNN using five widely adopted CNN benchmarks—AlexNet, SqueezeNet, ResNet18, VGG16, and MobileNetV2, which represent a diverse range of network architectures, model complexities, and deployment characteristics commonly used in edge AI research. The evaluation is conducted on heterogeneous Eyeriss and SIMBA accelerator profiles under various runtime fault scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate an average improvement of 9.81 percentage points in Top-1 inference accuracy and 36.86% reduction on an average in expected fault-induced accuracy degradation, while incurring only 2.38% average latency overhead and 3.34% average energy overhead relative to the fault-agnostic CNNParted baseline. The evaluation includes robustness analysis under varying fault injection probabilities and bit-level fault models, latency-energy-reliability trade-off analysis, optimization convergence, and ablation studies. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews related works and identifies the underlying research gap. Section 3 presents the threat model and fault characterization. Section 4 formulates the optimization problem. Section 5 describes the proposed methodology. Section 6 presents the experimental setup, evaluation results, ablation analysis, and a discussion of the limitations of the proposed framework. Finally, Section 7 concludes the paper and outlines future research directions.

42.1 DNN Partitioning and Edge Deployment 

Existing DNN partitioning frameworks primarily focus on optimizing latency, throughput, and energy efficiency across heterogeneous computing platforms. CNNParted [1] formulates heterogeneous DNN partitioning as a multi-objective NSGA-II optimization problem that jointly minimizes inference latency and energy consumption using hardware performance estimates obtained from Timeloop [13] and Accelergy [14], two widely adopted analytical tools for hardware-aware evaluation of DNN accelerators. Although effective for performance-oriented deployment, the framework assumes fault-free execution and therefore does not consider runtime hardware reliability. Timeloop estimates execution latency, memory traffic and resource utilization for different accelerator architectures and dataflows, whereas Accelergy provides architecture-level energy esti-mation based on hardware component models. Together, they enable hardware-aware performance estimation and accelerator design-space exploration, making them a standard profiling infrastructure for performance-driven DNN deployment and partitioning studies. Similarly, RoaDRuNNer [5] investigates runtime scheduling and deadline-aware task offloading, whereas Edgent [2] and Neuro-surgeon [3] propose adaptive edge-cloud partitioning through runtime profiling and layer placement strategies. Complementary to these deployment frameworks, analytical tools have been developed to improve accelerator-aware optimization. MAESTRO [6] provides a data-centric analytical model for evaluating DNN dataflows and hardware costs, enabling informed accelerator design and mapping decisions. SPINN [4] introduces hardware-aware scheduling and code generation for progressive DNN inference across device and cloud resources, while HeteroSplit [16] jointly optimizes DNN partitioning under heterogeneous computation and communication constraints. Collectively, these studies have significantly advanced performance-driven DNN deployment. Nevertheless, they generally assume fault-free hardware execution and do not explicitly incorporate runtime hardware reliability into the partitioning process. 

2.2 Fault Injection and Reliability Analysis in DNN 

The reliability of DNN inference under bit-level hardware faults has received considerable attention with the growing adoption of AI workloads on resource-constrained edge accelerators. Fault injection techniques are widely employed to evaluate the effects of transient and permanent hardware faults by introducing controlled perturbations into weights, activations, and intermediate computations [7, 8, 30, 31]. SiFi-AI [9] extends this capability through RTL-level fault simulation supporting bit-level injection across memories, processing elements, and compute logic, revealing architecture-dependent vulnerability patterns. Similarly, [23] demonstrates that inference degradation strongly depends on fault location, bit significance, and numerical representation. Existing studies consistently demonstrate that hardware faults affect DNN inference in a highly non-uniform manner, with different layers and numerical representations exhibiting markedly different vulnerability characteristics. In particular, sign-bit and higher-order bit corruptions typically produce substantially greater accuracy degradation than least significant bit (LSB) errors [18, 25]. Furthermore, architectural characteristics, including memory hierarchy, compute organization, and dataflow, significantly influence fault propagation and reliability [11,15]. Despite these insights, most existing approaches employ fault injection primarily as a post-deployment evaluation methodology, where robustness is assessed after deployment rather than incorporated into deployment-time optimization. 5Figure 1: System model, threat model, and fault injection methodology adopted in RAPID-DNN for reliability evaluation of partitioned DNN inference on heterogeneous edge accelerators. 

2.3 Adversarial Fault Injection and Hardware Security 

Another line of research investigates adversarial hardware fault injection through voltage glitching, electromagnetic interference, memory disturbance attacks and targeted bit-flip attacks [17–19, 25]. These studies demonstrate that carefully induced bit-level perturbations can significantly degrade DNN inference accuracy or alter model predictions. However, their primary objective is attack generation, vulnerability analysis and security evaluation, rather than improving robustness of deployment under runtime hardware faults. Fault attacks have been exploited to reverse engineer neural network architectures using clock glitches, voltage glitches, laser fault injection and Rowhammer-style memory disturbances [33], further highlighting the security implications of hardware fault mechanisms. Complementary to these attack-oriented studies, TensorFI [32] provides a software-level fault injection framework for TensorFlow applications that enables resilience evaluation through controlled perturbation of neural network computations, the objective being reliability assessment instead of deployment-time optimization. Recent surveys categorize existing reliability assessment methodologies into RTL-, architectural-, software-, and application-level fault injection techniques while emphasizing that the majority of existing approaches evaluate reliability after deployment rather than incorporating reliability information into deployment planning and DNN partition optimization [30, 31]. 

2.4 Research Gap 

The comparison in Table 1 highlights the limitations of existing DNN partitioning and reliability frameworks. Unlike prior approaches, RAPID-DNN integrates layer-wise reliability characterization, sensitivity-guided partition evaluation, and reliability-aware optimization within a unified deployment 6framework, enabling joint optimization of latency, energy, and inference robustness. The analysis of existing approaches reveals three key research gaps that remain to be addressed. 

First , existing DNN partitioning frameworks [1–6] primarily optimize deployment with respect to latency and energy while assuming fault-free hardware execution. Consequently, runtime reliability is not considered during partition optimization. 

Second , existing fault injection and reliability studies [7–9, 23] provide valuable insights into fault propagation and accelerator vulnerability, but they largely treat reliability as a post-deployment evaluation problem. Furthermore, they do not exploit layer-wise vulnerability information to guide deployment or partitioning decisions. 

Third , adversarial fault injection studies [17, 18, 25] primarily focus on attack generation, security evaluation and vulnerability assessment rather than developing deployment strategies that improve inference reliability under transient runtime hardware faults. These research gaps motivate the reliability-aware DNN partitioning framework proposed in this work. 

# 3 System and Threat Model 

3.1 System Model 

We consider the deployment of pretrained DNNs across heterogeneous edge accelerators that collab-oratively execute partitioned DNN inference. The network is partitioned into sequential execution stages mapped onto multiple accelerators, with intermediate feature maps exchanged between devices during inference. The deployment objective is to identify reliability-aware partitioning strategies that preserve inference accuracy while satisfying latency and energy constraints under heterogeneous hardware characteristics. Figure 1 illustrates the system model together with the considered threat model and fault injection methodology. 

3.2 Threat Model 

The primary threat considered in this work comprises transient runtime hardware faults affecting heterogeneous edge accelerators during DNN inference. Such faults may arise naturally from environmental radiation (e.g., single-event upsets (SEUs)), process variations, voltage fluctuations, timing violations caused by aggressive voltage/frequency scaling, thermal stress during sustained operation, and progressive device aging. These conditions are commonly encountered in resource-constrained edge platforms operating under diverse workload and environmental conditions, where they may induce transient bit-level corruptions in memories, processing elements, and on-chip data paths. The resulting bit corruptions can affect DNN weights, activations, or intermediate feature maps, propagate through subsequent layers, and ultimately degrade inference accuracy or produce incorrect predictions. Hardware fault injection techniques, including voltage glitching, electromagnetic (EM) fault injection, laser-based fault injection, and memory disturbance mechanisms (e.g., Rowhammer), are used to emulate transient bit-level corruptions during inference [10, 17, 18, 25, 33, 36, 37]. These techniques are employed solely for experimental evaluation and do not imply the presence of an active adversary. Runtime faults are abstracted using an architecture-independent fault model in which bit-level corruptions affect DNN weights, activations, and intermediate feature maps. This abstraction is widely adopted in prior DNN reliability studies [7, 9, 10, 23, 35] and enables consistent evaluation across heterogeneous accelerator architectures. 7As illustrated in Figure 1, the deployed DNN architecture, pretrained model parameters, compiler, runtime software stack, and communication framework are assumed to be trusted. Active adversaries capable of manipulating model parameters, injecting malicious firmware, compromising accelerator hardware, crafting adversarial inputs, or inducing deliberate hardware faults are outside the scope of this work. Likewise, permanent hardware failures requiring hardware redundancy or replacement are not considered. 

3.3 Fault Injection Configuration 

Hardware faults are emulated through stochastic bit-level fault injection during DNN inference. Let 

pf denote the fault injection probability, defined as the probability that an eligible tensor element is independently selected for bit-level corruption during a fault- injection trial. For a given fault injection probability pf , each eligible weight or activation element is independently sampled using a Bernoulli trial with probability pf . For every selected element, exactly one bit is corrupted according to the specified fault model (LSB, higher-order, sign-bit, or random). Fault injection is independently performed in the weight and activation domains to capture the different impacts of runtime hardware faults. Weight-domain faults model transient corruption of stored network parameters during inference, whereas activation-domain faults capture errors affecting intermediate feature maps and computations. Following established practice in DNN fault-injection studies [7, 9, 10, 20], RAPID-DNN employs stochastic bit-flip fault injection with configurable fault rates, including least significant bit (LSB), higher-order bit, and sign-bit corruptions. The numerical impact of a runtime fault depends strongly on the significance of the corrupted bit. In fixed-point DNN representations, higher-order and sign-bit corruptions generally produce substantially larger numerical perturbations than LSB faults, resulting in more severe degradation of inference accuracy [9, 18, 25]. Each fault configuration is evaluated over repeated independent fault-injection trials, with fault locations randomly sampled according to the specified fault injection probability. The expected fault-induced accuracy degradation is computed as the average across all trials and serves as the reliability objective in the optimization problem described in Section 4. 

# 4 Problem Formulation 

We formulate DNN partitioning as a three-objective optimization problem that determines a layer-to-device mapping while jointly optimizing inference latency, energy consumption, and fault resilience. Unlike conventional partitioning approaches that assume fault-free execution, the proposed formulation explicitly incorporates expected fault-induced accuracy degradation as the third optimization objective.

# 5 Proposed Methodology 

Figure 2 provides an overview of the proposed RAPID-DNN workflow. The subsequent subsections describe the individual components of the framework and their integration into the optimization process. 

5.1 Reliability-Aware Partition Optimization 

RAPID-DNN performs reliability-aware DNN partitioning through two sequential stages, as illus-trated in Fig. 2: (i) layer-wise reliability characterization and (ii) reliability-aware multi-objective optimization. Prior to optimization, hardware performance profiles are generated for each accelerator using Timeloop and Accelergy [13, 14]. For every DNN layer, the profiling process estimates latency and energy under the target precision. These profiles are subsequently used to evaluate candidate partitions during optimization. Table 2 summarizes the accelerator platforms considered in this work. Eyeriss represents an energy-efficient spatial dataflow architecture for CNN inference, whereas SIMBA represents a high-performance multi-chip accelerator. The first stage performs layer-wise reliability characterization by independently evaluating each DNN layer through stochastic fault injection. The resulting sensitivity profile identifies vulnerability-critical layers that guide the proposed sensitivity-guided evaluation strategy. In the second stage, candidate partitions are encoded as chromosomes and evaluated using the three-objective optimization formulation presented in Section 4. Latency and energy are obtained from 11 the precomputed hardware profiles, while the reliability objective is estimated using the sensitivity-guided evaluation strategy. NSGA-II iteratively evolves the population to obtain Pareto-optimal partitioning solutions that jointly optimize inference latency, energy consumption, and expected fault-induced accuracy degradation. Each feasible candidate partition is then evaluated using the latency, energy, and reliability objectives. Latency and energy are obtained from the precomputed hardware performance profiles, whereas reliability is estimated using the guided stochastic fault-injection procedure described in Algorithm 3. During each generation, offspring are generated using two-point crossover with probability pc and uniform integer mutation with probability pm. Since these operations may violate the maximum contiguous-segment constraint, a repair operator is applied before fitness evaluation. The repair procedure merges violating contiguous layer segments until the constraint is satisfied, ensuring that only feasible partition configurations are evaluated. The repaired offspring are then evaluated and merged with the parent population. NSGA-II performs non-dominated sorting on the three-objective fitness values, computes the crowding distance, and applies elitist environmental selection to construct the next generation. The optimization converges to a Pareto front representing the trade-offs among inference latency, energy consumption, and expected fault-induced accuracy degradation. The resulting Pareto-optimal partitions provide alternative deployment strategies, allowing designers to select a solution that best meets the performance and reliability requirements of the target deployment. 

# 6 Experimental Evaluation 

This section evaluates the effectiveness of the proposed RAPID-DNN framework using five represen-tative CNN models deployed on heterogeneous edge accelerator profiles under transient runtime hardware faults. The evaluation examines the proposed layer-wise reliability characterization, as-sesses the effectiveness of reliability-aware partition optimization, analyzes robustness under different fault models and injection probabilities, and quantifies the associated latency–energy trade-offs. 16 The experimental evaluation is designed to comprehensively assess the effectiveness of RAPID-DNN from five complementary perspectives. First, we examine the ability of the proposed layer-wise reliability characterization to accurately identify vulnerability-critical DNN layers. Next, we evaluate whether RAPID-DNN improves inference robustness compared with fault-agnostic DNN partitioning under runtime hardware faults. We then investigate the robustness of the proposed framework under varying fault injection probabilities and bit-level fault models. Subsequently, we analyze the latency and energy overheads incurred in achieving improved runtime reliability. Finally, an ablation study is conducted to quantify the contribution of each major component of RAPID-DNN to the overall reliability improvement. 

6.1 Setup 

Table 3 summarizes the experimental configuration. RAPID-DNN is implemented in Python using PyTorch for DNN inference and the DEAP library for NSGA-II optimization. Per-layer latency and energy profiles are generated using Timeloop and Accelergy [13, 14] following the profiling methodology adopted by CNNParted. Latency and energy for each partition are obtained by aggregating the corresponding layer profiles. All DNN models are imported in ONNX format and executed using the PyTorch inference framework. Experiments are conducted using a fixed evaluation subset of 512 images from the publicly available ImageNet-10K dataset [34], derived from the official ILSVRC2012 ImageNet validation set [29]. The same fixed evaluation subset is used consistently for both RAPID-DNN and CNNParted throughout all experiments to ensure a fair comparative evaluation. Two representative accelerator architectures are considered: Eyeriss, an energy-efficient spatial DNN accelerator, and SIMBA, a high-performance multi-chip accelerator. Fault injection follows the threat model described in Section 3. Runtime faults are injected into the weight and activation domains using random, LSB, higher-order, and sign-bit fault models under multiple fault injection probabilities. The corresponding parameters are summarized in Table 4. Fault locations are sampled independently according to the specified fault probability. Partition optimization is performed using NSGA-II with a population size of 60 and 60 generations. The crossover and mutation probabilities were fixed to pc = 0 .7 and pm = 0 .3, respectively, and were used consistently throughout all experiments. The optimization jointly considers the three objectives defined in Section 4: expected fault-induced accuracy degradation, end-to-end inference latency, and inference energy consumption. Preliminary convergence analysis showed that both the estimated fault-induced accuracy degradation and its 95% confidence interval stabilized after approximately 40–50 trials; therefore, N = 50 was adopted throughout the experiments to balance statistical confidence and computational cost. RAPID-DNN is compared with CNNParted, a multi-objective DNN partitioning framework that optimizes inference latency and energy consumption without explicitly considering runtime reliability. 6.2 Layer-Wise Reliability Evaluation 

This subsection evaluates the proposed layer-wise reliability characterization by analyzing the vulnerability of individual DNN layers to runtime hardware faults and assessing its effectiveness in identifying vulnerability-critical layers. Each DNN layer is evaluated independently by injecting transient runtime faults while all remaining layers execute fault-free. The average Top-1 accuracy degradation over multiple stochastic fault-injection trials is used to compute the layer sensitivity score defined in Section 5. Layers exhibiting the highest sensitivity are selected as the vulnerability-critical layer set C, which subsequently guides the reliability-aware partition optimization.

6.6 Reliability Estimation Convergence 

RAPID-DNN estimates the reliability objective using repeated stochastic fault-injection trials. Consequently, the number of trials directly influences both the statistical confidence of the estimated expected accuracy degradation and the computational cost of the optimization. This experiment evaluates the convergence of the reliability estimate with respect to the number of fault-injection trials. Figure 9 illustrates the convergence of the estimated reliability metric for ResNet18 as a repre-sentative example. As the number of stochastic fault-injection trials increases, the estimated mean rapidly stabilizes, while the 95% confidence interval progressively narrows. Beyond approximately 40-50 trials, both the estimated mean and confidence interval exhibit only minor variation, indicating that additional trials provide diminishing improvements in estimation accuracy. Similar convergence behavior was observed for the other evaluated DNN models. Based on these observations, all experiments in this work use N = 50 fault-injection trials, providing a practical balance between statistical stability and computational cost. 

6.7 Discussion 

RAPID-DNN incorporates runtime reliability into the partitioning process, enabling deployment strategies that improve robustness against hardware faults while incurring only modest latency and energy overheads. Unlike conventional performance-driven partitioning, it exploits the non-uniform fault sensitivity of DNN layers to guide partition selection. The ablation study shows that introducing reliability as an optimization objective provides the primary robustness improvement, while the proposed layer-wise sensitivity characterization further refines partition decisions by prioritizing vulnerability-critical layers. These results highlight the importance of treating runtime reliability as a co-optimization objective alongside latency and energy for DNN deployment on heterogeneous edge accelerators. Despite these encouraging results, the proposed framework has several limitations. First, runtime reliability is evaluated using stochastic bit-level fault injection rather than technology-specific device-26 20 40 60 80 100 456Number of Stochastic Fault-Injection Trials (N) Expected Accuracy Degradation (pp)

Although this abstraction enables architecture-independent evaluation, it does not capture the detailed electrical behavior of fault mechanisms such as voltage glitches, electromagnetic interference, and process variation. Extending the framework with physics-based fault models is left for future work. Second, RAPID-DNN focuses on reliability-aware deployment planning rather than runtime fault detection, recovery, or hardware implementation. Hardware latency and energy are estimated using Timeloop and Accelergy, consistent with the evaluation methodology adopted by CNNParted. The reported improvements reflect the proposed optimization methodology under a common architectural evaluation framework. However, analytical performance models cannot capture all implementation-specific effects, including routing congestion, timing closure, and memory-system behavior, that may influence execution on physical FPGA platforms. Finally, this work focuses on convolutional neural network inference on heterogeneous edge accelerators. Future work will extend the proposed methodology to transformer-based architectures, large language models, and emerging heterogeneous AI accelerators, together with experimental validation on physical FPGA-based platforms. 

# 7 Conclusion 

RAPID-DNN introduces a reliability-aware DNN partitioning framework for heterogeneous edge accelerators operating under runtime hardware faults. By combining layer-wise reliability characteri-zation with multi-objective partition optimization, the framework jointly optimizes inference latency, 27 energy consumption, and runtime reliability. Layer-wise sensitivity information is incorporated directly into the optimization process, enabling partition decisions that improve robustness while maintaining efficient execution. Experimental evaluation on five CNN architectures using hetero-geneous Eyeriss and SIMBA accelerator profiles shows that RAPID-DNN consistently improves inference robustness over the fault-agnostic CNNParted baseline across different fault injection probabilities and bit-level fault models, while incurring only modest latency and energy overheads. The layer-wise sensitivity analysis, ablation study, optimization convergence, and trade-off analy-sis further demonstrate the effectiveness of incorporating runtime reliability into DNN partition optimization. Future work will focus on validation using physical heterogeneous FPGA-based plat-forms, extending the framework to transformer-based architectures and large language models, and investigating adaptive runtime repartitioning under dynamically varying hardware fault conditions.
