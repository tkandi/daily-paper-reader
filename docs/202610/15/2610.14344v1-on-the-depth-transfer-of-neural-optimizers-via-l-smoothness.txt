## ABSTRACT 

Scaling deep learning models efficiently requires predictable performance transfer across compute regimes. Establishing reliable hyperparameter transfer allows optimal training dynamics and struc-tural choices to be discovered on low-cost proxy models, avoiding catastrophic failures during massive-scale training. Existing depth-transfer analyses are architecture-specific and heuristic: hy-perparameter transfer is only justified through toy blocks, and both rely on infinite-depth dynamics, a specific initialization, and feature-learning criteria. Building on L-smoothness framework of Xu et al. (2026), we understand hyperparameter transfer as how do the Lipschitz and L-smoothness constants of the loss scale with the depth K? We show that inverse-depth residual branch scaling 

α = Θ(1 /K ) is sufficient to make both constants depth-independent at fixed width, uniformly over the bounded parameter domain and for arbitrary p → q geometries. Because the bounds depend on each residual branch only through its Lipschitz and smoothness constants, the argument ap-plies to general residual networks—including branches of block depth ≥ 2 such as transformer and ResNet blocks—without assumptions on initialization or correlations between layer weights. Since the smoothness constant controls the curvature penalty in the descent inequality, our bounds mo-tivate a learning-rate scale without an additional depth-dependent curvature correction for updates bounded in the prescribed norm. This provides a geometric explanation of inverse-depth residual scaling and its application to the operator-norm geometries associated with AdamW, Muon, and MOGA. Learning-rate sweeps on GPT-2 (depths 2–128) and CIFAR-100 ResNets (8–32 residual blocks) confirm that Muon and MOGA exhibit stable learning-rate transfer under these prescrip-tions, with a common optimal rate across a 64-fold increase in depth. 

## 1 Introduction 

Hyperparameter choices play a central role in the stability, efficiency, and performance of neural network training. Finding suitable values often requires many training runs, making hyperparameter tuning increasingly expensive as models grow (Yang et al., 2022, 2023a,b; Dey et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026; Mlodozeniec et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026; Ma et al., 2026; Shigida et al., 2026). Moreover, settings that work well for a small model may become ineffective at a larger scale, limiting the value of inexpensive preliminary experiments. Understanding how hyperparameters should change with model size is therefore essential for translating insights from small experiments into reliable and efficient training of large models. In practice, many state-of-the-art optimizers show optimal learning rate drifts as depth scales up (Figure 1). How can we design optimization algorithms so that optimal learning rates transfer reliably as network depth increases? 

Prior work has approached depthwise hyperparameter transfer through parameterizations that preserve feature learning as network depth increases. Depth μP (Yang et al., 2023b) analyze training dynamics as width tends to infinity followed by depth, identifying feature learning and feature diversity as central design criteria for residual networks with one layer per branch. CompleteP (Dey et al., 2025) introduce complete feature learning to motivate residual scaling inversely proportional to depth and corresponding learning rate prescriptions for architectures with multiple layers per branch, including transformers. These theoretical analyses rely on specific architectural and initialization assumptions, are tailored to particular optimizers, and primarily characterize feature behavior in asymptotic regimes. However, preserving feature learning does not by itself establish that the optimal learning rate remains unchanged across depths. This raises a broader question: can we establish a general theory of depthwise learning rate transfer that applies to a broader class of architectures and optimizers, without relying on specific random initialization schemes or infinite width and depth limits? To understand depthwise learning-rate transfer, we follow Xu et al. (2026) and characterize the stable learning rate through a scale-independent L-smoothness control. The L smoothness condition controls how much the loss gradient changes as the network weights vary, measuring weight perturbations in a norm adapted to the optimizer and gradient changes in its dual. L-smoothness governs step-size selection: a gradient step is guaranteed to decrease the loss only when the learning rate is of order 1/L , and larger steps overshoot the region where the linearization remains valid. Consequently, the stable learning rate scales inversely with L. The obstacle to a uniform depth bound L is that the influence of an optimization step is not local. A full-network gradient update needs to perturb all layers simultaneously, requiring control of both perturbation propagation and second-order interactions between layers. Our framework extends the depth scaling analysis beyond the Adam prescriptions developed in Depth μP and CompleteP to a broader class of optimizers used in LLM pretraining, including Muon and row normalization. Our bounds apply directly to finite networks and do not require the sequential limit used in Depth μP and CompleteP, where the width is taken to infinity before depth. Numerical results support our theoretical observations. We evaluate learning-rate transfer under this prescription through sweeps on GPT-2 language models and CIFAR-100 ResNets. The training loss sweeps show an aligned low-2Depth Transfer of Neural Optimizers A P REPRINT 

loss learning-rate region for Muon and MOGA across GPT-2 depths from 2 to 128. On CIFAR-100, all three evaluated MOGA settings share the same best tested learning rate across ResNets with 8, 16, and 32 residual blocks, measured by final-epoch training loss. AdamW also retains a common low-loss region, although its best tested rate shifts toward smaller values at the largest GPT-2 depths. Together, the theoretical bounds and empirical results provide a geometric account of depth scaling and support learning-rate transfer across the evaluated architectures and optimizer geometries.

Hyperparameter Transfer. Hyperparameter transfer, specifically learning-rate transfer, is the technique of reusing a learning rate tuned on a smaller network for the training of a larger model which eliminates the cost of redundant hyperparameter tuning. In particular, we study learning rate transfer over depth. Depth-μP (Yang et al., 2023b) uses the tensor programs framework Yang (2020); Yang et al. (2022) to study the infinite-depth limit of training dynamics and derive parameterization rules that satisfy the activation and gradient scales feature learning rules as depth varies. However, depth-μP holds only for block depth 1 models and their scaling rules deteriorate for the commonly used transformer, which has block depth 2. To fix the limitation of Depth-μP, Complete-P (Dey et al., 2025) takes the μP framework and derives another set of rules targeted at block-depth ≥ 2 architectures. Both works are limited to block-depth constraints and rely heavily on infinite-depth initialization and early training dynamics. Furthermore, rigorous convergence analysis of training dynamics are only established for block-depth 1, meaning 4Depth Transfer of Neural Optimizers A P REPRINT 

that feature learning analysis cannot solely establish the connection to how the network evolves under training for block-depth ≥ 2 architecture. In contrast, our analysis is robust to block-depth; in particular, we provide a worst-case guarantee that is sufficient for architectures of any block-depth. Our analysis also directly controls the gradient scale and loss sensitivity of the network as depth increases which provides a more principled alternative explanation to learning rate transfer scaling rules for transformers. Lastly, our approach does not require specific initialization configurations or weight correlation assumptions, providing learning-rate scaling rules beyond specialized cases in prior works. Zheng et al. (2026) develop spectral conditions for joint width-depth scaling across residual architectures and opti-mizers, including Muon. Their parameterizations include inverse-depth residual scaling and learning rates without a depth correction at fixed width. Our analysis provides a complementary, unified framework through depth-uniform bounds on loss sensitivity and curvature under prescribed parameter norms. By expressing the bounds in terms of common residual-function regularity conditions, the same argument applies across architectures and norm geometries satisfying these conditions. 

Norm Geometry and L-smoothness. Large et al. (2024) establish Lipschitz and smoothness guarantees in a modu-lar norm constructed recursively from module sensitivities and mass allocations, including depth-independent bounds for a suitably scaled residual construction (Propositions 4–5). We study depth scaling directly in a prescribed param-eter geometry. For the identity-skip recurrence yk = yk−1 + ( β/K )fk(yk−1), we establish depth-uniform Lipschitz and smoothness bounds at fixed width in the unweighted maximum of prescribed parameter-block norms, under uni-form branch and loss regularity assumptions. Specializing these norms to p → q operator norms makes the associated unit-ball steepest-descent directions explicit and computable blockwise, without introducing separate module-mass parameters. The resulting smoothness bound gives a depth-independent curvature penalty for updates measured in this geometry, providing the basis for the learning-rate discussion in Section 3.2 and our optimizer-specific prescriptions. The analysis treats each residual branch through its regularity constants and therefore also covers multi-layer branches satisfying these assumptions.

3.2 Depth-Independent L-smoothness Theorem 

While Lipschitz continuity is enough to control the sensitivity of the network to weight perturbations, it provides limited information on the actual optimization dynamics during training. We show that inverse depth branch scaling provides a depth-independent L-smoothness constant that directly controls how much gradients can vary along the optimization trajectory. Bounding this quantity is crucial, as it ensures that the steps we take in optimization are stable as depth varies.

Why L-Smoothness Motivates Learning Rate Transfer Following Xu et al. (2026), we use the smoothness con-stant L to guide learning rate selection. For an update x+ = x + ηD along a unit norm steepest descent direction, the linear approximation predicts a loss decrease of η∥∇ f (x)∥∗, while the curvature penalty is bounded by Lη 2/2. Min-imizing the resulting upper bound on the loss gives η∗ = ∥∇ f (x)∥∗/L . A common smoothness bound across depths therefore ensures that the curvature term does not require smaller learning rates as the network becomes deeper. If the dual gradient norm also remains comparable across depths, this rule yields learning rates of comparable magnitude, providing a theoretical motivation for depthwise learning rate transfer.

In this paper, we adopt the matrix-operator norm steepest descent framework from Xu et al. (2026) to investigate how optimizer geometry can yield depthwise learning rate scaling rules. We show that many state-of-the-art optimizers-such as AdamW and MUON- can be studied through associated matrix-operator norm geometries. However, our analysis shows that neural networks are not depth-independent under the standard branch scaling; the worst case Lipschitz bound becomes unstable as network depth increases. 8Depth Transfer of Neural Optimizers A P REPRINT 

We propose inverse-depth branch scaling to bound aggregate sensitivity to layer updates uniformly in depth and hence induce an L-Lipschitz constant that is independent of depth. Our further analysis of gradient sensitivity shows that the objective loss function under inverse-depth branch scaling sufficiently induces a depth-independent L-smooth constant which motivates a learning rate scale without an additional depth-dependent curvature correction. Our experiments evaluate the resulting learning-rate transfer for practical optimizers. Our experiments of large-scale pre-training tasks on GPT-2 and image classification tasks on ResNet show that optimal learning rates stay nearly invariant on the tested state-of-the-art optimizers, which allows models over a 64-fold depth range to effectively transfer learning rates from small models to deeper models, reducing the computation budget of hyperparameter tuning. Our analysis establishes inverse-depth residual scaling as a worst-case guarantee for depth-uniform sensitivity and smoothness bounds. Larger residual multipliers can also support learning-rate transfer in certain settings. For example, Depth-μP (Yang et al., 2023b) uses ( K−1/2) residual scaling and demonstrates transfer in residual networks with single-layer branches under its parameterization. Our present bounds do not explain this behavior. Extending the analysis to account for the architectural or statistical structure that enables transfer in these special settings is a natural direction for future work. 9Depth Transfer of Neural Optimizers A P REPRINT
