# Federated Zeroth-Order Optimization with Direction Aggregation and Variance Reduction 

Qianlong Dang, Member, IEEE , Baosheng Li 

Abstract — We study constrained nonsmooth nonconvex stochastic optimization in federated settings, where clients access only stochastic function evaluations. Existing feder-ated zeroth-order methods primarily combine local zeroth-order updates with model averaging. However, they strug-gle with client drift induced by local projected updates and sampling variance in stochastic zeroth-order estimates. In this paper, we propose a federated zeroth-order frame-work based on direction aggregation, equipped with two local sampling schemes. Specifically, FedZOO constructs a local direction from a minibatch shared across multiple spherical queries, thereby controlling the stochastic errors arising from data sampling and gradient approximation. In contrast, FedVRZO forms its local estimator from inde-pendent sample–direction pairs, so that its sampling error is controlled directly by the number of sample–direction pairs. In both algorithms, clients compute the mean of the directions evaluated along their projected local trajectories, and the server performs a single projected update using the weighted aggregate of the client directions. Furthermore, we establish convergence guarantees for FedZOO and Fed-VRZO, respectively. Experiments on black-box adversar-ial attacks demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods. 

Index Terms — Federated optimization, zeroth-order op-timization, nonsmooth nonconvex optimization, variance reduction, randomized smoothing.

A. Related Work 

The absence of gradient information motivates centralized zeroth-order optimization (ZOO) [7], where true gradients are replaced by finite differences of function values along sampled directions. To handle nonsmoothness [8], randomized smoothing constructs a differentiable surrogate of the stochas-tic objective, allowing two-point function-value differences [9] to approximate the gradient of the smoothed function. Ghadimi and Lan [10] developed a randomized zeroth-order algorithms for nonconvex stochastic programming, establishing stationar-ity complexity bounds with explicit dimension dependence. Nesterov and Spokoiny [11] analyzed Gaussian smoothing for gradient-free minimization and quantified the effects of the smoothing radius and directional sampling. In nonsmooth nonconvex settings, gradient-free algorithms replace classical gradient-norm criteria with smoothed or neighborhood-based measures of stationarity [12]–[14]. To enforce feasibility [15], [16], projected ZOO methods control stationarity over a closed convex constraint set [17]–[20], whereas zeroth-order Frank– Wolfe variants offer a projection-free alternative [21]. Central-ized ZOO methods share a unified single-trajectory recursion, where local directions are estimated at the current iterate and projected once per iteration. This unified recursion allows stationarity to be naturally characterized through the projected gradient mapping of the smoothed objective. However, the same property disappears when clients perform multiple local updates between server synchronizations. The single-trajectory property fails when function evalu-ations are carried out by multiple clients rather than at a common iterate. Early works [25], [26] establish consensus-based architectures [27] and primal–dual frameworks [28] for distributed nonconvex zeroth-order optimization. In every round, each client evaluates several local zeroth-order directions [37], so that the query points may drift from the server iterate and form client-specific projected trajectories. Building on this line of work, Chen et al. [24] analyze FedZO generalization and stability under smoothness and heavy-tailed noise assumptions. However, these methods fail to account for the scenario where local directions evaluated along client-specific projected trajectories are aggregated prior to a server-side projection. Consequently, it remains a critical theoretical challenge to establish convergence guarantees for this direction-aggregation paradigm. 

B. Motivation 

Federated zeroth-order methods reduce communication by performing multiple local function-value queries, but methods with multiple local steps commonly aggregate terminal client models [22], [23]. Under constraints, each terminal model mixes the evaluated directions with the displacement and pro-jection effects accumulated along a client-specific trajectory, which are further influenced by client drift [5], [38], [39]. Since projection and weighted averaging do not commute, the averaged model generally cannot be interpreted as a projected step along the weighted global direction. This precludes a di-rect application of centralized projected ZO analysis [17], [18] and motivates aggregating local directions before performing the server projection. 

C. Main Contributions 

This paper concerns constrained nonsmooth nonconvex stochastic optimization in a synchronous federated setting, where each client accesses only stochastic function evalu-ations. In contrast to FedZO that aggregates local models, we consider a novel recursion in which clients aggregate zeroth-order estimates evaluated along their projected local trajectories and the server performs one weighted projected update. This architecture keeps the server recursion on the shared feasible set while exposing the effects of local drift and stochastic sampling. The main characteristics of the proposed methods and the corresponding analysis are summarized as follows. 1) We propose a federated zeroth-order optimization (Fed-ZOO) algorithm based on local-direction aggregation. Each client uses a common minibatch of Bt samples together with Qt spherical directions to form a two-point estimate and then uploads the mean of the K

directions evaluated along its projected local trajectory. Then, the server aggregates these directions with the objective weights and performs one projection onto the shared constraint set. 2) We propose a federated variance-reduced zeroth-order (FedVRZO) algorithm, which retains the same client-server update recursion but constructs each local es-timator from Nt independent sample–direction pairs. The samples are drawn from Di, and the directions are drawn uniformly from the unit sphere. This construction produces a conditional mean-zero sampling error whose second moment is of order O(n/N t).3) We establish the finite-time convergence guarantees for FedZOO and FedVRZO under nonsmooth nonconvex settings. For both algorithms, our direction-error anal-ysis quantifies the coupled effects of projected local-trajectory drift, smoothed-gradient heterogeneity, and sampling-scheme variance. 4) We characterize the communication and function-query complexities of FedZOO and FedVRZO. Under a fixed local budget of P two-point differences, the sampling-error factors of FedZOO and FedVRZO scale as P −1/2

and P −1, respectively. The resulting function-query complexities are O(M Knϵ −2 +M n κ2ϵ−6/K ) for Fed-ZOO and O(M Knϵ −2 + M n κϵ−4) for FedVRZO. We validate the performance of FedZOO and FedVRZO through comparative experiments with existing federated zeroth-order baselines on black-box adversarial attacks. 3

VI. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we developed FedZOO and FedVRZO for constrained nonsmooth nonconvex stochastic optimization in federated settings. FedZOO aggregates zeroth-order directions evaluated along projected local query trajectories and performs a single weighted projection at the server. FedVRZO preserves the same direction-aggregation recursion while constructing each local estimate from independent sample–direction pairs, thereby improving sampling efficiency. For both algorithms, we established nonasymptotic bounds on the expected squared projected gradient mapping and characterized their commu-nication and function-query complexities. Numerical experi-ments on black-box adversarial attacks illustrated the empirical performance of the proposed methods.
