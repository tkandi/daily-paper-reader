## ABSTRACT 

Decentralized optimization on the Stiefel manifold has broad applications in ma-chine learning and signal processing. We propose P LADOS , a parameter-free landing algorithm for decentralized optimization on the Stiefel manifold. Each iteration combines local stochastic gradient updates with a single retraction-free communication step over the communication graph. A key convex-like property of the intersection of the nonconvex Stiefel manifold and consensus constraints is characterized through a restricted secant inequality for the penalty-only scheme, ensuring local contraction of the manifold consensus error without introducing addi-tional parameters that require tuning. For n nodes using independent local batches of size b, we prove that P LADOS achieves linear speedup with respect to the number of nodes with an asymptotic convergence rate of O(σ/ √nbK + ( nξ 2)1/3/K 2/3)

after K iterations, where σ2 > 0 is the sampling variance and ξ2 quantifies gradi-ent heterogeneity. Experiments demonstrate stability across tested stepsizes and networks, time efficiency, and linear speedup with respect to the number of nodes. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Decentralized optimization enables a network of n nodes to learn from local data using only neighbor communication (Tsitsiklis et al., 1986; Nedic & Ozdaglar, 2009; Duchi et al., 2011). Distributing both data and computation makes this framework useful for large-scale learning and signal processing. Many such applications also require orthonormal decision matrices, including principal component analysis (Chen et al., 2021), low-rank estimation (Deng & Hu, 2025a), orthogonal representation learning (Huang et al., 2018), and low-rank adaptation (Zhang et al., 2026). These problems motivate decentralized stochastic optimization on the Stiefel manifold (Boumal, 2023, Section 7.3):

Solving equation (1) requires the nodes to reach consensus while satisfying a nonconvex orthogonality constraint. Even when all local matrices have orthonormal columns, their weighted average can leave the Stiefel manifold. Retractions restore feasibility after each update, but the required QR or polar factorizations add computation at every node (Chen et al., 2021; Deng & Hu, 2025a). Retraction-free landing methods offer a cheaper way to reduce constraint violation (Ablin & Peyr´ e, 2022; Zhang et al., 2026). In a decentralized algorithm, however, landing must work jointly with communica-tion and optimization. Stochastic gradients and heterogeneous data make this interaction more delicate. This raises the question: can a simple retraction-free decentralized algorithm control both consensus and orthogonality errors and achieve stochastic linear speedup with respect to the number of nodes? We answer this question with P LADOS , a parameter-free landing algorithm that combines a penalty-only scheme with one communication round and one local stochastic gradient step per iteration. To the best of our knowledge, P LADOS is the first decentralized stochastic landing method with proven convergence on the Stiefel manifold. 1.1 RELATED WORK 

Decentralized optimization in Euclidean space. Classical consensus and gradient methods laid the foundations (Tsitsiklis et al., 1986; Nedic & Ozdaglar, 2009; Duchi et al., 2011; Yuan et al., 2016). D-PSGD and subsequent analyses establish nonconvex stochastic linear speedup and characterize topology, compression, and heterogeneity effects (Lian et al., 2017; Koloskova et al., 2019; Tang et al., 2018; Alghunaim & Yuan, 2022; Takezawa et al., 2026). EXTRA, exact diffusion, NIDS, and gradient tracking use auxiliary information for exact convergence or reduced local drift (Shi et al., 2015; Yuan et al., 2018; Li et al., 2019; Nedi´ c et al., 2017; Qu & Li, 2017; Li et al., 2022; Pu & Nedi´ c, 2021; Koloskova et al., 2021; Xin et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2025). In D-PSGD, heterogeneity contributes a K−2/3 term, while independent batches reduce stochastic error (Alghunaim & Yuan, 2022). By contrast, D2 uses a history correction to remove dependence on global gradient heterogeneity (Tang et al., 2018). Extending these Euclidean techniques to nonconvex manifold constraints is nontrivial. 

Retraction-based decentralized optimization on manifolds. Early work studied consensus in-trinsically on manifolds (Sarlette & Sepulchre, 2009; Tron et al., 2012). On the Stiefel manifold, DRSGD combines stochastic Riemannian steps with retractions and multi-step consensus, while DRGTA adds gradient tracking (Chen et al., 2021). DPRGD, DPRGT, and DPRSRM extend projected Riemannian techniques to compact embedded submanifolds and stochastic recursive momentum (Deng & Hu, 2025a;b). Proximal tracking, Riemannian EXTRA, local-consensus, conjugate-gradient, natural-gradient, compressed, and quantized variants further broaden this class (Wang et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2025b; Chen et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2025a; Hu & Deng, 2024; Chen et al., 2025). Repeated factorizations and vector transport can limit scalability, especially on GPUs (Song et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026). 

Retraction-free optimization on the Stiefel manifold. Centralized infeasible, landing, and constraint-dissolving methods avoid orthonormalization (Gao et al., 2019; Ablin & Peyr´ e, 2022; Xiao et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2026). Centrally coordinated EF-Landing combines stochastic landing with compressed communication (Song et al., 2025). Decentralized examples include the approximate augmented-Lagrangian method DESTINY, landing with gradient tracking in DRFGT, and the deter-ministic primal–dual RF-EXTRA correction (Wang & Liu, 2022; Sun et al., 2024; Li & Hu, 2026). Stochastic linear speedup with respect to the number of nodes without gradient tracking remains a key challenge for decentralized methods with independent mini-batches and heterogeneous objectives. Penalty selection is another central issue in constrained optimization: Byrd & Nocedal (1998) asked at the International Congress of Mathematicians 1998 whether sensitivity to penalty parameters could limit large-scale exact penalty methods. Our P LADOS answers this question for the considered constrained problem by a landing correction that requires no problem-dependent penalty tuning. 1.2 CONTRIBUTIONS

## 3 CONVERGENCE ANALYSIS 

We establish local linear convergence of the penalty-only scheme, then control stochastic disagreement and objective descent. Together, these results yield convergence with diminishing stepsizes and linear speedup with respect to the number of nodes under a horizon-dependent stepsize. For the network comparison, ER graphs threshold shared edge uniforms, giving nested edge sets; graphs are generated independently of the data. Figure 2 and LRMC use fresh uniform minibatches, independent across iterations and nodes, without replacement within each batch. The heterogeneity experiment uses exact local gradients. We adapt the label-partitioned fraction of Hsieh et al. (2020) to MNIST PCA. A fixed balanced pool contains 50000 training images, 5000 per digit. Images are vectorized in Rp with p = 784 and divided by 255 without centering; we set r = 5 . This pool and its global empirical reference subspace are fixed across partitions, keeping the global optimization problem unchanged. Images are randomly ordered within each digit, concatenated in label order, and split into 8 blocks of 6250. Each block is shuffled once. At level ρ, node i retains its first ⌊6250 ρ⌋ images; the remaining images are pooled, randomly permuted, and redistributed equally. Thus every node always has 6250 images and the global pool is preserved. We use ρ ∈ { 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1}: ρ = 0 gives random assignment and ρ = 1 gives label-sorted blocks, with labels potentially shared across nodes.
