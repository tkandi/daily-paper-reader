# An Optical KV Cache Network for Disaggregated LLM Serving 

Seongwon Yoon and Shimeng Yu 

School of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Georgia Institute of Technology, Atlanta, GA, USA gabrielyoon@gatech.edu 

Abstract —Disaggregated LLM serving moves each request’s KV cache from a prefill GPU to a decode GPU before the response begins, and a coding-agent turn often moves the KV of its whole long context. Behind an oversubscribed packet fabric this transfer adds to time to first token (TTFT). An optical circuit switch (OCS) can give every transfer a full-rate circuit for less than an oversubscribed packet fabric costs, but a mechanical OCS needs tens of milliseconds to reconfigure. We hide this delay inside the prefill. A reservation scheduler commits each circuit while the prefill is still computing, so the circuit is ready when the KV is, and each GPU reaches the OCS over its own wavelength-multiplexed (WDM) optics on one fiber pair. In simulations of GPU clusters on real serving traces with prefill times measured on H100 GPUs, this optical KV cache network matches 400G packet fabrics with adaptive routing at 0.55 to 0.78 times the cost of a 2:1 fabric and tolerates the 50 ms reconfiguration delay of a 384-port mechanical OCS. 

I. Introduction Prefill/decode (P/D) disaggregation runs the two phases of LLM serving on separate GPU pools [1], [2], [3], so every request’s KV cache crosses the network and adds to TTFT unless the transfer ends within the prefill. In our analysis of one P/D application in Alibaba’s production trace [4], the hourly mean KV transfer time is a median 1.10 times the prefill time over one week. Deployed P/D systems choose the decode instance by load [3], [5], [6], so a coding-agent turn [7] that appends a tool result to a long context often moves the KV of the whole context behind a prefill of only the new tokens. A non-blocking 400G fabric mostly hides this move, but operators oversubscribe the upper tiers [8], [9], [10], and at half of 400G per GPU the median migrated turn of Llama-3.1-70B on H100 GPUs adds 67 ms to TTFT. A non-blocking packet fabric costs 1.27 to 1.31 times a 2:1 fabric, and packet schedulers [11], [8] reduce contention on the same links. An OCS port draws far less power than a packet switch port and passes light without transceivers [12], [13], so a circuit per transfer can give every GPU its full link rate for less than an oversubscribed fabric, yet a mechanical OCS, steered by MEMS mirrors or piezoelectric actuators, needs 10 to 50 ms to reconfigure [13], [14], [15], which we hide inside the prefill. Unlike OCS fabrics that reconfigure per training job, phase or workload mode [13], [12], [16], [17], [18], [19], our circuits follow single KV transfers. Our contributions are an optical KV cache network with GPU-side WDM optics and the requirements it sets on them, one fiber pair per 400G link, up to 50 ms of reconfiguration and 2 dB of link margin (Section III-A, Table I), a reservation scheduler that commits the prefix circuit at prefill start and the new-KV circuit at the last prefill chunk and reuses standing circuits (Section III-B), and simulations of H100 clusters with measured prefill times on real serving traces (Section IV). II. Background 

A. Prefill, decode, and KV cache transfer 

Prefill reads the whole prompt at once and decode generates one token at a time, both through the key and value vectors stored per token and layer, the KV cache. For Llama-3.1-70B in BF16 it holds 320 KiB per token [20], so a 64K-token context occupies about 21 GB. Prefill is compute bound and decode is memory bound, so P/D disaggregation places them on separate pools [1], [2], as in Mooncake, NVIDIA Dynamo and DeepSeek [3], [6], [5]. Mooncake streams the KV cache layer by layer during prefill, which hides the transfer while it ends within the prefill, and the vLLM NIXL connector pulls it after prefill [21], which adds the whole transfer to TTFT because decode emits the leading token. 

B. Multi-turn agent sessions and KV cache migration 

Coding agents append a short input, such as a tool result, to a long shared context every turn. Among the steps that fit the 128K window of Llama-3.1-70B in a trace of 4,300 coding-agent sessions from 43 developers [7], the median context is 66.7K tokens and the median new input 1.1K tokens, against 88.8K and 1.6K for main-agent turns in the public AgentX Claude Code sessions [22] and a 68K-token median prompt in GitHub Copilot [23]. A turn migrates when the prefill side still holds its prefix [24], [25], [26] but the chosen decode instance does not, and its full KV cache then crosses the network before decode can respond. Mooncake and DeepSeek send the full KV to a decode instance chosen by load every turn, Dynamo routes decode without regard to prefix reuse [3], [5], [6], and in llm-d runs on coding-agent traces about half of the decode-side KV arrived from prefill [27]. In our analysis of Alibaba’s production P/D telemetry [4], the least-utilized decode instance of a group changes in 42% of consecutive 5-minute windows (16.5% by more than one SM point). With session affinity 7.6% of turns still miss decode memory in our modeled replay, and a KV store still sends the KV over the fabric [5].

Both NIC paths cross leaf and spine fat trees of electrical packet switches (EPS), whose upper tiers are often oversubscribed [8], [9], [10] and which spread flows by hashing (ECMP), letting large transfers collide [28], or by adaptive routing [29] under RDMA congestion control [30]. An OCS steers light from an input fiber to an output fiber without converting it to an electrical signal [31], [32], [33], as in Google’s TPU and datacenter networks [18], [34], [35]. It does not inspect packets, so one port serves any line rate at 0.39 W against 39.4 W for a 400G packet switch port [12], and at $520 against $583 to $1,090 [12], [13]. In exchange a circuit joins one pair of ports at a time, and changing it takes up to 25 ms in 192-port and 50 ms in 384-port mechanical switches [13], [14], [15] and about a microsecond in silicon photonic switches [36]. Each prefill and decode GPU drives one 400G link of pluggable or co-packaged WDM optics [37] into the OCS, 45 GB/s at an assumed 90% efficiency as for every fabric. A fiber OCS switches fibers rather than wavelengths, so the GPU-side optics set its size. A 400GBASE-FR4 link carries four wavelengths on one fiber pair, while a parallel-fiber 400GBASE-DR4 link occupies four pairs [38] and needs four times the switch ports. Eight 384-port planes, each joining one GPU position of every 8-GPU node, reach 3,072 GPUs in one stage [15], so KV moves between equal GPU positions.

The head carries prefix KV that prefix caching already holds in the prefill GPU and that this prefill only reads, so it does not depend on the tail, which carries the new tokens’ KV as their layers finish. Committing the head at prefill start makes its start exact, and committing the tail when the last chunk of up to 2,048 tokens starts leaves only the error of the remaining prefill, while a booking at admission would absorb the error of every prefill queued ahead. Preferring a standing circuit (line 4) makes the decode choice circuit-aware and favors reuse. IV. Evaluation 

A. Setup Design space. A request’s KV demand per GPU 𝐷 is the KV bytes one GPU sends over the prefill time, and 𝑆 is the effective KV bandwidth per GPU, so layer-wise streaming exposes max (0, 1 − 𝑆 /𝐷 ) of TTFT. Prefill times use a cost model fitted to 33 vLLM prefill runs of Llama-3.1-70B that we measured on four H100 GPUs (median error 4.6%), without scaling. We use H100 GPUs because they are the most advanced GPU system available to us, and the method applies in principle to newer GPUs by refitting the cost model. 

Simulation. A flow-level event-driven simulator models 128 and 256 H100 GPUs as slices of a larger fabric with 32-GPU racks, prefill and decode in separate racks and each decode instance as a KV sink without decode compute, so the results cover the KV fabric and TTFT. As in prior P/D studies [1], [2], [11], request traces drive the simulator. A TraceLab tenant [7], whose decode instances keep no session KV between turns as in Mooncake and DeepSeek, runs beside Azure 2024 chat and code [45], [46] and Mooncake [3] tenants, all on the measured Llama-3.1-70B configuration on four H100 GPUs. The base point gives TraceLab 25% of the GPUs at 60% prefill load, and the grid adds a 50% share and 75% load, in layer-wise and pull transfer. Results average five paired 330 s trace windows, with prediction errors drawn from the measured runs. B. Performance and cost 

Migrated turns of Llama-3.1-70B demand a median 28.6 GB/s per H100 GPU, within a 400G link at 45 GB/s, so a non-blocking fabric exposes 5% of mean TTFT, or 10% at the 32.5 GB/s NCCL rate we measured over 400G InfiniBand, and half or a third of 400G per GPU exposes 19% and 31% (Fig. 3(a)). At these rates AgentX turns give 12% and 20%, and FP8 KV cuts the TraceLab shares to 5% and 11%. Under load at 128 GPUs the OCS stays within 0.4 points of the agentic SLO attainment of EPS 1:1 with SRPT at every grid point and mode, with P99 TTFT within 1.8%. Adaptive routing with fair sharing stays within 0.5 points of the OCS even at 3:1, so the loss behind oversubscription comes from ECMP collisions of migrated turns. In pull transfer the OCS gains 3.4 to 6.6 points over 2:1 ECMP and 4.6 to 10.7 points over 3:1 ECMP, and 0.2 to 3.2 points in layer-wise transfer, where it hides 71 to 74% of migrated turns within 10% of their prefill time against 50 to 58% and 43 to 53%. At 256 GPUs the pull gains over ECMP are 2.7 to 6.2 points (Fig. 3(b)). The gain follows the share of migrated turns, 1.3 to 1.8 and 1.8 to 2.7 points in pull when half the turns migrate, as half the decode-side KV does in llm-d [27], and within 0.6 points for every fabric under session affinity (7.6%), where the OCS keeps its cost advantage. With a NIC and a WDM pluggable per link at two sets of published prices [13], [12], the OCS costs 0.55 to 0.78 times a 2:1 EPS and 0.61 to 0.85 times a 3:1 EPS from 256 to 3,072 GPUs, the reach of one OCS stage (Fig. 3(c) shows one price set). Parallel-fiber DR4 costs 0.77 to 1.36 times a 2:1 EPS in one stage (Table I), so WDM optics pay off best. 

C. What the optical layer must provide Overlap. On our two-H100 testbed (Llama-3.1-8B over NVLink, Fig. 4(a)), an injected delay started at admission stays hidden while it is shorter than the prefill, and one started after prefill costs 0.95 to 1.12 times its length. 

Prediction. With last-chunk commit agentic P99 TTFT rises 1.5% at the measured 4.6% median error and 3.6% at 10%, while admission commit rises 3.5% and 9.6% (Fig. 4(b)), so P99 stays within 5% up to 11.6% error against 5.9%. 

Reconfiguration delay. Without reservation, layer-wise transfers hold decode ports through every prefill, so with the decode chosen by load the Mooncake tenant overloads in every trace window even with a 10 𝜇 s switch (agentic SLO attainment 71%), while reservation with the same decode choice holds 85% at 25 ms. Circuit-aware decode choice keeps the same decode for 92% of consecutive agentic requests, with the busiest decode at 1.07 times the mean, so with reuse the SLO attainment stays at 85% up to 100 ms (Fig. 4(c)). Without reuse all tenants keep P99 and SLO within 5% of EPS 1:1 with SRPT up to 50 ms at 60% load, so a mechanical OCS suffices. 

Link budget. The worst path through four connectors and a 384-port OCS at 2.7 dB [15] loses 5.95 dB, above the 3 and 4 dB channel allocations of DR4 and FR4, so 2 dB of margin needs a launch near the maximum and TDECQ up to 1.5 dB with DR4 and 1.7 dB with WDM, or spliced OCS fibers with typical loss for any compliant TDECQ at the maximum launch. V. Related Work Optical datacenter networks reconfigure for aggregate traf-fic [31], [32] or training jobs [49], [17], or rotate through traffic-oblivious schedules [50], [51], and KV compres-sion [52], [53] loses to raw transfer above 50 to 110 Gb/s [54]. MoSE reallocates OCS ports per workload mode without modeling reconfiguration delay [16], xSwitch pairs a port-reduced EPS with an OCS for scale-up [55], a photonic CXL appliance replaces the P/D hop with a memory pool [56], and Opus names KV cache pipelines as an unevaluated target [12]. VI. Conclusion Carrying the KV cache of each migrated turn over an optical circuit committed during prefill hides the delay of a mechanical OCS and matches 400G packet fabrics with adaptive routing at 0.55 to 0.78 times the cost of a 2:1 fabric. Against the same cluster with a 2:1 or 3:1 packet fabric and ECMP, it raises agentic SLO attainment by 2.7 to 6.6 and 4.1 to 10.7 points in pull transfer. References
