# BSIPipe: Bidirectional Sequence-Level Interleaved Pipeline Parallelism for Long-Sequence Large Language Model Training

Abstract 

Pipeline parallelism has become a critical technique for train-ing large language models. To address the throughput limita-tion of existing sequence-level methods that schedule subse-quences unidirectionally, we propose bidirectional sequence-level interleaved pipeline parallelism (BSIPipe), which co-schedules two opposing V-shaped interleaved pipelines at the subsequence level. To bound the activation accumula-tion from subsequence scaling, BSIPipe introduces a basic-unit concatenation mechanism that keeps activation memory invariant to the subsequence count. To eliminate communi-cation deadlock risk from bidirectional scheduling, BSIPipe employs a communication optimization strategy that com-bines topology-aware device mapping with communication-computation overlap. In addition, BSIPipe composes with tensor parallelism to overcome the memory limitation of single-dimensional parallelism, neutralizing its bidirectional memory overhead while maintaining throughput. Experi-ments on GPT and LLaMA3 demonstrate that BSIPipe achieves average speedups of 1.11 × to 1.13 × in long-sequence training over SOTA sequence-level baselines. 

## Introduction 

The rapid advancement of Transformer-based large lan-guage models (LLMs), e.g., GPT and LLaMA (Tou-vron et al. 2023), has driven remarkable breakthroughs across various domains, including natural language pro-cessing (Ouyang et al. 2022), code generation (Roziere et al. 2023), and intelligent question answering (Chowd-hury and Soni 2025). As model scale grows, training these models on a single device has become practically infeasi-ble: GPT-3 (Brown et al. 2020), for instance, requires over 350 GB of memory at half precision and would demand ap-proximately 288 years to train on a single NVIDIA V100 GPU (Narayanan et al. 2021b). Beyond model scale, the growing need to capture long-range dependencies is driving training sequence lengths substantially higher, with state-of-the-art (SOTA) LLMs trained on sequences ranging from thousands to tens of thousands of tokens (Chen et al. 2023; Liu, Zaharia, and Abbeel 2024). The growth along these two dimensions jointly imposes severe demands on memory and computational throughput, making efficient distributed training indispensable for modern LLM development.

As a key parallelism paradigm in distributed training, pipeline parallelism (PP) (Guan et al. 2024) partitions a model into a sequence of stages across multiple devices. Each batch is then split into multiple micro-batches that tra-verse these stages in a pipelined manner. This design al-leviates the single-device memory bottleneck while over-lapping communication with computation, and has there-fore been widely adopted in LLM training (Liu et al. 2024; Hui et al. 2024). As sequence lengths grow, the compu-tation time per micro-batch and the device idle time (also called pipeline bubbles) increase correspondingly, hindering further throughput gains. To mitigate this, sequence-level pipeline parallelism (Li et al. 2021) further partitions each micro-batch into multiple subsequences, which serve as finer-grained schedulable units. This partitioning increases the number of subsequences traversing the pipeline, thereby reducing the bubble ratio. Recent studies further explore the 1F1B schedule and its interleaved variant to improve train-ing throughput (Ao et al. 2025); others investigate memory optimization through bubble insertion or recomputation with activation offloading (Sun et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025). How-ever, all these 1F1B-based methods introduce substantial pipeline bubbles during the warmup and cooldown phases due to strict inter-stage dependencies and periodic synchro-nization, reducing device utilization and limiting training ef-ficiency. To address this limitation, a natural approach is to in-troduce bidirectional scheduling, which co-schedules two opposing pipelines to better utilize idle devices. Despite its potential, integrating bidirectional scheduling with fine-grained sequence-level scheduling is non-trivial, posing three key challenges: (1) Activation accumulation from subsequence scaling. Under unidirectional 1F1B schedul-ing, forward and backward computations alternate, and activations can be released once their backward passes complete, so increasing the number of subsequences does not raise memory consumption. In contrast, bidirectional scheduling exhibits a forward-then-backward execution boundary: as the number of subsequences grows, forward activations persistently accumulate in memory until the backward phase begins, causing memory consumption to grow linearly with the number of subsequences. (2) Com-munication deadlock risk from bidirectional scheduling. 

Under unidirectional 1F1B scheduling, inter-device commu-arXiv:2610.14935v1 [cs.LG] 10 Oct 2026 nication has a fixed direction, and adjacent devices con-sistently maintain a well-ordered “one-send-one-receive” pattern. By contrast, bidirectional scheduling concurrently drives two pipelines in opposite directions, in which adja-cent devices may simultaneously issue send operations and thus fail to receive, triggering communication deadlocks. (3) Memory limitation of single-dimensional parallelism. Asingle parallelism dimension struggles to balance computa-tional efficiency and memory utilization, and bidirectional scheduling further amplifies memory pressure while boost-ing training throughput. Consequently, memory limitation remains a critical bottleneck for practical deployment. To this end, we propose BSIPipe , a Bidirectional 

Sequence-Level Interleaved Pipeline Parallelism. To the best of our knowledge, BSIPipe is the first work to introduce bidirectional scheduling into sequence-level pipeline paral-lelism. By co-scheduling two V-shaped interleaved pipelines running in opposite directions, BSIPipe achieves almost zero-bubble execution in the steady phase and substan-tially mitigates pipeline bubble overhead in the warmup and cooldown phases. The main contributions are as follows: • We propose a basic-unit concatenation mechanism that takes the schedule satisfying the minimum number of subsequences required for the warmup phase as a basic unit, which extends along the subsequence dimension by concatenating multiple basic units. By bounding activa-tion accumulation within a single basic unit, we alleviate memory pressure caused by the forward-then-backward execution pattern. • We propose a communication optimization strategy for bidirectional scheduling, which combines an early-receive mechanism to avoid deadlocks, topology-aware device mapping to confine gradient synchronization within intra-node high-bandwidth interconnects, and communication-computation overlap to improve training throughput. • We introduce tensor parallelism to construct a multi-dimensional parallelism strategy, which reduces per-device parameter and activation memory as the tensor parallelism degree increases. In addition, we further con-fine both gradient synchronization and tensor-parallel collective communication to intra-node high-bandwidth interconnects, reducing communication latency in multi-node training scenarios. • Extensive experiments show that BSIPipe achieves the highest training throughput on GPT and LLaMA3 in long-sequence scenarios, delivering average speedups of 1.11 ×–1.13 × over SOTA sequence-level methods. 

## Related Work 

Tensor Parallelism 

Tensor parallelism (TP) partitions compute-intensive op-erators across devices and synchronizes results via col-lective communication. General-purpose sharding frame-works (Lepikhin et al. 2020) support flexible partitioning schemes, while Megatron-LM (Narayanan et al. 2021b) establishes the canonical 1D TP scheme for Transformer layers. To reduce activation memory, Megatron-SP (Kor-thikanti et al. 2022) partitions inter-layer activations along the sequence dimension, while context parallelism (NVIDIA 2024), Ring Attention (Liu, Zaharia, and Abbeel 2024), and DeepSpeed-Ulysses (Jacobs et al. 2024) further parti-tion intra-layer attention activations for long-context train-ing. 2D TP schemes such as Optimus (Xu and You 2023) adapt classical parallel matrix-multiplication algorithms to 2D device layouts, reducing memory overhead over the 1D partitioning of Megatron-LM. Overlap techniques such as Flux (Chang et al. 2024) fuse tile-level collectives into matrix-multiplication kernels to hide their latency. These ad-vances establish TP as a mature and orthogonal parallelism dimension, complementary to PP for LLM training. 

Pipeline Parallelism 

Pipeline parallelism is broadly divided into synchronous and asynchronous paradigms. Synchronous methods dominate LLM training, which flush the pipeline each iteration to pre-serve strict optimizer semantics. GPipe (Huang et al. 2019) introduces micro-batch partitioning but lets activations ac-cumulate until the backward phase; DAPPLE (Fan et al. 2021) introduces the 1F1B schedule that releases activa-tions promptly and has since become the mainstream foun-dation; 1F1B-I (Narayanan et al. 2021b) splits each stage into smaller chunks distributed in an interleaved layout to further reduce the bubble ratio. These methods schedule micro-batches unidirectionally, leaving downstream devices idle during warmup and cooldown. To overcome this lim-itation, ZeroBubble (Qi et al. 2024) decomposes the back-ward pass into input-gradient and weight-gradient computa-tions to fill remaining bubbles, while Chimera (Li and Hoe-fler 2021) and its V-shaped extension BitPipe (Wu, Chen, and Yu 2024) co-schedule two opposing pipelines to oc-cupy these idle devices, at the cost of doubled parame-ter memory. All of these methods operate at the micro-batch level: as sequence length grows, pipeline bubbles grow correspondingly, motivating finer-grained schedul-ing along the sequence dimension. Asynchronous methods such as PipeDream (Narayanan et al. 2019), PipeDream-2BW (Narayanan et al. 2021a), and PipeMare (Yang et al. 2021) eliminate pipeline flushes for bubble-free execution at the cost of relaxed convergence guarantees, which lie out-side the scope of this work. 

Sequence-Level Pipeline Parallelism 

Sequence-level pipeline parallelism partitions each micro-batch into multiple subsequences as finer-grained schedula-ble units, reducing the bubble ratio in long-sequence train-ing. ## Methodology 

Bidirectional V-Shaped Subsequence Scheduling 

BSIPipe co-schedules two opposing V-shaped pipelines, mirrored along the device axis, to occupy downstream de-vices that would otherwise remain idle under unidirectional scheduling. As illustrated in Figure 1(d), the two pipelines launch subsequences from opposite ends of the device axis during warmup, run concurrently through the steady phase to achieve almost zero-bubble execution, and complete their cooldown in parallel from both ends. Within each pipeline, the majority of forward passes are executed before back-ward passes begin, forming a forward-then-backward execu-tion boundary. After all forward and backward passes com-plete, devices holding the same model chunk perform gradi-ent synchronization, followed by a parameter update. 

V-shaped chunk layout. Let w denote the pipeline depth and p the interleaving factor (the number of model chunks per device). The model is partitioned into pw chunks 

c0, . . . , c pw −1. For the standard configuration p = 2 il-lustrated in Figure 1(d), the down pipeline assigns chunks 

c0, . . . , c w−1 to devices 0, . . . , w − 1 in order and chunks 

cw, . . . , c 2w−1 to devices w − 1, . . . , 0 in reverse, forming a V-shaped layout; the up pipeline mirrors this layout. Unlike the sequential chunk assignment in Seq1F1B-I, the V-shape places one pair of adjacent chunks ( cw−1 and cw) within the same device at the V’s turning point, reducing the corre-sponding inter-device transfer to an intra-device data copy. 

Subsequence partitioning. BSIPipe partitions each micro-batch into k compute-balanced subsequences follow-ing Seq1F1B, so that all subsequences yield equal FLOPs and the inter-subsequence bubbles induced by the causal at-tention pattern are eliminated. To achieve the minimum bub-ble ratio, the total number of subsequences per iteration Ns

must be a multiple of kw , and the number of model layers L

must be a multiple of pw .

Basic-Unit Concatenation 

Since BSIPipe’s bubble ratio decreases as Ns grows, scal-ing the subsequence count is an attractive approach for fur-ther throughput improvements. This scaling, however, is constrained by BSIPipe’s forward-then-backward execution boundary. As illustrated in Figure 2(a), injecting all Ns sub-sequences during warmup causes forward activations to ac-cumulate in device memory until the backward phase begins, so per-device activation memory grows linearly with Ns and quickly exhausts GPU memory. To eliminate this linear growth without sacrificing scala-bility, BSIPipe concatenates multiple basic units . As illus-trated in Figure 1(d), a basic unit is the smallest BSIPipe schedule that fully utilizes all devices and contains kw sub-sequences. To support a target subsequence count Ns (a mul-tiple of kw ), BSIPipe concatenates Ns/kw basic units along the time axis. As illustrated in Figure 2(b), the warmup of each subsequent basic unit overlaps with the cooldown of the preceding unit, filling the cooldown bubbles with for-ward computation. Communication Optimization 

In bidirectional scheduling, the two opposing pipelines run concurrently and each device handles communication for both. The resulting cross-pipeline communication can lead to deadlock if left unoptimized. To eliminate this risk and further improve communication efficiency, we apply three optimizations to BSIPipe. 

Topology-aware device mapping. GPU clusters typi-cally exhibit high intra-node bandwidth (NVLink, PCIe) and lower inter-node bandwidth (Wu and Chen 2026). The dom-inant communication cost in BSIPipe comes from param-eter gradient synchronization between mirrored devices— those holding the same model chunk in the two opposing pipelines—while inter-stage activation transfers are much smaller in volume. BSIPipe co-locates each mirrored pair on the same node, confining gradient synchronization to intra-node high-bandwidth interconnects, while only activations cross node boundaries. This substantially improves multi-node scalability. 

Deadlock avoidance with early receive. The bidirec-tional schedule may cause two adjacent pipeline stages to simultaneously issue send operations and thus fail to re-ceive, resulting in a deadlock. BSIPipe enforces comple-mentary send/receive ordering between adjacent stages: one device follows “send-then-receive,” and its neighbor fol-lows “receive-then-send.” This pairing guarantees that one Data path  High bandwidth  GPU

The ordering also enables early receive —a device posts an asynchronous receive immedi-ately after finishing the preceding forward pass, rather than waiting for the next forward pass to begin—so the activation transfer overlaps with subsequent computation and reduces stall time once the data are required. 

Forward-communication and backward-computation overlap. Even with this send/receive ordering, one scenario still forces computation to wait for communication. Con-sider a device running a backward pass while its neighbor computes a forward activation that the device will need for its next forward pass. The “send-then-receive” constraint blocks the device from receiving the activation until the backward pass completes, delaying the next forward com-putation. To eliminate this wait, BSIPipe reverses the or-dering whenever a backward pass overlaps with a neigh-bor’s forward pass. The device executing the backward pass switches to “receive-then-send,” and its neighbor to “send-then-receive,” so the forward activation arrives during the ongoing backward computation. This eliminates the final blocking wait and maximizes communication-computation overlap. 

Multi-Dimensional Parallelism 

To further reduce per-device memory, BSIPipe composes with TP (Korthikanti et al. 2022) along an orthogonal di-mension. TP splits each Transformer layer’s parameters and activations across Dt devices, reducing per-device parame-ter and activation memory by a factor of Dt.Combining BSIPipe with TP introduces two high-frequency communication patterns. Within each layer, the TP group performs AllGather and ReduceScatter collec-tives (NVIDIA 2020). Across the bidirectional pipelines, the mirrored devices synchronize gradients. Both patterns must remain on intra-node high-bandwidth interconnects to avoid throughput collapse. BSIPipe co-locates both communication patterns within the same node. Each node hosts two TP groups (one from each pipeline) and the gradient-synchronization groups for their mirrored devices. Since both types of communication groups remain intra-node, TP collectives and bidirectional gradient synchronization both run on high-bandwidth inter-connects (e.g., NVLink). Only inter-stage activation trans-fers cross node via lower-bandwidth links (e.g., InfiniBand). As a result, the practical value of Dt is bounded by the num-ber of GPUs per node. Beyond that, TP collectives cross inter-node links and become the dominant bottleneck. As illustrated in Figure 3, Node 0 holds the two TP groups at the V-shape’s endpoints (TP groups 0 and 3), while Node 1 holds the two midpoint (TP groups 1 and 2). The dashed arrows trace the bidirectional data path, which traverses all four TP groups in opposite directions for the two pipelines. Under this combined parallelism, per-device parameter memory decreases from 2MΘ to 2MΘ/D t, neutralizing BSIPipe’s bidirectional memory overhead at Dt ≥ 2 while preserving its low bubble ratio. 

## Experiments 

Experimental Setup 

Hardware, software, and implementation. We conduct all experiments on a cluster of four servers (8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs per server, NVLink at 400 GB/s intra-node, 200 Gbps InfiniBand inter-node). The software stack consists of Py-Torch 2.3.0, Transformer-Engine 1.4.0, and CUDA 12.2 on Ubuntu 20.04. We implement BSIPipe within Megatron-LM (Narayanan et al. 2021b) and integrate all baselines into the same framework to ensure fair comparison. 

Models, dataset, and metrics. We train GPT-1.3B and LLaMA3-1.3B, each comprising 64 Transformer layers, a hidden size of 1280, and 32 attention heads. The dataset is OpenWebText (Peterson, Meylan, and Bourgin 2019), with sequence lengths ranging from 5K to 20K tokens and a micro-batch size of 1. We evaluate all approaches in terms of training throughput (tokens per second) and peak per-device memory usage (GiB). Following standard practice in pipeline parallelism (Guan et al. 2024), throughput serves as a practical proxy for the bubble ratio. Each configuration runs for 15 iterations, and we take the average throughput over the final two iterations.

Performance Comparison 

We evaluate BSIPipe along two scaling dimensions: se-quence length at a fixed GPU count (16 GPUs, 64 micro-batches), and GPU count at a fixed sequence length (20K, with the number of micro-batches scaled proportionally to the GPU count). 

Throughput. The throughput comparison results are shown in Table 2, from which we can derive the follow-ing observations: (1) Against the strongest sequence-level baselines Seq1F1B and Seq1F1B-I, BSIPipe attains average speedups of 1.12 × to 1.13 × on GPT and 1.11 × against both on LLaMA3, confirming the gain of bidirectional scheduling at the subsequence level. (2) BSIPipe consistently achieves the highest throughput at sequence lengths ≥10K, with speedups of 1.08 × to 1.11 × over the second-best method on GPT and 1.02 × to 1.10 × on LLaMA3. The advantage of BSIPipe over the second-best method generally grows with sequence length, as a larger number of subsequences amplifies the benefit of BSIPipe’s lower bubble ratio. (3) At 5K, BSIPipe trails Chimera by a small margin, as sub-sequence partitioning at such short sequences yields small per-subsequence tensor shapes that reduce GPU kernel effi-ciency and partially offset the gain from a lower bubble ra-tio. (4) At a sequence length of 20K, BSIPipe maintains the highest throughput from 8 to 32 GPUs, with the advantage peaking on 16 GPUs. On 32 GPUs, the deeper pipeline in-troduces more cross-stage communication and narrows the speedup margin, yet BSIPipe remains the leader, demon-strating robust scaling of the bidirectional V-shaped sched-ule across cluster sizes. 

Memory footprint. Figure 4 shows the memory foot-print distributions of BSIPipe and baselines, from which we can discern the following tendencies: (1) BSIPipe attains highly concentrated memory distributions across devices, varying within a tight range in every configuration. The reason is that the V-shaped stage assignment places sym-metric computational loads on the down and up pipelines, mitigating the memory imbalance across devices that arises under unidirectional scheduling. (2) On 16 GPUs at a se-quence length of 20K, BSIPipe reaches peak memory of 60.1 GiB on GPT and 67.9 GiB on LLaMA3, exceeding Seq1F1B-I by 24% and 27%, respectively, but staying be-low Seq1F1B and TeraPipe. The overhead over Seq1F1B-I arises from two sources. First, BSIPipe replicates the model across two pipelines for bidirectional execution, doubling per-device parameter memory. Second, even with the basic-unit concatenation mechanism, the forward-then-backward execution within each basic unit still accumulates activations during the forward phase until the backward phase begins. In contrast, Seq1F1B-I interleaves forward and backward in the steady phase and releases activations promptly.

## Conclusions 

In this paper, we propose BSIPipe, a bidirectional sequence-level interleaved pipeline parallelism for long-sequence LLM training. Extensive experiments on GPT and LLaMA3 across up to 32 GPUs show that BSIPipe achieves SOTA throughput with balanced memory distributions, demon-strating its scalability for large-scale distributed training. Furthermore, the comparison of parallel configurations shows that a suitable TP degree neutralizes BSIPipe’s bidi-rectional memory overhead while maintaining throughput. Ablation studies confirm that the communication optimiza-tions contribute positively to BSIPipe’s performance. References
