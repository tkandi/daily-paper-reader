Abstract 

Distributed learning with multiple local steps reduces communication but aggravates drift, and its practical performance often depends on carefully tuned hyperparameters under heterogeneous data and communication constraints. At the same time, recent Muon-type optimizers suggest that constraining updates within suitable norm balls provides a simple way to encode geometric priors into optimization. In this work, we propose NBCLT ( Norm-Ball Clipped Local Training), a unified local-training framework for both federated learning (FL) and decentralized learning (DL). NBCLT combines a predict-and-correct local update rule with Euclidean projections onto flexible norm balls, thereby performing norm-ball clipping under flexible update geometries while retaining a simple implementation. In FL, its problem-parameter-free regime achieves O(ϵ−2)communication complexity and O(ϵ−4) total stochastic-gradient complexity without assuming bounded heterogeneity, when the per-round local-computation budget is scaled appropriately with the horizon. In DL, NBCLT is compatible with both static and time-varying mixing and preserves these accuracy exponents under the analogous computation scaling, up to fixed network constants and an additive initialization cost.

Recent progress on problem-parameter-free optimization has reduced the burden of manual tuning, but a gap remains in both settings. In FL, methods such as PAdaMFed [36] and AdaMasFL [35] avoid prior knowledge of problem constants, yet their communication guarantees still fall short of the O(ϵ−2) benchmark achieved by well-tuned local training methods. In DL, the problem-parameter-free method D-NASA [16] does not support multiple local updates, which likewise leads to suboptimal communication efficiency. This raises a first question: 

Can a problem-parameter-free local-training algorithm match the O(1 /ϵ 2) communication complexity of well-tuned methods in both FL and DL, without sacrificing the benefits of multiple local updates? 

In addition to tuning the update scale, local training also depends on the geometry used to constrain local updates. We view a local update as lying in a norm budget D = {∆ : ∥∆∥g ≤ η},where the radius η sets the overall scale of motion and the norm ∥ · ∥ g determines the shape of admissible updates. This separates how far the algorithm moves from the geometry under which admissible updates are measured; Section 2 makes this distinction concrete through a quadratic example. This viewpoint is particularly relevant in distributed learning, where many clients may have highly heterogeneous local loss landscapes. Recent Muon-type optimizers make this geometric perspective explicit by constraining updates in carefully chosen norm balls [2, 10, 18, 24, 28]. Several recent works have begun integrating Muon into federated learning, but the current analyses remain 2limited to O(ϵ−3) communication complexity [19, 31, 38]. This leads to a second question: 

Can we design a distributed local-training algorithm that exploits flexible update geometry while retaining the communication and sample-complexity guarantees expected from efficient local methods? 

Motivated by these two questions, we propose NBCLT ( Norm-Ball Clipped Local Training). Our main contributions are as follows: • NBCLT provides a unified framework for both federated learning (FL) and decentralized learning (DL). It introduces two key local-update components: (i) Euclidean projection of tentative update variables onto flexible norm balls, i.e., norm-ball clipping under the chosen update geometry, and (ii) a novel predict-and-correct mechanism, conceptually adjacent to extragradient and optimistic ideas [22, 25], that stabilizes clipped local training. For spectral-norm matrix blocks, we instantiate this oracle by PolyGram-RCClip, a Gram-polynomial clipping projector. • In FL, we prove a rate of order T −1/2 + ( N KT )−1/4 plus lower-order terms without assuming bounded heterogeneity. With N K = Θ( T ) and N K ≤ T , this yields O(ϵ−2) communication and O(ϵ−4) total stochastic-gradient complexity for fixed problem/system constants. • In DL, to the best of our knowledge, NBCLT is the first problem-parameter-free algorithm that supports multiple local updates while remaining compatible with both static and time-varying gossip matrices. Under the analogous scaling M K = Θ( T ) with M K ≤ T , its accuracy exponents match those of well-tuned local-update baselines, up to fixed network constants and exact-initialization overhead.

3.3 NBCLT-DL 

NBCLT-DL is designed for decentralized learning over peer-to-peer networks, where nodes commu-nicate directly with their neighbors, and is summarized in Algorithm 2. Unlike classical gradient-tracking methods [34], NBCLT-DL follows a design philosophy similar to NBCLT-FL: each node maintains local control variates ˆ cti and cti, along with a local estimate of the global control variate, denoted by mti.At each communication round, every node first performs K local update steps. This is followed by a communication phase. In the absence of a central coordinator, local models and control variates cannot be directly averaged. Under FTC, one τ -round mixing block gives the exact average; the subsequent valid mixing sequence is indexed from t = 1. Both procedures ensure D1 = 0, and Assumption 2 gives B1 ≤ σ2 with one query per node. The overall counts are T + 2 h or T + τ communication rounds and KT + 1 gradient queries per node, hence M KT + M in total. These additive initialization costs do not change the accuracy exponents for a fixed network.

# E Experimental details and additional results 

E.1 Model architecture 

We use a CIFAR-style ResNet-18 for 32 × 32 inputs. The stem is a bias-free 3 × 3 convolution from 3 to 64 channels, followed by GroupNorm with 8 groups and a GELU activation; there is no ImageNet-style large stem or max-pooling layer. Each residual block contains two bias-free 3 × 3convolutions, GroupNorm after each convolution, GELU after the first normalization, and GELU after the residual addition. Downsampling blocks use a bias-free 1 × 1 convolution followed by GroupNorm. The four stages have widths 64 , 128 , 256 , 512, with two basic blocks per stage and strides 1 , 2, 2, 2. The network ends with adaptive average pooling and a bias-free linear classifier 

R512 → R10 . All GroupNorm layers keep the trainable scale parameter but remove the bias parameter. In the main NBCLT experiments, we use Scion initialization.

# F Limitations, Compute, and Societal Considerations 

Scale and comparison to Muon. The NBCLT update rule and the PolyGram-RCClip projector are not specific to federated aggregation and could in principle be instantiated as a centralized optimizer for large-scale neural-network training. This makes the method conceptually close in scope to Muon-type optimizers, while differing in the mathematical primitive: NBCLT clips tentative updates by projection, whereas Muon-type methods select LMO/orthogonalized directions. Our FL experiments include FedMuon as a federated Muon-type baseline and show favorable performance in that setting, but they do not replace a direct large-scale centralized comparison with Muon on language-model pretraining or other large neural-network benchmarks. Such comparisons are an important future direction. 

Empirical scope. The experiments evaluate CIFAR-10 federated learning with Dirichlet label-skew partitions and a CIFAR-style ResNet-18. The best run for each method is selected by average test accuracy over the final 100 communication rounds. Appendix E provides the training setup, hyperparameter grids, and implementation details. 

Choosing geometry at scale. NBCLT allows different update geometries for different parameter blocks, but the best norm choice for very large models is not yet understood. In particular, it remains open whether clipping-based flexible geometry can outperform LMO-based Muon geometry on large-scale language-model training, and which layerwise or blockwise norm choices are most effective in that regime. The current theory treats the chosen norm as fixed and analyzes the 63 resulting local-training dynamics; automatically selecting or adapting the geometry is beyond the scope of this work. 

Projector certification. The PolyGram-RCClip certificate requires a safe scale s ≥ ∥ Z∥2,a feasible polynomial with a certified fitting error, and an accumulated residual budget. The experiments use the deterministic safe-min scale in Appendix C.4, but the fixed-degree table implementation is a practical approximation; we do not certify its accumulated residual for arbitrary horizons. Heuristic scales without an upper-bound guarantee, table discretization, and floating-point effects also require additional verification. 

Compute and assets. The CIFAR-10 experiments were run on a workstation with four NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs. We use the public CIFAR-10 dataset and standard open-source deep-learning libraries; no new dataset or human-subject data are introduced. NBCLT is a general optimization method for distributed training. It may reduce communication and make distributed training more efficient, but it does not by itself provide privacy, security, fairness, or robustness guarantees. In particular, federated learning should not be interpreted as formal privacy protection unless combined with mechanisms such as differential privacy or secure aggregation. 64
