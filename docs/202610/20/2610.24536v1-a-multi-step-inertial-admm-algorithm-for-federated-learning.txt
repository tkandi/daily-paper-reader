Abstract 

Federated learning has recently emerged as an important paradigm for large-scale distributed model training, which can be naturally formulated as a class of distributed learning problems. The alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) is a widely used approach for solving such problems. However, its practical performance is often hindered by significant communication overhead and system heterogeneity. In this paper, we propose a multi-step inertial ADMM algorithm, called MiFedADMM, for federated learning. The proposed method leverages multiple historical iterates on the server side to construct a higher-order extrapolation after each aggregation to accelerate the convergence behavior. Under mild assumptions, we establish convergence results for the single-step variant of the proposed method and derive an O(1 /K ) convergence rate. Numerical experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and improved performance of the proposed method compared with existing approaches. 

Keywords: Federated optimization; Alternating direction method of multipliers; Inertial method; Multi-step scheme; Convergence analysis. 

# 1 Introduction 

Federated learning (FL) has become an important paradigm for distributed model training, where multiple clients collaboratively learn a global model under the coordination of a central server while keeping their local data locally [11, 12]. Such a paradigm can be naturally formulated as a class of distributed optimization problems. Owing to its privacy-preserving nature, FL has attracted considerable attention and has been widely applied in various areas such as mobile edge computing [17, 31, 33, 36], the Internet of Things [9], digital health [27], and vehicular networks [5, 25, 26, 28]. The typical FL training process, illustrated in Figure 1, consists of iterative communication between a central server and multiple clients. At every communication round (CR), each client first performs several local updates using its own dataset and uploads the updated parameters to the server. The server then aggregates these local parameters to produce a new global model and broadcasts it back to all clients. The above learning task can be formulated as the following problem. While this formulation preserves data privacy by avoiding the transmission of raw data, multiple CRs may incur significant communication overhead, which becomes a major bottleneck for large-scale FL. A foundational algorithm for FL is FedAvg [18], which aggregates local stochastic gradient descent (SGD) updates and has demonstrated strong empirical performance. However, under data heterogeneity (i.e., non-independent and identically distributed (non-i.i.d.) data across diverse clients), FedAvg suffers from client drift, where local models diverge toward their respective optima, thereby slowing down or even preventing convergence to a global optimum [3, 10, 14, 16, 19, 23]. To alleviate this issue, several methods have been proposed. For instance, FedProx [13] introduces a proximal term to penalize local deviations from the global model, though its performance is sensitive to the choice of the regularization parameter [30]. SCAFFOLD [10] employs control variates to reduce the variance of local updates and correct the client drift. Nevertheless, it incurs additional communication overhead by requiring the transmission of auxiliary variables, which can be prohibitive in communication-limited environments. These limitations suggest that existing approaches mainly address client drift by modifying local updates, without fully exploiting the consensus-based structure of distributed learning. Alternatively, the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) [2, 6, 7] has recently attracted increasing attention as an effective framework for distributed learning in FL, owing to its theoretical guaran-tees and robustness to data heterogeneity. In particular, the dual variables serve to correct the bias in local updates induced by non-i.i.d. data, while the quadratic penalty term enforces consensus among clients by balancing 2local objectives and global consistency. As a result, ADMM-based FL algorithms, including FedPD [35], 

FedADMM [37], and FedADMM-InSa [29], have demonstrated improved robustness to client drift compared with 

FedAvg .However, to accommodate system heterogeneity (e.g., varying computational capabilities across clients), most existing ADMM-based FL methods rely on a fully synchronous round-based update scheme, which enforces strict coordination across clients at every CR. While such strategies ensure stability, they significantly increase the number of required CRs, resulting in substantial communication overhead and slow practical convergence, especially under limited communication budgets. This limitation becomes more pronounced in large-scale heterogeneous FL systems, where synchronization delays can severely degrade overall efficiency. To overcome the above limitations, we introduce a multi-step inertial technique into the global updates of 

FedADMM , inspired by classical acceleration schemes [1, 22, 21] and recent developments in inertial algorithms [4, 15, 34]. Specifically, at each CR, after aggregating local updates, the server performs a multi-step extrapolation on the aggregated global model. The extrapolation direction is constructed using multiple past global iterates, enabling the method to exploit historical information for accelerating convergence. Importantly, the mechanism is implemented entirely on the server side, while keeping client-side update rules unchanged, incurring no additional communication cost per round, and posing no extra privacy risks, which makes it well-suited for practical heterogeneous FL scenarios. It is worth distinguishing our approach from momentum-based methods (e.g., FedNAG [32]). Momentum methods operate on gradients by accumulating past gradients and performing a single-step extrapolation. In contrast, our multi-step inertial strategy constructs extrapolation directions by leveraging differences be-tween successive past iterates, leading to a multi-step extrapolation mechanism that more effectively exploits historical information. Moreover, the proposed approach does not require additional gradient tracking or accumulation, making it fundamentally different from momentum-based schemes. More precisely, our main contributions are summarized as follows. a) Algorithm . We propose MiFedADMM , a novel ADMM-based FL algorithm that incorporates a multi-step inertial acceleration mechanism into the server-side updates. Specifically, after each global aggregation, the server performs a multi-step linear extrapolation based on differences of multiple past aggregated iterates. This fully server-side design introduces no additional communication overhead and requires no modification to client-side computations. b) Convergence Analysis . Under standard assumptions, including gradient Lipschitz continuity and coer-civity, we establish global convergence of the proposed algorithm to a stationary point. Moreover, we derive a sublinear convergence rate of O(1 /K ) in terms of the stationarity measure min s=1 ,...,K ∥∇ f (xτs )∥2.c) Experimental Validation . Extensive experiments on both synthetic datasets (linear regression) and real-world datasets (logistic regression) demonstrate that MiFedADMM significantly improves communica-tion efficiency by reducing the number of required CRs, outperforming several state-of-the-art methods, including FedADMM , FedAvg , FedProx , FedAlt , and FedSim [24]. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides necessary preliminaries. Section 3 presents the proposed algorithm and its key properties. In Section 4, we present the convergence results, includ-ing global convergence and the O(1 /K ) sublinear convergence rate of our algorithm. Numerical experiments and conclusions are presented in Sections 5 and 6, respectively.

# 4 Convergence analysis 

We establish the global convergence and convergence rate of MiFedADMM for the case of s = 1. In this setting, the inertial parameter set reduces to a single element a0,τ k+1 , which we denote simply as aτk+1 for notational simplicity. In addition to Assumptions 2.1 and 2.2, we introduce the following client selection scheme. 

Scheme 4.1. There exists a constant s0 ∈ N+ such that each client is selected at least once within any consecutive s0 CRs, i.e., for any k ≥ 0 and any client i ∈ [N ], there exists an index t ∈ { k + 1 , . . . , k + s0}

such that i ∈ N t.

Remark 4.1. Scheme 4.1 can be approximately satisfied under standard random sampling strategies. In particular, suppose that at each round, a subset N t is selected uniformly at random with a fixed participation rate ρ = |N t|/N , and the selections are independent across rounds. Then, for any client i ∈ [N ], the probability that it is selected at least once within s0 consecutive rounds is given by 

1 − (1 − ρ)s0 ,

which approaches 1 as s0 increases. Therefore, Scheme 4.1 can be viewed as a mild assumption that holds with high probability in practical implementations.

# 6 Conclusion 

We propose MiFedADMM , which extends FedADMM by incorporating a multi-step inertial technique during global updates to accelerate convergence and improve communication efficiency. For the single-step inertial variant, we establish global convergence to stationary points under mild assumptions and derive a non-ergodic O(1 /K )convergence rate. Numerical experiments on linear regression and logistic regression demonstrate that the proposed method significantly reduces the number of CRs required to achieve a desired accuracy, outperforming existing baselines. To fully realize the potential of MiFedADMM , future research should address two frontiers. The first is to extend the convergence analysis from the single-step to the full multi-step inertial scheme, providing complete theoretical guarantees for MiFedADMM . The second involves incorporating SGD into the local updates by replac-ing full-batch gradients, thereby significantly reducing the per-round computational cost and further enhancing overall efficiency. Establishing the convergence and convergence rate of this stochastic variant under non-i.i.d. data would be a valuable theoretical and practical contribution. 16 A Some basics
