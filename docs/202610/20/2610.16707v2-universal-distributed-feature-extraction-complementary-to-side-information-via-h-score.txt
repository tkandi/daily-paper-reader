Abstract—This paper considers a distributed estimation
system where edge nodes transmit averaged features to a
center node for parameter estimation. A key challenge
arises when the center node possesses auxiliary side
information: how to design local extractors that are uni-
versally optimal for any downstream task, while avoiding
redundancy with the side information. To tackle this
problem, we orthogonally decompose the joint function
space and demonstrate in the weak-dependence regime
that the modal decomposition of the canonical dependence
kernel’s conditional-additive component yields optimal,
task-independent features. We construct a distributed
nested H-score to learn this decomposition and show that,
under a limited feature budget, the mean-squared error
(MSE) gap to the best task-specific features is determined
solely by the task’s alignment with the discarded, least
informative modes, and vanishes to leading order for every
square-integrable task once the budget is sufficient.
Index Terms—distributed estimation, side information,
universal feature extraction, local information geometry,
nested H-score
I. Introduction
Distributed systems like integrated sensing and commu-
nication (ISAC) [1]–[3], IoT, and sensor networks typically
comprise edge nodes and a center node. Due to rate and
energy constraints, edge nodes transmit block averages of low-
dimensional features rather than raw observations. The center
node then estimates latent parameters, often aided by side
information unavailable at the edge.
For such systems, a primary question is how to design
local feature extraction mappings and central estimators. This
has long been studied in multi-terminal inference [4]–[6],
where local messages are optimized for a fixed task. However,
existing designs face two limitations. First, task-oriented de-
signs [7], [8], e.g., supervised learning with a mean-squared-
error (MSE) loss, are optimal only for the training task
and must be redesigned whenever the task changes. Second,
applying universal features [9], [10] directly is sub-optimal
with side information S: local features capturing information
already in S are redundant, wasting communication resources.

A universal feature-extraction scheme is thus desired that is
complementary to S and independent of the specific task.
Two further ingredients inform our design. On the physical
layer, computation over multiple-access channels [11] and
over-the-air aggregation [12], [13] directly deliver sums of
node-side preprocessings—an additive structure we formalize
at the level of statistical dependence; on the learning side,
the H-score objective underlying our approach originates from
the centralized Soft-HGR framework [14]. None of the above
lines, however, meets both requirements at once.
In this paper, we design such universal features from an
information-geometric perspective, building upon the feature
geometry framework [15]. Operating in the weak-dependence
regime, we first orthogonally decompose the joint function
space into a Markov subspace, a conditional subspace, and
an interaction subspace. By projecting the canonical depen-
dence kernel (CDK) onto them, we successfully isolate the
dependence that is neither captured by the side information
nor invisible to distributed implementations. We then show
that a distributed nested H-score simultaneously realizes this
subspace decomposition and the modal decomposition of the
conditional-additive component, whose left modes provide the
desired task-independent local features. Finally, we derive the
center-node estimator based on these features and characterize
its exact leading-order MSE gap to the best task-specific
features: the gap is determined by the task’s alignment with
the discarded modes and vanishes for every square-integrable
task once all modes are retained.

V. Conclusion
We studied universal local feature design for distributed es-
timation with side information at the center node. Under weak
dependence, a triple orthogonal decomposition shows that
only the conditional-additive component of the CDK is useful.
Its modal decomposition, learned by a distributed nested H-
score without knowledge of the task, yields features whose
MSE gap to the best task-specific features is determined by the
task’s alignment with the discarded modes and vanishes when
all modes are retained. We believe this offers a principled
basis for reusable, side-information-aware feature design in
distributed sensing.
