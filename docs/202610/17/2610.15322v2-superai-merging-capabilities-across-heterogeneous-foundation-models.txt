ABSTRACT 

Enabling the reuse and composition of knowledge and capabilities distributed across foundation models is an important step toward building scalable artificial intelligence (AI) systems. How-ever, architectural differences and incompatible representations make it difficult to transfer and in-tegrate these capabilities through a unified cross-model interface. To address this challenge, we propose SuperAI, a cross-architecture multi-model fusion framework that integrates complemen-tary capabilities from multiple source models into a host model through orthogonal alignment of low-dimensional task-relevant representations. Specifically, we first extract task-relevant represen-tations from the source models and the host model using shared anchor samples, and apply principal component analysis (PCA) to construct low-dimensional functional subspaces with the same di-mensionality. Next, we use orthogonal Procrustes mappings to align the source and host coordinate systems, establishing cross-model correspondences while preserving the internal geometry of each source subspace. Finally, we train a lightweight residual multilayer perceptron (MLP) with joint supervision on task outputs and source-model increments, learning input-dependent residual incre-ments that are injected into the host model for multi-model capability fusion. Experiments on three host models across 15 capability domains show that SuperAI achieves higher average domain scores than the evaluated cross-architecture fusion methods and better preserves general capabilities as the number of source models increases. Ablation studies further support the effectiveness of orthogonal alignment, nonlinear residual mapping, and joint supervision. 

1 Introduction 

Foundation models [Qwen et al., 2025, OLMo et al., 2024, Team et al., 2025, Shi et al., 2026a] develop complementary capabilities shaped by diverse training data, objectives, and architectures. These capabilities remain distributed across models [Ramtoula et al., 2026, Dou et al., 2026a, Wei et al., 2026, Shi et al., 2026b, Dou et al., 2026b, Li et al., 2026, Shi et al., 2026c], while acquiring new ones through further training is costly. Reusing and composing existing capabilities [Akiba et al., 2025] therefore offers an important path toward scalable artificial intelligence (AI) systems. Existing approaches [Wortsman et al., 2022, Ilharco et al., 2022, Yoshida et al., 2026] reuse capabilities through pa-rameter merging [Akiba et al., 2025], knowledge distillation [Ko et al., 2024], and multi-model collaboration [Wang et al., 2025]. Conventional parameter merging combines weights Yadav et al. [2023] or task vectors Ortiz-Jimenez et al. [2023] but generally requires compatible architectures and parameter structures. Knowledge distillation trans-fers information from source-model outputs or intermediate representations through additional host-model training, while routing and collaboration invoke different models according to the input and typically retain multiple models at deployment.

To accommodate architectural differences, cross-model fusion methods also use neuron match-ing [Xu et al., 2024], optimal transport [Cui et al., 2026, Imfeld et al., 2024], and representation alignment [Dery et al., 2026] to establish corre-spondences. However, as shown in Figure 1, integrating com-plementary information from multiple heteroge-neous source models into a common host repre-sentation space remains challenging, particularly when improving target-task performance must be balanced with retaining the host model’s existing capabilities. We hypothesize that heterogeneous models can learn structurally corresponding task representa-tions. Using shared anchor samples, we observe that these representations can be approximately aligned through orthogonal mappings in low-dimensional functional subspaces while preserving each source subspace’s geometry. However, alignment alone does not guarantee task improvements, as transferred information must also be compatible with downstream host computation. Building on this insight, we propose SuperAI, a cross-architecture multi-model capability fusion framework that in-tegrates complementary capabilities from multiple source models into a host model through orthogonal subspace alignment. Specifically, we first extract task-relevant representations from the source models and the host model us-ing shared anchor samples, and apply principal component analysis (PCA) to construct low-dimensional functional subspaces with the same dimensionality. We then use orthogonal Procrustes mappings to align their coordinate sys-tems. Finally, we train a lightweight residual multilayer perceptron (MLP) with joint supervision on task outputs and source-model increments to predict input-dependent residual increments, which are injected into the host model. This joint objective uses source-model information to guide residual learning while adapting the increments to the host model’s task objectives, connecting representation alignment to task-level capability fusion. Our main contributions are as follows: • We propose SuperAI, a cross-architecture multi-model capability fusion framework combining orthogonal subspace alignment with residual injection, providing a unified interface for reusing and composing comple-mentary capabilities from heterogeneous source models. • We identify approximately orthogonal cross-model correspondences between task-relevant representations. Ablations support the effectiveness of orthogonal alignment, nonlinear residual mapping, and joint supervi-sion on task outputs and source-model increments. • Across three host models and 15 capability domains, the proposed SuperAI outperforms the state-of-the-art (SOTA) fusion methods on average and better preserves general capabilities as the number of source models increases. 

2 Related Work 

Model Merging and Capability Fusion. Parameter-space merging integrates models [Wortsman et al., 2022, Ilharco et al., 2022] with compatible architectures through weight averaging or task-vector addition, as in model soups and task arithmetic. Subsequent methods reduce task interference through sign resolution, filtering, conditioning, or loss bal-ancing [Yadav et al., 2023, Qiu et al., 2026, Yoshida et al., 2026, Im et al., 2026], and improve efficiency through low-rank, orthogonal, or shared parameter structures [Crisostomi et al., 2026, Lee et al., 2026, Yang et al., 2026, Dhasade et al., 2026]. Beyond direct parameter merging, knowledge fusion transfers information through output distributions or token alignment [Wan et al., 2024, 2025], while neuron and component matching reduces representational mismatch before fusion [Ainsworth et al., 2022, Singh and Jaggi, 2020, Imfeld et al., 2024]. For cross-architecture fusion, Trans-port and Merge aligns activations and layers to guide weight fusion [Cui et al., 2026], whereas THESEUS [Rinaldi et al., 2026] combines functional matching with orthogonal Procrustes alignment to transfer task vectors across model widths. 

Cross-Model Representation Alignment. Representation comparison methods assess statistical similarity using SVCCA and CKA [Raghu et al., 2017, Kornblith et al., 2019], or functional compatibility through model stitch-ing [Lenc and Vedaldi, 2015, Bansal et al., 2021, Athanasiadis et al., 2025]. 4.2 Heterogeneous Multi-Source Merging Quantitative Comparison Across Host Models. To evaluate SuperAI across host architectures, we jointly fuse all 15 domain source models into each of three hosts, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B, and Pythia-2.8B, and compare the resulting models with THESEUS Rinaldi et al. [2026] and Transport and Merge Cui et al. [2026]. The results in Table 1 show that SuperAI achieves the highest average score for all three host models. Specifically, SuperAI improves the average scores of Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B, and Pythia-2.8B from 46.7, 56.2, and 22.9 to 54.2, 61.3, and 32.8, corresponding to gains of 7.5, 5.1, and 9.9 points, respectively. 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we introduced SuperAI, a framework for integrating multiple heterogeneous source models into a sin-gle host model through orthogonal subspace alignment and lightweight residual fusion. Our analysis reveals that task-relevant representations across different model architectures exhibit approximately orthogonal correspondences in low-dimensional functional subspaces, providing a shared geometric interface for cross-model capability transfer. Experiments across 15 capability domains and three host model families demonstrate that SuperAI consistently out-performs existing cross-architecture merging methods while better preserving the host model’s general capabilities. Further results across unseen datasets, model architectures, and parameter scales confirm the generalizability of this approach, while the scaling analysis shows that capability absorption is jointly constrained by model capacity and representational characteristics. Overall, our findings suggest that aligning and composing functional representations offers a promising alternative to parameter-level merging for modular and efficient multi-source integration.

A Domain-Specific Source Models, Datasets, and Metrics 

We describe the source model, merging-training data, and evaluation metric for each of the 15 capability domains. Training datasets refer to the data used in our merging procedure, rather than necessarily the original fine-tuning corpora of the source models. All reported metrics are higher-is-better. 

Science. We use ScienceQA-OFT-8B as the source model and ARC-Challenge Clark et al. [2018] for merging training and evaluation. The task consists of multiple-choice science questions. We report accuracy, measured by whether the predicted option matches the reference answer. 

Common Sense. We use CommonsenseQA-OFT-8B as the source model and CommonsenseQA Talmor et al. [2019] as the corresponding dataset. The task requires selecting the correct answer to questions involving everyday common-sense knowledge. The primary metric is multiple-choice accuracy. 

Social. We use Social-IQA-OFT-8B as the source model and the BIG-bench Social IQA Sap et al. [2019] task for merging training and evaluation. Questions concern people’s intentions, reactions, and likely consequences in social situations. We report multiple-choice accuracy. 

Math. We use NuminaMath-OFT-8B as the source model and SVAMP Patel et al. [2021] for merging training and evaluation. The task requires generating numeric answers to arithmetic word problems. We extract the final numeric value from the generated response and compare it with the reference using relative and absolute tolerances of 10 −4.The primary metric is numeric answer accuracy. 

Code. We use Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct Hui et al. [2024] as the source model and MBPP Austin et al. [2021] for merging training. We report Pass@1: a task is counted as solved only when a single generated program passes both the base and additional tests. 

Multilingual. We use MergeBench Llama-3.2-3B Multilingual He et al. [2026] as the source model and an eight-language subset of M-ARC Clark et al. [2018]. The task evaluates multiple-choice reasoning across languages. We report sample-averaged accuracy, pooling correct predictions over all evaluated examples rather than taking an un-weighted average of per-language accuracies. 

Safety. We use MergeBench Llama-3.2-3B Safety He et al. [2026] as the source model and PKU-SafeRLHF Ji et al. [2025] as the corresponding dataset. The task presents a user request and candidate responses, and asks the model to select the safer response. We report safer-response selection accuracy. This metric evaluates safety-related response selection rather than the safety of freely generated responses. 

Law. We use Saul-Instruct-v Colombo et al. [2024] as the source model. Merging training uses the local MMLU Law subset Hendrycks et al. [2020]. Both are formulated as multiple-choice legal questions. The primary metric is answer-selection accuracy. 

Finance/Business. We use Finance Llama-3.1-8B as the source model and FinQA Chen et al. [2022] for merging training and evaluation. The task requires answering questions based on financial information. Numeric answers are extracted and compared using relative and absolute tolerances of 10 −4. For nonnumeric references, we use case-insensitive reference-answer matching in the generated response. We report the fraction of answers judged correct under these rules. 

Astronomy. We use AstroSage-8B de Haan et al. [2025] as the source model and arXiv astro-ph text for merging training. Evaluation uses MMLU Astronomy multiple-choice questions. Shared anchor prompts are used to extract paired hidden representations from the source and host models, which are then normalized and projected into low-dimensional PCA subspaces. The resulting coordinates are aligned through an orthogonal mapping, and the alignment scores are subsequently used for source–host layer matching and shared host-layer selection. After alignment, the source representation is mapped into the host hidden space to construct a source-specific target feature increment, denoted by ∆s(ai). These increments provide representation-level supervision for the residual fusion module. During joint training, a lightweight residual MLP predicts input-dependent increments from the host hidden states, and the resulting update is injected into the selected host layer. The training objective combines task-output supervision with source-increment supervision, controlled by the corresponding loss weights. For each source model, the host-side normalization statistics, PCA mean, and PCA basis are fitted using the anchor prompts paired with that source model. Therefore, these quantities are technically source-dependent even when they belong to the host representation space. To simplify the notation, we omit the corresponding source index whenever it is unambiguous from the context. The same convention is used for other source-conditioned intermediate quantities where explicitly carrying the source index would unnecessarily complicate the presentation.

Considering the overall performance across all three host models, rank 128 achieves a solid balance between information preservation and training difficulty, and is therefore adopted as the default configuration.

E Limitation 

Our current evaluation focuses on offline benchmark-based capability fusion, where prompts and target tasks are pre-defined. We have not yet systematically evaluated SuperAI in interactive human–AI settings involving multi-turn dialogue, evolving user intent, or feedback-dependent behavior. While this does not affect the validity of the reported offline fusion results, interactive deployment may introduce additional challenges such as maintaining transferred capabilities consistently across dialogue turns and adapting residual updates to changing conversational context. Fu-ture work will extend SuperAI to interactive settings by incorporating conversation-level representations, turn-aware residual routing, and human-feedback-based evaluation to study how fused capabilities behave under dynamic user interactions. 

F More Discussion 

▷ Q1. The method uses ground-truth task labels to train the residual MLP. How can the authors distinguish capability transfer from ordinary supervised adaptation of the host model? 

We would like to clarify that SuperAI is not equivalent to standard supervised adaptation. The key distinction is that the residual MLP is jointly optimized with both the task loss and the source-derived increment supervision. As shown in Table 2, using only the task loss ( λinc = 0 ) consistently underperforms the joint objective across all three host models, while using only the increment loss improves representation matching but does not provide the same downstream gains. This suggests that the improvement does not arise from task supervision alone; rather, the source-derived increments provide an additional transfer signal that complements the task objective. The role of the task loss is to adapt the transferred source information to the downstream computation of the host model, which is necessary because representation correspondence alone does not guarantee functional compatibility. 19 SuperAI: Merging Capabilities Across Heterogeneous Foundation Models 

▷ Q2. The improvement over THESEUS and Transport-and-Merge is large. Could this simply be due to the additional trainable residual MLP rather than the proposed cross-model alignment mechanism? 

Our ablations indicate that the gain cannot be explained by the residual MLP alone. First, removing source-increment supervision and retaining the same task-trained residual module leads to substantially lower performance than the full joint objective. Second, Table 3 shows that, under the same downstream training setup, replacing PCA + orthogonal alignment with no alignment, PCA-only mapping, or unconstrained linear mapping consistently degrades performance across the three host architectures. Third, Table 4 shows that the nonlinear residual mapper further improves over a linear mapper, indicating that the alignment module and the host-adaptation module play complementary roles rather than one simply substituting for the other. Therefore, the empirical gains result from the combination of cross-model geometric alignment and host-compatible residual adaptation. 

▷ Q3. The paper reports very high post-alignment similarity. Could these scores merely reflect overfitting of the Procrustes mapping to paired anchor samples? 

The reported alignment scores are not computed on the same samples used to fit the mapping. As described in the Appendix, for every candidate source–host layer pair, the normalization, PCA projection, and orthogonal Procrustes mapping are fitted on Afit

▷ Q4. Table 1 shows that some domains degrade after multi-source fusion. For example, Code decreases from 39.7 to 28.3 on Llama-3.2-3B, from 55.6 to 44.2 on Qwen2.5-3B, and from 11.1 to 1.3 on Pythia-2.8B. How should these results be interpreted? 

The Code results reflect the trade-off inherent in joint multi-domain fusion rather than the overall effectiveness of capability integration. SuperAI optimizes a single host model across 15 heterogeneous domains, whose objectives and output formats differ substantially. In particular, code generation requires highly structured token-level generation, making its preferred representation updates different from those of many reasoning and classification domains. Con-sequently, optimizing the shared representation for broad multi-domain performance does not necessarily preserve the optimum of every individual domain. Importantly, the goal of this setting is joint capability composition rather than independent per-domain specialization, and SuperAI consistently improves the aggregate 15-domain performance across all three hosts. Future work will investigate domain-conditioned weighting or routing to further improve the balance among heterogeneous capabilities. 20
