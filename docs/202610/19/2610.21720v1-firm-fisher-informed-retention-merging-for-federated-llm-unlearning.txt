# FIRM: Fisher-Informed Retention Merging for Federated LLM Unlearning 

Reeyansh Shah Maram Hasan Subhasis Chaudhuri Biplab Banerjee 

Indian Institute of Technology Bombay 

shah.reeyansh@gmail.com 

Abstract 

Federated LLM unlearning addresses targeted knowledge removal when forget and retain data remain distributed across private clients and are inaccessible to the server. The resulting lo-cally trained adapters encode conflicting objec-tives, making their aggregation fundamentally different from conventional federated optimiza-tion. A key failure mode is magnitude explo-sion , whereby unnormalized cross-client aggre-gation increases the merged update magnitude and degrades retained utility under stronger un-learning. We propose FIRM (Fi sher-Informed 

Retention Merging), a single-round framework for stable and retention-aware server-side ag-gregation. FIRM uses client-local Fisher statis-tics as a unified sensitivity signal to construct a Fisher-normalized base merge and impose ex-plicit control over retain-sensitive parameters. Its second-order retention projection admits a unique minimum-distance solution, while the corresponding dual scales with the number of retain clients rather than the model dimen-sion. Experiments across WMDP, TOFU, and MUSE-HP using Zephyr-7B and Llama-3-8B show utility gains at comparable forgetting on WMDP and MUSE-HP, with metric-dependent trade-offs on TOFU. Controlled comparisons support benefits of the combined base merge and additional retention correction at selected operating points. 

1 Introduction 

The widespread deployment of large language mod-els (LLMs) has made selective knowledge removal practically important (Maini et al., 2024). De-ployed models may encode personal, copyrighted, or hazardous information that must be removed after training (Li et al., 2024). Regulatory frame-works such as the GDPR (European Parliament and Council of the European Union, 2016) and CCPA (State of California, 2018) further establish rights to erasure, while the cost of retraining billion-parameter models makes targeted post-hoc removal the only practical option in many settings. Despite this need, most LLM unlearning methods assume centralized access to both the data to be forgotten and representative retain data. A critical but under-studied setting is federated LLM unlearning , where deletion-related data re-main distributed across private clients and cannot be transferred to the model provider under local-ization constraints (Liu et al., 2021). Unlike cen-tralized unlearning, the server cannot jointly access forget and retain data to optimize or validate the resulting model. Unlike standard federated learn-ing, client objectives are not uniformly aligned to-ward minimizing a shared task loss. Forget clients produce updates that suppress targeted knowl-edge, whereas retain clients provide preservation-oriented updates and parameter-sensitivity informa-tion. The server must therefore arbitrate between destructive and protective signals without access to either underlying dataset (Romandini et al., 2025). Recent work has addressed this problem through server-side aggregation of locally trained adapters. FULM (Zhong et al., 2025), for example, applies TIES-based sign resolution (Yadav et al., 2023) within client groups, followed by unnormalized summation of the resulting domain-level centroids. Although this preserves data locality and avoids additional server-side training, the merged adapter can grow with heterogeneous contributions rather than remain calibrated to their collective impor-tance. We refer to this failure mode as magnitude explosion . As the aggregate update grows, it may suppress general capabilities together with targeted knowledge, causing retained-utility collapse. More-over, sign-based aggregation captures directional agreement but not the degree of parameter impor-tance to individual clients, and thus provides no explicit control over retain-side damage. Conventional adapter-merging methods do not directly resolve this problem. TIES (Yadav et al., 2023) and Fisher-weighted model merging (Matena arXiv:2610.21720v1 [cs.LG] 15 Oct 2026 and Raffel, 2022) are primarily designed to com-bine models optimized for compatible or coopera-tive objectives. Federated unlearning instead com-bines updates with opposing functional roles: a pa-rameter may be sensitive on both forget and retain data, while the corresponding task vectors favor opposing changes. A suitable aggregation mecha-nism must therefore control both the scale of the distributed update and its effect on retain-sensitive parameters. 

Our Approach. We introduce FIRM , a Fisher-informed framework for stable and retention-aware federated LLM unlearning. FIRM uses empirical Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) to quantify the parameter-wise importance of each client update and employs it as a common signal throughout aggregation. This allows the server to normalize heterogeneous unlearning contributions, thereby preventing magnitude explosion, while simultane-ously protecting parameters that are important to retain clients. Hence, FIRM jointly controls update scale and a retain-side perturbation proxy without requiring server-side access to either forget or re-tain data. 

Contributions. 

1. We identify magnitude explosion as a key failure mode in federated LLM unlearning, where unnormalized cross-client aggregation amplifies the merged update and degrades re-tained utility under stronger unlearning. 2. We propose FIRM , a single-round framework that uses client-local Fisher information to construct a Fisher-normalized base merge ,enabling stable server-side aggregation with-out access to forget or retain data. 3. We derive a second-order retention-constrained projection that bounds adiagonal-Fisher perturbation proxy. We estab-lish the uniqueness of its minimum-distance solution and obtain a dual formulation with one variable per retain constraint, enabling tractable server-side optimization. 

2 Related Work 

Machine Unlearning for LLMs. Early methods apply gradient ascent directly to increase the loss on forget examples (Jang et al., 2023), but can be unstable and degrade general utility. More robust formulations regularize the forget objective using retain data, including gradient difference (Liu et al., 2022a) and KL-divergence penalties (Maini et al., 2024). Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) sub-tracts a fine-tuned task vector from the pretrained model, whereas RMU (Li et al., 2024) redirects selected intermediate representations toward ran-dom targets. These methods assume centralized access to forget and retain data and therefore do not directly address distributed deletion requests under data-locality constraints. 

Adapter and Model Merging. The adoption of LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) has motivated methods for combining independently trained adapters. TIES-Merging (Yadav et al., 2023) mitigates interference through trimming, sign-conflict resolution, and se-lective merging, while KnOTS (Stoica et al., 2025) aligns adapters in a shared subspace using joint singular-value decomposition. Fisher-weighted merging (Matena and Raffel, 2022) estimates pa-rameter importance using diagonal Fisher infor-mation and combines model parameters through importance-weighted averaging, giving greater in-fluence to models with higher sensitivity at each co-ordinate. UniFLoW (Liang et al., 2026) addresses aggregation inconsistency in multimodal federated LoRA fine-tuning: averaging low-rank factors sep-arately generally differs from averaging their in-duced weight updates. It averages one factor and analytically reconstructs the other through regu-larized least squares. CrossEarth-Gate (Cao et al., 2026) uses empirical Fisher information for remote-sensing adaptation, aggregating parameter sensitiv-ities into module-level scores to periodically select which modules to train. FIRM applies Fisher sensi-tivities to a different objective: combining updates with opposing forget and retain roles. It couples coordinate-wise Fisher-normalized merging with a retention-constrained projection that explicitly bounds a Fisher-weighted perturbation proxy. 

Federated Unlearning. Earlier methods target con-ventional predictive models through selective re-construction (Liu et al., 2021), rapid retraining (Liu et al., 2022b), or class-discriminative activation pruning (Wang et al., 2022). FedOSD (Pan et al., 2025) addresses forget–retain gradient conflict dur-ing iterative federated unlearning by constructing an unlearning direction that avoids interference with remaining-client gradients. It relies on re-peated client-gradient access, whereas FIRM oper-ates in an LLM adapter setting where each client transmits one task vector and Fisher summary for training-free server-side aggregation. Recent work extends federated unlearning to LLMs. Oblivionis (Zhang et al., 2026) evaluates federated LLM fine-tuning and targeted unlearning within an iterative framework, evaluating combi-nations of federated optimizers and local unlearn-ing objectives. FULM (Zhong et al., 2025), the closest protocol-compatible framework, aggregates locally trained unlearning and retention adapters through hierarchical TIES merging followed by inter-group summation. However, its aggregation is unnormalized and provides no explicit retain-side constraint. FIRM addresses this aggregation gap through a single-round, Fisher-normalized merge and a retention-constrained projection, without server-side access to forget or retain data.

4 Method 

FIRM follows a single-round protocol: the server broadcasts the pretrained model, clients perform local adaptation and transmit one summary, and the server computes the final model without further participation. A retention-constrained projection then limits updates along retain-sensitive parame-ters. Clients may assume forget or retain roles in different rounds; each client has a single role per round. The following guarantees characterize the exact optimum of the per-tensor program, rather than every numerical solver output: 

Proposition 1 (Proxy Retention Bound) . The op-timal solution ∆Θ∗ of the second-order primal program (7) satisfies bk(∆ Θ∗) ≤ ϵ for all retain clients k = 1 , . . . , m .

Proposition 2 (Minimal Modification) . ∆Θ∗ is the unique ℓ2-closest tensor update to ∆Θbase sat-isfying all m retention-proxy constraints. 

Proposition 3 (Scalable Dual Solution) . The dual of problem (7) has m variables per tensor, indepen-dent of its dimension d. Strong duality holds for 

ϵ > 0; projected gradient ascent with an appropri-ate step size recovers the dual optimum. Evaluation cost still depends on d.

5.1 Setup Models. Primary experiments use Zephyr-7B-

β (Tunstall et al., 2023), a 7B-parameter instruction-tuned model based on Mistral-7B, loaded in 4-bit NF4 quantisation (Dettmers et al., 2023). Robustness experiments use Llama-3-8B-Instruct (Meta AI, 2024) on the MUSE-HP bench-mark. We use LoRA rank r = 16 with scaling coefficient αLoRA = 32 throughout. Fisher di-agonals are computed from a separately loaded bfloat16 copy to estimate sensitivities without 4-bit quantisation (Appendix C). 

WMDP Setup (Zephyr-7B). We use six forget clients: three hold disjoint shards of WMDP-Biology (1,273 questions, ≈424 per client), and three hold disjoint shards of WMDP-Cybersecurity (1,987 questions, ≈662 per client). A single re-tain client trains on a random 600-sample subset of MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021). Forget adapters use an RMU-style representation-misdirection ob-jective (Li et al., 2024), updating down-projection weights in layers 5–7. Forget clients optimize the forgetting term; retention is handled by the separate retain client and server merge. 

TOFU Setup (Zephyr-7B). The model is first fine-tuned on the full TOFU dataset (200 fictional au-thors, 4,000 QA pairs) to establish a knowledgeable baseline. Five forget clients each hold a disjoint shard of the forget01 split (40 QA pairs, 2 authors total). A single retain client trains on 200 sam-ples from the retain90 split. Forget adapters use gradient ascent on all linear layers. 

MUSE-HP Setup (Llama-3-8B). Three forget clients each train on a disjoint shard of 1,676 passages ( ≈4,096 chars each) obtained from the MUSE-Books raw splits (Shi et al., 2024). Forget evaluation: 100 KnowMem QA pairs from the for-get_qa split, measured by ROUGE-L, while Utility is evaluated on MMLU (14,042 questions). Forget adapters use gradient ascent on all linear layers. 

Baselines. AVG : uniform mean of all task vec-tors. SUM : direct summation of forget task vectors. 

TIES (Yadav et al., 2023): sign-vote merging of forget updates. FULM (Zhong et al., 2025): hierar-chical TIES intra-cluster, summation inter-cluster. All baselines use identical training conditions and hardware. 7 Conclusion 

We introduced FIRM , a single-round framework for federated LLM unlearning that combines lo-cal forget and retain updates without server access to client data. FIRM couples Fisher-normalized merging with retention control based on a diagonal-Fisher perturbation proxy. The per-tensor projec-tion formulation has a unique minimum-distance optimum and an m-variable dual. Experiments across two model families show utility gains at comparable forgetting on WMDP and MUSE-HP, with metric-dependent trade-offs on TOFU. Scale-tuned comparisons support benefits of the com-bined Fisher-based design beyond global rescaling, while matched-forgetting comparisons demonstrate additional benefits from retention correction at se-lected operating points. Future work will examine larger models, mixed-role clients, broader hetero-geneity, and the relationship between Fisher-proxy control and actual retention. 

Limitations 

FIRM constrains a second-order empirical Fisher proxy rather than the exact retain-loss change. Al-though this provides non-negative, parameter-wise retention control, its fidelity depends on how well the diagonal empirical Fisher represents the local retain-loss curvature. Establishing tighter bounds between the proxy and the true loss change remains an important theoretical direction. Our evaluation covers two model families across WMDP, TOFU, and MUSE-HP, including cross-model tests on WMDP and MUSE-HP. Validation beyond the 8B scale, on additional model families, and under broader client and domain heterogeneity remains future work. The current protocol assigns each client a single role for a given request, either contributing a forget update or a retain summary. Extending FIRM to mixed-role clients that simultaneously hold both deletion-requested and retained data is left for fu-ture work. Our protocol assumes a trusted central server and honest-but-curious clients. Robustness to ma-nipulated Fisher statistics, poisoned task vectors, client dropout, and fully decentralized aggregation remains open.

C Implementation Details 

Fisher Computation. Fisher diagonals are com-puted at pretrained reference weights θF on a fresh bfloat16 model copy, avoiding 4-bit NF4 quantisa-tion noise. For WMDP and MUSE-HP, this is also the client initialization θ0. For TOFU, client train-ing starts from the expert-adapted model, while Fisher estimation loads the original backbone with-out that expert adapter. The Fisher batch size is one sequence, with 20 batches per client and gradients reset before each backward pass. Each sequence uses the causal-LM loss averaged over non-padding target tokens. Its gradient is squared element-wise before averaging across the 20 batches: bFc(j) =

Sequences are truncated to 512 tokens; batches follow the supplied data or-der, cycling if fewer than 20 examples are available. Only entries aligned with adapter task-vector keys are used during merging. 

Server-Side Fisher Processing. The server first averages raw Fisher tensors within each forget do-main. It then divides each pooled forget tensor and each separate retain tensor by its own maxi-mum plus ϵ0, and applies the Top-αd threshold in Eq. (3). Top-αd keeps values at least as large as the 

max(1 , ⌊αd ⌋)-th largest entry, including ties. Pool-ing precedes both normalization and sparsification; these operations do not generally commute. Multi-retain experiments disable retain-client grouping to preserve separate constraints.

C.1 Efficiency Analysis 

We instrumented one complete WMDP client run and the server-side pipeline. Table 13 reports the corresponding runtime, memory, and communica-tion costs. 

Computational Cost. FIRM introduces a one-time client-side Fisher estimation step after local adapter training. With 20 Fisher batches, this is the dominant client-side cost, but it is performed inde-pendently and can run in parallel across clients. Server-side computation remains comparatively light: the FIRM merge takes 217 s, compared with 547 s for FULM, while the constrained dual pro-jection adds only 1.2 s. Thus, FIRM’s additional optimization cost is concentrated in local Fisher estimation rather than the server-side projection. 

Memory Cost. Local adapter training requires 19.7 GB of peak GPU memory, whereas the Fisher pass reaches 38.4 GB because the measurement retains the 4-bit training model while loading a separate bf16 copy for gradient-stable Fisher esti-mation. An isolated Fisher pass was not measured. The server operates only on transmitted task vec-tors and Fisher summaries and therefore requires no access to client data or full client models. 

Communication Cost. Each client sends a task vector and raw Fisher summary once; server-side projection needs no further communica-tion. The profiled task-vector artifact occupies 336 MiB. The original full Fisher checkpoint oc-cupies 13,562 MiB; restricting its bf16 values to task-vector support gives a derived dense payload of 336 MiB. These are artifact sizes and payload estimates, not measured network traffic. Top-10% sparsification occurs after server-side pooling and therefore does not reduce the evaluated upload pay-load. 

Compute Budget. NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (96 GB VRAM).

D.2 FULM with Inter-Cluster Averaging 

Using the same regenerated checkpoints and evaluation sets, we preserve FULM’s similarity-based clustering (threshold 0.6) and intra-cluster TIES (Top-30%), replacing only inter-cluster SUM with MEAN. The three clusters con-tain the Bio clients, Cyber clients, and retain client, respectively; all three centroids enter the mean. We evaluate seven scales, γ ∈{0.30 , 0.35 , 0.40 , 0.45 , 0.50 , 0.55 , 0.60 }. Each is matched to the 17 FIRM-base settings by minimiz-ing the maximum absolute Bio/Cyber gap, break-ing ties by the sum of gaps and then lower reference 

γ, without using MMLU. The 0.35/0.60 pair in Ta-ble 6 is the only base comparison with both gaps at most 0.5 percentage points. Its 0.612-point utility gain supports a local benefit over FULM-MEAN; the other pairs have larger residual gaps. For fixed centroids, MEAN at γ equals SUM at γ/ 3 in real arithmetic, so averaging itself changes scale rather than update direction. 

D.3 Support- and Norm-Matched Weighting Control 

A separate 20-evaluation control compares con-tinuous Fisher weights with uniform weights on identical Fisher-selected Top-10% masks, preserv-ing clients, task vectors, and forget/retain aggre-gation and denominator normalization. Neither method uses QP. The non-Fisher update is globally rescaled to match the Fisher update’s ℓ2 norm; both use γ = 0 .5, 0.6, . . . , 1.4. Selection-mask mis-matches are zero, and the maximum relative norm mismatch after BF16 rounding is 0.00212%. At 

γ = 1 .1, Bio accuracy is identical (42.97%), while Fisher/uniform Cyber accuracy is 32.76/32.56% and MMLU is 49.76/49.94%. Fisher has localized utility gains at nearest Cyber matches, but these do not also match Bio; neither method dominates the observed frontiers. This control does not estab-lish a consistent advantage for continuous Fisher weighting alone. It is distinct from dense averaging, which also changes sparse support.
