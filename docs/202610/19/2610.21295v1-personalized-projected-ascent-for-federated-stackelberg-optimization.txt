## Abstract 

Federated minimax problems, such as distri-butionally robust optimization and adversar-ial training, include an inner maximization variable built from each client’s local data. These variables differ in meaning and dimen-sion from one client to the next, so averaging them across clients, as standard federated min-imax methods do, is ill-posed. We therefore study a federated minimax problem where the minimization variable x is shared and aggre-gated, while each client retains the maximiza-tion variable yi (personalized). Because these applications constrain yi to a compact convex set, we analyze projected ascent directly. We show that, in the nonconvex-concave regime, this projected personalized scheme attains the standard sublinear rate. In the nonconvex-PL regime, however, it hits a stepsize-invariant noise floor due to stochastic yi updates. To mitigate this, we propose VR-SGDA, incorpo-rating a variance-reduced ascent step where each client maintains a private variable snap-shot. By evaluating current and snapshot gradients on the same sample, the constant variance is replaced by a vanishing drift un-der a two-timescale regime. VR-SGDA elimi-nates the noise floor, attaining sublinear lead-ing rates while preserving personalization and projection. Experiments on subpopulation-robustness and NLP adversarial training con-firm both the worst-group robustness benefits and the two-timescale tracking mechanism. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Federated Learning (FL) [17] is a distributed learning framework to train a shared model across clients whose data cannot be centralized. In many of the settings where this matters most, the goal is not merely good av-erage performance but robust performance: the learner must account for worst-case distribution shifts in the data. Two canonical instances are distributionally ro-bust optimization (DRO), where one optimizes against the worst reweighting of the data within an ambiguity set [1, 12, 19], and adversarial training, where one op-timizes against worst-case input perturbations [16, 30]. Both are naturally minimax problems: an outer min-imization over the model and an inner maximization that constructs the worst case. Bringing either into the federated setting therefore requires solving a federated minimax problem over heterogeneous clients. The key feature of these problems, and the one that has been overlooked, is that the inner maximization variable is associated with the client’s own data : in federated DRO it is a reweighting pi ∈ ∆mi of client 

i’s local samples; in federated adversarial training it is a perturbation of client i’s local inputs. These vari-ables live in client-specific spaces, ∆mi vs. ∆mj when clients hold different sample counts, with client-specific feasible sets, so aggregating them at the server is not a meaningful operation. Existing federated minimax methods nonetheless maintain a single global inner vari-able and average it at the server [6, 7]; while one could define aggregation rules, the resulting shared variable would not correspond to the per-client robust objec-tive that DRO and adversarial training are designed to optimize. inner ascent, not for the cross-client reconciliation that motivated snapshot devices in prior work [6]. To eliminate this floor, we propose Personalized VR-SGDA, which uses model snapshots at synchronization boundaries as per-client control variates to remove inner-gradient variance and restore convergence to a true stationary point. • We instantiate both use cases concretely, the DRO and adversarial training, and confirm the predicted behavior empirically. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Convergence theory for minimax problems is well under-stood in the convex-concave regime, first-order primal-dual and mirror-prox methods achieve sub-linear rates [9, 20, 21], and in the strongly-convex–strongly-concave regime, gradient descent-ascent and its variants con-verge linearly [18]. Our setting sits outside this spec-trum on every axis: the outer problem is nonconvex, the inner problem is only concave or PL, the feasible set is constrained, and the whole is solved in a federated, personalized configuration. The federated minimax literature that does address nonconvex outer problems inherits further assumptions we discard. We organize it along four axes: what the maximization variable is (a single global object or per-client); whether the analysis is tied to a specific adversary or application M. Yashwanth, S. to control the inner update. Deng and Mahdavi [6] establish convergence of Local SGDA in the federated nonconvex-concave setting, with a single global maximization variable y averaged at the server. Their analysis controls an inner-variable con-sensus error from averaging y across clients, a quantity with no analogue in our setting, where yi is per-client and never communicated. To handle the federated non-convex regime, they introduce Local SGDA +, which uses a snapshot of the outer variable inside the inner up-date to decouple the inner-maximization analysis from the moving local iterates. Sharma et al. [28, 29] extend this line to broader nonconvex-nonconcave regimes, again with an unconstrained shared maximization vari-able and a snapshot-style algorithm. The closest application precedent is (DRFA) Deng et al. [7], also about federated DRO but with a fundamen-tally different configuration. ## 3 METHOD 

Motivation for Stackelberg: The minimax problem ad-mits two solution concepts. The symmetric Nash equi-librium guarantees existence and order-invariance in convex-concave settings, but fails under non-convexity: pure equilibria often do not exist, and computing ap-proximate local min-max points is PPAD-complete [4]. Therefore, the asymmetric Stackelberg equilib-rium, where the leader commits to x and followers best-respond with Y is adopted [15]. This matches our sequential objective, guarantees well-posedness via our compact Yi assumption, and serves as the proper local optimality notion for nonconvex problems by respecting the inherent min − max asymmetry.

Scope: We currently consider full participation setting and extending this personalized framework to partial client participation introduces challenges centered on asynchronous primal-dual coupling. The issue is that when a dormant client is eventually re-sampled, its local ascent step must be evaluated using a severely stale inner variable yt−di against a global model xt that has drifted across the network for d communication rounds. This mismatch potentially breaks the tracking bounds established in the current analysis.

## 6 CONCLUSION 

We introduce a practical framework for federated mini-max problems by keeping the maximization variables personalized to each client and using projected ascent. This approach succeeds in standard nonconvex-concave settings without requiring snapshot mechanism. In the nonconvex-pl regime, the standard ascent projection step creates a persistent noise floor, a baseline level of error that cannot be fixed simply by tuning the learn-ing rate. We empirically demonstrate that this is a limitation of the algorithm. To fix this, we introduce Personalized VR-SGDA. By using local snapshots of the variables, our method smoothly phases out this constant noise and restores the optimal convergence speed. Finally, this solution works perfectly when all clients participate. In the partial participation setting when inactive clients are sampled, their outdated local variables severely clash with the newly updated global model, potentially breaking the current techniques and the extension remains open.

The block-structured inner analysis of Lin et al. [14, 15], in which the inner variable is updated through 

τ consecutive steps with intra-block drift controlled by stepsize separation, was originally introduced as an algorithmic batching device in the centralized setting; the parameter τ had no operational meaning beyond internal accounting. We observe that the same algebraic structure admits a natural federated reinterpretation: the block boundary is precisely a synchronization point, and τ is the communication gap, the number of local steps between successive server-synchronizations. Under this reinterpretation, the algebra (single-client drift over an uninterrupted local trajectory) becomes a direct statement about federated communication architecture, and the centralized two-timescale guarantee transfers additively, with the bound reducing exactly to its centralized form at τ = 1 (full synchronization). The contribution is not in the intra-block analysis itself, which is Lin’s, but in identifying that Lin’s block primitive is operationally a communication-interval primitive, a reinterpretation that, combined with the personalized maximization variable, removes the consensus-error machinery prior federated minimax analyses required.
