Abstract —Minimizing the wall-clock time required to reach a target accuracy is crucial in federated learning (FL), where many applications require frequent model updates as data distributions evolve over time. Nevertheless, in heterogeneous environments, slow clients with limited computational and communication capabilities can delay global synchronization, forcing faster clients to wait and increasing the overall training time. To mitigate this straggler effect, recent studies have in-troduced per-round time constraints and adapted client work-loads within the available time. However, these approaches primarily focus on local computation, whereas communication can become a major bottleneck in communication-limited environments, motivating the joint optimization of training workload and communication compression. Although previous studies have explored such joint optimization, determining an effective training–communication balance under a fixed per-round time budget remains challenging, as the resulting model performance depends on both training progress and compression-induced degradation. We therefore propose TAL-LOP, which jointly optimizes the local training workload and communication compression under per-round time constraints. TALLOP predicts the training loss achievable under different computation budgets from the early-stage mini-batch loss trajectory (LENS), and estimates the degradation caused by communication compression using a compression-error model (CLEN). TALLOP combines these predictions to select the training-communication allocation that minimizes the post-compression loss under the given round time constraint. Exper-iments in heterogeneous FL environments show that TALLOP generally achieves competitive or improved time-to-accuracy compared with approaches that optimize only local training or neither, although the benefit over training-only adaptation depends on the setting.

## 1. Introduction 

Federated learning (FL) [1] enables collaborative model training while preserving data privacy by keeping training data on edge devices. In many practical applications, such as personalized recommendation [2] and keyboard predic-tion [3], data distributions continuously evolve over time, requiring frequent model updates. Therefore, reducing the wall-clock time to reach a target model accuracy is an important objective alongside merely improving accuracy. This is particularly challenging under device heterogene-ity, where differences in computational capability and com-munication bandwidth cause substantial variation in local training and model transmission time across clients. Under conventional synchronous FL, the duration of each commu-nication round is determined by the slowest client, causing faster clients to remain idle while waiting for synchroniza-tion. This problem, known as the straggler effect [4], can increase the time required to reach a target model accuracy. To alleviate this problem, recent studies assign a fixed time budget to each communication round and adjust each client’s training workload so that more clients can com-plete training within the deadline [5]–[9]. However, these approaches primarily focus on adapting the local training workload and implicitly assume that communication occu-pies only a small fraction of each round. This assumption no longer holds in communication-limited environments, such as wireless edge networks, where transmitting model updates can take as long as, or even longer than, local training. In such environments, communication compres-sion [10], [11] is as important as adaptive training workload for achieving time-efficient training. Consequently, the per-round time budget creates a trade-off between local training and communication, as shown in Fig. 1. While allocating more time to training enhances the training quality, shorter communication time requires a higher compression ratio, resulting in greater information loss in model transmission. Existing methods address only part of this problem. Deadline-aware FL methods optimize the local training workload, such as training steps [7], [8] or trained sam-ples [5]. However, they typically focus on computation, ignoring communication time. Meanwhile, some studies address the joint optimization of local training and com-munication compression in FL [12], [13]. In particular, FedLamp [13] optimizes local updating frequencies and compression ratios for heterogeneous clients based on con-vergence bounds. However, it does not directly optimize the training-communication time allocation under a fixed per-round time based on the predicted loss. Consequently, prediction-based joint optimization of lo-cal training and communication compression under fixed per-round deadlines remains insufficiently explored, partic-ularly in heterogeneous and communication-limited envi-ronments. TALLOP predicts the model loss achievable under different training-communication time allocations and selects the allocation expected to minimize the post-compression loss. To enable this prediction, we introduce two key components. LENS 

predicts the model loss after future local training by extrap-olating the mini-batch loss trajectory, while CLEN estimates the additional loss caused by communication compression using a lightweight compression-error model. By combining these two components, TALLOP efficiently evaluates can-didate training-communication time allocations during local training. Experiments demonstrate that (1) jointly optimizing the training workload and communication compression gen-erally provides competitive or improved time-to-accuracy compared with optimizing either or neither, and (2) the computational overhead of TALLOP is equivalent to the cost of training on only a few samples, resulting in a negligible increase in the overall training time. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Time-to-Accuracy Optimization under Device Heterogeneity in FL 

In FL, improving time-to-accuracy under device hetero-geneity is challenging because a small number of stragglers can significantly delay global synchronization [4]. Existing approaches can be broadly classified into two categories. One approach is asynchronous FL [14]–[16], which removes synchronization barriers by allowing clients to upload model updates independently. This alleviates the straggler problem but introduces the stale update problem, in which outdated model updates may degrade convergence. Moreover, it is incompatible with mechanisms that rely on synchronous rounds, such as secure aggregation. In contrast, another line of work retains synchronous aggregation while adapting the local training workload ac-cording to each client’s computational capability. By as-signing more local computation to fast clients and less to slow ones, these methods reduce idle waiting time while preventing slow devices from becoming stragglers. This idea has been explored by adapting various aspects of local training, including the number of local epochs [17], [18], batch sizes [19], [20], and parameters updated during local training [21], [22]. This idea can be further enhanced by introducing a fixed deadline for each communication round [23] and adapting the local workload so that clients can complete their computation and model upload within the available time. FedBalancer [5], for example, jointly determines the round deadline and training sample selection, while Pyra-midFL [6], TimelyFL [8], and FedState [7] personalize the amount of local computation, such as training iterations, epochs, updated parameters, or training samples. These methods improve time-to-accuracy by making better use of heterogeneous computation capabilities, but generally focus on local computation and do not explicitly optimize communication time. This limitation becomes important in communication-limited environments, where model transmission can occupy a substantial fraction of the round time. In such settings, the communication compression ratio also determines how much local training can be performed within a fixed time budget: stronger compression can shorten communication but may introduce greater information loss. Thus, train-ing workload and communication compression are cou-pled through the per-round time constraint, motivating ap-proaches that jointly optimize the two. 

2.2. Joint Optimization of Training and Communi-cation in FL 

Meanwhile, several studies have jointly optimized the local training workload and communication cost to improve training efficiency. For example, Wang and Joshi [24], [25] optimized the model synchronization interval, while Han et al. [26] adaptively adjusted the communication compression ratio to minimize training time. AdaQuantFL [27] optimized the quantization level based on convergence analysis under a communication budget, and FFL [12] jointly optimized the local training workload and communication sparsity through theoretical convergence analysis. Most closely related to our work, Xu et al. proposed FedLamp [13], which jointly optimizes local updating fre-quencies and model compression ratios for heterogeneous clients based on a theoretical convergence bound. However, convergence-bound-based optimization does not directly estimate the actual learning progress achieved by different training-communication allocations within a fixed round deadline. In heterogeneous and communication-limited environments, the effectiveness of each allocation can vary across clients and training rounds, depending on both system capabilities and the current learning behavior. Therefore, a prediction-based approach that estimates the loss after local training and compression is needed to select an effective allocation under a given time budget. Such an approach could better utilize the available round time and improve time-to-accuracy.

Since each evaluation requires model inference on a suf-ficiently large number of samples, repeating this process in-curs substantial computational overhead, which contradicts the objective of improving time efficiency. 3.2.1. LENS: Loss-Curve Extrapolation via Smoothing. 

This part describes how to estimate l(θ(s)) for an arbitrary training step s. Since future training steps have not yet been executed, their corresponding losses cannot be directly observed. Moreover, evaluating the loss on a validation dataset at every completed training step incurs prohibitive computational overhead. Instead, the proposed method estimates the future loss using the history of mini-batch losses observed up to the current training step s0:

To address this issue, we model the underlying loss curve as a monotonically decreasing function that asymptotically converges during training. Specifically, the loss curve is represented by

By fitting this model, LENS both smooths the noisy mini-batch loss trajectory and extrapolates the trajectory to unobserved training steps. The predicted loss at an arbitrary step s is therefore given by

## 4. Experiments and Discussion

We consider two configurations: Configuration 1 has relatively low computa-tional capability, while Configuration 2 has relatively high computational capability, making communication the domi-nant bottleneck. The corresponding distribution parameters are summarized in Table 1. We compare TALLOP with three baseline methods: FFL [12], TimelyFL [8], and FedBalancer [5]. We also consider three ablations to isolate the effects of the round deadline, adaptive local training, and communication com-pression: 

(i) Vanilla FL w/o deadline : All clients perform one local epoch in every round and transmit uncom-pressed model updates. The round duration is de-termined by the slowest participating client. 

(ii) Vanilla FL w/ deadline : All clients perform one local epoch and transmit uncompressed model up-dates. Updates arriving after the round deadline are discarded. 

(iii) Training-only optimization : Each client adapts its local training workload to maximize learning progress within the round deadline while always transmitting uncompressed model updates. TALLOP and the deadline-based ablations use per-round deadlines of 50 and 25 seconds under Configurations 1 and 2, respectively. The global wall-clock training limits for CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 are 20,000 and 40,000 seconds under Configuration 1, and 2,500 and 5,000 seconds under Configuration 2, respectively. Final accuracy is measured at the global training limit, whereas TTA is the first wall-clock time at which the evaluated global model reaches the specified target accuracy. FFL, TimelyFL, and FedBalancer are evaluated only on MobileNetV2/CIFAR-10 under both system configura-tions, using the same data-heterogeneity and system set-tings as TALLOP. Then, the number of evaluations of PREDICT COMPRESSED LOSS (ρ) is O(log(1 /ε )) .Next, we estimate the computational complexity of a sin-gle call to P REDICT COMPRESSED LOSS (ρ). The dominant operations are (i) generating the sparsified model parameters 

θ(s0, p (ρ)) and (ii) computing the ℓ2 distance to the orig-inal parameters θ. Let P = |θ| denote the total number of model parameters. Operation (i) requires selecting or sorting parameters according to their magnitudes, resulting in a computational complexity of O(P log P ). Operation (ii) requires scanning all model parameters to compute the Euclidean distance, resulting in a computational complexity of O(P ).Therefore, the overall additional computational com-plexity of TALLOP is estimated as O(P log P log(1 /ε )) 

per communication round. As a concrete example, consider MobileNetV2, which has approximately P ≈ 2 × 10 6

parameters. Assuming log(1 /ε ) ≈ 10 , the additional com-putation introduced by TALLOP is on the order of approx-imately 10 8 to 10 9 operations per communication round. In contrast, training MobileNetV2 requires approximately 

9 × 10 8 floating-point multiply-add operations per training sample. Therefore, the additional computation introduced by TALLOP corresponds to, at most, the computational cost of training on only a few samples. Since federated learning typically performs local training on many samples in each communication round, this additional overhead has only a negligible impact on the overall training time. 

## 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we addressed time-to-accuracy optimiza-tion in FL under heterogeneous environments where com-munication time is non-negligible. We proposed TAL-LOP, which jointly optimizes the local training work-load and communication compression ratio under a fixed per-round time budget by predicting the post-compression model loss for different training-communication time alloca-tions. In the evaluated settings, TALLOP generally matched or improved TTA relative to training-only optimization and was competitive with the three baseline methods on MobileNetV2/CIFAR-10. However, the improvement was modest or absent in some settings, and the baseline com-parison was limited to a single model and random seed. This work assumes that the round time budget is predefined. An important direction for future work is to jointly optimize the round time budget and the training-communication time allocation. Another direction is to eval-uate TALLOP under more realistic deployment scenarios with dynamically changing computation capabilities, fluctu-ating communication bandwidths, and network congestion, as well as to extend TALLOP to asynchronous federated learning.
