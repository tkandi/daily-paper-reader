ABSTRACT 

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models reduce computation through sparse activation, but storing their expert parameters remains a major memory bottleneck. We in-troduce GS-MoE, a training-free compression framework that addresses redun-dancy across and within experts through two compression axes: shared-subspace rank and expert intermediate width. Groupwise low-rank representations cap-ture shared structure across experts while retaining expert-specific projections, and Nystr¨ om-based neuron compression reduces intermediate width within each expert. For fixed shared subspaces, we derive per-expert reconstruction-error re-ductions from the reconstruction objective and use them to guide expert grouping. Budgets are allocated hierarchically using a gradient-free layer-importance score to guide the allocation of rank and width budgets across layers, which eigenvalues and leverage then distribute as non-uniform ranks across groups and intermedi-ate widths across experts. Experiments demonstrate the complementary benefits of compression along both axes, particularly at high compression rates on fine-grained MoE models. On DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base, at a 60% reduction in to-tal model parameters, GS-MoE retains approximately 90% of the uncompressed model’s average reasoning performance, compared with 73% for D 2-MoE, while achieving 20% lower out-of-domain perplexity. We provide a code implementa-tion at: https://github.com/cbacary/GS-MoE 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models increase parameter capacity while limiting computation by acti-vating only a subset of experts for each token (Rajbhandari et al., 2022; DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025). Sparse activation, however, does not eliminate the storage cost of inactive experts. The resulting ex-pert parameter footprint remains a major obstacle to deployment, motivating compression methods that reduce memory requirements while preserving model quality (Li et al., 2025b; Gu et al., 2025). Expert compression must account for both structure shared across experts and redundancy within each expert. Shared-factor methods such as MoE-SVD address the former with a common low-rank factor across experts where D 2-MoE stores a shared base with compressed expert-specific residuals (Li et al., 2025b; Gu et al., 2025). These parameterizations impose different constraints on how shared and expert-specific information is represented. In particular, MoE-SVD requires the corresponding expert factors to use one common subspace; directions outside that subspace cannot be recovered by changing the expert-specific projections alone. Intermediate-neuron pruning addresses a different limitation by reducing expert width (He et al., 2026b;a), but pruning alone still stores separate weight representations for the retained neurons of different experts. Thus, a fixed shared subspace restricts where expert-specific variation can be represented, whereas width reduction alone does not encode common structure through shared parameters. This motivates a representation that allows subspaces to vary across expert groups and intermediate widths to vary across experts, with both capacities controlled under a common parameter budget. We introduce GS-MoE , a training-free framework that compresses MoE parameters along shared-subspace rank and expert intermediate width. Group-shared projections represent common struc-ture across experts, while expert-specific projections express individual transformations within each group’s subspaces. This makes the scope of parameter sharing explicit rather than fixing a single 1arXiv:2610.32009v1 [cs.LG] 23 Oct 2026 Preprint basis for the entire layer. Ridge-leverage neuron selection reduces intermediate width before the up projections are factorized. We estimate the shared up projections using a group-level surrogate and fit the expert-specific projections using each expert’s input statistics. The down reconstruction objec-tive admits a closed-form solution before width compression; Nystr¨ om reconstruction (Williams & Seeger, 2000) then maps the retained hidden activations to the down projection’s shared coordinates. Group rank and expert width therefore control two dimensions of the same compressed representa-tion: the capacity of shared subspaces and the size of expert-specific intermediate activations. GS-MoE determines which experts share a subspace and how capacity is distributed under a param-eter budget using calibration statistics. For fixed neuron selections and candidate shared projections, fitting the expert-specific projections expresses the reconstruction objective as a constant minus per-expert error reductions, which guide expert grouping. A gradient-free layer-importance score based on normalized, gate-weighted expert output magnitudes (Lasby et al., 2026) guides rank and width budgets across layers. Within each layer, neuron leverage scores determine expert widths, and eigenvalue-based scores and parameter costs guide group ranks. The rank scores reflect the up sur-rogate and the down reconstruction objective before width compression. This allocation adapts the compressed representation at the layer, group, and expert levels without post-compression training. Experiments demonstrate the complementary benefits of compression along both axes, particularly at high compression rates on fine-grained MoE models. On DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base, at 60% compression, GS-MoE retains 90% of the uncompressed model’s average reasoning performance, compared with 73% for D 2-MoE, while achieving 20% lower out-of-domain perplexity. Our contri-butions are: • We introduce GS-MoE, a training-free framework that compresses expert parameters along shared-subspace rank and intermediate width, addressing shared structure across experts and redundancy within experts. • We derive per-expert reconstruction-error reductions for fixed neuron selections and shared projections, providing an error-guided criterion for expert grouping. • We allocate capacity across layers, groups, and experts using a normalized expert-output score, eigenvalue-based rank scores, and neuron leverage scores under a parameter budget. • We demonstrate the benefit of compression along both axes and improved reasoning reten-tion and out-of-domain perplexity over D 2-MoE at 60% compression on DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Base. 

2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 PRUNING AND MERGING OF MIXTURE -OF -E XPERTS 

Through sparse activation the MoE architecture effectively limits computational demand but intro-duces significant parameter overhead and redundancy making MoE compression techniques vital. These techniques generally operate through one of expert merging, expert pruning, neuron prun-ing, or decomposition. Expert merging methods reduce parameters by consolidating similar ex-perts: HC-SMoE (Chen et al., 2025) uses hierarchical clustering to merge similar experts without retraining while DERN (Zhou et al., 2025) redistributes neuron-level segments from pruned ex-perts to compatible retained experts before merging them. Not All Experts are Equal (Lu et al., 2024) introduces expert pruning which reduces parameter overhead through whole expert removal; REAP (Lasby et al., 2026) builds on this by applying router-weighted output norms to score expert prunability. Further, REAP (Lasby et al., 2026) identifies that merging-based methods incur signifi-cant error that pruning-based methods do not. Rather than discarding entire modules, finer-grained frameworks such as TENP (He et al., 2026b) and Less-Is-MoE (He et al., 2026a) compress interme-diate expert widths, out-performing whole expert removal. However, pruning techniques rely solely on parameter deletion, leaving shared inter-expert structures unexploited. In contrast, our method avoids the distortion of expert merging and the finality of pure pruning by coupling group-shared subspaces that capture inter-expert redundancy with intra-expert width compression. 2Preprint 2.2 LOW -R ANK DECOMPOSITION AND PARAMETER SHARING 

Decomposition-based frameworks replace expert weight matrices with compact low-rank factors. Early decomposition-based approaches apply low-rank approximation in conjunction with expert pruning (Yang et al., 2024). Alternative methods employ parameter sharing to exploit cross-expert redundancy. MoE-SVD (Li et al., 2025b) shares singular vectors from the most frequently activated experts and D 2-MoE (Gu et al., 2025) represents each expert as a shared base weight plus a low-rank expert-specific residual. TD-MoE stacks expert weights into a tensor for joint Tucker decomposi-tion with adaptive three-dimensional rank allocation and multi-linear whitening (Xu et al., 2026). In each of these three methods, the shared component, whether a common factor, an additive base, or a set of mode factors, spans every expert in the layer. An expert whose weights diverge must ab-sorb the mismatch in its expert-specific component; under MoE-SVD, directions outside the shared subspace cannot be recovered by any choice of expert-specific factors. GS-MoE shares subspaces within groups of experts, and it forms those groups to maximize the reconstruction-error reduc-tion each expert obtains, so experts with dissimilar input or output subspaces are not forced onto a common basis. Additionally, GS-MoE adapts MoDeGPT (Lin et al., 2025), an intermediate-width compression method for dense LLMs, to its MoE compression framework. GS-MoE minimizes re-construction error over width-reduced up projections, effectively folding an additional compression axis directly into the subspace. Other methods relax the single-shared subspace constraint. LorExperts (Chakrabarti et al., 2026) keeps one full-precision dominant expert per cluster and represents the other members as low-rank corrections to it. MoBE (Chen et al., 2026) builds each expert’s up and gate matrices from its own combination of basis matrices shared across the layer, fitted by gradient-based weight reconstruction, leaving the down projections uncompressed. Sub-MoE (Li et al., 2025a) utilizes k-means clustering to group experts clusters by cosine similarity, align clusters to a shared basis by SVD, then merge the clusters into a single expert. Our method uses balanced medoid clustering with k-means++ initial-ization to group experts using reconstruction-error reductions. MoLAE (Liu et al., 2025) is closest to GS-MoE’s reparameterization through its group-shared latent projections with expert-specific trans-formations via stacked SVD. However, MoLAE forms arbitrary groups, and is forced to preserve the up projections entirely and restrict intermediate width to the latent rank forming compression factors of Rm×m, bounding its compression by the ratio of the two dimensions. GS-MoE retains and compresses every expert, including its down projections, and treats group rank and expert width as separate budgeted quantities allocated across layers, groups, and experts from calibration statis-tics, so its compression is set by the parameter budget rather than by a fixed dimension ratio or an uncompressed component.

4 EXPERIMENTS

5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we introduce GS-MoE, a novel training-free compression framework that addresses MoE parameter overhead through group-wise shared subspaces and intra-expert intermediate width reduction. We propose a novel grouping framework that accurately groups experts into error-minimized shared subspaces. Explicit group assignment drastically improves the efficiency of weight-sharing against fine-grained architectures and yields minimal throughput-overhead relative to accuracy improvement. In addition to our expert-grouping framework, we provide a comple-mentary eigenvalue-based group-rank allocation scheme and guide expert-width allocation through leverage scores. We find that, for fine-grained architectures, combining group-wise subspaces and expert intermediate width reduction into a single compression framework retains state-of-the-art fidelity under aggressive parameter budgets. 9Preprint

A.5 DISCRETE GROUP -R ANK ALLOCATION 

For fixed groups and neuron selections, Obj. 10 has separable scores Vg and per-rank costs cg .Searching a multiplier for a shared resource constraint is a standard approach in continuous resource allocation (Patriksson, 2008); water-filling provides a related continuous example (Boyd & Vanden-berghe, 2004, Example 5.2). These references motivate the multiplier search, rather than establish optimality of the discrete procedure below. For a multiplier λ ≥ 0, each group independently chooses an integer rank maximizing Vg (t)−λ·cg ·t

over rmin ≤ t ≤ rmax . Because the eigenvalues defining Vg are non-increasing, successive rank increments have non-increasing gains. Choosing the smaller rank in a tie makes total parameter cost non-increasing in λ. Bisection increases λ when the total cost exceeds r · P 

> g

cg and decreases it 15 Preprint when the cost is below that budget, retaining a feasible allocation. This assumes that the minimum allowed ranks fit within the budget. Starting from a feasible allocation, residual budget can be assigned by repeatedly selecting the af-fordable next rank increment with the largest gain per parameter, [Vg (rg + 1) − Vg (rg )] /c g , subject to the rank upper bounds. The procedure stops when no positive-gain increment fits. Different cg

and integer ranks can leave budget slack, and this multiplier search with greedy completion does not generally guarantee a global optimum of Obj. 10. A.6 LAYER ALLOCATION AND BUDGET CORRECTION
