Abstract—Federated learning (FL) is a distributed paradigm
for Internet of Things (IoT) edge networks. However, deploying
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) over wireless edge networks
presents distinct challenges: large model parameters increase
uplink transmission latency over bandwidth-constrained fading
channels, and non-independent and identically distributed (Non-
IID) data exacerbates client drift. Existing feature alignment
solutions often introduce auxiliary computations that consume
time slots allocated for wireless parameter transmission, in-
creasing the likelihood of straggler dropouts under system de-
lay constraints. To address these issues, we propose DPGR-
GCAM, a communication-efficient federated domain generaliza-
tion framework. By employing a dual-parameter-efficient fine-
tuning (PEFT) architecture, we design a channel-aware tiered
transmission mechanism that adaptively truncates payloads to
maximize node survival rates under fading conditions. To mit-
igate client drift, we introduce the Gradient-Conflict Aware
Modulation (GCAM) mechanism. GCAM operates within the
low-dimensional feature manifold to dynamically resolve multi-
objective optimization conflicts with minimal edge computation
latency, thereby reducing computation-induced network outages.
Experiments demonstrate that DPGR-GCAM improves cross-
domain generalization and sustains convergence under network
fading scenarios with a 10% client participation rate.
Index Terms—Federated learning, Internet of Things, Domain
generalization, Parameter-efficient fine-tuning, Gradient conflict.
I. INTRODUCTION
The deployment of 6G and the Internet of Things (IoT) has
led to an increase in multimodal data generated by mobile
edge devices. To utilize this data while maintaining privacy
and reducing backhaul transmission, Federated Learning (FL)
serves as a distributed collaborative training paradigm [1].
Recently, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown ca-
pability in zero-shot generalization [2]. However, deploying
these foundation models over wireless edge networks requires
transmitting large parameter sets, which increases uplink trans-
mission latency over bandwidth-constrained fading wireless
channels. Under system maximum tolerable delay (Tmax)
constraints, these payloads can lead to communication outages,
reducing the active client participation rate and affecting global
convergence.
In realistic wide-area IoT scenarios, heterogeneous edge
nodes exhibit non-independent and identically distributed
(Non-IID) data characteristics [3]–[5]. Domain shifts caused
by varying geographical locations and environments contribute
to client drift. In conventional federated domain generalization
(FedDG) frameworks [6], solutions that address feature space
fragmentation often require additional local computation over-
head. From a network perspective, increased local computa-
tion latency (T comp
k
) reduces the available time for wireless
parameter transmission, which increases the vulnerability of
edge devices to channel fading and straggler issues.
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) [7] methods ex-
change lightweight parameters between clients and the server,
reducing the communication burden. However, some PEFT-
based federated mechanisms (e.g., PromptFL [8] and FedCLIP
[9]) lack feature correction tailored for data heterogeneity.
Consequently, local models can overfit to private distributions,
which limits the generalization capability of the aggregated
global model on unseen target domains [10].
To address the trade-off between communication latency,
computation overhead, and cross-domain generalization, we
propose DPGR-GCAM. We formulate the deployment of fed-
erated VLMs as a joint optimization problem bounded by
Shannon capacity, transmit power, and system maximum delay
constraints. This approach provides a distributed paradigm
designed for fading channels and Non-IID scenarios. The main
contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
• We introduce a dual-PEFT architecture in OFDMA feder-
ated networks. Combining visual adapters with Gradient-
Conflict Aware Modulation (GCAM) mitigates local over-
fitting and gradient conflicts under Non-IID conditions.
This maintains node participation rates under Tmax limits
and reduces cumulative communication overhead.
• To address client drift, we design a communication-
aware global prototype aggregation scheme. We also
propose the GCAM mechanism. Operating within the
low-dimensional feature manifold via gradient approxi-
mation, it modulates optimization conflicts with minimal
computation latency.
• Experiments on DomainNet and Office-Caltech10 bench-
marks evaluate the cross-domain generalization perfor-
mance of DPGR-GCAM compared to baseline methods.

II. SYSTEM MODEL AND METHODOLOGY
In this section, we first establish the system model for
FedDG over edge networks and introduce the physical-
layer wireless communication and computation delay con-
straints. Subsequently, we present the PEFT architecture based
on VLMs. To overcome model drift under extreme data
heterogeneity and unreliable wireless links, we propose a
communication-aware distributed prototype alignment mech-
anism, synergized with an innovative GCAM mechanism.
Finally, we formulate the entire framework as a joint op-
timization problem strictly bounded by communication and
computation constraints. The overall workflow of the proposed
DPGR-GCAM framework is illustrated in Fig.1.

B. Communication-Efficient PEFT Architecture
Deploying vision foundation models CLIP with robust gen-

heterogeneous nodes with extremely low communication over-

D. GCAM
In FedDG, prototype regularization with static weights fre-

IID data. We strictly formulate the local update as a bi-
objective optimization problem, balancing the local cross-
entropy loss LCE and the prototype alignment loss Lreg.
In wireless edge networks, conventional second-order gra-
dient computations drastically increase the local computa-

III. EXPERIMENTS AND ANALYSIS
A. Experimental Setting
To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the pro-
posed DPGR-GCAM framework over bandwidth-constrained
and fading IoT edge networks, we conduct extensive system-
level FedDG simulations under realistic physical-layer con-
straints.
Wireless Network and System Settings: We consider
an OFDMA-based federated edge network scenario. In this
scenario, K edge devices are uniformly distributed within a
cell radius of 500 meters from the central server. The wireless
channel is modeled using Rayleigh fading, where the channel
gain |h(r)
k |2 follows an exponential distribution with unit mean.
The path loss is modeled as PL(d) = 128.1 + 37.6 log10(d) in
dB, where d is the distance in kilometers. The total system
bandwidth is 20 MHz, dynamically and equally allocated
among all active devices. The maximum transmit power of
edge nodes Pmax is set to 20 dBm, and the AWGN power
spectral density N0 is set to -114 dBm/MHz. Crucially, to
emulate strict edge computing and transmission bottlenecks,
the maximum tolerable system delay per federated aggregation
round Tmax is set to 5.0 seconds. Any device failing to com-
plete local computation and parameter upload within Tmax is
strictly adjudged as a straggler and directly dropped according
to our physical-layer outage indicator function I(r)
k .
Datasets and Data Heterogeneity: We employ the Office-
Caltech10 [15] benchmark and the large-scale multi-domain
dataset DomainNet [16] for cross-domain research. To charac-
terize and simulate the heterogeneous data distribution of edge
nodes in federated networks, we utilize a Dirichlet distribution
Dir(α) governed by a concentration parameter α. We set
α ∈{0.01, 0.5}, where α = 0.01 simulates extreme label
skew, and α = 0.5 represents a moderate heterogeneity level
commonly encountered in realistic federated collaborations.
Baselines and Implementation Details: We benchmark
DPGR-GCAM against a suite of state-of-the-art federated
learning methods. The baselines encompass traditional fed-
erated alignment algorithms (FedAvg, FedProto), as well as
the latest PEFT federated collaboration strategies designed
for large VLMs, including PromptFL, FedCLIP, FedAPT, and
FedOTP. Across all VLM-based baselines and our framework,
we uniformly adopt the pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/16 as the
backbone network, strictly freezing all underlying parameters
of both the image and text encoders. During local fine-tuning,
clients solely update the lightweight visual adapters and text
prompt learners. The local training epochs are set to 5, the
global communication rounds R to 50, and optimization is
driven by SGD with a base learning rate of 0.01 and a batch
size of 32. For the GCAM mechanism, the base regularization
strength γ0 is set to 1.0.

Ablation Studies. Table III provides system ablation stud-

on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT only) yields a sub-

architecture in facilitating multi-modal domain adaptation.
Ultimately, the full DPGR-GCAM achieves a peak accuracy of
98.93% on Office-Caltech10 and secures an absolute advantage
of 76.04% on the massive DomainNet dataset, substantiating
the superiority of this joint architectural design.
