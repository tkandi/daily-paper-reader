## 摘要
人工智能（AI）的快速增长依赖于相互关联的水、能源和数据系统，这些系统跨越基础设施的全生命周期及多样化的地理区域，并可能加剧水资源与能源不安全、扩大社会不平等，进而在全球范围内引发级联式可持续性问题。然而，现有评估通常将这些系统分开考察，限制了我们对资源压力产生于何处、如何在空间上转移以及由谁承担其后果的理解。为应对这些挑战，我们建立了一个水—能源—数据（WED）纽带框架，以捕捉人工智能基础设施在空间和生命周期维度上的依赖关系；并利用大语言模型综合分析了12,825项与水或能源相关的研究，这些研究涵盖人工智能基础设施中四类资源密集型活动：矿产开采、半导体制造、数据中心运行和电子废弃物处理。证据图谱揭示了支撑人工智能基础设施的相互关联活动与用于评估这些活动的边界之间存在不匹配。研究人员对运行阶段能源的评估多于对用水及其他间接需求的评估，而不同地点和生命周期活动之间的相互作用仍未得到充分研究。这些缺口掩盖了资源需求如何在基础设施节点和区域之间转移，以及数据赋能的收益如何与其基础成本相比较。WED纽带为对空间元耦合系统开展综合评估奠定了基础，而该证据图谱可为管理人工智能热潮的可持续性影响提供优先事项参考。

## Abstract
The rapid growth of artificial intelligence (AI) depends on interconnected water, energy, and data systems spanning infrastructure life cycles and diverse geographies, with the potential to intensify water and energy insecurity, exacerbate social inequities and lead to cascading sustainability issues globally. Yet assessments commonly examine them separately, limiting our understanding of where resource pressures arise, how they are transferred across space, and who bears their consequences. To address these challenges, we established a water-energy-data (WED) nexus framework that captures the spatial and life-cycle dependencies of AI infrastructure, and use large language models to synthesize 12,825 water- or energy-related studies across four resource-intensive AI infrastructure activities: mineral extraction, semiconductor manufacturing, data center operation and electronic-waste processing. The evidence map identifies a mismatch between the interconnected activities supporting AI infrastructure and boundaries used to assess them. Researchers assess operational energy more than water use and other indirect demands, while interactions across locations and life-cycle activities remain understudied. These gaps obscure how resource demands shift among infrastructure nodes and regions, and how data-enabled benefits compare with their underlying costs. The WED nexus provides a foundation for integrated assessment of spatially metacoupled systems, while the evidence map can inform priorities for managing the sustainability implications of the AI boom.
