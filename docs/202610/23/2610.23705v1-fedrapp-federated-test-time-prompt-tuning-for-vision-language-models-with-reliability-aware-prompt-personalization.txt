FedRAPP: Federated Test-Time Prompt Tuning for Vision-Language Models
with Reliability-Aware Prompt Personalization

Abstract
Federated prompt learning enables vision-language models
like CLIP to collaboratively adapt lightweight soft prompts
across distributed clients without sharing raw data. How-
ever, existing federated prompt learning methods are de-
signed primarily for supervised settings with labeled client
data and assumed relatively stable training distributions.
At test time, source-free federated prompt tuning remains
largely unexplored.
Clients may receive unlabeled tar-
get samples with heterogeneous, often non-IID distributions
that differ substantially across clients and from the source
training distribution, causing locally adapted prompts to
drift in different directions and reducing the effectiveness of
federated aggregation. In this work, we explore Source-Free
Federated Test-Time Prompt Tuning (SF-FTTPT), where
distributed clients collaboratively adapt soft prompts on un-
labeled test streams without access to source data. Our
study reveals that conventional federated aggregation meth-
ods struggle under heterogeneous client distributions, as lo-
cally adapted prompts become increasingly specialized and
difficult to combine effectively. To address this challenge,
we propose FedRAPP, a personalized aggregation frame-
work that combines reliability-aware client assessment,
server-side exponential moving-average prompt memory,
and similarity-guided peer aggregation. FedRAPP selec-
tively incorporates information from reliable and compat-
ible clients while preserving local specialization, enabling
more robust adaptation under distribution shift. Extensive
experiments across nine CLIP benchmark datasets and Ima-
geNet variants demonstrate that FedRAPP consistently out-
performs standard federated aggregation baselines under
heterogeneous client distributions while maintaining the ef-
ficiency advantages of prompt-only adaptation.

1. Introduction
Vision-language models (VLMs) such as CLIP [26] have
achieved impressive zero-shot transfer performance by
aligning visual and textual representations in a shared em-
bedding space [10, 15, 42]. However, their performance
depends critically on the prompts used at inference, and
distribution shifts can substantially degrade their effective-
ness. Because end-to-end fine-tuning of these large models
is computationally infeasible for edge devices, parameter-
efficient approaches such as prompt tuning optimize task-
specific soft prompts while keeping the pretrained VLM
frozen [11, 14, 46, 47].
In practice, test streams are rarely centralized. Modern
AI systems operate across distributed edge environments
such as mobile devices, autonomous vehicles, hospitals,
and IoT sensors, where data remains local due to privacy,
communication, and latency constraints [12, 16, 33, 39].

Each client therefore observes only a partial and potentially
biased view of the target distribution, making centralized
continual test-time prompt tuning impractical. Federated
Learning (FL) provides a natural framework for collabora-
tion by enabling clients to exchange model updates rather
than raw data [23]. Classical approaches such as FedAvg
[23] and FedProx [16] learn shared models through pa-
rameter aggregation, while personalized methods such as
FedAMP [9] and pFedGraph [40] account for client hetero-
geneity. However, these methods have been studied primar-
ily with labeled and relatively stationary client data. Recent
Federated Test-Time Adaptation (FedTTA) methods extend
test-time adaptation to distributed environments [27, 32],
but focus on vision-only models and typically aggregate
full model parameters or normalization statistics. COTE
[48] extends federated prompt learning to test-time adap-
tation for VLMs using unlabeled target data, but its adap-
tation starts from prompts pretrained using source-domain
data. Thus, source-free federated test-time prompt tuning,
where adaptation begins without access to source data, re-
mains largely unexplored.
We study Source-Free Federated Test-Time Prompt Tun-
ing (SF-FTTPT), a setting in which distributed clients col-
laboratively adapt soft prompts on unlabeled test streams
through federated aggregation without access to source
data.
Each client performs local test-time prompt tun-
ing with a frozen CLIP backbone and periodically shares
only lightweight prompt parameters, preserving privacy and
communication efficiency. By combining local adaptation
with knowledge transfer across clients, SF-FTTPT enables
collaborative adaptation beyond isolated local tuning in het-
erogeneous distributed environments.
Extending federated learning to source-free test-time
prompt tuning introduces several compounding challenges.
First, neither the server nor the clients have access to labels,
so useful client updates must be assessed entirely from un-
labeled test-time signals. Second, under non-IID client par-
titions, strong label shift can cause different clients to spe-
cialize toward different regions of the label space [20, 35],
making naive aggregation harmful when local adaptations
are incompatible. Third, test-time adaptation is inherently
noisy because prompts are updated from small unlabeled
mini-batches, making individual updates potentially unreli-
able or biased toward the current batch. Standard aggrega-
tion methods that treat clients equally therefore cannot dis-
tinguish useful updates from unstable ones, creating prompt
drift and potentially destroying client-specific specializa-
tion.
To address these challenges, we propose FedRAPP (Fed-
erated Reliability-Aware Prompt Personalization), a per-
sonalized federated prompt aggregation strategy designed
specifically for the source-free test-time setting. FedRAPP
operates through three complementary mechanisms. First,
it estimates a reliability score for each client’s prompt up-
date using label-free signals derived from the entropy of
model predictions before and after local adaptation, as well
as the magnitude of the prompt update direction. Updates
that reduce prediction uncertainty while remaining stable
are assigned higher reliability. Second, FedRAPP maintains
a server-side EMA prompt memory for each client, which
smooths prompt updates over time and prevents abrupt,
mini-batch-induced changes from dominating aggregation.
The EMA coefficient is made reliability-dependent, so that
confident and stable updates are incorporated more aggres-
sively while uncertain updates are smoothed conservatively.
Third, FedRAPP performs personalized peer aggregation
by computing collaboration weights that simultaneously
consider the similarity of prompt update directions across
clients and the reliability of each source client’s update, al-
lowing each client to selectively draw from compatible col-
laborators rather than all clients equally. Figure 1 shows that
FedRAPP consistently performs well under severe data het-
erogeneity across the clients. Our contributions are sum-
marized as follows:
• We formulate and systematically study Source-Free Fed-
erated Test-Time Prompt Tuning (SF-FTTPT), where
distributed clients collaboratively adapt soft prompts on
unlabeled test streams without access to source data,
and establish a benchmark spanning multiple continual
prompt tuning methods, federated aggregation strategies,
and both IID and non-IID client distributions.
• We identify key challenges of source-free federated
prompt adaptation under heterogeneous client distribu-
tions, including prompt drift and the loss of client-specific
specialization, which limit the effectiveness of conven-
tional aggregation methods.
• We propose FedRAPP, a reliability-aware personalized
aggregation framework that combines client reliability
estimation, EMA-based prompt memory, and similarity-
guided collaboration, achieving consistent improvements
across nine CLIP benchmarks and five ImageNet variants.
2. Related Work
2.1. Test-Time Prompt Tuning
Vision-language models such as CLIP [26] are effective for
zero-shot image recognition, but their performance can de-
grade when inference prompts are mismatched with the tar-
get distribution. Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods for
vision models [18, 24, 30, 34, 37] address distribution shift
by adapting model parameters during inference using sig-
nals such as entropy and pseudo-labels. However, adapting
VLM parameters is costly and may induce catastrophic for-
getting [29]. Prompt tuning provides a lightweight alterna-
tive by optimizing learnable context vectors while keeping
the backbone frozen; methods such as CoOp [47], CoCoOp
2


[46], and PLOT [3] demonstrate its effectiveness, but re-
quire labeled training data.
Test-time prompt tuning removes this supervision re-
quirement by adapting prompts from unlabeled test data.
TPT [29] minimizes prediction entropy across augmented
views, while DiffTPT [6], C-TPT [41], O-TPT [28], and
RLCF [45] improve augmentation diversity, calibration, or
label-free adaptation signals. These methods are typically
episodic and do not retain knowledge across the test stream.
Continual approaches address this limitation by maintaining
knowledge across incoming samples: AdaPrompt [43] uses
a confidence buffer and prompt ensemble, SwapPrompt [22]
uses knowledge distillation, DART [19] jointly adapts vi-
sual and textual prompts, and DynaPrompt [38] maintains
dynamic prompt buffers. However, these approaches as-
sume that the test stream is locally available on a single ma-
chine, leaving collaborative prompt adaptation across dis-
tributed clients unexplored.
2.2. Federated Learning
Federated Learning (FL) enables distributed clients to col-
laboratively learn by exchanging parameters rather than
data [23].
FedAvg aggregates local models through pa-
rameter averaging, but heterogeneous client distributions
can cause client drift [8, 44].
FedProx [16] anchors lo-
cal updates to the global model, while SCAFFOLD [13]
explicitly corrects drift. Personalized FL methods instead
allow client-specific solutions under heterogeneous data
[4, 5, 25, 31]; Ditto [17] combines global and personal-
ized models, FedAMP [9] promotes selective collaboration
among similar clients, and pFedGraph [40] models collab-
oration through a graph.
In the VLM setting, PromptFL [7] enables federated
CLIP prompt learning, while Luo et al.
[21] propose
personalized mixture-of-experts federated prompt learning.
Privacy-preserving prompt tuning for multimodal LLMs
[33] further considers privacy, but these approaches re-
main supervised and rely on labeled or source-domain data.
Recently, different works have explored Federated Test-
time Adaptation (FTTA): ATP [1] and FedICON [32] ad-
dress heterogeneous test-time adaptation, FedCTTA [27]
introduces similarity-aware aggregation for continual test-
time shifts, and BPFedCTTA [36] employs Bayesian prior-
guided local adaptation and uncertainty-gated aggregation
to improve stability under sequential, heterogeneous test-
time shifts. However, these methods are designed for stan-
dard vision models and aggregate full model parameters or
normalization statistics rather than soft prompts. Although
Latte [2] and COTE [48] adopt VLM-based federated learn-
ing, Latte shares class prototypes across clients, raising pri-
vacy concerns, while COTE relies on supervised source pre-
training to initialize prompts before test-time adaptation.
This leaves source-free federated test-time prompt tuning
under heterogeneous client distributions largely unexplored.
3. Methodology
In federated test-time prompt tuning, each client adapts its
prompt using only the unlabeled samples it observes locally.
Unlike supervised federated learning, the server cannot rely
on labels or validation accuracy to determine which client
updates are useful, and the clients themselves may observe
highly different class distributions.
In practical deploy-
ments, different devices, users, or environments may en-
counter different subsets of classes, causing locally adapted
prompts to specialize toward different regions of the la-
bel space. This makes prompt aggregation especially chal-
lenging as collaboration must be driven by label-free sig-
nals while avoiding the loss of useful client-specific adapta-
tion. To this end, we propose Federated Reliability-Aware
Prompt Personalization (FedRAPP), a personalized aggre-
gation strategy that allows clients to benefit from compat-
ible collaborators while preserving local test-time special-
ization. FedRAPP aggregates prompts by considering two
central signals: how similarly clients adapt their prompts,
and how reliable each client’s local adaptation appears from
unlabeled test-time statistics.

Implementation Details.
We use CLIP with a frozen
ViT-B/16 image encoder as the vision-language backbone
and evaluate four test-time prompt tuning methods: TPT
[29], AdaPrompt [43], SwapPrompt [22], and DART [19].
We compare FedAvg [23], FedProx [16], FedAMP [9],
pFedGraph [40], and pFedMoAP [21], with “None-Fed”
denoting independent client adaptation. Experiments use 10
clients under IID and pathological non-IID partitions, where
clients receive non-overlapping classes. Only prompts are
tuned and aggregated; the backbone remains frozen and lo-
cal, with batch size 16. We set λent = 0.5, λdrift = 0.25,
λself = 0.4, using OxfordPets, Flower102, and Caltech101
for tuning, and set τ = 1, ϵ = 10−12, µ0 = 0.8, and
η = 0.1. All experiments use three random seeds, and we
report mean accuracy and standard deviation.
5. Result analysis
5.1. Performance on CLIP Datasets
Table 1 reports the average top-1 accuracy across the nine
CLIP datasets, with the per-dataset results for AdaPrompt
under non-IID shown in Table 2; complete results are pro-
vided in the supplementary material. The four TTA meth-
ods respond differently to federation, showing that the lo-
cal adaptation mechanism strongly influences whether col-
laboration is beneficial.
TPT collapses in the continual
test-time setting due to error accumulation from repeated
entropy minimization, but most federated methods par-
tially recover its performance, with FedRAPP providing
the strongest non-IID improvement.
In contrast, pFed-
MoAP substantially degrades TPT, consistent with its su-
pervised attention-gating mechanism being poorly suited
to the source-free setting.
A detailed analysis on pFed-
MoAP is provided in Section D of the supplementary mate-
rial. DART achieves strong standalone performance but col-
lapses under naive prompt sharing, as its jointly tuned visual
and class-specific text prompts become incompatible across
clients with heterogeneous label distributions. Personalized
aggregation partially recovers this performance, although it
remains below the standalone setting. We analyze this be-
havior in detail in Section C of the supplementary mate-
rial. SwapPrompt and AdaPrompt are substantially more
robust to federation due to their EMA-based consistency
and confidence-aware memory mechanisms, respectively,
with FedRAPP achieving the strongest overall performance
for both (Table 1). The per-dataset results further show that
FedRAPP consistently matches or exceeds None-Fed under
non-IID partitioning, while naive aggregation can degrade
performance on heterogeneous datasets; this indicates that
reliability-aware collaboration helps avoid the destructive
averaging of incompatible client updates (Table 2).

5.3. Ablation Analysis
Component ablation.
We examine the contribution of
the three FedRAPP components: update similarity (Sim.),
reliability (Rel.), and server-side EMA memory (EMA) un-
der the non-IID partition with AdaPrompt local adaptation
for the OxfordPets, Flower102, and Caltech-101 datasets.
As shown in Table 4, removing all three components gives
the lowest performance, with accuracies of 86.89, 93.96,
and 72.12 on OxfordPets, Caltech101, and Flower102, re-
spectively. Among the individual components, EMA pro-
vides a significant improvement with stable aggregation of
the prompts, followed by reliability and update similarity,
reaching average accuracies of 84.97, 84.78, and 84.64, re-
spectively. Combining components further improves per-
formance: Rel.+ EMA reaches 85.57 average accuracy,
while Sim.+EMA and Sim.+Rel. achieve 85.38 and 85.14,
respectively. The full combination of all three components
gives the best results across all three datasets, achieving
an average accuracy of 86.30.
These results show that
the three components provide complementary benefits, with
reliability-aware assessment and EMA memory providing
the strongest individual gains, while update similarity fur-
ther improves aggregation when combined with the other
components.
Sensitivity analysis of λent (uncertainty penalty coeffi-
cient) and λdrift (drift penalty coefficient).
First, we set
the value of λself (personalization coefficient) to 0, to find
the optimum values of λent and λdrift used in reliability
score computation. We use the OxfordPets, Caltech-101,
and Flower102 for this purpose and monitor their average
accuracies. We sweep the uncertainty penalty coefficient
λent and drift penalty coefficient λdrift over 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0.
It can be seen from table 5 that the optimum values for λent
and λdrift are 0.5 and 0.25 respectively.
Sensitivity analysis of λself (personalization coefficient).
After we fix λent = 0.5 and λdrift = 0.25, we find the opti-
mum value for λself by observing the average performance
on OxfordPets, Caltech-101, and Flower102 datasets as
shown in Figure 3(e). The personalization coefficient con-
trols the balance between the client’s own adapted prompt
and the personalized peer prompt. Accuracy remains con-
sistently high across the moderate range of 0.4–0.8, achiev-
ing the best performance at λself = 0.4 (86.30%). How-
ever, the accuracy decreases noticeably when λself = 0.2
(85.73%). A low value of λself places excessive reliance
on the shared peer prompt while reducing the contribution
of the client’s own adaptation, which leads to degraded per-
formance. Overall, a balanced combination of the client-
specific prompt and the shared peer prompt yields the best
results.

6. Conclusion
In this work, we presented FedRAPP, a reliability-aware
personalized aggregation framework for source-free feder-
ated test-time prompt tuning of vision-language models.
Through reliability-guided client assessment, EMA-based
prompt memory, and similarity-aware collaboration, Fe-
dRAPP effectively preserves local prompt specialization
while enabling beneficial knowledge sharing among dis-
tributed clients.
Experimental results across nine CLIP
benchmark datasets and five ImageNet variants showed that
FedRAPP consistently outperforms conventional federated
aggregation strategies, particularly under challenging non-
IID settings where client heterogeneity significantly im-
pacts adaptation. The ablation and sensitivity analyses fur-
ther demonstrated the complementary benefits of the pro-
posed components, while experiments with varying batch
sizes, client participation, and federated rounds demon-
strated the robustness of the framework across different de-
ployment conditions. These findings suggest that person-
alized and reliability-aware collaboration is essential for
effective SF-FTTPT of vision-language models.
As fu-
ture work, we plan to extend FedRAPP to larger mul-
timodal foundation models, investigate asynchronous and
resource-constrained federated settings, and explore adap-
tive collaboration mechanisms for evolving test distribu-
tions.

A. Performance analysis on CLIP Benchmark
datasets
Table A1 and Table A2 show the detailed performance
comparison of different federated learning methods com-
bined with multiple test-time adaptation (TTA) techniques
under both IID and non-IID data partitions across the
CLIP benchmark datasets. Overall, the proposed FedRAPP
framework demonstrates strong and consistent performance
across most datasets and partition settings, particularly un-
der challenging non-IID scenarios.
For the TPT setting, FedRAPP achieves the best perfor-
mance in most non-IID cases, including Flower102, Cal-
tech101, OxfordPets, DTD, and SUN397, indicating that
the proposed method effectively improves robustness un-
der heterogeneous client distributions. Although FedAvg
and FedProx obtain slightly higher scores on a few datasets,
such as Food101, UCF101, and StanfordCars, FedRAPP
consistently provides strong adaptation under distribution
shifts. pFedMoAP shows substantially lower performance
and high variability across datasets, likely because its atten-
tion gating module is difficult to train reliably in the source-
free setting; this behavior is further analyzed in Section D.
TPT in the continual setup performs poorly across all the
datasets. However, federated aggregation improves the per-
formance of TPT-continual, showing that collaboration for
prompt tuning improves the results. Under the DART set-
ting, the non-federated baseline achieves the highest over-
all accuracy, suggesting that DART is sensitive to feder-
ated optimization and client heterogeneity. This can be at-
tributed to the fact that DART uses both text and visual
prompts and class-specific text prompts, which may over-
complicate the federated aggregation. Nevertheless, among
federated approaches, FedRAPP consistently provides the
best or near-best performance, achieving the best federated
performance across the non-IID datasets and demonstrating
improved stability compared to existing federated baselines.
For SWAPPROMPT, FedRAPP achieves the best or near-
best performance across most non-IID datasets, includ-
ing Flower102, Caltech101, OxfordPets, DTD, Food101,
UCF101, and SUN397. This indicates that FedRAPP ef-
fectively preserves prompt adaptation capability even un-
der decentralized heterogeneous data distributions. The im-
provements are particularly noticeable in Flower102, Ox-
fordPets, Food101, and UCF101 under non-IID partitions.
In contrast, pFedMoAP exhibits substantially degraded per-
formance across datasets, consistent with the instability of
its learned attention-based aggregation mechanism in the
source-free setting. Similarly, under the ADAPROMPT set-
ting, FedRAPP consistently achieves the best performance
across the datasets, especially in non-IID environments.
FedRAPP obtains the highest scores on Flower102, Cal-
tech101, OxfordPets, DTD, Food101, UCF101, SUN397,
FGVCAircraft, and StanfordCars, highlighting its effective-
ness in handling client-specific feature variations and severe
data heterogeneity.
Overall, the results demonstrate that the proposed Fe-
dRAPP framework provides strong generalization and ro-
bustness across diverse datasets and TTA methods. In par-
ticular, its consistent improvements under non-IID settings
indicate that the method is highly effective for practical
federated vision-language adaptation scenarios where client
data distributions are inherently heterogeneous.

C. Federated Aggregation Under DART
DART differs from the other test-time methods in this study
by maintaining four distinct sets of prompts rather than one.
It learns a separate learnable text prompt for every class, to-
gether with a single learnable visual prompt inserted into
the image encoder, and adapts both on each incoming sam-
ple. Alongside these, it carries a second pair of knowledge-
retention prompts one per class on the text side and one per
class on the visual side which accumulate a running aver-
age of the adapted prompts and are blended back in before
the next sample arrives, so that information from earlier test
images is preserved. Only the two adapted prompt sets are
trainable; the retention pair is a memory. For consistency

D. Effect of Attention Module in pFedMoAP
pFedMoAP treats federated prompt learning as a mixture-
of-experts problem: each client holds its own prompt as a
local expert, downloads a set of peer prompts as non-local
experts, and learns a lightweight single-head attention mod-
ule that fuses them into an enhanced text feature used for
classification. The attention module is trained on the client,
is never aggregated, only the prompts are. In the main tables
we show the default setup of pFedMoAP without tampering
with any of the components. In Table A5, we test the pFed-
MoAP across 3 datasets for 2 setups: the trainable gate
version is the default setting for pFedMoAP with all the
components: the attention module is randomly initialised
on each client and co-trained with the prompt on the same
unlabelled test-time objective the base adaptation method
already minimises, and the fused logits are what the loss is
computed on. The dot-product only version removes the
learnable attention module entirely and scores peers by di-
rect similarity between the prompts.
The trainable gate is not only unhelpful but actively de-
structive, costing between 15 to 31 points on every dataset
and partition. The dot-product variant, by contrast, remains
competitive: with no learned parameters to bootstrap, its
attention weights stay close to uniform and it therefore be-
haves much like simple averaging, which is a reasonable
outcome rather than a failure. The explanation is that a ran-
domly initialized projection module has no signal to learn
from here.

The two blocks reproduce, under natural distri-
bution shift, the behaviour already observed on the CLIP
benchmarks. TPT is by far the weakest standalone adapta-

dRAPP obtains the best average in the block. DART shows
the opposite pattern.
Its local-only (None-Fed) configu-
ration is the strongest entry in the whole table and every
federated variant falls far below it, because DART’s class-
conditioned text prompts and visual prompt are not com-
patible with cross-client averaging when clients hold dis-
joint label subsets; see Table A4 for the detailed federation-
target study for DART. Among the federated rules, Fe-
dRAPP is nonetheless the best or the second-best, confirm-
ing that reliability-weighted personalized aggregation is the
least destructive of the aggregation strategies even when the
underlying prompt representation resists sharing.

F. Stability of Prompt updates
In Figure A2, we show the similarity of the consecutive
prompts updates for the OxfordPets dataset across 10 clients
using FedRAPP for non-iid scenario. We calculate the co-
sine similarity of the prompt updates per client for consec-
utive rounds and show the average of the cosine similarities
for all the clients per round. We observe that prompt up-
dates become increasingly consistent (more similar in their
direction) and stable across the federated rounds, as shown
by the rising cosine similarity.
