Interflow: Breaking the Offline–Online Barrier for
Private AI at the Edge
Hanieh Totonchi Asl, Ebrahim Nouri, Zhuoran Li, Danella Zhao
Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Arizona, Tucson, United States
{haniehta, ebinouri, zli1122, danellazhao}@arizona.edu
Abstract—Privacy-preserving machine learning (PPML) offers
a promising path toward bringing powerful AI services to the
edge without exposing sensitive user data. However, its practical
deployment remains constrained by the high communication,
computation, and data-movement costs of secure multi-party
computation (MPC), particularly during nonlinear inference.
Existing MPC systems typically decouple input-independent
correlated randomness generation from online execution, cre-
ating substantial memory pressure to buffer single-use crypto-
graphic data. Moreover, limited storage can force a stop-and-
refill execution pattern that repeatedly stalls the pipeline to
replenish these buffers, bringing randomness generation back
onto the critical path. We propose Interflow, an algorithm–
hardware co-design that rethinks this separation by batch-
wise processing and pipelining correlated-randomness generation
with online execution: while the current batch consumes pre-
generated randomness to execute secure nonlinear operations,
correlation generation proceeds concurrently for the next batch.
By leveraging interleaved execution, efficient data movement, and
hardware acceleration, the design transforms preprocessing from
a serialized, storage-intensive offline phase into a continuously
overlapped pipeline that generates correlated randomness closer
to the point of consumption, reducing memory footprint and
data movement overhead. Implemented on the Zynq UltraScale+
ZCU102, Interflow improves nonlinear evaluation throughput by
up to 1.55× for CNN operators and 1.48× for transformer
operators. It achieves up to 1.26× speedup for ResNet-50 and up
to 1.21× speedup for BERT-base, and reduces memory footprint
by up to 37%, compared with baseline frameworks.
I. INTRODUCTION
Machine Learning as a Service (MLaaS) enables users to
outsource inference to the cloud; however, its deployment in
sensitive domains such as healthcare, finance, and personal ser-
vices raises fundamental privacy concerns. Privacy-preserving
machine learning (PPML) addresses this challenge through
cryptographic primitives such as homomorphic encryption
(HE) and secure multi-party computation (MPC), enabling
computation over encrypted data without exposing raw in-
puts and model weights [1]–[5]. Practical two-party PPML
frameworks commonly adopt a hybrid approach, evaluating
linear layers via HE and nonlinear operations (such as ReLU,
GELU, MaxPooling, and Softmax) through secret-sharing
(SS)-based MPC protocols [6]–[10]. Despite their benefits,
secure nonlinear operations incur substantial computation and
communication overhead and often require large amounts of
correlated randomness, making them a critical bottleneck on
resource-constrained edge devices [11], [12].
Modern MPC protocols adopt an offline–online paradigm,
in which input-independent cryptographic correlations such
as oblivious transfers (OTs) and Beaver triples are generated
during an offline preprocessing phase, shifting much of the
expensive computation and communication away from the
online phase [6], [7]. Pre-generating correlated randomness
requires substantial storage; in practice, systems maintain only
a bounded pool of precomputed OTs or Beaver triples (e.g.,
107 OTs in Cheetah). Once exhausted, regeneration stalls
execution and introduces significant latency, bringing offline
preprocessing onto the online critical path. This bottleneck
becomes especially severe on resource-constrained devices.
Key Observations. Conventional preprocessing treats cor-
relation generation as a decoupled phase. Under resource
constraints, however, correlated randomness becomes a con-
tinuous lifecycle challenge: fresh values must be generated,
stored in different representations and widths, converted for
protocol-specific operations, delivered to their consumption
points, and discarded after a single use. Our characterization
of a baseline framework on an edge client shows that re-
peated correlation regeneration accounts for 43% of client-side
secure-comparison cycles during ResNet-50 inference. This
substantial overhead motivates optimizing the entire correla-
tion lifecycle, as accelerating generation alone may simply
shift the bottleneck to storage, conversion, or data movement
rather than eliminating it.
Design Insights. Based on these observations, we present
Interflow, an algorithm–hardware co-design for OT-based se-
cure nonlinear inference that jointly manages the complete
correlation lifecycle, from generation and representation-aware
storage to sender/receiver-specific conversion and online con-
sumption. Our central insight is to split preprocessing at its
final communication dependency and consumption boundary.
This allows receiver-local COT generation to be deferred until
near consumption and integrated directly into the execution
dataflow. Building on this decomposition, Interflow employs
batch-wise, role-aware processing to overlap correlation gen-
eration, conversion, and consumption, avoiding the need to
generate and store the full set of expanded correlations in
advance.

II. PRELIMINARIES AND BACKGROUND
A. Fundamental MPC primitives for Nonlinear Evaluation
MPC-based nonlinear inference can be largely decomposed
into two fundamental secure primitives: comparison and mul-
tiplication. Fig. 1 summarizes how representative nonlinear
operations in Cheetah [7] and BumbleBee [9] are decomposed
into secure computation primitives. ReLU and MaxPooling
rely primarily on secure comparisons (∼97%), followed by
secure multiplication or selection to produce the corresponding
outputs. More complex nonlinearities, such as GELU and
Softmax, are typically evaluated via piecewise or polyno-
mial approximations that similarly decompose their execu-
tion into secure comparisons (∼56−67%) and multiplications
(∼29−37%). These two primitives therefore constitute key
building blocks of secure nonlinear inference: comparison
resolves signs, ordering, and selection predicates, while multi-
plication governs arithmetic evaluation and masked selection.
Their frequent execution makes the associated correlation
generation and interactive execution major sources of com-
putation, communication, and storage overhead.
Despite their different functionality, these primitives rely
heavily on a common class of input-independent correlated
randomness to lighten online execution: OTs and Boolean
triples for secure comparison and secure AND operations, and
arithmetic triples for secure multiplication.

preprocessing and input-dependent execution, whose storage
and refill costs are quantified in Section III.

III. MOTIVATION AND CHALLENGES
While secure protocols reduce online execution overhead
through input-independent preprocessing, their implementa-
tion on resource-constrained platforms exposes important pro-
tocol–architecture mismatches. Fig. 2 highlights two bottle-
necks we observe in secure nonlinear inference. First, finite
correlation storage couples preprocessing back to online ex-
ecution, causing regeneration to stall the execution pipeline
when precomputed correlations are exhausted. Second, the
asymmetric sender and receiver roles exhibit different com-
putation and data-lifetime requirements, leading to inefficient
resource utilization and correlation processing when handled
uniformly. We quantify their impact and identify correspond-
ing design opportunities through profiling of Cheetah [7] on
ResNet-50 inference with 43-bit secure comparisons.
A. Offline Computation on the Online Critical Path
Modern
nonlinear
protocols
adopt
an
offline–online
paradigm that moves expensive, input-independent correlation
generation off the input-dependent execution path, reducing
online computation and communication. This approach typi-
cally pre-generates correlations in large batches and buffers
them for subsequent online consumption, trading online cost
for storage, buffering, and data movement (Fig. 2 (a)). Our
profiling of Cheetah, however, reveals a fundamental tension
between correlation storage and regeneration. Because each
correlation is consumed only once, the buffer must be contin-
uously replenished as inference proceeds.
On resource-constrained edge devices, limited memory
forces smaller batches, turning correlation processing into a
continuous lifecycle rather than a one-time offline task: fresh
COTs must be generated batch-by-batch, stored in interme-
diate representations, converted into operation-specific forms,
delivered to their consumption points, and discarded after
one-time online use. Therefore, correlation exhaustion induces
a recurring stop-and-refill pattern that brings preprocessing
directly onto the online critical path, accounting for 43% of
the client’s secure-comparison cycles (Table I).
This observation exposes a key design opportunity: rather
than treating correlation generation, conversion, and consump-
tion as separate phases, their lifecycle can be reorganized as a

B. Protocol-Imposed Asymmetries
Secure nonlinear inference processes correlated randomness
through role-dependent protocol steps. Although correlations
can be pre-generated, their online production and consumption
expose three architectural asymmetries: temporal, computa-
tional, and role assignment (Fig. 2 (b)).
Temporal Asymmetry. Sender- and receiver-side correla-
tions are consumed at different points in the protocol execution
path. As shown in Fig. 2 1⃝, sender-side masking and trans-
mission must complete before receiver-side payload recovery
can occur, even under concurrent execution of different OT
instances and preprocessing batches. A sender pad remains live
until the application message is masked, whereas a receiver
pad remains live until the corresponding masked payload
arrives for recovery. Pre-generating full-epoch correlations
inflates the memory footprint across execution phases. While
streaming generation can reduce the buffer size, it cannot
alter these protocol dependencies; the memory subsystem must
therefore incorporate decoupled, role-aware, index-aligned
buffers to manage asymmetric correlation lifespans.
Computational Asymmetry. As shown in Fig. 2 2⃝, COT-
to-ROT conversion also assigns imbalanced local computation
to the two roles. For a given OT index i, the sender evaluates
the CRH on both correlated branches (Hi(qi) and Hi(qi ⊕
∆)), whereas the receiver evaluates only the selected branch
Hi(qi ⊕bi∆). This compute asymmetry manifests after raw
valid COTs become available, constraining performance re-
gardless of how the correlation generator is structured, whether
implemented as a prefilled memory pool, a background refill
engine, or a streaming generator.
Role Asymmetry. Composing role-dependent primitives
into a nonlinear operator creates an application-level dataflow
asymmetry between the two parties. In the single-core Cheetah
ResNet-50 profile, the client consumes 416.0 M receiver-role
COTs and 99.4 M sender-role COTs, establishing a 4.18×
imbalance. As shown in Fig. 2 3⃝, the baseline software
implementation covers this imbalance by mirroring party roles
across alternating worker threads, but at the cost of replicat-
ing complete generator instances. With nt online consumers,
each party maintains 2nt independent COT generators, each
with a dedicated COT buffer and LPN seed table. At nt=4,
these instances occupy 1, 445.6 MB, or 56.3% of the client’s
measured peak memory footprint. Concurrent accesses to the
replicated seed tables further exacerbate the cache pressure
identified in Section III-A. This state duplication exposes a
hardware opportunity for memory optimization.
Together, the three asymmetries highlight potential head-
room for improvement beyond scaling the cryptographic ker-

IV. PROPOSED DESIGN
Throughout, we consider the standard semi-honest (honest-
but-curious) two-party setting used by CryptFlow2 [6] and
Cheetah [7], in which both parties follow the specified proto-
col, and neither misuses correlated primitives.
A. Design Overview
Fig. 3 presents the proposed hardware–software architecture
for secure nonlinear inference. The design partitions execution
between the processing system (PS) and the programmable
logic (PL) according to the protocol’s computational and com-
munication characteristics. The PL accelerates the compute-
intensive input-independent preprocessing kernels, including
COT generation, COT-to-ROT conversion, and Beaver triple
generation. At the same time, the PS executes the interactive
input-dependent online phase, including protocol coordination,
communication with the remote party, and lightweight local
operations. The PS also configures and launches the accelera-
tor and manages the AXI interface through which it exchanges
intermediate streaming data with the PL. Correlations are
tagged by logical OT direction and batch so that subsequent
stages preserve their protocol association and consumption or-
der. This organization integrates hardware-accelerated prepro-
cessing computation, inter-party communication, and shared-
memory data movement within a unified execution flow.
To improve pipeline utilization and reduce inter-stage stalls,
the PL employs a stage-balanced streaming architecture in
which each stage begins processing as soon as its input
becomes available. Separate correlation streams are maintained
for the sender and receiver roles, with correlation generation
scheduled according to the demands of upcoming nonlinear
operations. The design further overlaps online processing
of the current batch with generating and converting corre-
lated randomness for the next batch. Consequently, succes-
sive pipeline stages operate concurrently, and the steady-state
processing rate is governed primarily by the slowest stage

B. Role-aware COT Extension Deferral
Motivated by the temporal asymmetry identified in Sec-
tion III-B, we propose Role-Aware Deferred COT Extension,
which separates interactive preprocessing from receiver-side
local computation. As shown in Fig. 4, the sender follows the
conventional expansion flow required to construct the protocol
messages, whereas the receiver keeps a compact record and
defers tree reconstruction, LPN combination, and final COT
generation until a later processing interval. This transformation
exposes receiver extension as a decoupled, stream-aligned
stage without modifying the interactive protocol.
Role-asymmetric expansion dependencies. Expanding the
full set creates a long-lived intermediate representation: ex-
panded tree outputs are generated during preprocessing but
remain unused until their corresponding correlations are con-
sumed. Deferring this local computation shortens the life-
time of the expanded representation and allows the compact
communication-dependent information to be retained instead.
However, the feasibility of deferral is not the same for sender
and receiver OT roles. Each COT extension epoch processes
T GGM trees of height h and combines their outputs with an
LPN operand derived from the preceding extension state. The
sender must expand every tree during preprocessing because
the level-wise protocol messages and per-tree correlation term
depend on the expanded values. Postponing sender expansion
would delay the interactive exchange, while discarding and
regenerating the expanded values would duplicate computa-
tion. In contrast, once the interactive exchange completes, the
receiver has all information required for local tree reconstruc-
tion and requires no further input from the peer. We therefore
retain the sender’s required expansion but defer receiver-side
reconstruction and subsequent local processing until closer to
COT consumption.
Compact representation. As in the conventional extension,
the receiver performs the same GGM tree reconstruction and
LPN computation; our deferral enables retaining the expanded
intermediate state in a compact form. For a tree of height h
and correlation width λ, the receiver’s compact record contains
h selected λ-bit node values, h selection bits, and one λ-bit
correlation term. Therefore, the retained payload per tree for
the conventional immediate (I) and our proposed deferred (D)
expansion is I(h, λ) = 2hλ and D(h, λ) = hλ + h + λ bits,
respectively. The payload-level reduction is consequently

C. Cross-Phase Batch-Streaming Architecture
The index-aligned COT batches exposed by Role-Aware
Deferred COT Extension enable preprocessing and online ex-
ecution to be reorganized as a cross-phase streaming dataflow.
Rather than generating and retaining a complete correlation
pool, the architecture produces, transfers, and consumes cor-
relations at batch granularity. While the PS executes the input-
dependent protocol for the current batch, the PL prepares the
input-independent correlations required by a future batch. This
organization introduces concurrency across the conventional
offline–online boundary while ensuring that each batch is
complete before its associated online operation begins.
Architecture-aligned batching. The batch size is derived
jointly from the GGM-tree structure and the fixed geometry
of the accelerator datapath, rather than from an arbitrary
transfer size. A GGM tree of height h yields Lt = 2h leaf
positions, while the engine delivers correlations in groups of
Lg = β (cbtg + con) positions, where β is the configured
number of concurrent online consumers and cbtg, con are the
per-group triple and online consumption demand. Since neither
Lt nor Lg divides the other, a batch boundary can fall inside a
tree. This enforces re-deriving any straddled tree in each batch
that touches it and incurs repeated GGM work (2.34× the
ideal number of expansions). We therefore select the smallest
batch containing an integral number of both trees and delivery
groups: Lb = LCM(Lt, Lg).
For the evaluated configuration (n=218, h=8, β = nt=4,
cbtg=11, con=35), Lt = 256 and Lg = 184, yielding a batch
of Lb = 5,888 positions, equivalent to 23 trees and 32 delivery
groups. The selected batch then serves as the common unit
of expansion, intermediate retention, and online consumption,
while the cross-party message size remains independently
configurable.
Cross-phase pipeline. Because correlation preparation is
input-independent, the PL can process a future batch while
the PS executes the input-dependent protocol for the current
batch. After the initial pipeline fill, online execution of batch
j overlaps preprocessing of batch j + ℓ, where ℓis the
bounded lookahead distance. The PS and PL thereby operate
5


as concurrent consumer and producer stages, reducing the
steady-state interval from the sum of their latencies toward
that of the slower stage. Preprocessing latency is fully hidden
only when the future batch completes before its consumption
point; otherwise, the uncovered portion remains on the online
critical path.
The architecture accommodates both full-epoch background
preprocessing and fine-grained deferred processing through a
common batch interface, while preserving each batch’s logical
indices, OT direction, and consumer assignment.
D. Dual-path Correlation Processing
Beaver triple generation consumes index-matched correla-
tions from two opposite OT directions, requiring each party
to process both sender-view and receiver-view data streams.
Because time-multiplexing a single engine between roles in-
troduces serialization stalls and limits throughput, we design a
dual-path correlation-processing architecture. By provisioning
dedicated, role-specific processing paths under a common
delivery control, both OT directions required for Boolean
triple generation advance concurrently without coarse-grained
role reconfiguration. This spatial separation enables the sender
path to construct protocol messages concurrently with deferred
expansion on the receiver path (Section IV-B), while isolating
their correlation state.
Correlation alignment and triple processing. Beaver
triple processing aligns sender-view and receiver-view cor-
relations from opposite OT directions by logical generation
and index. Because the two streams have a fixed generation
offset, a bounded alignment buffer retains the earlier stream
until its matching counterpart becomes available. The aligned
correlations are then combined into local Boolean-triple shares
within the PL.
The generated COTs are split among online OT use, Beaver
triple processing, and correlations required as the LPN seed
table for the next extension epoch according to public pro-
tocol parameters. Correlations assigned to triple processing
remain within the PL, while only completed triple shares and
correlations required by the online protocol are transferred
to the PS. This placement removes the need for keeping
intermediate correlations used exclusively for triple formation
on the PS while preserving their logical ordering and cross-
party association.
E. Dual-CRH Split-stream Reformulation
Motivated by the computational asymmetry identified in
Section III-B, we reformulate the sender computation as two
index-aligned CRH streams. Rather than merely replicating
the CRH operator, the proposed transformation reorganizes
the input layout and its processing into two parallel operators,
thereby preserving branch-level parallelism across the sender
computation.
For each OT index i, the sender computes pad0[i] =
Hκ(s,i)(mi), and pad1[i] = Hκ(s,i)(mi ⊕∆). where s is the
session seed and κ(s, i) derives the CRH key for index i. Ex-
isting implementations commonly represent the corresponding
inputs in a single interleaved layout,
Layoutinterleaved = [m0, m0 ⊕∆, m1, m1 ⊕∆, . . . ].
Directly mapping this representation to parallel CRH units
forces their outputs to converge at a shared FIFO or memory
port and incur multiple-writer contention. Simultaneous results
must then be arbitrated, packed into a wider word, or buffered
before serialization. The shared write boundary can therefore
limit the aggregate processing rate even when both CRH units
individually sustain an initiation interval of II = 1.
We instead transform the interleaved computation into two
persistent streams:
Layoutsplit =
(
[m0, m1, m2, . . . ],
[m0 ⊕∆, m1 ⊕∆, m2 ⊕∆, . . . ].
Each stream is assigned a dedicated CRH unit and an inde-
pendent output FIFO, eliminating branch arbitration at the
CRH write boundary. Both CRH units are initialized with
the same session seed, and advance their local index coun-
ters synchronously from the same origin. Consequently, the
two streams preserve the index correspondence between the
correlated inputs and produce the same indexed pads as the
reference computation.
F. Protocol Preservation
The transformations modify only scheduling and dataflow.
Deferred extension changes when a receiver-side COT be-
comes available, while preserving its value, index, order, and
preprocessing messages. Split-stream processing changes how
the sender-side branch hashes are performed after availability,
while preserving the mapping among each OT index, corre-
lated branch, CRH key, and output pad. Their shared interface
therefore presents the same indexed outputs to the downstream
protocol as the reference execution. Reformulations depend
only on public protocol parameters and the known inference
sequence, introducing no secret-dependent control flow.

Fig. 5 summarizes end-to-end secure inference results. Un-

baseline executes slower than software (0.73–0.75×) at the

are returned to memory. Interflow instead delivers batch-level
correlations, keeping correlations available to the online phase.

nonlinear block by 1.44× and 1.34×, respectively, yielding
end-to-end speedups of 1.21× and 1.17× for a single-layer
BERT-base model. The smaller end-to-end gains reflect the
small contribution of nonlinear blocks to the baseline inference
time.
VI. CONCLUSION
This paper presented Interflow, an algorithm–hardware co-
design that coordinates correlation preprocessing and online
execution for OT-based secure nonlinear inference. Interflow
resolves system bottlenecks by decoupling preprocessing at
its final communication dependency and executing in a uni-
fied, role-aware datapath. Evaluated on the Zynq UltraScale+
ZCU102, Interflow improves nonlinear operator throughput by
up to 1.55× for CNN operators and 1.48× for transformer
operators, yielding geometric-mean end-to-end speedups of
1.28× over the protocol-matched software baseline and 1.72×
over an FPGA baseline without triple generation offload, while
reducing the client memory footprint by up to 37%. These
results demonstrate that managing the complete correlation
lifecycle is essential for efficient secure edge inference.
