# Adaptive Role Assignment and Controlled Feature Reconstruction for Federated Image Semantic Communication 

Yuxuan Chen †, Jiao Bie †, Hang Qi, Jingjin Wu 

Guangdong Prov./Zhuhai Key Lab of IRADS, Beijing Normal-Hong Kong Baptist University, Guangdong, P. R. China Email: t330018009@mail.bnbu.edu.cn; t330018001@mail.bnbu.edu.cn; t330202706@mail.bnbu.edu.cn; jj.wu@ieee.org 

Abstract —Hybrid federated semantic communication enables user equipment (UEs) to contribute model updates or semantic features under a shared training framework. However, heteroge-neous role costs and the interaction between model aggregation and feature reconstruction (FR) remain key challenges in practical radio access network (RAN) deployment. To address these issues, we propose ARC-FedSC, a RAN-aware framework that coordi-nates UE role assignment and server-side controlled FR under a fixed communication budget. Within ARC-FedSC, RAN Aware Role-Bandit learns the historical contribution of UEs in different roles together with current uplink conditions, while Temporal Consistency Alignment (TCA) regulates FR according to recent model aggregation directions. Experiments on CIFAR-10 with time-varying RAN channels show that ARC-FedSC outperforms baselines across all five tested role configurations in the later training stage, with the best configurations achieving about 24 .17 

dB average PSNR over the last 20 rounds. Component level results further show that Role-Bandit maintains an average rolling UE coverage of 85 .3% , compared with about 65 .7% for baseline, while TCA reduces the probability of harmful FR events from 

0.245 to 0.093 , corresponding to a 62 .0% reduction. Ablation and sensitivity studies further support the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed design. 

Index Terms —Federated learning, Semantic feature reconstruc-tion, Radio access network, Client selection, Adaptive aggregation 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

In future RANs, semantic communication has emerged as a promising paradigm for improving communication efficiency by focusing on the delivery of information relevant to the communication objective [1]. For image transmission, deep joint source and channel coding (JSCC) learns the encoder and decoder in an end to end manner, enabling source images to be directly mapped into channel symbols and reconstructed at the receiver [2], [3]. Since the performance of deep JSCC relies on learned neural models, these models may require periodic updates after deployment to maintain their performance in practical wireless networks [4]. Federated learning (FL) pro-vides a distributed approach for collaboratively updating shared

models while keeping raw user data at local devices [5], [6], and has recently been introduced into semantic communication systems to enable collaborative model training across dis-tributed UEs [7], [8], [9]. Recent studies have further explored federated semantic communication in dynamic applications such as UAV swarms and vehicular networks [10], [11], [12]. In practical cellular edge networks with mobile cameras, varying uplink conditions may prevent some UEs from transmitting model updates within the required deadline, highlighting the importance of communication-efficient collaborative training. However, applying FL to wireless semantic communication incurs considerable uplink communication overhead because participating UEs repeatedly transmit model updates over lim-ited radio resources [13]. Accordingly, client selection and radio resource allocation have been widely studied to im-prove the efficiency of wireless FL [14], [15]. Recent works further couple client selection with communication efficient model transmission. For example, FedCG jointly considers client selection and gradient compression under heterogeneous edge conditions [16], while MFedMC jointly selects clients and modality encoders by considering learning contribution, communication cost, and historical recency [17]. In semantic communication, hybrid learning frameworks have explored the joint use of model updates and semantic information for collaborative training [18], while heterogeneous IoT terminals have been coordinated for model training and semantic extrac-tion [19]. For image semantic communication, FedSFD em-ploys semantic feature distillation [20], while FedSFR further adopts a hybrid architecture in which model role UEs transmit sparsified local updates and feature role UEs transmit compact semantic features to the server [4]. The server aggregates the received model updates and subsequently performs FR using the received features. Despite these advances, two issues remain insufficiently addressed in hybrid federated semantic communication. First, existing studies have investigated utility based client selection, communication efficient model transmission, and hybrid se-mantic learning [17], [21], [22]. However, they do not explic-itly consider role-specific historical contributions for model and feature transmission under a shared RAN communica-arXiv:2610.15623v1 [cs.LG] 11 Oct 2026 tion budget. Although FedSFR supports both roles, its role assignment mainly depends on current channel conditions and does not learn the historical contribution of individual UEs in different roles [4]. Thus, how to jointly exploit role-specific historical contributions and current uplink radio conditions under a common communication budget remains insufficiently explored. Second, although FedSFR analyzes the directional relation between the server-side update and sparsification er-ror [4], it does not explicitly coordinate FR with the preceding model aggregation during training. Consequently, the realized server-side FR update may partially counteract the aggregation update. These limitations raise a central question: how can heterogeneous UE contributions from different transmission roles be effectively selected under constrained RAN resources and consistently integrated during federated semantic model training? To address these issues, we propose ARC-FedSC, which co-ordinates UE participation and server-side FR within a unified federated semantic learning process. ARC-FedSC consists of two components. For UE participation, ARC-FedSC maintains separate historical utility estimates for the model and feature roles, and jointly determines UE role assignments according to their learned contributions, instantaneous RAN feasibility, and the shared communication budget. For server-side updat-ing, ARC-FedSC uses recent model aggregation directions to construct a temporal reference for FR and corrects the raw FR direction when persistent inconsistency is detected. The overall architecture of ARC-FedSC is illustrated in Fig. 1. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:  

> ∙

We formulate UE selection and role assignment for hybrid federated semantic communication under a common RAN communication budget, where model and feature roles have different transmission costs and deadline constraints. We then develop a RAN Aware Role-Bandit mechanism that learns the historical contribution of each UE in different roles and combines it with current uplink radio conditions for role assignment.  

> ∙

We develop TCA for controlled FR after model aggrega-tion. By incorporating temporal information from previous aggregation updates, TCA reduces the conflict between model aggregation and subsequent FR, thereby improving the reliability of server-side FR.  

> ∙

We conduct extensive experiments under time-varying RAN channels with multiple role configurations and ran-dom seeds. Under the same communication budget, ARC-FedSC achieves better reconstruction performance than baselines in the later training stage and requires fewer training rounds to reach the target PSNR. It also enables broader UE participation and reduces harmful FR events. Ablation and sensitivity studies further demonstrate the contributions of both modules in ARC-FedSC and their robustness to the tested parameter settings. II. S YSTEM MODEL

In this paper, we proposed ARC-FedSC to coordinate UE role assignment and server side FR under constrained RAN resources. In our proposed framework, role-Bandit combines role-specific historical contributions with current uplink con-ditions, while TCA regulates FR using recent aggregation directions. Experiments demonstrated improves reconstruction quality, UE coverage, convergence speed, and FR reliability over the baselines.
