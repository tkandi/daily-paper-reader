## 摘要
我们研究基于持续到达数据的联邦学习：每个客户端均从其自身分布中采样，这些分布随时间发生漂移，而服务器必须跟踪客户端加权平均损失的最小化器。由于数据收集速率、噪声水平和变化速度各不相同，客户端之间并不相同。核心问题是：每个客户端应保留多少历史数据？单一的共享窗口必须同时服务于所有客户端，因此会使快速变化的客户端承受来自缓慢变化、近乎平稳客户端的采样噪声。我们为每个客户端赋予独立的记忆窗口，其大小根据该客户端的噪声、到达速率和漂移情况确定，并通过从共同起点出发的重启加速方法对其进行更新。对于权重为 $w_m$、噪声为 $σ_m$、到达速率为 $λ_m$、漂移速率为 $γ_m$，且定义域直径为 $R$ 的客户端，跟踪误差可界为 \[R\left(\sum_m w_m^{3/2}\,σ_m\sqrt{γ_m/λ_m}\right)^{2/3},\]。该界从不劣于任何公共窗口所能达到的结果；当噪声和漂移在客户端之间分布不均匀时，它严格更优。一个令不同客户端群组遵循不同漂移时钟的下界表明，该量是恰当的。只要客户端所处的更新尺度数量有界，上下界便在常数因子内一致；在一般情况下，两者也仅相差更新尺度数量的六次方根因子。实验表明，性能增益源于记忆机制而非求解器：无论采用加速方法、随机梯度方法还是单步方法，相对于单独调优的公共记忆基线，这一增益均能持续存在。

## Abstract
We study federated learning from continuously arriving data: each client samples from its own distribution, these distributions drift over time, and the server must track the minimizer of the clients' weighted average loss. Clients are not alike as they collect data at different rates, with different noise, and change at different speeds. The main question is how much history should each one keep? A single shared window must serve every client at once, and so makes a fast-changing client carry the sampling noise of a slow, nearly stationary one.   We give each client a memory of its own, sized to that client's noise, arrival rate, and drift, and refresh it by restarted accelerated method that starts from a common point. The tracking error is then bounded by \[   R\left(\sum_m w_m^{3/2}\,σ_m\sqrt{γ_m/λ_m}\right)^{2/3}, \] for clients with weight $w_m$, noise $σ_m$, arrival rate $λ_m$, and drift rate $γ_m$ on a domain of diameter $R$. This is never worse than what any common window can achieve, and strictly better whenever noise and drift are spread unevenly across clients.   A lower bound that puts different groups of clients on different drift clocks shows this quantity is the right one. The two bounds agree up to a constant whenever the clients occupy boundedly many refresh scales, and in general differ only by the sixth root of the number of scales. Experiments show the gain comes from the memory rather than from the solver: it persists across accelerated, stochastic-gradient, and one-step methods, and against a separately tuned common-memory baseline.
