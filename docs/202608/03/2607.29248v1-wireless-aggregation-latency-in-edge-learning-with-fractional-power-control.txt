# Wireless Aggregation Latency in Edge Learning with Fractional Power Control 

## A. C. Vamshi Karthik 1, S. Tayyaba ∗1, and S. Vanka 11Department of Electrical Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Hyderabad, Hyderabad, Telangana, India 

Abstract 

When multiple wireless edge servers communicate with a common core server, their uplink transmissions create a multiple-access bottleneck that affects the la-tency of distributed edge learning systems. This paper analyzes this bottleneck and investigates the use of fractional power control (FPC) to mitigate it in hierarchi-cal federated learning (HFL). Modeling the spatial deployment of edge servers and wireless channel gains using stochastic wireless models, we analytically character-ize the mean core aggregation latency (CAL) under a TDMA aggregation policy. We formulate the cumulative core aggregation latency as a renewal reward pro-cess, where each learning round constitutes a renewal cycle and task completion defines the stopping time. This representation yields an exact decomposition of the mean cumulative aggregation latency into the product of the expected stopping round and the expected per-round aggregation latency under iid server selection. We then derive analytical upper bounds on the expected per-round latency under fractional power control (FPC). Numerical results demonstrate that even modest FPC exponents substantially reduce CAL across a wide range of deployment scenar-ios. These results highlight fractional power control as a simple and model-agnostic mechanism for mitigating wireless aggregation bottlenecks in edge learning systems. 

Keywords: Power Control; Latency; Mobile Edge Computing; Learning over Wireless Networks

1arXiv:2607.29248v1 [eess.SP] 31 Jul 2026 1 Introduction 

The integration of distributed learning with wireless edge networks has enabled a new class of large-scale data-driven applications. In hierarchical edge learning systems, client updates are first aggregated at edge servers and then transmitted to a central server for global model updates. While this hierarchical architecture reduces uplink congestion and improves scalability, the wireless communication between edge servers and the core server can become a significant system bottleneck. In particular, core aggregation latency (CAL), the time for selected edge servers to upload aggregated updates to the core server, directly affects training time. Since trans-missions occur over heterogeneous wireless channels with varying path loss and fading, their impact on latency merits attention. This paper derives bounds on the cumulative core aggregation latency (C-CAL) over rounds under fractional power control (FPC). Assuming iid server selection across rounds, we formulate C-CAL as a renewal reward process, where each learning round forms a renewal cycle and task completion defines the stopping time. We show this representation yields a decomposition of the mean cu-mulative aggregation latency into the expected number of learning rounds and the mean per-round aggregation latency. This permits the effects of learning dynamics and wireless communication to be analyzed separately. Our findings show that even modest FPC ex-ponents can substantially reduce C-CAL, offering a simple and model-agnostic approach to accelerate large-scale wireless HFL. 

Related Work and Main Contributions: Recent work on wireless FL has studied client selection, communication-efficient learning, and resource allocation [1–4]. However, the edge–core wireless latency bottleneck, particularly the role of channel variability and power control in determining aggregation latency is relatively unexplored. In contrast to prior work that focuses on convergence or resource allocation, this letter isolates the edge–core aggregation phase and analytically characterizes its latency under random wire-less deployments. ## 2.5 C-CAL for Random HFL Workloads 

Let Θ ≜ (H, E, Ξ) denote the randomness induced by the wireless channels, ES participa-tion, and the indices used for stochastic gradient computation, respectively. A random learning workload is the stochastic process ( w(k), T(k)cal ) (indexed by k) that governs the evolution of the global model w(k) and the per-round core aggregation latency T(k)cal , in-duced by Θ under the HFL algorithm described in Section 2.3. The stopping round associated with the model process ( w(k)) is denoted by S, and the mean C-CAL is the ex-pectation with respect to the randomness induced by Θ. Since a smaller C-CAL enables more efficient utilization of MEC resources, we also investigate the impact of fractional power control on the expected C-CAL. ## 5.1 Learning Model 

5.1.1 The Learning Task 

We analyze MC-CAL for the image classification task for two different datasets D: the MNIST [16] and the CIFAR-10 [17]. In each case, the client datasets Dm,n are sampled from D (details below) and are distributed among 100 clients, served in clusters of M = 20 each by N = 5 ES nodes. Every client trains a three-layer CNN of dimension d =21,840 for MNIST and d =5,852,170 for CIFAR-10. The loss functions fm,n in (3) are categorical cross-entropy losses. The SGD minibatch sizes are B = 20 , 64, respectively, with the number of local edge iterations L = 10 , 120, respectively. 

5.1.2 Dataset Distributions 

We simulate two client dataset distributions: (a) IID datasets. Each client receives D

data points sampled uniformly randomly across all 10 classes. D = 6000 for MNIST, 

D = 5000 for CIFAR-10 (test sets are similarly sampled, with 1000 points per client), and (b) Label Skew. The 10 classes are divided among 5 client clusters, each restricted to a subset of labels (e.g., {{ 1, 2}, {3, 4}, . . . }). Clients sample datapoints only from their assigned label subset for both training and test sets. 

5.1.3 Stopping Criterion and Estimating ¯S

Clients send 1-bit feedback per round indicating if validation accuracy exceeds 85%. An ES declares its cluster trained once all clients succeed; training stops when all ESs notify the CS. Mean rounds ¯S are estimated via 500-trial Monte Carlo simulations for server counts K. ¯S exists due to the time-out rule.

# 6 Conclusions and Future Work 

This paper introduced a renewal-theoretic representation of cumulative core aggregation latency in hierarchical federated learning. By modeling each learning round as a renewal cycle and task completion as a stopping time, we showed that the mean cumulative ag-gregation latency decomposes into the product of the expected learning duration and the mean per-round aggregation latency. This decomposition separates the learning dynam-ics from the communication latency, allowing the latter to be analyzed independently.
