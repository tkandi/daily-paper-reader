## 摘要
人工智能的快速扩张正推动数据中心成为全球电力需求增长最快的来源之一。随着人工智能系统在规模和能力上不断提升，支撑计算的物理基础设施正逐渐逼近能源供应、冷却能力、土地利用、淡水消耗和碳管理等方面的关键极限。传统陆基数据中心日益受到城市土地竞争、电网拥堵、环境压力以及漫长审批流程的制约，由此引发了一个根本性问题：未来的计算基础设施究竟应在何处实现可持续发展。本文探讨浮动式与海上数据中心这一新兴数字基础设施模式。通过将计算迁移至海洋环境，海上系统能够利用海洋的天然冷却能力，减少对淡水的依赖，并实现与海上风能、波浪能和潮汐能等可再生能源的直接整合。早期部署已表明，与传统设施相比，这类系统有望显著提升能源效率和运行可靠性，同时为分布式、具备韧性的计算架构开辟新的可能。本文考察海上计算如何重塑电气化、可再生能源与大规模人工智能基础设施之间的未来关系，并分析海洋部署所带来的机遇与权衡，包括环境影响、工程设计挑战、经济可行性和监管治理等方面。本文并不将海上数据中心视为实验性的奇特事物，而是将其视为更广泛的系统性转型的一部分，即社会为下一代计算增长提供能源、冷却并实现可持续发展的方式正在发生改变。

## Abstract
The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence is transforming data centres into one of the world's fastest-growing sources of electricity demand. As AI systems scale in size and capability, the physical infrastructure supporting computation is approaching critical limits in energy availability, cooling capacity, land use, freshwater consumption, and carbon management. Conventional land-based data centres are increasingly constrained by urban land competition, grid congestion, environmental pressures, and lengthy permitting processes, raising fundamental questions about where future computing infrastructure can sustainably exist. This article examines floating and offshore data centres as an emerging alternative model for digital infrastructure. By relocating computation to marine environments, offshore systems can exploit the ocean's natural cooling capacity, reduce freshwater dependence, and enable direct integration with offshore renewable energy resources such as wind, wave, and tidal power. Early deployments have demonstrated the potential for significantly improved energy efficiency and operational reliability compared with conventional facilities, while also opening new possibilities for distributed and resilient computing architectures. The article explores how offshore computing may reshape the future relationship between electrification, renewable energy, and large-scale AI infrastructure. It analyses the opportunities and trade-offs associated with marine deployment, including environmental impacts, engineering design challenges, economic feasibility, and regulatory governance. Rather than treating offshore data centres as experimental novelties, the article presents them as part of a broader systems-level transition in how society may power, cool, and sustain the next generation of computational growth.
