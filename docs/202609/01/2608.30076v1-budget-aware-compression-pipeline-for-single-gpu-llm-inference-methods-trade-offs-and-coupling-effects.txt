# Budget-Aware Compression Pipeline for Single-GPU LLM Inference: Methods, Trade-offs, and Coupling Effects 

Hongyu Yu * 1 Yifei Shen ∗ 21 Lenovo Research 2 University of Washington 

Abstract 

Single-GPU deployment of 70B-parameter language models on an NVIDIA GPU is constrained by device memory, long-context throughput, and engineering integration cost. We cast single-GPU inference as a budget-aware design problem over these three axes and study how pruning, quantization, and KV-cache compression interact under realistic ex-ecution. Controlled ablations show that layer-wise pruning makes weight quantization more robust. KV-cache sparsification complements INT8 KV quantization by reducing memory without hurting decoding speed, while static vector quantizers often conflict with dynamic caching. Guided by these coupling results and explicit budget tracking, we assembled a prac-tical pipeline and compressed a 70B model to about 33 GB, sustained about 57 tokens/s on 10k token prompts on a single A40, and kept absolute accuracy within 5% on common and reasoning benchmarks. We contribute design rules and a reproducible evaluation protocol that jointly report quality, memory, and end-to-end speed, and we provide a foundation for au-tomated pipeline search under realistic single-GPU constraints. 

1 Introduction 

Large language models (LLMs) have grown rapidly in parameter count and context length, pushing the memory and compute requirements of inference beyond what a single commodity GPU can provide in straightforward FP16 deployments. For instance, a 70B-parameter model in half precision requires well over 100 GB of device memory, while widely available accelerators such as the NVIDIA A40 of-fer only 48 GB of VRAM. Long-context inference further amplifies the footprint due to the KV-cache that scales linearly with sequence length. As a re-sult, deploying state-of-the-art LLMs on a single GPU is often bottlenecked simultaneously by (i) memory, (ii) compute/latency, and (iii) integration and compiler constraints (Zhou et al., 2024). A large body of work proposes point solutions in three directions: post-training quantization (PTQ)

Goal and approach. Our objective is to en-able efficient inference of 70B-parameter language models on a single NVIDIA A40 (48 GB) for long-context inputs ( ≥10,000 tokens) under strict quality and latency constraints. We cast the design as an op-timization under a three-dimensional budget: mem-ory footprint, throughput/latency, and integration cost, and develop a unified compression pipeline that integrates quantization, pruning, and KV-cache sparsification. Concretely, our implementation ap-plies 4-bit weight-only quantization, followed by layer-wise weight pruning, and a hierarchical KV-cache sparsification scheme combined with 8-bit KV cache quantization. Contributions. This work makes four contribu-tions. 1. Unified, budget-aware pipeline. We formu-late single-GPU LLM inference as a memory, latency, and integration joint budgeting problem and present a practical pipeline that combines PTQ, structured pruning, and KV cache com-pression into a single workflow that runs on commodity hardware. 2. Design principles under coupling. We char-acterize positive/negative interactions among techniques and distill actionable rules that avoid common failure modes while preserving accu-racy. Each rule is supported by targeted abla-tions isolating the mechanism responsible for the observed gain or degradation. 3. End-to-end validation. On a 70B model, the pipeline achieves fit within 48 GB, supports 

≥10k-token inputs, and meets ≥10 tokens/s with ≤5% absolute accuracy loss, demonstrat-ing practical single-GPU deployment. 4. Toward automated pipeline search. We out-line an extensible search space over quantiza-tion bit widths, pruning ratios, and KV retention rates, allowing Pareto exploration under explicit budgets. Taken together, our results indicate that com-posing lightweight compression techniques, rather than optimizing any one in isolation, is key to prac-tical single-GPU inference of modern LLMs. 

2 Related Work 

Post-training Quantization Post-training quan-tization (PTQ) is the most direct way to shrink LLM weights for single-GPU inference. Weight-only PTQs such as AWQ and GPTQ preserve ac-curacy at 4-bit by protecting activation-sensitive channels (AWQ) or solving a local second-order ap-proximation (GPTQ) (Lin et al., 2023; Frantar et al., 2022). Going beyond weight-only, SmoothQuant reduces activation outliers to enable W#A# con-figurations with small loss (Xiao et al., 2023a). Rotation-based methods (e.g., QuaRot) mitigate outliers via orthogonal transforms for stable 4-bit inference (Ashkboos et al., 2024b). Vector quanti-zation families (e.g. QuIP # and QTIP) push to very low bit rates (down to 2-bit) by codebooks/trellises while maintaining quality, although at higher in-tegration cost in current engines (Tseng et al., 2024a,b). Outlier distribution approaches such as DuQuant and omnidirectional calibration (Omni-Quant) further improve robustness at low precision (Lin et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2023). 

Pruning and Sparsity Structured and unstruc-tured pruning reduce FLOPs/VRAM and can com-plement quantization. Unstructured pruning con-structs sparse weight matrices by ranking parame-ter importance and setting low-importance entries to zero. SparseGPT (Frantar and Alistarh, 2023) es-timates importance using squared weights scaled by an approximation to the inverse Hessian, whereas Wanda (Sun et al., 2023) relies on the elementwise product of layer weights and activation magnitudes. Structure-altering methods such as SliceGPT operate at the width level by removing rows and columns that are provably redundant under an or-thogonal reparameterization of the weight matri-ces (Ashkboos et al., 2024a). Layer pruning as in ShortGPT assumes that the network depth is over-parameterized and scores transformer blocks using BI coefficients, removing those ranked as least important (Men et al., 2024). 

KV-Cache Compression and Long-Context Op-timization The KV cache is often the dominant runtime memory for long contexts. Approaches fall into three buckets: (i) quantization of KV (e.g., KIVI, KVQuant) to 2–3 bits with minimal perplex-ity loss (Liu et al., 2024; Hooper et al., 2024); (ii) eviction/streaming policies (StreamingLLM; H2O) that drop less-useful tokens dynamically to bound memory (Zhang et al., 2023); and (iii) sparse retrieval/compression that keeps only salient KV entries (SnapKV, ShadowKV) or allocates budget adaptively across layers (PyramidKV) (Li et al., 2024b; Sun et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2024). These methods substantially shrink per-sequence memory and enable longer contexts on a single GPU, but differ in throughput/memory trade-offs and engine compatibility. 

Serving Systems, Kernels, and Compiler Con-straints Modern serving systems co-design mem-ory and scheduling to maximize single-GPU throughput. vLLM introduced PagedAttention, which eliminates KV-cache fragmentation with a paged layout and enables continuous batching and cache sharing; FlashAttention/FlashAttention-2 provide exact, IO-aware attention kernels that many engines integrate; SGLang adds program-level optimizations (e.g., radix/prefix caching) for Figure 2: Unified AWQ → ShortGPT → PyramidKV pipeline. In practice, com-piler/toolchain constraints (e.g., static shape re-quirements and kernel fusion limits) also influence which compression combinations are feasible in production engines, an issue reflected in our sys-tem integration. Despite rapid advances in quantization, pruning, and KV-cache management, prior work typically evaluates each technique in isolation. As a result, the field lacks unified experimental designs that measure interaction effects across techniques. For instance, how pruning alters activation/weight out-lier distributions that quantization must handle, or how KV eviction/quantization reshapes attention dynamics and thus the efficacy of sparsity patterns. Existing surveys primarily catalog method fami-lies but rarely offer controlled factorial ablations across method combinations (Zhou et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024a). We address this gap through a systematic comparative study of com-pression methods, quantifying their synergies and interference under a unified evaluation protocol. The resulting evidence identifies robust, mutually compatible combinations that form a principled and composable recipe for single-GPU inference. 

3 Problem Formulation and Budget-Aware Analysis Framework 

Deploying 70B-parameter language models on a single NVIDIA A40 (48 GB) poses fundamental challenges due to the large memory footprint of both model weights and runtime activations. The FP16 parameters alone exceed 140 GB, while the KV-cache grows linearly with the input sequence length (Dao et al., 2022; Kwon et al., 2023). To make such large-scale models feasible for long-context inference (e.g., ≥10,000 tokens) within limited hardware budgets, it is essential to com-press the model while maintaining generation qual-ity and throughput. We explore a spectrum of post-training compression strategies, including quan-tization, structured pruning, and KV-cache spar-sification, and identify an effective combination that meets strict memory and latency constraints. Specifically, we formulate the deployment prob-lem as satisfying a three-dimensional budget en-compassing (1) memory footprint, (2) through-put/latency, and (3) integration cost, inspired by recent budget-aware optimization frameworks for efficient LLM inference (Zhou et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2023). 

3.1 Deployment Budget and Constraints 

The deployment budget is formalized as B =(Bmem , B thr , B int ). The memory budget Bmem 

caps the combined footprint of weights, activa-tions, and KV caches below the 48 GB capacity of the A40. The end-to-end throughput budget Bthr 

mandates at least 10 tokens/sec under 10k-token contexts, reflecting our production latency enve-lope. The integration budget Bint quantifies the en-gineering cost of adopting a method on an existing serving stack (kernel availability, compiler assump-tions, and compatibility with dynamic batching). Model quality is treated as a strict constraint: down-stream accuracy must remain within a 5% margin on common and reasoning task evaluations, such as Arc-easy (Clark et al., 2018) and GPQA-Diamond (Rein et al., 2024). 

3.2 Compression Modules and Decision Variables 

To satisfy B we compose a pipeline from three module families whose hyperparameters de-fine the decision space. Post-training quan-tization (PTQ) covers weight-only methods (AWQ, GPTQ), weight-activation techniques (SmoothQuant, QuaRot), and vector/rotation-based approaches (QuIP#, QTIP, DuQuant, OmniQuant) (Lin et al., 2023; Frantar et al., 2022; Xiao et al., 2023a; Ashkboos et al., 2024b; Tseng et al., 2024a,b; Lin et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2023). Prun-ing and sparsification spans layer removal (Short-GPT), width slimming (SliceGPT), and weight sparsity (Wanda, SparseGPT) (Men et al., 2024; Ashkboos et al., 2024a; Sun et al., 2023; Fran-tar and Alistarh, 2023). KV-cache optimiza-tion includes retrieval-aware caching (SnapKV, H2O, StreamingLLM), hierarchical eviction (Pyra-midKV), and quantized KV storage (KVQuant, KIVI) (Li et al., 2024b; Zhang et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2024; Hooper et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2023b). These modules interact: prun-ing alters activation distributions that quantizers must absorb, KV eviction changes attention local-ity and thus sparsity effectiveness, and vector quan-tizers impose static-shape constraints that can clash with dynamic KV sparsity. Our framework there-fore evaluates both standalone configurations and composite pipelines. Figure 2 summarizes the pipeline geometry and highlights where each module consumes the mem-ory, throughput, and integration budgets. The schematic also anticipates the ordering and com-patibility constraints we explore in later sections. 

3.3 Budget-Aware Evaluation Protocol 

Each pipeline is instantiated on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B checkpoints (DeepSeek-AI, 2025). Measurements capture (i) peak device mem-ory via NVML profiling during 10k-token decod-ing, (ii) generated tokens per second under Trans-formers framework (Wolf et al., 2020), and (iii) integration effort, scored on a three-level scale cap-turing required code modifications (plug-and-play, kernel adaptation, or compiler reimplementation). For quality, we track perplexity on WikiText2/C4 (Merity et al., 2016; Raffel et al., 2020) for screen-ing and task accuracy on QA and commonsense subsets aligned with our target applications. The framework also records ordering effects, such as applying ShortGPT before AutoAWQ versus post-quantization pruning, to surface positive coupling and negative coupling.

4.3 KV-Cache Compression 

KV-Cache sparsification and quantization govern the feasibility of 10k-token prompts on a 48 GB de-vice. Table 8 reports all screening runs on 11 427-token prompts and shows that, on 8B models, Shad-owKV delivers the highest throughput but conflicts with subsequent quantization, whereas PyramidKV and SnapKV provide consistent memory savings with moderate latency overhead (Sun et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b). These techniques also retain low perplexity, validating them as can-didates for the final system. Running the AWQ-Quantized 70B model corrob-orates these trends. Table 9 indicates that full KV quantization alone reduces memory yet slows de-code; adding PyramidKV yields a peak usage drop from 40 690 MB to 39 029 MB while sustaining 57 tok/s, keeping the end-to-end pipeline within the single-A40 memory budget. Without KV optimiza-tion the run exhausts memory before completing 10k-token decodes, underscoring the necessity of pairing sparsification with INT8 cache quantization (Hooper et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024). 

5 Coupling Effects in Method Stacking 

Budget-aware compression requires a precise view of how modules interact once stacked on a sin-gle A40. We observe the strongest positive cou-pling when ShortGPT depth pruning is applied af-ter AWQ quantization. AWQ’s channel protection and scaling calibration depend on activation statis-tics and error sensitivity under the dense model. If pruning is applied first, it reshapes layer-wise in-formation flow, shifts activation distributions, and reassigns sensitivity, making calibration harder and quantization noise more likely to land in critical directions. Quantizing the full model first yields more stable scaling and protection, while subse-quent depth pruning shortens the path over which quantization errors accumulate and propagate. This ordering improves compute efficiency while pre-serving end-to-end accuracy within a fixed prun-ing budget (Table 2) (Lin et al., 2023; Men et al., 2024). A second positive interaction arises between PyramidKV sparsification and INT8 KV quantiza-tion. Integration conflicts give rise to negative cou-pling. Vector-quantization methods such as QTIP and QuIP# rely on compiler-based optimizations for speed and therefore assume a statically allo-cated KV cache (Tseng et al., 2024b,a). In our experiments, enabling these methods disabled dy-namic KV caching; although their INT2 compres-sion preserved accuracy and reduced weight size, the achieved throughput fell below a denser AWQ baseline. Moreover, when combined with KV-cache sparsification methods (e.g., SnapKV), the pre-allocation required by static caches prevented any material savings in the actual KV footprint (Li et al., 2024b). Applying unstructured sparsity such as Wanda after AWQ undermines the quantizer’s outlier-aware reconstruction of weights. In our early tests, this combination produced unstable decoding on mathematical prompts. The model sometimes emit-ted nonsensical symbol sequences, and perplexity rose to the thousands rather than the ranges ob-served for the structured pruning variants (Table 6) (Sun et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2023). Both conflict types inflate the integration-cost budget: developers must recompile kernels, modify graph boundaries, and in some cases forgo dynamic KV handling altogether. Recognizing these cou-plings early allows the final pipeline to prioritize compatibility and end-to-end efficiency rather than isolated, per-method benchmark gains. The contrast between two pipeline explorations underscores these effects. A QTIP-centric pipeline (INT2 vector quantization + ShortGPT pruning )achieved extreme compression on screening runs and hinted at deployment on 24 GB consumer GPUs, yet the combination failed to coexist with PyramidKV, leading to repeated compilation fail-ures and unacceptable integration overhead. In contrast, the AWQ+ShortGPT+PyramidKV con-figuration sacrifices some compression headroom but remains within memory, throughput, and in-tegration budgets on the A40, making it a practi-cal choice for production. This comparison illus-trates the guideline that pipeline-optimal choices differ from method-optimal ones: the strongest standalone quantizer was not selected because of negative coupling, whereas the ultimately adopted trio succeeds precisely because its interactions are benign and budget-aligned.

The throughput budget is met with sustained decode speed above the Bthr = 57 tokens/s target on 10k-token prompts. Finally, all components use open-source implementations with existing integra-tion pathways, so the integration budget Bint is respected: engineering effort is limited to kernel adaptation rather than bespoke compiler work. The incremental runs clarify why the final con-figuration is selected by joint budget satisfaction rather than by a single compression ratio. Dense FP16 and AWQ-only variants exceed the A40 mem-ory budget once the long prompt and KV cache are allocated. AWQ plus depth pruning fits the weight artifact but still leaves long-context decoding ex-posed to cache growth. Adding PyramidKV and KV quantization shifts the remaining pressure from model storage to runtime state, which is the bottle-neck that determines whether the 10k-token setting can run fully on device. 

7 Conclusion 

We presented a unified, budget-aware compression pipeline for deploying 70B-class language models on a single NVIDIA A40 with 10k-token contexts. Our formulation makes three constraints, memory, throughput, and integration, explicit, and uses them to jointly design quantization, pruning, and KV-cache compression. Instantiated with AWQ weight quantization, ShortGPT-style depth pruning, and PyramidKV-based KV compression, the resulting DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B deployment fits within a 33 GB VRAM envelope while maintaining competitive long-context reasoning accuracy. Beyond combining existing techniques, we show that naive composition is insufficient: unstruc-tured sparsity from Wanda yields limited speedups and destabilizes quantized activations, and aggres-sively widening or deepening pruning budgets with SliceGPT or ShortGPT leads to substantial accu-racy degradation. To address this, we introduce continuity-aware layer reordering and layer-fusion distillation, which exploit architecture-specific BI importance profiles to safely extend depth-only pruning to higher ratios. Together with a modular benchmarking protocol, these components reveal where different methods violate the deployment budget and how their interactions shape end-to-end behavior. Overall, our study frames compression for large-scale LLMs as a coupled, budget-driven system design problem rather than a collection of inde-pendent tricks. The resulting analysis and method-ology provide a blueprint for automated pipeline search that jointly optimizes bit-width, pruning ra-tios, and KV retention under explicit deployment targets. Limitations 

Our study is limited to single-GPU inference for a 70B dense checkpoint. Larger mixture-of-experts systems and consumer GPUs with ≤24 GB VRAM would require additional engineering, and their bot-tlenecks may differ from the A40 setting studied here. Our evaluation focuses on English-language QA and reasoning benchmarks. We do not test multilingual generation, safety-critical domains, or tasks requiring calibrated factuality under distribu-tion shift. The integration-cost budget is measured in the serving stack used for our experiments. En-gines with native sparse-KV support or specialized vector-quantization kernels could change the trade-off between memory savings and implementation cost. The proposed recipe is therefore a deployment design for the studied hardware, model family, and 10k-token setting. It should not be interpreted as a universal compression schedule for all LLM de-ployments. 

Ethical Considerations 

This paper studies budget-aware compression and systems integration for deploying 70B-class lan-guage models on a single GPU with long-context support. The primary positive impact is improved accessibility. Reducing memory and compute re-quirements can lower the cost and energy footprint of deploying capable models, benefiting practition-ers with limited hardware and enabling on-device or private deployments where data need not leave a local environment. At the same time, making large models easier to deploy may increase their availability for harm-ful uses, including large-scale generation of mis-leading or spam content. Our methods optimize inference efficiency but do not mitigate underlying model issues such as bias, privacy leakage, or un-safe content generation. We therefore recommend standard deployment safeguards, including access control, rate limiting, logging, and content filtering, and compliance with the model license and appli-cable data governance policies when integrating compressed checkpoints into production systems.

A Additional Experimental Details 

A.1 Calibration Corpora and Tasks 

All quantization and pruning calibration follows the same corpora used in Section 4.1. For the 8B screening experiments we sample 128 sequences from WikiText2 and C4 with lengths in {256, 512} tokens, mirroring the distributions recom-mended by the HuggingFace evaluation harness. For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B we reuse 512 prompts drawn from the training mixture provided with the checkpoint, ensuring that the sequences used for calibration are disjoint from the down-stream benchmarks (ARC-Challenge/Easy, Wino-grande, and GPQA-Diamond). LongBench (Bai et al., 2023) workloads employ the official valida-tion splits with 11 427-token contexts to match the memory profiles reported in Table 9. 

A.2 Hardware and Software Configuration 

All measurements are executed on NVIDIA A40 GPUs with 48 GB of VRAM connected to dual-socket Xeon hosts. We rely on CUDA 12.2, cuBLAS 12.2, and PyTorch 2.3 compiled with com-pute capability 8.6 support. AutoAWQ provides W4A16 quantization, and the ShortGPT implemen-tation is sourced from the official repository. Pyra-midKV experiments use the open-source reference implementation with hierarchical cache size set to 1 024 tokens. Unless otherwise stated, decod-ing uses a batch size of 1 with greedy sampling; continuous batching is enabled during throughput measurements to match production serving condi-tions.

C Reproducibility Notes 

We fix pseudo-random seeds to 42 across PyTorch, NumPy, and the evaluation harness. Measurements are taken after discarding the first 32 generated to-kens to avoid cold-start effects, and each configura-tion is profiled for at least three full decoding passes to ensure stable NVML readings. The preprocess-ing scripts that normalize calibration corpora and the YAML files that define each pipeline stage are available upon request; they contain no proprietary assets and depend only on public model check-points. Hyperparameters for PyramidKV (window size 512, retention ratio 0.25) and KV quantization (symmetric INT8 with per-head scaling) are shared with the code release of the respective papers to facilitate cross-study comparisons. We also log failed allocation states rather than only successful generations. OOM rows in Ta-ble 10 are counted only after the model, prompt ten-sors, and cache allocation reach the same sequence-length target used by the successful rows. This pre-vents a partially loaded model from being treated as a comparable deployment point. The reported pipeline uses public checkpoints and public implementations. We do not redistribute third-party checkpoints, datasets, or derived model weights; artifact access follows the original pub-lic release pages and their stated terms. When a method requires a specialized runtime or a fixed-shape compiler path, we record that requirement as part of the integration budget instead of treating it as a timing-only detail. This convention keeps memory, speed, and implementation assumptions tied to the same deployment setting. For throughput, we keep prefill and decode speeds separate in the raw logs before reporting total tokens per second. This separation matters be-cause cache-compression methods often shift cost between the two phases. The final tables therefore report enough detail to distinguish a method that only accelerates prefill from one that improves the sustained decoding regime targeted by this paper.
