## 摘要
标准联邦学习（FL）需要设备在多个通信轮次中保持持续连接，因此不适用于车辆网络等动态环境，在此类环境中设备频繁加入和离开。一次性联邦学习通过在单个通信轮次内完成训练，提供了一种颇具前景的替代方案。然而，一次性联邦学习存在模型固化问题，难以适应后续的客户端动态变化，包括新客户端的贡献以及先前贡献的撤回。因此，我们考虑精确模型编辑，即无需从头重新训练，便可通过遗忘移除已撤回的贡献，或通过增量学习纳入新贡献。我们发现，高效编辑从根本上依赖于学习稳定性。为此，我们提出F-SOSA，这是首个支持快速且精确模型编辑的稳定一次性联邦学习算法。F-SOSA利用经过精心调节采样率的本地模型子采样实现稳定聚合，并执行曲率感知的模型融合以保持模型效用。我们证明，F-SOSA能够实现趋于零的训练误差以及合理较小的泛化误差界。在此稳定性的基础上，我们进一步开发了两种单轮编辑算法：用于遗忘的F-SOSA-U和用于增量学习的F-SOSA-I。它们保证编辑后的模型及所有中间算法状态在统计上与重新训练不可区分，同时其成本仅为重新训练成本期望值的O(ρ)比例，其中ρ用于量化稳定性。实验表明，在现实的城市移动仿真条件下，我们的方法能够达到与重新训练相当的性能，同时将累计时间开销和通信开销分别至少降低36%和22%。

## Abstract
Standard federated learning (FL) requires persistent device connectivity across multiple communication rounds, making it unsuitable for dynamic environments such as vehicular networks, where devices frequently join and leave. One-shot FL offers a promising alternative by completing training in a single communication round. However, one-shot FL suffers from model solidification and cannot readily adapt to subsequent client dynamics, including new client contributions and the withdrawal of previous contributions. We therefore consider exact model editing, which removes withdrawn contributions through unlearning or incorporates newly contributed ones through incremental learning without retraining from scratch. We identify that efficient editing fundamentally hinges on learning stability. To this end, we propose F-SOSA, the first stable one-shot FL algorithm that enables rapid and exact model editing. F-SOSA leverages sub-sampling of local models with carefully tuned rate for stable aggregation and performs curvature-aware model fusion to preserve utility. We prove that F-SOSA achieves vanishing training error and reasonably small generalization error bounds. Building on this stability, we further develop two single-round editing algorithms: F-SOSA-U for unlearning and F-SOSA-I for incremental learning. They ensure that the edited model, along with all intermediate algorithmic states, is statistically indistinguishable from retraining while incurring only an expected $O(ρ)$ fraction of its cost, where $ρ$ quantifies stability. Experiments demonstrate that our approach matches retraining performance while reducing cumulative time and communication overhead by at least 36% and 22%, respectively, under realistic urban mobility simulations.
