Task Relevance-Driven Prompt Expansion
for Federated Continual Learning
Jiahua Dong, Wenqi Liang, Yang Cong, Yichen Li, Hongyuan Yu, Henghui Ding,
Yulun Zhang, Salman Khan and Fahad Shahbaz Khan
Abstract—Federated continual learning (FCL) has attracted considerable interest by enabling local clients to continually acquire
knowledge from new tasks. However, most FCL methods assume that the global server has prior knowledge of the arrival times and
number of categories for new tasks, which is unrealistic in the real world. Furthermore, they neglect the importance of positive inter-task
semantic relationships when learning new tasks and are significantly affected by irrelevant relationships from old tasks, thereby
exacerbating catastrophic forgetting. To resolve the above challenges, we develop a novel Task Relevance-driven Prompt Expansion
(TRPE) model. Specifically, to eliminate the reliance on prior knowledge of new tasks, we propose an out-of-distribution detector to identify
the arrival of new tasks within local clients, along with a radius-adaptive DBSCAN to infer the quantity of categories in new tasks on the
proxy server. Moreover, to explore positive inter-task relationships, we calculate the affinity between the original gradient for learning new
task and its gradient orthogonal to the feature space of old tasks to evaluate semantic relevance between old and new tasks from a global
perspective. Then, we use this global task relevance to determine the key-prompt pair for new tasks and identify highly related old tasks.
After concatenating their prompts for training, we can leverage such positive inter-task relations to improve the continual learning of newly
arriving tasks. To balance the learning of new tasks and the forgetting of old ones, a soft orthogonality constraint is applied to the local
key and prompt gradients. Experiments spanning diverse datasets and backbones confirm the efficacy of our TRPE model.
Index Terms—Federated Continual Learning, Task Relevance Measure, Prior Knowledge, Catastrophic Forgetting.

[28], [34], [69] unreasonably assume that the global server
knows both the arrival times and the total class number
of new tasks in advance. This assumption violates privacy
protection requirement of FCL [54]. Once this prior knowl-
edge is unavailable, they cannot continue to learn new tasks.
Moreover, existing FCL methods [17], [29], [54] overlook
the importance of inherent semantic relationships between
new and old tasks in addressing forgetting of previous tasks
while facilitating the learning of newly arriving tasks. They
cannot determine which types of inter-task relationships
are effective for facilitating the learning of new tasks from
a global perspective, and the improper use of irrelevant
semantic relations may exacerbate catastrophic forgetting.
To better comprehend these challenges, we introduce an
intuitive example in this paper. The structural information
of the bus learned from old tasks can provide positive
guidance for learning the truck in new tasks, and their
shared characteristics help mitigate forgetting related to
the bus. Some irrelevant relations, such as correlations in
modalities and appearances between the bus and truck,
may hinder the learning of new tasks. To explore positive
semantic relations between the bus and truck, the global
server is assumed to know in advance when local clients
receive novel tasks, which is not realistic in the real world.
Inspired by the example above, we summarize two key
aspects to address the limitations of existing FCL methods:
• How to Address Dependence on Prior Knowledge:
Owing to the heterogeneous data distributions across
local clients, the global server cannot realistically obtain
prior information about when new tasks emerge or how
many classes each incoming task contains. The lack
of this prior knowledge makes it difficult for existing
FCL methods [10], [54] to learn new tasks continually.
Thus, in this work, we aim to enable the global server
to automatically detect newly arriving tasks and the
quantity of classes in new tasks, so that we exploit
intrinsic inter-task relations to facilitate the learning
of new tasks while alleviating the forgetting of old tasks.
• How to Explore Positive Inter-Task Relationships: It is
challenging to manually evaluate which old task has a
closer relationship with the new task, especially when
data distributions across local clients are heterogeneous
in FCL [29], [34], [37], [69]. In light of this, we focus on
developing a novel task relevance measure to accurately
quantify the affinity between each old task and the
new task from a global perspective. This task relevance
measure is essential for exploring positive semantic
relations between the new task and highly relevant old
tasks, while disregarding irrelevant and noisy inter-task
relationships to alleviate forgetting of previous tasks.
To tackle the above challenges, we develop a novel
Task Relevance-driven Prompt Expansion (TRPE) model,
which falls under the category of prompt-based rehearsal-
free FCL [41]. Our model is a pioneering work to eliminate
the reliance on prior knowledge of new tasks while exploring
positive inter-task relationships to address the forgetting of
previous tasks in the FCL field. Specifically, to address the
dependence on prior knowledge of new tasks, we propose
an out-of-distribution detector to identify when local clients
encounter new tasks. Moreover, we design a radius-adaptive
DBSCAN module on the proxy server to accurately infer the
quantity of classes in each new task without prior knowledge.
Additionally, to exploit positive inter-task relations, we
utilize singular value decomposition to build feature spaces
for old tasks and compute the affinity between the original
gradient for learning a novel task and its gradient orthogonal
to the feature space of old tasks. This enables us to measure
the semantic relevance between new and old tasks from a
global perspective. We then use the global task relevance
to determine whether the global server expands a new key-
prompt pair for the new task or reuses the key-prompt pair
from the previous task most relevant to the new one. After
concatenating the prompts of the new task with those of some
highly relevant old tasks, we inject this concatenated prompt
into a pretrained ViT [14] to leverage positive inter-task
relations for learning new tasks. Finally, a soft orthogonality
constraint is imposed on the key and prompt gradients,
achieving a better balance between learning new tasks and
mitigating forgetting of previously learned tasks.
The superiority of the proposed TRPE model is validated
through extensive experimental studies across diverse bench-
marks. The contributions of this work are listed as follows:
• We design a novel Task Relevance-driven Prompt Expan-
sion (TRPE) model, a pioneering work that eliminates
strong reliance on prior knowledge of new tasks and
explores positive inter-task relationships to mitigate the
catastrophic forgetting of old tasks in the FCL field.
• We develop an out-of-distribution detector and a radius-
adaptive DBSCAN to address the dependence on prior
knowledge. They can detect when new tasks emerge
and how many classes each incoming task contains.
• We devise an effective task relevance measure to eval-
uate intrinsic semantic relations between new and old
tasks from a global perspective, which explores positive
inter-task relationships for the learning of new tasks.
• We conduct extensive experiments to validate that our
model has state-of-the-art performance in addressing
diverse class/domain-incremental settings of FCL.
This work extends the methodology proposed in our
conference paper [11] from the centralized continual learning
paradigm to the more challenging FCL setting, encompassing
both class- and domain-incremental scenarios, in which task

RELATED WORK
Federated Learning (FL) [23], [27], [48], [58], [60] is a privacy-
preserving distributed learning paradigm that enables local
clients to jointly optimize a shared model while keeping
their data decentralized. McMahan et al. [38] are the first
to construct a global model by aggregating the parame-
ters learned by multiple local clients. Motivated by [38],
[73] develops a Bayesian non-parametric FL framework
to generate a more expressive global model. To reduce
communication costs [25] between local clients and central
server, [6] introduces an asynchronous learning strategy for
temporally aggregating local clients, while [60] proposes
matching and averaging hidden layers with similar encoding
signals. [52] enforces different local clients to reach a shared
optimum by introducing a penalty regularizer. Additionally,
[55], [61], [75] focus on addressing the heterogeneous data
distributions across local clients. Nevertheless, these FL
methods [23], [38], [60] cannot continually learn new tasks.
Continual Learning (CL) [11], [19], [36], [78] involves
learning from a stream of consecutive tasks without sacrific-
ing knowledge accumulated from previous experiences. As
introduced in [63], it is divided into four categories. Some
regularizer-based CL methods introduce a penalty term to
constrain the updating of network parameters related to
old tasks [21], [30], [46], [74], or they perform knowledge
distillation [30], [44], [51] between old and new models.
The replay-based CL methods store some exemplars of old
categories [4], [33] or rely on generative models to synthesize
some images belonging to old classes [50], [56], [67]. In order
to continually learn new tasks, architecture-based methods
[1], [15], [39], [70] dynamically expand their networks to
encode additional semantic knowledge while minimizing
forgetting. Moreover, some representation-based methods
use contrastive learning [16], [35], [40] or prompt learning
[53], [66] to continually learn new tasks. However, the above
CL methods [11], [46], [70] cannot be directly applied to FCL,
as they require privacy-sensitive prior knowledge of when
new incremental tasks are introduced on local clients.
Federated Continual Learning (FCL) [12], [17], [69],
[72] allows multiple local clients to continually learn new
tasks while collaborating with others without compromising
privacy. Yoon et al. [69] propose an inter-client knowledge
transfer framework to address the task-incremental setting,
while Dong et al. [10], [13] focus on tackling the class-
incremental setting by mitigating both local and global
forgetting. Inspired by [13], some works [2], [41], [42], [76]
employ a data generator to synthesize some images of old
classes for local clients. Additionally, [57] introduces the text
embeddings to enrich semantic information of old classes,
effectively addressing catastrophic forgetting of old tasks. To
improve the communication efficiency, some prompt-based
FCL methods [3], [17], [37], [41] leverage prompt learning
[18], [53], [65] to help local clients collaboratively alleviate
forgetting. However, most existing FCL approaches [10],
[13], [17], [69] neglect the importance of leveraging intrinsic
inter-task relationships to reduce forgetting of old tasks and
improve continual learning on newly emerging tasks.

4.2
Task Relevance-Driven Prompt Expansion
Despite leveraging some prompt technologies [53], [66] to
improve communication efficiency, existing prompt-based
FCL approaches [37], [41], [71], [72] require prior knowledge
of the arrival times and the total number of classes for
new tasks to enable continual learning. This requirement
severely compromises privacy protection and is impractical
in real-world applications. Additionally, the aforementioned
FCL methods [37], [72] overlook the importance of intrinsic
relationships between old and new tasks in facilitating the
learning of new tasks. Therefore, they are susceptible to the
negative effects of irrelevant inter-task relationships, which
may aggravate catastrophic forgetting of previous tasks. To
tackle these issues, as presented in Fig. 2, we develop a novel
Task Relevance-driven Prompt Expansion (TRPE) model. The
proposed TRPE model effectively addresses the dependence
on advance task information in Sec. 4.2.1, while exploring
positive inter-task relations through a task relevance measure
to enhance the continual learning of new tasks in Sec. 4.2.2.
4.2.1
How to Address Dependence on Prior Knowledge
If local clients intend to learn consecutive new tasks, existing
FCL methods [13], [37], [41], [69], [72] require advance
information about when local clients encounter new tasks
and how many classes each incoming task contains. They use
such prior knowledge to initialize the global task classifier
for the continual learning of new tasks. However, it heavily
compromises the privacy of local data, thereby violating
the privacy protection requirements in FCL. Furthermore,
because clients exhibit heterogeneous data distributions in
real-world settings, it is unrealistic for the global server to
know the class number of a new task in advance or to detect
new tasks through class labels. To eliminate the dependence
on prior knowledge, as shown in Fig. 2, we propose an out-
of-distribution detector to identify the arrival of new tasks at
local clients, and a radius-adaptive DBSCAN module at the
proxy server to detect the quantity of classes in new tasks.

4.2.2
How to Explore Positive Inter-Task Relationships
As introduced in [33], [43], [77], when the updating gradient
of the new task is strictly orthogonal to the feature spaces
of old tasks, the learning of the new task does not interfere
with the semantic knowledge acquired from the old tasks,
effectively preventing catastrophic forgetting. Inspired by
this phenomenon, we argue that the affinity between the
original gradient for learning the new task and its gradient
orthogonal to the feature space of old tasks can be employed
to evaluate the semantic relevance between old and new tasks
(i.e., task relevance measure). This task relevance measure
is crucial for identifying positive semantic relationships be-
tween the new task and highly relevant previous tasks while
ignoring irrelevant inter-task relationships. Undoubtedly,
exploring such positive inter-task relationships can facilitate
the learning of new tasks and alleviate the catastrophic
forgetting of old tasks. Nevertheless, existing FCL methods
[13], [17], [37], [41], [72] cannot determine which types of
inter-task relationships are important for addressing the FCL
problem and therefore suffer substantial performance drop
from irrelevant relations between old and new tasks.
To leverage positive inter-task semantic relationships,
as illustrated in Fig. 2, the proxy server Sp first constructs
feature spaces for the t-th new task. Then, Sp incorporates
the prompt gradients collected from local clients to measure
task relevance from a global perspective, addressing the het-
erogeneity of data distribution in FCL. These task relevance
values are transmitted from Sp to the global server Sg and
local clients. On the one hand, Sg relies on these values to
determine whether it should expand a new key-prompt pair
for the new task or reuse the key-prompt pair from the old
task most relevant to the novel task. On the other hand, local
clients select some semantically relevant old tasks using the
global task relevance values and concatenate their prompts
for training, thereby facilitating the learning of task t.

EXPERIMENTS
5.1
Implementation Details
Datasets: To validate the effectiveness of our proposed
TRPE model, we set a series of successive class/domain-
incremental tasks under the FCL setting. Specifically, we set
up class-incremental configurations on CIFAR-100 [22] with
100 classes, CUB-200 [59] with 200 classes, and ImageNet-
R [66] with 200 classes. As introduced in [62], ImageNet-
R [66] is composed of 30,000 difficult examples from
ImageNet-1K [8]. Following [13], we split all images of
CIFAR-100 [22] and CUB-200 [59] into T = {5, 10, 20}
class-incremental tasks, and divide ImageNet-R [66] into
T = {10, 20, 40} class-incremental tasks. Notably, class sets
assigned to distinct tasks do not overlap. For the domain-
incremental setting, , we compare overall performance using
the Office [47] and Digit [29] datasets. The Office dataset
[47] consists of 31 categories and three domain-incremental
tasks: DSLR→Webcam→Amazon. The Digit dataset [29]
contains 10 classes and four domain-incremental tasks: USPS
[20]→MNIST [24]→EMNIST [7]→Fashion-MNIST (FMNIST)
[68]. In FCL, each domain is considered a task, and the label
space is shared across different domain-incremental tasks.
Experimental Settings: In the rehearsal-free FCL setting,
we initialize 30 local clients for the first incremental task.
Starting from the second task, we randomly introduce 10
new local clients at each incremental task. In each global

CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a new Task Relevance-driven
Prompt Expansion (TRPE) model to resolve the FCL prob-
lem. The proposed model not only eliminates the strong
dependence on prior knowledge but also explores positive
inter-task relationships. Specifically, to address the reliance
on prior knowledge of novel tasks, we propose an out-of-
distribution detector and a radius-adaptive DBSCAN module.
They can detect the arrival of novel tasks and the quantity
of classes in each new task without any prior knowledge.
Furthermore, to explore positive inter-task relationships,
we compute the affinity between the original gradient for
learning the new task and its component that is orthogonal
to the old-task feature space, thereby accurately measuring
the semantic relevance between old and new tasks from a
global perspective. Subsequently, this global task relevance
is used to initialize the key-prompt pair for new tasks
and select old tasks that are highly related to the new
ones. Once their prompts are concatenated for training,
we can harness these positive inter-task relationships to
promote the continual learning of new tasks. Then a soft
orthogonality constraint is applied to the local key and
prompt gradients to balance new-task learning against the
forgetting of old ones. Experiments on diverse datasets and
backbones demonstrate the effectiveness of our TRPE model
in tackling both class-incremental and domain-incremental
settings of FCL. In future work, our efforts will be directed
toward designing more effective algorithms that reduce
communication and computational costs, particularly in
federated settings characterized by severe heterogeneity.
