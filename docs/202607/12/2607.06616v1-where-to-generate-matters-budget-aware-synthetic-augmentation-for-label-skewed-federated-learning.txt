# WHERE to Generate Matters: Budget-Aware Synthetic Augmentation for Label Skewed Federated Learning 

Sangwoo Lee, Sunghwan Park , and Jaewoo Lee 

Chung-Ang University, Seoul, South Korea 

{dltkddn,tjdghks994,jaewoolee}@cau.ac.kr 

Abstract. Label skew in federated learning (FL) causes client drift and degrades global accuracy. Synthetic data augmentation can reduce this imbalance; however, full class balancing requires substantial computation cost. We propose FedEAS, a policy that assigns each client an entropy-adaptive per-class generation budget computed from its local label dis-tribution. The budget jointly decides how much each client generates and 

WHERE the samples go. Accordingly, the total generation budget follows from the per-client budgets rather than being fixed in advance. FedEAS recovers most of the accuracy gain of full class balancing while reducing the generation budget by 94.1%. At the same total generation budget, it outperforms Uniform allocation by up to 18.82% across CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. 

Keywords: Federated Learning · Label Skew · Synthetic Data Augmen-tation · Budget Allocation · Diffusion Models 

## 1 Introduction 

Label skew is a fundamental challenge in federated learning (FL) [21,30]. Clients hold locally imbalanced class distributions, so their local updates drift apart and degrade the global model [20, 27, 32, 57]. Optimization- and model-level meth-ods mitigate this client drift with proximal regularization, control variates, con-trastive objectives, and classifier calibration, leaving the local data distribution unchanged [23, 28, 31, 34, 36]. Existing synthetic data augmentation methods correct the imbalance itself, generating the samples that each client’s local distribution lacks [39, 58]. The dominant policy is full class balancing, where every client generates until each of its classes reaches the size of its largest one [39, 58]. However, full class balancing is an expensive policy. As shown in Fig. 1(a), reaching it on a 20-client CIFAR-10 partition under Dirichlet label skew with 

α = 0 .1 requires 226,783 synthetic samples. The cost stems from treating the amount to generate as a balancing requirement rather than a decision. Recent studies relax this requirement by treating generation budget and client resources as explicit constraints [26, 52]. As illustrated in Fig. 1(b), the same total generation budget yields very different accuracy depending on where the samples go. Therefore, we ask the central question of this paper. How much synthetic data does each client actually need, and WHERE should the samples go? 

To answer this question, we propose FedEAS (Federated Entropy-Adaptive Synthesis), which assigns each client a per-class generation budget computed from its local label distribution. The budget jointly decides how much each client generates and WHERE the samples go. A more skewed client receives a larger budget, a near-balanced client receives almost none, and in the IID (Independent and Identically Distributed) limit FedEAS reduces to standard FedAvg [37]. Within each client, generation fills only the classes that fall below the budget. These are exactly the client’s absent and scarce classes. Accordingly, the total generation budget follows from the per-client budgets rather than being fixed in advance. On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 under Dirichlet label skew, FedEAS recov-ers most of the accuracy gain of full class balancing while generating 94.1% fewer synthetic samples (13,437 vs. 226,783 samples on CIFAR-10; Section 4.2). The saving translates into 3.7 × lower end-to-end runtime on a single GPU (Sec-tion 4.3). Accuracy also rises monotonically as the budget grows, so the operating point can be chosen by the available generation resources alone (Section 4.3). This efficiency depends on WHERE the samples go. We therefore compare allocation policies under a matched-budget protocol, in which every policy shares the same generator and the same total generation budget, so that accuracy dif-Budget-Aware Synthetic Augmentation for Label Skewed FL 3

Under this protocol, FedEAS outperforms Uni-form allocation by up to 18.82% across CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, and reaches the 70% accuracy threshold on CIFAR-10 up to 2× faster than Missing-only generation (Sections 4.2 and 4.3). In summary, this paper makes the following contributions. 

– Entropy-adaptive budget allocation policy. We propose FedEAS, which decides how much and WHERE to generate for reducing imbalance in label skewed FL. It gives skewed clients larger budgets and allocates samples only to each client’s scarce classes. The total generation budget follows from the per-client budgets (Section 3.1). 

– Generation-efficient accuracy. FedEAS recovers most of the accuracy gain of full class balancing with 94.1% less generation budget and 3.7 × lower end-to-end runtime (Sections 4.2 and 4.3). 

– Allocation dominance at matched budgets. Under the same total gen-eration budget, FedEAS outperforms Uniform allocation by up to 18.82% and converges up to 2× faster than Missing-only generation (Sections 4.2 and 4.3).

3.2 FedEAS in the Training Loop 

FedEAS changes only how each client builds its local dataset. Algorithm 1 shows the full procedure. The server broadcasts β once at initialization. On first par-ticipation, client k computes Eqs. (8)–(9), generates the required images, and stores them in the cache Ck. The client then trains on Dk ∪ C k, and aggregation remains standard FedAvg weighted by the original dataset sizes Nk.

One-time generation. Generation runs once per client and the cache is reused in all later rounds. The saving over Full-Balance therefore comes from the smaller cache, and the communication protocol does not change. 

Generator-agnostic. Eqs. (8)–(9) read only local label counts. Any class-conditional generator [13, 24, 40, 46] can be substituted without changing the policy (Section 4.2).

In this section, we validate the entropy-adaptive budget allocation policy on both parts of the central question of Section 1. Section 4.2 measures how much 

generation budget FedEAS needs and isolates the effect of WHERE the samples go under the matched-budget protocol. Section 4.3 sweeps the budget parameter, Budget-Aware Synthetic Augmentation for Label Skewed FL 9

4.1 Setup and Matched-Budget Protocol Datasets and partitioning. We evaluate on CIFAR-10 ( C = 10 ) and CIFAR-100 ( C = 100 ) [25], inducing label skew via Dirichlet partitioning with concen-tration α (primary α = 0 .1, with sweeps α ∈ { 0.05 , 0.1, 0.3} on CIFAR-10). We report K = 20 (50% participation) and K = 100 (10% participation), each for 

T = 200 rounds. Accuracy, in this section, is the last-20-round mean, the mean global test accuracy over rounds 181–200. 

Model and generator. The local model is ResNet-18 [15], and each client trains E = 10 local epochs per round with SGD (lr 1×10 −3) and batch size 64. For the primary experiments we train a separate class-conditional DDPM [17,45] on each dataset’s full training set, isolating allocation from generator design. Un-curated samples from this DDPM appear in Section C of the supplementary material. Section 4.2 validates the allocation gap under a generator change with an off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion (SD-turbo) backbone [43], and deployment limi-tations are discussed in Section 6. Remaining hyperparameters appear in Section D of the supplementary material. 

Baselines and budget matching. We compare against six established FL baselines [23, 28, 31, 37, 53, 60] and three augmentation policies under the same DDPM backbone and schedule. Uniform and Missing-only policies are compared under the matched-budget protocol. Each shares the same generator and the same total generation budget BFE realized by FedEAS (Section 3.1), so accuracy differences reflect allocation alone. 

Uniform assigns ⌊BFE /(KC )⌋ samples to every client–class pair and dis-tributes the remainder one at a time, spending exactly BFE samples. This is the equal-allocation rule that prior budget-constrained generation work rec-ommends [52]. Missing-only applies the same rule over absent pairs ( nck = 0 )only. Full-Balance generates until each class reaches max c nck, instantiating Gen-FedSD’s policy [39]. It exceeds the shared budget and serves as an upper-bound reference rather than a matched competitor. Unless stated otherwise, FedEAS uses β = 12 , selected in Section 4.3. Samples are cached before a client’s first update, and aggregation weights use the original local dataset sizes.

FedEAS outperforms Uniform by 2.46% under SD-turbo, against 6.34% under the controlled DDPM, while absolute accuracy drops from generator and downsampling mismatch. 12 S. Lee et al. 

4.3 Budget Sensitivity and Selection 

Accuracy rises monotonically as the budget grows. We vary the budget parameter over β ∈ { 4, 8, 12 , 16 , 20 } on CIFAR-10 ( K = 20 , α = 0 .1). As shown in Fig. 5(a), the accuracy increases from 66.11% to 74.05%, exceeding Uniform by 5.30–7.59% at every matched budget, so the operating point can be chosen by the available generation resources alone. 

The default budget parameter follows from two conditions. The realized budget should remove at least 50% of the initial imbalance score I(0) 

of Section 2.2, and synthetic samples should stay below 25% of the combined training data, so real samples outnumber synthetic ones at least three to one. Both conditions read only label counts. As shown in Fig. 5(b), the budget path satisfies both conditions only between β ≈ 11 .4 and β ≈ 13 .4 on this partition, and the sweep grid meets them only at β = 12 , which we adopt as the default. At this operating point FedEAS removes 51.8% of the initial imbalance while synthetic samples stay at 21.2% of the combined training data. A practitioner can re-run the same selection under different resource limits, and Fig. 5(a) shows the accuracy consequence of varying β.

Efficiency falls as generation proceeds. We define the efficiency η =

∆Acc × 10 3/total generation budget, where ∆Acc is the accuracy gain over Fe-dAvg (55.52%). Across this sweep, η decreases monotonically from 2.575 to 0.788 while remaining 1.7×–2.0× the Uniform efficiency at each matched budget, de-tailed in Section F of the supplementary material. The imbalance that remains at larger budgets is more expensive to remove. Full-Balance, the upper-bound reference, uses 226,783 samples and has η = 0 .108 , so full class balancing raises accuracy but lowers efficiency. 

Wall-clock cost. Under the same implementation on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, FedEAS’s runtime, including one-time generation and FL training, is 3h7m against 11h36m for Full-Balance. The 3.7× reduction shows the budget saving translates into measured end-to-end cost.

The gains trace to allocation quality rather than to the budget level alone. Table 2 compares FedEAS at β = 8 with two variants on CIFAR-10 ( K = 20 ,

## 5 Related Work 

Optimization- and model-level FL under label skew. Methods that do not alter local datasets intervene through the optimizer, representation, classi-fier, or loss [5, 11, 29, 35]. FedProx [31], SCAFFOLD [23], and MOON [28] use proximal regularization, control variates, and contrastive learning, respectively, while FedDyn [2] and FedNova [50] correct client drift through dynamic regular-ization and normalized aggregation. Label-skew-specific methods refine this via restricted softmax [33], logits calibration [56], classifier calibration [4,22,36], fixed classifiers [34], and label-distribution-aware concatenation [10]. FedVLS [14] tar-gets vacant classes via distillation and logit suppression at the model and loss level rather than by augmentation. These are complementary to FedEAS, which intervenes at the data level before local training. 

Data-level augmentation in FL. Existing FL augmentation methods adapt classical augmentation [6–8, 44, 54] but generally do not formulate aug-mentation as a client–class budget-allocation problem. Gen-FedSD [39] balances every client with text-to-image diffusion. Our experiments evaluate this full class balancing as the upper-bound reference. FedMix [53] shares averaged data to approximate cross-client Mixup [55], and FedGen [60] learns a data-free server-side generator over latent features. FedFA [59] augments in feature space and FedRDN [51] injects federation-wide statistics at the input level, both targeting feature rather than label skew, while Hong et al. [19] learn a shared augmentation policy. Budget-constrained generation [26, 52] fixes the total generation budget in advance and allocates it uniformly or by data size and device resources rather than by label skew. FedEAS instead derives both the budget and its allocation from each client’s label skew. Generating only for absent pairs, our Missing-only baseline, is the closest policy but leaves scarce classes deficient (Section 4.2). 

Centralized synthetic augmentation. Centralized studies show both the promise and the setup dependence of synthetic augmentation [42, 47]. He et al. [16] examine its benefits and shortcomings for recognition, Azizi et al. [3] show ImageNet gains from diffusion samples, and Trabucco et al. [48] use diffu-sion edits for few-shot augmentation. These studies motivate the budget-aware premise but not allocation across federated clients. 14 S. Lee et al. 

## 6 Discussion 

Limitations. Fixing a separate class-conditional DDPM [17] per dataset isolates allocation from generator design, but each DDPM is trained on its dataset’s full training set, which limits deployment realism. The SD-turbo comparison of Section 4.2 partially decouples the allocation gap from the DDPM, yet privacy-preserving generator training remains open. Related FL augmentation methods share this limitation [39, 53, 60]. The policy reads only local label counts, so a generator trained with privacy-preserving methods [1, 12, 38] can replace the DDPM. DDPM sampling is also stochastic with no per-sample fidelity guarantee, which adds a generator-quality variance orthogonal to allocation. Quality control such as FID filtering can be combined with the policy. 

Future work. Three directions extend this framework. Federated-trained or stronger generators would address the deployment limitation above. A tun-able exponent ¯nγk would generalize the √¯nk factor. Budget allocation can also be paired with optimization-level corrections, and a preliminary FedEAS+SCAFFOLD run on CIFAR-10 ( α = 0 .3) reaches 78.18%, exceeding either alone. 

## 7 Conclusion 

We recast synthetic augmentation for label skewed FL as a budget-allocation problem. FedEAS addresses it with a single entropy-adaptive rule computed from local label counts. Skewed clients receive larger budgets, samples go only to each client’s scarce classes, and generation stops well before full class balancing. The rule changes only the synthetic cache of each client, and the total generation budget follows from the per-client budgets. FedEAS reduces the generation budget by 94.1% and the end-to-end runtime by 3.7× while recovering most of the accuracy gain of full class balancing. With the total generation budget held equal, FedEAS outperforms Uniform allocation by up to 18.82% on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Allocation therefore decides how much of a fixed budget turns into accuracy, and WHERE to generate is a primary design problem for synthetic augmentation in label skewed FL. FedEAS instan-tiates this principle through the entropy-adaptive, sub-linear rule of Eq. (8), validated empirically in Section 4, where FedEAS uses 5.9% of the Full-Balance generation budget on CIFAR-10 at α = 0 .1.
